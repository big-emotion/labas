# Zanzibar: neighbourhoods and routes

> Built 2026-11-14 from the city file and targeted searches. Research material, not published text. Every named place has a source; nothing is invented. Prices are dated asking prices, not what people pay.

Scope: Zanzibar City (Stone Town and Ng'ambo) on Unguja, plus the island areas residents actually live in (Mbweni, Mazizini, Fumba, Paje, Nungwi), as in the city file. Exchange rate: ~2,920 TZS = 1 EUR (dossier, implied by Numbeo 2026-11-08). USD figures stay in USD. Source numbers in brackets refer to "Sources read"; "city file" = `research/cities/tanzanie/zanzibar.md`, cited with the original source and date it logs. Weekly figures divide a monthly price by 4.33.

Research-pass note: the session's shared WebSearch budget was already exhausted when this city was researched, so all new material comes from pages opened directly (Wikivoyage, Wikipedia, Expat.com, The Africanvestor, Fumba Town, The Rio Times). Wikivoyage listings are often undated or old (several hotel prices are from 2014); they prove a place exists and where, and their prices are flagged. Wikivoyage is not used alone for safety.

Island-wide context (city file unless stated):
- About 98% of residents are Muslim (Wikipedia). Most businesses close during Friday prayers; covered shoulders and knees are expected in Stone Town and villages (Expat.com Zanzibar, 2025-12-09). The UK says authorities can fine "inappropriate" public clothing US$700 or more (UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14). Ramadan 2027 runs about 8 Feb–8 Mar; most restaurants close until dusk [4].
- Power: Unguja receives 135.7 MW against 165 MW demand, with three months of load-shedding before emergency generators and a power ship were announced (The Citizen, 2026-02-23); the 27 Jun 2026 national grid failure also cut Zanzibar (The Chanzo).
- Safety: the UK reports crime in Stone Town, at hotels and on popular beaches, armed robbery and bag snatching (UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14). Same-sex acts and unions are criminalised under Zanzibar's own penal code (Wikipedia "LGBTQ rights in Tanzania").
- Leases often need 3–12 months upfront; shorter furnished rentals are becoming more common (Expat.com Zanzibar, 2025-12-09). Ordinary rentals leave electricity, internet, gas and water to the tenant; backup power adds TZS 250k–600k/month (The Africanvestor, 2026-09-21).
- Transport: ZanBus electric buses R1 and R2 (to the airport) from Kijangwani, 5 am–9 pm, flat TZS 700 by travel card [3]; dala dala TZS 2,000 a ride, boda about TZS 1,000 per 5 minutes (Expat.com, city file); Bolt waits 15–20 minutes, no Uber [3]; shared taxi US$10 a person anywhere on the island [4].

## Neighbourhoods

### Stone Town (Shangani, Malindi, Mkunazini, Kiponda)
- Feel: the UNESCO-listed historic town (inscribed 2000), about 16,000 people (2016 estimate); lanes too narrow for cars, so bicycles and motorbikes; stone benches (baraza) outside houses used for sitting and socialising [11]. Expat.com calls it the cultural centre with street life and the ferry terminal [5]. A blogger describes a relaxed pace and loose business hours (Travelling King, 2024-03, city file).
- Rent: furnished 1-bed US$300–600/month (Expat.com 2025-12-09 [5]; its budget section says US$400–600); Stone Town and Malindi TZS 2M–5M (≈ €685–1,712) (The Africanvestor 2026-09-21, city file); Stone Town 2-bed TZS 1M–2.5M (≈ €342–856) (Expat.com Tanzania 2025-11-07, city file). Small spaces US$25–35/m² a month (Jarnias, 2026-01-28, city file). The sources disagree; not averaged.
- Getting around: on foot inside; Darajani is the main dala dala station [3][11]; the Azam fast ferry to Dar takes about 1 h 45, US$35–50 for non-residents (Expat.com, city file); airport about 5 km south [4].
- Watch: on 9 Oct 2026 the minister halted hotel works in Shangani after residents said lanes were blocked and some homes damaged or demolished (The Rio Times, 2026-10-11, city file). The UK names Stone Town for crime (FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14, city file).
- Places:
  - Darajani Market (main open-air market, spices and produce, Creek Road) — Wikivoyage [3]; Expat.com [5]
  - Forodhani Gardens (seafront park with an evening food market) — Wikipedia "Stone Town" [11]; Wikivoyage [3]
  - St Joseph's Roman Catholic Cathedral (still holds Sunday Mass) and Christ Church Anglican Cathedral (Mkunazini Road, on the former slave market) — Wikipedia [11]
  - Malindi Mosque (built 1830s) — Wikivoyage [3]
  - Mnazi Mmoja Hospital (public referral hospital) — Expat.com [5]
  - Lukmaan (cheap local restaurant) and Passing Show Restaurant (budget, Malawi Road) — Wikivoyage [3]
  - Old Fort (Ngome Kongwe; courtyard shops and daily music and dance shows) — Wikipedia [11]
  - Halai's (fixed-price textile shop, Hurumzi Street) — Wikivoyage [3]
- Fits: Q8 "Je reste en ville" and the culture-minded; Q7 "Prend son temps"; Q4 "Seul·e" or "En couple"; Q5 "Création, culture" (Sauti za Busara, ZIFF, city file). Not Q4 "Avec mes enfants" (no garden space, ISZ is outside town).

### Ng'ambo and inland Mjini (Michenzani, Kisauni, Mwanakwerekwe)
- Feel: the larger post-revolution half of Zanzibar City, with the Michenzani apartment blocks; the city as a whole has 219,007 people on 15.4 km² (Wikipedia "Zanzibar City", city file). This is where most islanders live; no first-hand description of daily life was found (Gaps).
- Rent: The Africanvestor says the cheapest studios and 1-beds are in Kisauni, Chukwani, Tunguu and inland Mjini, without a figure (city file). Numbeo's "outside centre" 1-bed is TZS 264k (≈ €91), from 8 contributors (city file); The Rio Times calls Numbeo's Zanzibar rents too sparse to rely on (city file).
- Getting around: ZanBus from Kijangwani (TZS 700) [3]; Mwanakwerekwe Market is the dala dala terminal for the east-coast beaches [5].
- Places:
  - Mwanakwerekwe Market (market and east-coast dala dala terminal) — Expat.com [5]
  - Amaan Stadium (stadium, Mwembe Makumbi; Brazilian jiu-jitsu competition 6–7 Nov 2026, adult TZS 20,000) — The Rio Times [13]
  - Kijangwani ZanBus terminus (bus station) — Wikivoyage [3]
  - Darajani Market (on the Creek Road boundary with Stone Town) — Wikivoyage [3]
- Fits: Q3 "Serré"; Q7 "Prend son temps"; anyone wanting Swahili daily life rather than expat life. Thin data: verify on the ground.

### Mbweni and Mazizini
- Feel: Mbweni is a settlement about 7 km south of Stone Town, with St John's Church (part of a freed-slave estate, c. 1880), mission ruins and botanical gardens, and a popular day-trip spot [9]. Expat.com describes Mbweni (with Fumba) as newer, family-friendly developments near the airport [5]; The Africanvestor calls Mbweni and Bububu quieter suburban living near town, and Mazizini convenient for the airport and ISZ [6].
- Rent: furnished studio or flat in Mbweni about US$800/month (Jarnias 2026-01-28, low confidence, city file); 3-bed house in Mbweni or Fumba US$800–1,500 (Expat.com [5]).
- Getting around: ISZ is about 10 minutes from Stone Town and 5 minutes from the airport [5]; boda about TZS 1,000 per 5 minutes [5]. No bus route through Mbweni sourced.
- Places:
  - International School of Zanzibar (Cambridge curriculum, ages 3–18, Mazizini; about US$2,925–16,410/year) — Expat.com [5]; The Africanvestor [6]
  - St John's Church, Mbweni (church, c. 1880) — Wikipedia "Mbweni, Zanzibar" [9]
  - Mbweni mission ruins and botanical gardens (heritage site and gardens) — Wikipedia [9]
  - Abeid Amani Karume International Airport (nearby) — Expat.com [5]
- Fits: Q4 "Avec mes enfants" and "Pour fonder une famille" (ISZ, quieter streets); Q2 "Tranquille chez moi"; Q4 "En couple" with a job in town; Q3 "Moyen".

### Fumba Town (Fumba peninsula)
- Feel: a private new town in the Fumba free economic zone, about 15 km south of the airport; over 1,500 residents by late 2025 from more than 50 countries, mainly Kenya, the US and Oman; criticised for gentrification and for being far beyond local incomes (the cheapest unit costs about 24 years of an average local income) [7]. Ground broken 2017, first houses 2018 [7].
- Rent: TZS 2.5M–5M/month (≈ €856–1,712) (The Africanvestor 2026-09-21, city file); 3-bed US$800–1,500 with Mbweni (Expat.com [5]). Purchase: 99-year leases, homes listed US$50,000–500,000 in March 2026 (The Rio Times 2026-09-26, city file); a US$100,000+ ZIPA-endorsed purchase qualifies for a Class C11 residence permit (US$550, no work right) (Fumba Town, 2024-07-04, city file).
- Getting around: about 15 km from the airport [7]; no shuttle, bus or fare sourced (Gaps). Shared taxi US$10 a person anywhere on the island [4].
- Watch: coastal degradation and seawater encroachment are cited as concerns [7]. Island-wide load-shedding applies (The Citizen 2026-02-23, city file).
- Places:
  - IIT Madras Zanzibar Campus (university) and African School of Economics campus — Wikipedia [7]; city file
  - On-site supermarket, gym, coworking spaces, library, food court, beach bar and playgrounds (unnamed on the developer's homepage) — fumba.town [8]
  - About 1.5 km of coastline (beach) — Wikipedia [7]
- Fits: Q5 "J'entreprends" and Q5 "Télétravail" (coworking, Silicon Zanzibar zone, 10-year tax holiday for investors, city file); Q1 "Investir sans y vivre"; Q10 "Retrouver d'autres repats" (international resident base); Q3 "Le prix compte peu". Not for Q3 "Serré".

### Paje and Jambiani (south-east coast)
- Feel: relaxed beach area popular with remote workers and kitesurfers, with coworking spaces [5]. Paje's lagoon suits kiteboarding: side-onshore winds most of the year, shallow sandy water at low tide, a reef that blocks waves [10]. Jambiani is a fishing village strung for miles along the beach [4].
- Rent: beachfront villa in Paje or Nungwi US$1,000–2,500/month (Expat.com [5]); beach villas in Nungwi, Paje or Kendwa TZS 2M–5M (≈ €685–1,712) (Expat.com Tanzania, city file). No 1-bed figure specific to Paje.
- Getting around: about 1 hour from the airport or Stone Town, taxi from TZS 50,000 (≈ €17) [4]; dala dala route 309 (Jambiani) and 324 (Bwejuu) from Creek Road [3].
- Watch: the UK names popular beaches and hotels for crime and reports assaults on foreigners at beach resorts (FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14, city file).
- Places:
  - MedExpress Hospital, Paje (emergency care, surgery, ICU) — Expat.com [5]
  - Paje lagoon (beach, kiteboarding) — Wikipedia "Paje, Zanzibar" [10]
  - Dhow Inn (hotel, Paje Beach, US$65) and Teddy's Place (budget lodge, Paje, US$12–25) — Wikivoyage, undated [3]
  - Jozani Forest (national park on the road from town, entry US$8) and Zanzibar Butterfly Centre (9 am–5 pm, US$5) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Mustapha's Place (lodge, Bwejuu, US$15–60) — Wikivoyage, undated [3]
- Fits: Q5 "Télétravail" + Q4 "Seul·e"; Q8 "À la plage"; Q7 "Prend son temps"; Q2 "Tranquille chez moi". Risky for anyone who needs reliable power for client deadlines.

### Nungwi and Kendwa (north)
- Feel: beach areas with nightlife, diving centres and resort amenities [5]; Nungwi is a small resort town on the northern tip, Kendwa a small fishing village nearby [4]. Wikipedia notes litter on streets and beaches is a problem, especially in the north (city file).
- Rent: Nungwi and Kendwa TZS 4M–8M/month (≈ €1,370–2,740) (The Africanvestor 2026-09-21, city file), the island's dearest.
- Getting around: about 1½ h from Stone Town, from TZS 69,789 by taxi; airport–Nungwi TZS 65,000; dala dala about 2 h [4], route 116 from Creek Road [3].
- Watch: a US$145M borehole project aims at round-the-clock water in North Unguja on a 2–2.5 year timeline, implying supply is not 24 h today (Daily News, Oct 2026, city file).
- Places:
  - Kendwa Beach (swimmable at low and high tide; sea urchins at low tide) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Kendwa Full Moon Party (beach party on the Saturday near the full moon) — Wikivoyage [4]; a 21 Nov 2026 date is unconfirmed by any organiser (The Rio Times [13])
  - The Zanzibari (hotel, Nungwi) — Wikivoyage [3]
- Fits: Q2 "Danser jusqu'au matin"; Q8 "À la plage"; Q3 "Le prix compte peu". Thin on everyday services (no school, clinic or market sourced).

## Living: typical weeks

Arithmetic convention: weekly = monthly ÷ 4.33; EUR at 2,920 TZS; USD kept apart. Numbeo's Zanzibar prices rest on 8 contributors (low confidence).

### 1. Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "Télétravail" + Q4 "Seul·e"
- Base: Paje, the area sources name for remote workers and coworking [5]; fallback Stone Town for cheaper rent and town life.
- Week:
  - Mon: work at home with a backup-power plan (load-shedding, city file); home internet about TZS 130k/month, the dearest of the three cities (Numbeo, city file); Vodacom described as the most reliable mobile network (Expat.com, city file).
  - Tue: a Paje coworking space (exists per Expat.com [5]; none named or priced); lunch at a local place.
  - Wed: kitesurf lesson on the lagoon [10] (price not sourced).
  - Thu: shared taxi to Stone Town (US$10 each way [4]) for errands and Darajani Market [3].
  - Fri: midday Friday prayers close most businesses (Expat.com, city file): plan work around it.
  - Sat: see below.
  - Sun: Jozani Forest walk (US$8) on the way back from town [4].
- Saturday night:
  - "Danser jusqu'au matin": Kendwa Full Moon Party near the full moon [4] (1½–2 h away); or Tatu's upstairs nightclub, Shangani [3].
  - "Un bon dîner entre amis": Forodhani night market in town [3] or Emerson on Hurumzi's set menu with taarab, booking needed [3].
  - "Tranquille chez moi": the default in Paje (relaxed beach area [5]).
- Weekend outing:
  - "À la plage": on the doorstep (Paje, Jambiani, Bwejuu) [4].
  - "En montagne, en forêt": forest yes, mountain no: Jozani Chwaka Bay National Park (red colobus, mangroves; est. 2004) [12], entry US$8 [4].
  - "Dans la savane ou le désert": not realistic from Zanzibar for a weekend; it means a flight or ferry to the mainland, and no fare or park route was sourced.
  - "Je reste en ville": Stone Town museums (see Visiting).
- Rough weekly spend:
  - Rent: island 1-bed TZS 1.2M–2.8M ÷ 4.33 = 277,100–646,700 (≈ €95–221) (The Africanvestor, city file). Paje-specific figures exist only for villas (US$1,000–2,500).

  - Meals: 7 cheap meals × 12,500 = 87,500; half a dinner for two = 28,750 (Numbeo, city file). (Cooking the rest at home; no grocery basket for Paje.)
  - Utilities: US$80–150/month ÷ 4.33 = US$18–35 (Expat.com, city file). Two shared-taxi rides to town × US$10 = US$20 [4].
  - Backup power (optional): TZS 250k–600k/month ÷ 4.33 = 57,700–138,600 (The Africanvestor, city file).
  - Total: TZS 423,350–792,950 (≈ €145–272) + US$40–57, plus backup power if needed.

## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances". Dress: cover legs and arms away from resort beaches; be discreet with alcohol; public displays of affection are considered disrespectful [4]. Fines for "inappropriate" clothing (UK FCDO, city file).

### One day (Stone Town)
- Morning: Darajani Market (Creek Road) [3]; walk to Christ Church Anglican Cathedral and the Slave Monument (cathedral US$3 per Wikivoyage, US$5 per Expat.com; slave market US$5 in May 2018) [3][5][11].
- Midday: lunch at Lukmaan [3]; Old Fort courtyard [11].
- Afternoon: Palace Museum on the seafront [3][11]; Freddie Mercury Museum, Kenyatta Road (10 am–6 pm, US$10) [3][5]. All on foot.
- Evening: sunset at Africa House's Sunset Bar, Shangani [3]; Forodhani Gardens night market (Zanzibar pizza TZS 3,000–6,000, city file) [3].

### One week
- Day 1: Stone Town as above.
- Day 2 ("what daily life looks like"): early morning at Darajani as the stalls open and dala dalas leave for the villages [3]; then ZanBus R1 through Ng'ambo (TZS 700) [3] to see the Michenzani blocks (city file).
- Day 3: spice tour: Mitus Spice Tour, Malindi Road, half day with lunch, US$12–25 [3]; or Kizimbani farms about TZS 22,500 for two [4].
