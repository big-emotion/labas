# Marrakech: neighbourhoods and routes

> Built 2026-11-14 from the city file and targeted searches. Research material, not published text. Every named place has a source; nothing is invented. Prices are dated asking prices, not what people pay.

Conventions. EUR at the dossier's editorial rate, 1 EUR ≈ 10.8 MAD (unsourced in the dossier). "City file" means `research/cities/maroc/marrakech.md`; its source is named after it. Monthly figures are turned into weekly ones by dividing by 4.3. USD prices from the city file are converted at the Rio Times rate of about 9.4 MAD per USD (Sep 2026, in the Casablanca city file). Source numbers [n] refer to "Sources read". ## Neighbourhoods

### Guéliz (modern centre)

- Feel: the oldest part of the new town, planned in 1913; since the early 2000s most colonial villas have given way to apartment blocks; main shops along avenue Mohammed V [1]. Expat.com calls it the most European-feeling area and the easiest for newcomers, with supermarkets, banks and the widest range of international restaurants [2] (upd. 2025-12-08). OpenShores: popular with working expats, flats with lift, parking and security [3] (2026-03-10). 188,333 residents in the arrondissement in 2014 [1].
- Rent: studio 3,000–4,500 MAD (≈ €278–417), 1-bed 4,000–7,000 (≈ €370–648), 2-bed 6,500–11,000 (≈ €602–1,019), 3-bed 10,000–18,000 (≈ €926–1,667) a month [3] (also in the city file); ReaConsult 62–95 MAD/m²/month (city file, 2026).
- Getting around: metered petit taxi from the station to Jemaa el-Fna about 12–13 MAD by day, about 20 MAD seen as a good deal [4]; Guéliz to the medina about 10–15 MAD (city file, Wikivoyage). City bus no. 1 runs to Guéliz [4]; ticket 5 MAD until the end of AFCON, increases approved after (city file, Morocco World News 2025-12-18).
- Watch: heightened vigilance over motorbike thieves snatching bags and phones in Marrakech (France Diplomatie 2026-09-15, city file). Summer: mean highs 37.7 °C in July (city file, Wikipedia); air conditioning raises bills to 800–1,500 MAD a month (city file, OpenShores).
- Places:
  - Carré Eden (shopping centre, opened 2014 on the site of the old Guéliz market) — French Wikipedia [1]
  - Cafés Les Négociants and La Renaissance (colonial-era cafés, avenue Mohammed V × boulevard Zerktouni) — French Wikipedia [1]
  - Église des Saints-Martyrs (Catholic church, served by Franciscans; the only church in the region) — French Wikipedia [5]
  - Jardin Majorelle (garden, Rue Yves Saint Laurent; 170 MAD, Berber Museum +30, combined with the YSL Museum 180) — Wikipedia [6], Wikivoyage [4]
  - Café du Livre (café, sandwiches about 40–60 MAD) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Le Grand Café de la Poste (restaurant, about 300 MAD for three courses) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Colisée (cinema, boulevard Zerktouni; hosts the film festival) — French Wikipedia [1]
  - Ô Bain de Lina (hammam, 500 MAD) — Wikivoyage [4]
- Fits: Q5 "Télétravail" (a blog lists Guéliz as the most reliable fibre area and some cafés with 20+ Mbps Wi-Fi, city file); Q1 "Tester quelques mois" (furnished stock, easiest start [2]); Q2 "Un bon dîner entre amis"; Q3 "Moyen".

### Hivernage (quiet, upscale)

- Feel: affluent area west of Bab Jdid and south of Guéliz with luxury villas and large hotel complexes [7]; Expat.com: luxury and quiet, near the main sights [2]; OpenShores: calmer and more residential than Guéliz, higher-standing flats [3].
- Rent: 1-bed 6,000–9,000 MAD (≈ €556–833), 2-bed 9,000–14,000 (≈ €833–1,296) [3]; ReaConsult 75–115 MAD/m²/month, the city's highest band (city file).
- Getting around: walking distance to the medina walls (Bab Jdid) [7]; petit taxis.
- Watch: tourist-rental conversions in upscale areas cut family supply and pushed rents up (agency owner via Bladi, 2025-08-22, city file).
- Places:
  - Stade El Harti (sports ground, between Guéliz and Hivernage) — French Wikipedia [7]
  - Menara Gardens (historic olive orchard and reservoir west of the walls; free) — Wikipedia [8], Wikivoyage [4]
  - Menara Mall (mall, on the L19 airport bus route) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Comptoir Darna (cocktail bar and nightclub, avenue Echouhada, outside the medina) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Only four places could be tied to Hivernage or its edge (see Gaps).
- Fits: Q3 "Le prix compte peu"; Q2 "Tranquille chez moi" with nightlife a short walk away; Q4 "En couple".

### Médina (historic core)

- Feel: walled historic centre of about 600 ha, with the Mellah in the south-east [7]; riads, souks and UNESCO heritage [2]. 4 % of quake-affected households in the city had not started rebuilding in Sep 2025 (Hespress, city file).
- Rent: medina 1-bed $250–400 (≈ 2,350–3,760 MAD) and renovated riad $400–700 (getwherenext 2026-02, city file, low confidence); 2-bed basic riad 8,000–11,000 MAD (≈ €741–1,019), renovated 3-bed 11,000–16,000 [3].
- Getting around: on foot; taxis wait at the gates.
- Watch: people posing as official guides harass visitors in medinas; scams and card fraud are common (FCDO, city file). Jemaa el-Fna was the site of the 2011 bombing that killed 17 (France Diplomatie 2026-09-15, city file). France Diplomatie advises knowing what to do in an earthquake in the Marrakech region (city file).
- Places:
  - Jemaa el-Fna (square and evening food stalls, about 10–100 MAD a meal) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Koutoubia Mosque (mosque, closed to non-Muslims) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Hamam Essalama (local hammam, 12 MAD entry, 50 MAD scrub) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Chez Chegrouni (restaurant, about 30 MAD) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Henna Café (café-restaurant, Bab Doukkala, mains about 40 MAD) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Le 18 Derb El Ferrane (coworking; day pass $5–10) — city file, getwherenext (blog)
  - Le Salama (bar, Rue des Banques, happy hour from 17:00) — Wikivoyage [4]
- Fits: Q1 "D'abord des vacances" more than living; Q5 "Création, culture" (the city file reports an artistic expat scene, OpenShores, low confidence); Q7 "Vibre jour et nuit". Less suited to Q4 "Avec mes enfants" (no car access, crowds).

### Targa and the Route de la Targa (families, west)

- Feel: upscale villas and planned residential estates in the west [7]; near the Jardin Majorelle, popular with families [2].
- Rent: ReaConsult reference for Targa/Route de l'Ourika 38–58 MAD/m²/month (≈ €3.5–5.4), the cheapest band it lists (city file); a 3-bed citywide is 5,000–10,000 MAD (≈ €463–926) (city file, Expat.com Dec 2025). No Targa listing sample was found.
- Getting around: car or petit taxi; no bus line to Targa was confirmed.
- Places:
  - Lycée Victor Hugo (AEFE directly-run school, nursery to bac; Route de la Targa / Guéliz) — school website [9]
  - Jardin Majorelle (nearest landmark) — Expat.com [2]
  - Only two places could be tied to Targa (see Gaps).
- Fits: Q4 "Avec mes enfants" (French school on the road, family housing); Q2 "Tranquille chez moi"; Q7 "Prend son temps".

### Palmeraie (villas, north)

- Feel: luxury villas and contemporary riads with pools and gardens [3].
- Rent: 3-bed villa without pool 12,000–18,000 MAD (≈ €1,111–1,667); 4-bed villa with pool 18,000–30,000 (≈ €1,667–2,778) [3]; ReaConsult 65–95 MAD/m² (city file).
- Getting around: taxi Guéliz to Palmeraie 30–50 MAD (city file, OpenShores); a car in practice (Expat.com says many expats rent or buy one [2]).
- Places:
  - Musée de la Palmeraie (museum, 40 MAD) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Le Grand Salon (restaurant, about 600 MAD for three courses) — Wikivoyage [4]
  - Chez Ali Fantasia (evening horse show, about 10 km north, €15) — Wikivoyage [4]
- Fits: Q3 "Le prix compte peu"; Q4 "Avec mes enfants" with a car and a big budget; Q7 "Prend son temps".

### Daoudiate, Massira and M'hamid (popular districts)

- Feel: Daoudiate is a popular northern district from the 1960s–70s, near the Cadi Ayyad faculties in Amerchich; Massira is a planned 1980s district in the west with commercial streets; M'hamid is a large popular district beyond the airport with over 100,000 residents [7].
- Rent: no district figure found. Bladi (2025-08-22) puts a small flat citywide at 4,000–5,000 MAD and reports a tenant in a working-class area finding a 30 m² studio at nearly 4,500 MAD too expensive (city file, anecdote).
- Getting around: city buses (5 MAD, new Supratours fleet with air conditioning from Dec 2025, city file); riders complained of long waits at poorly kept stops (Bladi 2025-12-14, city file).
- Places:
  - Université Cadi Ayyad faculties of letters, humanities and law (Amerchich, north-west of Daoudiate) — French Wikipedia [7]
  - Stade de Marrakech (Kawkab Marrakech's ground, about 11 km north of the centre) — Wikipedia [10]
  - Only two places sourced (see Gaps).
- Fits: Q3 "Serré" (if a rent sample confirms lower prices); Q5 "Santé, enseignement" near the university.

## Living: typical weeks

Weekly totals use only prices in the city file or in pages read in this pass; quantities are assumptions and stated.

### Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "Télétravail" + Q4 "Seul·e"

- Base: Guéliz (fibre, cafés, walkable shops [1][2]).
- Week:
  - Mon: work from home; morning coffee at Les Négociants [1].
  - Tue: coworking day (Le 18, Sun Desk or The Spot are named by a blog; only Le 18's address is known) — taxi to the medina 10–15 MAD.
  - Wed: work at Café du Livre [4]; groceries (the city file names Marjane, Acima and Carrefour, OpenShores).
  - Thu: gym (300–700 MAD/month, OpenShores; no named gym found).
  - Fri: Friday is the main rest day (city file, Expat.com); evening at Le Grand Café de la Poste [4].
  - Sat: see options.
  - Sun: Menara Gardens walk (free) [4].
  - In June–September plan work indoors 11:00–17:00 (city file, OpenShores).
- Saturday night:
  - "Danser jusqu'au matin": Comptoir Darna or Montecristo (Rue Ibn Aicha) [4]; alcohol is mainly sold in licensed venues inside hotels and resorts (city file, Expat.com).
  - "Un bon dîner entre amis": Al Fassia (boulevard Zerktouni) [4] or the Jemaa el-Fna stalls at 10–100 MAD [4].
  - "Tranquille chez moi": a film at the UGC cinema (60–90 MAD, city file) or the Colisée [1]; a scrub at Hamam Essalama (12 + 50 MAD) [4].
- Weekend outing:
  - "À la plage": Essaouira, bus about 3 h, 55 MAD (local) or 80 MAD (CTM) [4].
  - "En montagne, en forêt": Imlil, 50 km south, base for Toubkal, at about 1,800 m [11]; Ourika valley and Oukaimeden (ski lift at 3,268 m) [4]; minibuses leave from Jardin Sidi Mimoun (no fare given) [4].
  - "Dans la savane ou le désert": Agafay desert day trip (city file, Expat.com; Wikivoyage, no price).
  - "Je reste en ville": Saadian Tombs and Bahia Palace at the resident rate of 30 MAD instead of 100 [4].
- Weekly spend (MAD): Guéliz 1-bed 4,000–7,000 ÷ 4.3 = 930–1,628 [3] · groceries 1,000–2,500 ÷ 4.3 = 233–581 (Expat.com) · fibre 250–400 ÷ 4.3 = 58–93 (OpenShores) · power and water 350–700 ÷ 4.3 = 81–163 (OpenShores; up to 349 in summer) · coworking $60–120/month × 9.4 ÷ 4.3 = 131–262 (getwherenext) · 3 local meals × 50 = 150 (OpenShores) · one dinner at 300 (Wikivoyage) · 4 taxi rides × 10–20 = 40–80 (OpenShores) · gym 300–700 ÷ 4.3 = 70–163. Total ≈ 1,993–3,420 MAD (≈ €185–317). Cross-check: Expat.com's single person at about $900/month ≈ 8,460 MAD ≈ 1,967 a week.

### Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "Salarié·e" + Q4 "En couple"

- Base: Guéliz, close to the hotel, school and tour-operator employers named by Expat.com (Marriott, Hilton, language and international schools) (city file). Hivernage if the budget allows [3].
- Week:
  - Mon–Fri: work; commute by petit taxi or bus (5 MAD). Business hours often stop in the afternoon heat and reopen in the evening (city file, Expat.com).
  - Wed: drink at Chesterfield Pub, Hotel Nassim, avenue Mohammed V [4].
  - Fri: Friday is the main rest day, so plan errands around it (city file, Expat.com).
  - Sat: see options. Sun: Majorelle (170 MAD each) or the free Menara Gardens.
- Saturday night: as profile 1; plus Sky Lounge at the Pearl Hotel [4].
- Weekend outing: as profile 1.
- Weekly spend (MAD): Guéliz 2-bed 6,500–11,000 ÷ 4.3 = 1,512–2,558 [3] · groceries for two, markets plus supermarkets 2,500–4,000 ÷ 4.3 = 581–930 (OpenShores) · power and water 81–163 · fibre 58–93 · 10 bus tickets each × 5 × 2 = 100 · two dinners for two at Le Grand Café de la Poste 4 × 300 ÷ 2 = 600 · cinema 2 × 60–90 = 120–180. Total ≈ 3,052–4,624 MAD (≈ €283–428). Expat.com warns employer-sponsored work permits can take months; secure the job first (city file).

### Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "J'entreprends"

- Base: Guéliz, with days in the medina. Marrakech has no Technopark site (dossier); a blog says that outside hotels and schools, starting a business with capital is often foreigners' only route (city file, OpenShores, low confidence).
- Week:
  - Mon, Wed: Le 18 coworking day passes in the medina.
  - Tue: client lunches at Café du Livre [4].
  - Thu: paperwork; a blog says residence and work permits move more slowly here than in Casablanca or Rabat (city file, low confidence).
  - Fri: networking dinner at Le Grand Café de la Poste [4].
  - Weekend as profile 1.
- Weekly spend (MAD): Guéliz 1-bed 930–1,628 · groceries 233–581 · fibre 58–93 · power and water 81–163 · 5 coworking day passes × $5–10 × 9.4 = 235–470 · 3 lunches × 40–60 = 120–180 · 6 taxi rides × 10–15 = 60–90 · one dinner 300. Total ≈ 2,017–3,505 MAD (≈ €187–325).

### Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q4 "Avec mes enfants"

- Base: Targa (families, Lycée Victor Hugo on the Route de la Targa [2][9]). For an American curriculum, the American School of Marrakesh is at km 9 on the Route de Ouarzazate, PK–12 with IB [12]; British Academy School Marrakech is also named (location not found) (city file).
- Week:
  - Mon–Fri: school run by car; schools have waiting lists, especially in primary (city file, Expat.com).
  - Wed: Carré Eden or Menara Mall [1][4].
  - Sat: Jardin Majorelle or a Palmeraie outing (Chez Ali horse show, €15 [4]).
  - Sun: Mass at Église des Saints-Martyrs [5] or Friday at the neighbourhood mosque; Menara Gardens picnic.
- Saturday night: "Danser jusqu'au matin" as profile 1 with a sitter (live-in housekeeper $200–300/month, getwherenext, city file); "Un bon dîner entre amis" at Al Fassia [4]; "Tranquille chez moi" family cinema.
- Weekend outing: as profile 1; Oukaimeden for snow in winter [4].
- Weekly spend (MAD), two children: 3-bed 5,000–10,000 ÷ 4.3 = 1,163–2,326 (Expat.com) · school fees 2 × 40,000–120,000 ÷ 52 = 1,538–4,615 (Expat.com range across the three schools) · groceries, twice the couple figure, 5,000–8,000 ÷ 4.3 = 1,163–1,860 (OpenShores; the doubling is an assumption) · power and water in summer 800–1,500 ÷ 4.3 = 186–349 (OpenShores) · fibre 58–93. Total ≈ 4,108–9,243 MAD (≈ €380–856), the spread coming mostly from school choice. Lycée Victor Hugo's 2026–27 fees could not be read (scanned PDF, city file).

### Q1 "Tester quelques mois": the first week

- Where to stay first: Guéliz, the easiest area for newcomers [2]. No nightly hotel or riad price was sourced (see Gaps).
- Day 1: airport bus L19 to town, 30 MAD one way, every 30 min, 06:00–23:30, 15–20 min; avoid the fixed 200 MAD terminal taxi (about 60 MAD if hailed outside the car park) [4].
- Day 2: SIM card. Prepaid SIMs are easy to get from Maroc Telecom, Orange and Inwi (national, Tanger city file); no Marrakech mobile-data price was found (gap in the city file). No mobile-money service found.
- Day 3–5: flat hunting on Avito.ma, Facebook groups and through neighbourhood shopkeepers (Expat.com 2022, city file). Deposit is two months plus one month in advance; standard lease one year; furnished flats are widely available (Expat.com Dec 2025, city file); agencies usually charge one month (Expat.com 2022). Rent can be renegotiated face to face (city file, low confidence). No separate visit fee was found.
- Day 6: bank: convertible dirham accounts need a passport, residence permit and proof of income (national, Casablanca city file, low confidence); carte de séjour takes 1–3 months (dossier).
- Day 7: Jemaa el-Fna in the evening; Hamam Essalama.
- Spend for the week (MAD, excluding lodging): L19 bus 30 · 14 local meals × 50 = 700 (OpenShores) · groceries 233–581 · 6 taxi rides × 10–20 = 60–120. Total ≈ 1,023–1,431 MAD (≈ €95–133) plus lodging. If a Guéliz 1-bed is signed: deposit 2 + advance 1 + agency fee 1 = 4 months × 4,000–7,000 = 16,000–28,000 MAD (≈ €1,481–2,593) upfront.

## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances".

### One day

- Morning: Jardin Majorelle (170 MAD; book online) and the YSL Museum (combined 180 MAD) in Guéliz [4]; coffee at Les Négociants [1].
- Midday: petit taxi to the medina (10–15 MAD, insist on the meter; the minimum fare is 7 MAD by day) [4]; lunch at Chez Chegrouni (about 30 MAD) [4].
- Afternoon: Bahia Palace (100 MAD for foreigners) and Ben Youssef Madrasa (50 MAD), through the souks, where bargaining is expected [4]. Ignore anyone posing as an official guide (FCDO, city file).
- Evening: Jemaa el-Fna food stalls (10–100 MAD), confirming prices before ordering (city file, Wikivoyage) [4].

### One week

- Day 1: the one-day route above.
- Day 2: Kasbah: Saadian Tombs and El Badi Palace (100 MAD each), the Mellah, Slat Al Azama Synagogue (10 MAD) [4]; evening at Hamam Essalama.
- Day 3, trip: Imlil and the High Atlas (50 km south, base for Toubkal) [11], or the Ourika valley by minibus from Jardin Sidi Mimoun [4].
- Day 4, trip: Essaouira by bus, about 3 h, 55–80 MAD [4].
- Day 5: Agafay desert day trip (city file, Expat.com).
- Day 6, daily life: the Tuesday souk at Amizmiz, a High Atlas town with a large weekly market [4]; or a Kawkab Marrakech match at Stade de Marrakech if one is scheduled [10]. Back in town, ride a city bus at the evening rush (5 MAD) to see the new fleet and the waits riders complain about (city file).
- Day 7: Guéliz on a Sunday: Mass at Église des Saints-Martyrs [5], Menara Gardens [8], Carré Eden [1]. Compare rents in the agency windows with the Guéliz figures above.
