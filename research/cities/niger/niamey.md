## Items

- [cost.living] Pump prices stand at 499 XOF/L for super petrol (≈ €0.76) and 618 XOF/L for diesel (≈ €0.94), down from 540 and 668 XOF; on 9 October 2026 the government rejected a price rise recommended by its consultative council (CCR) and blamed shortages partly on informal and fraudulent circuits. — La Nouvelle Tribune, 2026-10-10, intl-media, confidence: high
- [cost.living] A ministerial order of 12 October 2026 banned exports of super petrol and diesel; the ministry reported a shortfall of 196.62 million litres of super and 52.60 million litres of diesel over January–August 2026, and the only working refinery (Zinder, 20,000 barrels/day) cannot cover demand. — AFP via Connaissance des Énergies, 2026-10-18, intl-media, confidence: high
- [daily.transport] The fuel shortage reached Niamey on 18 October 2026: the few supplied stations were overwhelmed, some managers said their tanks had been empty for four days, and one resident queued from 21:00 to 03:00 to be served. — AFP via Connaissance des Énergies, 2026-10-18, intl-media, confidence: high
- [daily.transport] Smuggled Nigerian petrol, once up to half the Nigerien market, has dwindled since Nigeria ended its subsidies, removing the informal fallback during shortages. — Benin Web TV, 2026-10-19, intl-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] By 23 October 2026 supply was "gradually" returning to normal after nearly two weeks of shortage; before that some drivers went two to three days without petrol, and one station still sold out its overnight delivery by about 11:00. — AIP citing ANP, 2026-10-23, intl-media, confidence: high
- [daily.power] A resident told AFP that power cuts in Niamey can last up to three hours; in 2023, after Nigeria cut most exports, load-shedding sometimes lasted whole days, and Nigeria was still supplying only 46 MW instead of the usual 80 MW. — AFP via Connaissance des Énergies, 2025-04-16 (older), intl-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.power] Households turn to solar: AFP reports good-quality panels now cost under 50,000 XOF (≈ €76), half the earlier price; national electrification is below 20%. — AFP via Connaissance des Énergies, 2025-04-16 (older), intl-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.money] Niamey ATMs accept international cards but often run out of cash; Belgium advises withdrawing only at secure ATMs such as those in large hotels. — Belgian MFA, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [daily.money] Canada describes Niger as a cash economy with "no ATMs" and cards accepted only at a few large hotels and restaurants (contradicts the Belgian advice above; both kept). — Global Affairs Canada, updated 2026-10-15, official, confidence: medium
- [daily.money] Exchanging more than 1 million XOF (≈ €1,524) requires Ministry of Finance authorisation, which major banks can obtain. — Global Affairs Canada, updated 2026-10-15, official, confidence: medium
- [health.care] France calls Niamey's medical infrastructure limited, names the private clinics Gamkalley and Kaba, says medevac capacity outside the capital is very thin and strongly advises repatriation to France for any surgery. — France Diplomatie (santé), updated 2026-11-10, official, confidence: high
- [health.care] Meningitis (W135) epidemics recur in the dry season, February to July; France strongly recommends ACYW135 vaccination. Local authorities consider Niamey tap water up to standard. — France Diplomatie (santé), updated 2026-11-10, official, confidence: high
- [health.care] Canada says facilities, including in Niamey, do not meet Canadian standards, supplies can run short, and a deposit or advance payment is often demanded. — Global Affairs Canada, updated 2026-10-15, official, confidence: high
- [family.schools] The French Lycée La Fontaine in Niamey was to be put on standby ("mise en veille") from September 2024; the ministry pointed families to CNED distance learning, with exam candidates sent to Lomé. — French Senate written question 11054, 2024-04-04 / answer 2024-05-30 (older), official, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] France formally advises against all travel to Niger (whole country red, zoning of 2026-03-11) and rates the terrorist attack risk as high in Niamey and its surroundings. — France Diplomatie (sécurité), updated 2026-11-10, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] France tells residents to keep the times of daytime trips in Niamey discreet and to avoid public events and unsecured or out-of-the-way hotels, bars, restaurants and nightclubs. — France Diplomatie (sécurité), updated 2026-11-10, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Diori Hamani airport and Air Base 101 in Niamey were attacked on 29 January 2026 and the airport again on 18 June 2026; gunfire was exchanged in the city centre and at the airport on 29 August 2026. — Belgian MFA, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] The UK reports an attempted military-led mutiny in August 2026 with heavy gunfire near the Presidential Palace and the airport, and warns flights from Niamey can be disrupted at short notice. — UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14 (dossier: updated 2026-10-23), official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] A Western national was kidnapped in central Niamey in October 2025; Belgium rates the kidnap risk for Westerners as high everywhere, the capital included. — Belgian MFA, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] The UK rates terrorist kidnapping as a very high threat throughout Niger including Niamey, says criminal gangs may sell hostages to terrorist groups, and advises not crossing the Niger River bridges on foot. — UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] The UK says crime is high in Niamey: muggings around the Gaweye Hotel, the National Museum and Petit Marché, attacks on people leaving banks and ATMs, and theft of 4x4s. — UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Belgium lists places to avoid in Niamey: the airport and presidential palace area, Grand and Petit Marché, the right bank, and the northern districts (where petty crime is common); move by car only and avoid all movement after sunset. — Belgian MFA, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Canada advises travelling between the airport and the city only in a convoy of at least two vehicles and being extremely vigilant after dark. — Global Affairs Canada, updated 2026-10-15, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Belgium advises its nationals who must stay in Niger to live only in Niamey, and says leaving the city requires prior approval from the authorities and a paid armed escort. — Belgian MFA, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.women] Canada says women travelling alone may face harassment or verbal abuse; head covering is not required for foreign women, but short or tight clothing is considered inappropriate. — Global Affairs Canada, updated 2026-10-15, official, confidence: medium
- [community.expat] The French embassy in Niamey is closed "jusqu'à nouvel ordre"; only a consular section remains. Canada relies on an honorary consul in Niamey and its embassy in Bamako. — France Diplomatie 2026-11-10; Global Affairs Canada 2026-10-15, official, confidence: high
- [admin.visa] French passport holders need a visa before travel (€90 single entry, €150 multiple entry); no visa is issued on arrival at the airport, and the passport must be valid more than six months after return. — France Diplomatie (entrée/séjour), updated 2026-11-10, official, confidence: high
- [admin.visa] Belgium reports that international passports are sometimes held on arrival at Niamey airport, usually for a few days, and that Belgians with ordinary passports cannot apply for a Nigerien visa in Brussels. — Belgian MFA, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [admin.residency] Working in Niger requires a work permit or work visa; Belgium tells people to check registration with the authorities with their employer before leaving. — Belgian MFA (législation locale), updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [admin.nationality] Niger has allowed dual nationality since Law 2014-60 of 5 November 2014; a child born abroad to at least one Nigerien parent is Nigerien at birth, and mothers have transmitted on equal terms since 1999. — Wikipedia "Nigerien nationality law" (citing the laws), accessed 2026-11-14, academic (reference digest), confidence: medium
- [admin.nationality] Canada's advisory states that dual citizenship is "not legally recognized" and that a dual national may be refused Canadian consular help (contradicts the 2014 law above; both kept). — Global Affairs Canada, updated 2026-10-15, official, confidence: medium
- [rights.lgbt] The penal code in force since June 2026 punishes same-sex relations with 5 to 10 years in prison and same-sex marriage (including officiants and witnesses) with 10 to 20 years; Belgium says homosexuality is not socially tolerated. — Belgian MFA (législation locale), updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [rights.lgbt] Fines run from 10 to 100 million XOF (≈ €15,245–152,449) for acts and 50 to 500 million XOF for organisations, with no suspended sentences; local media reported dozens of arrests nationwide. — Wikipedia "LGBT rights in Niger" citing The Guardian 2026-07-01, intl-media (digest), confidence: medium
