# Alger: neighbourhoods and routes

> Built 2026-11-14 from the city file and targeted searches. Research material, not published text. Every named place has a source; nothing is invented. Prices are dated asking prices, not what people pay.

Conventions. "City file" means `research/cities/algerie/alger.md`; the source it cites is named after it. EUR uses the city file's rate: Bank of Algeria official rate €1 ≈ 152 DZD (9 Nov 2026, Mediaterranee). Someone changing cash euros on the informal market at about 276 DZD gets nearly twice as many dinars, so every EUR figure below roughly halves for them (city file, Mediaterranee and Observ'Algérie, 2026-11-10). That informal market carries legal risk (Wikivoyage [1] says it "may be illegal"). Monthly figures become weekly by dividing by 4.3. Numbers in [n] refer to "Sources read" at the bottom. The shared web-search budget ran out after six searches in this pass, so most new places come from pages opened directly (Wikipedia communes, Wikivoyage, guides, venue directories).

Calendar note. The work week runs Sunday to Thursday, and Friday and Saturday are the weekend. Many shops partly close for Friday prayer (city file, Cyril Jarnias "Pratiques religieuses", 2026-05-20). So "Saturday night" is the last night of the weekend, and Sunday is a working day. The next Ramadan starts in February 2027 (city file, FCDO 2026-09-25).

## Neighbourhoods

### Hydra (with Paradou, Val d'Hydra and Sidi Yahia)

- Feel: the most upmarket commune, mainly residential, on the heights about 210 m above the city. It hosts about thirty embassies and the Sonatrach head office [6]. Hydra and El Biar together hold over half the capital's diplomatic missions and the most foreigners (city file, lepetitjournal 2025-09-06) [2]. Sidi Yahia, below Les Crêtes [6], has upscale shops, design cafés and restaurants, and heavy peak-hour traffic (Algérie Nomades, updated 2026-07-03) [4].
- Rent: median F2/F3 asking rent of 220,000 DZD (≈ €1,447), from 12 listings (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23). Furnished expat flats cost 80,000–200,000 DZD (≈ €526–1,316) and F4s 180,000–280,000 (city file, Cyril Jarnias 2026-05-13). A 2026 guide gives F3s at 150,000–400,000 DZD (≈ €987–2,632) (city file, ZoomAlgérie, low confidence).
- Getting around: about 6 km south-west of the centre, with ETUSA lines 31 and 88 to the centre and a bus station near Bir Mourad Raïs (lines 33, 34) [6]. A taxi ride of about 5 km in town costs 300–600 DZD (≈ €2–4) (city file, Cyril Jarnias "Transports publics", 2026). No metro or tram stop.
- Watch: parking is short almost everywhere; secured residences with underground parking are mostly in the western outskirts and the heights [2]. Lepetitjournal (updated 2025-09-06) counts Hydra among the areas it calls safe zones [2].
- Places:
  - KeepWork Coworking (coworking, Sidi Yahia; Mon–Fri 9:00–17:00; desk 900 DZD a day or 9,900 a month excluding tax, private office 5,000 a day) — workin.space [14]
  - IN Network (coworking, Parc Paradou, Mon–Fri 9:00–18:00) — workin.space, seen only as a search-result listing [15]
  - Clinique El Chiffa (private clinic) — French Wikipedia, Hydra [6]
  - Notre-Dame de Lourdes (Catholic church) and the El Ghazali and al-Qods mosques — French Wikipedia [6]
  - Le Ciloc (restaurant, Constantine cuisine, 6 Rue de Savoie; Sat–Thu 08:00–midnight) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Handmade Art (craft shop, 28 Rue des Frères Kadri; Sat–Thu 10:00–18:00) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Lycée Said Hamdine (public lycée) — French Wikipedia [6]
  - Paradou AC (football club) — French Wikipedia [6]
- Fits: Q3 "Le prix compte peu" (the dearest commune in the Algérie360 study); Q5 "J'entreprends" and "Télétravail" (three coworking spaces, ministries and Sonatrach on site); Q10 "Me sentir en sécurité" (embassies and guarded buildings [4]); not Q3 "Serré".

### El Biar

- Feel: affluent, with a business centre and high-end homes alongside some working-class areas. It has several embassies and the Supreme Court, and its central square is Place Kennedy [7]. A blog calls it a "premium residential filter", with colonial villas, cafés and more street life than Hydra (Cyril Jarnias, 2026-05-13) [5]. Another guide describes bay views and a quieter residential feel [4].
- Rent: F2 85,000–115,000 DZD (≈ €559–757), F3 105,000–145,000 (≈ €691–954), F4 135,000–185,000 (city file and [5], Cyril Jarnias 2026-05-13, low confidence). A 2026 guide gives F3s at 120,000–300,000 for El Biar, Poirson and Paradou (city file, ZoomAlgérie, low confidence). Hauts d'Alger F3 median about 190,000 DZD (≈ €1,250) (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23).
- Getting around: ETUSA lines 10, 40, 43, 45, 48, 50, 58 and 181, plus private buses and shared taxis; traffic congestion noted [7]. No distance to the centre is given on the page.
- Watch: lepetitjournal includes El Biar among its "safe zones" (updated 2025-09-06) [2].
- Places:
  - Place John-F.-Kennedy (central square, ex-Place Carnot) — French Wikipedia [7], Algérie Nomades [4]
  - Parc des pins, Balcon Saint-Raphaël and Jardin de Tunis (parks and viewpoints) — French Wikipedia [7]
  - Al Khoulafa Arachidine and El Forkane (mosques) — French Wikipedia [7]
  - Former Notre-Dame du Mont-Carmel church, now the municipal library — French Wikipedia [7]
  - JS El Biar (sports club) — French Wikipedia [7]
  - Alliance Française (cultural centre, named for El Biar by a blog; address not given) — Cyril Jarnias [5]
- Fits: Q2 "Un bon dîner entre amis" and Q7 "Reste à taille humaine" (cafés and a central square, quieter than the centre); Q3 "Moyen" to "Le prix compte peu"; Q4 "En couple".

### Ben Aknoun and Dely Brahim (schools and families)

- Feel: Ben Aknoun, 6 km west of Alger-Centre, is a large administrative and diplomatic hub, with eight ministries and nine embassies; buildings other than housing cover about two-thirds of it [8]. It holds the French lycée (LIAD) and the American international school [8]. A blog finds it calmer and greener than Hydra (Cyril Jarnias, 2026-05-13) [5]. Dely Brahim is described as spacious family housing with many local services but chronic traffic jams [5].
- Rent: Ben Aknoun F2/F3 median 150,000 DZD (≈ €987), from 9 listings (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23). Ben Aknoun/Le Golf F3 100,000–250,000 (≈ €658–1,645) and Dely Ibrahim/El Achour F3 70,000–150,000 (≈ €461–987) (city file, ZoomAlgérie 2026, low confidence). Dely Ibrahim about 150,000 DZD for 100 m² (city file, Cyril Jarnias, low confidence).
- Getting around: no metro or tram. The metro extension list includes Rostomia–Ouled Fayet and Rostomia–Draria branches, but these are plans, with no date [17]. Car or taxi otherwise; a blog notes relatively quick road access to the centre from Ben Aknoun [5].
- Watch: Dely Brahim traffic jams [5].
- Places:
  - Lycée International Alexandre Dumas (French AEFE school, Chemin Arezki Mouri; about 705,000 DZD a year for French and Algerian pupils, blog figure) — LIAD site [27], city file (Cyril Jarnias, low confidence)
  - American International School of Algiers (English-language, kindergarten to grade 7 per Wikipedia; about 30,000 USD a year per a blog) — French Wikipedia, Ben Aknoun [8]; city file
  - Ben Aknoun zoo and amusement park (zoo, leisure park) — French Wikipedia [8]
  - ES Ben Aknoun (multi-sport club, founded 1935) — French Wikipedia [8]
  - École des Hautes Études Commerciales and the Faculty of Medicine (higher education) — French Wikipedia [8]
- Fits: Q4 "Avec mes enfants" and Q10 "La santé et l'école" (both international schools are in the commune); Q7 "Prend son temps"; Q3 "Moyen" (Dely Brahim) or "Le prix compte peu" (Ben Aknoun).

### Alger-Centre and Telemly

- Feel: the administrative and commercial heart, with government, parliament and ministries; 75,541 residents in 2008 [9]. Telemly, close to the centre, mixes Haussmann-style buildings and 1950s blocks, many with bay views; rents can be lower than elsewhere but parking is very hard [2]. A guide warns of density and noise in the centre and Bab El Oued (OpenShores, 2026-04-13) [3].
- Rent: Alger-Centre F2/F3 median 85,000 DZD (≈ €559) (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23); centre one-bedrooms 40,000–65,000 (≈ €263–428) (city file, Cyril Jarnias, low confidence). OpenShores gives about 350 USD a month for a central one-bedroom [3].
- Getting around: metro stations Tafourah–Grande Poste, Khelifa Boukhalfa and Ali Boumendjel [9], 5:00–23:00 [17]. Metro fares in the sources differ: 25 DZD (city file, Africarrières 2026-08-07), 38–50 DZD (city file, Cyril Jarnias) and DA50 single or DA400 for ten (Wikivoyage) [1]. Gare d'Alger is in the commune [9].
- Watch: unofficial currency exchange happens at Square Port-Saïd [1][9]; it carries legal risk (see Conventions). FCDO (2026-09-25) warns of pickpocketing and petty theft in larger cities (city file).
- Places:
  - Marché Clauzel (fresh-produce market) — OpenShores [3]
  - L'épicerie (grocery with local products, 5 Rue Professeur Curtillet, Sacré-Cœur; 09:00–19:00) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Librairie Le 88 (French and Arabic bookshop, 88 Rue Didouche Mourad) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Cathédrale du Sacré-Cœur (Catholic cathedral, free) — Wikivoyage [1], French Wikipedia [9]
  - Parc de la Liberté (park) — French Wikipedia [9]
  - Cinémathèque d'Alger (cinema) and MAMA, the modern art museum — OpenShores [3], French Wikipedia [9]
  - Central Touring Hotel (budget hotel, 9 Rue Abane Ramdane, DA2,000–5,000) — Wikivoyage [1]
- Fits: Q1 "Tester quelques mois" (hotels, metro, everything on foot); Q7 "Vibre jour et nuit" (the densest district); Q8 "Je reste en ville"; Q3 "Moyen".

### Kouba

- Feel: a large commune of 116,021 people (2020) on the Algiers Sahel, about 6 km south-east of the centre [10]. Algérie Nomades (2026-07-03) describes local markets, bakeries, butchers and clinics and more affordable rents [4]. A blog describes newer buildings with parking and lifts in a mixed setting (Cyril Jarnias, 2026-05-13) [5]. An investment guide says Kouba is more affordable but varies street by street (city file, ZoomAlgérie 2026-08).
- Rent: F2/F3 median 75,000 DZD (≈ €493) (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23); F3 from about 55,000 (≈ €362) (city file, Cyril Jarnias, low confidence).
- Getting around: metro at the multimodal Les Fusillés station, the tram terminus at Les Fusillés/Ruisseau, a cable car to the Plateau des Annassers, and ETUSA lines 4, 79, 89, 96 and 101 [10][16]. The metro runs from Les Fusillés through Jardin d'essai to the centre [17].
- Places:
  - Parc Ben Omar and the Forêt de Kouba (5 ha around an Ottoman fort) — French Wikipedia [10]
  - Stade Mohamed Benhaddad (10,000 seats, RC Kouba) and the semi-Olympic pool in Ben Omar — French Wikipedia [10]
  - Mosquée de Ben Omar (mosque) — French Wikipedia [10]
  - Clinique les Oliviers (private clinic) — French Wikipedia [10]
  - Palais de la culture Moufdi Zakaria (cultural centre) — French Wikipedia [10]
  - Fruit and vegetable markets (not named) — Algérie Nomades [4]
- Fits: Q4 "En couple" and Q5 "Salarié·e" (metro and tram to the centre and the east); Q3 "Moyen"; Q10 "Une vie abordable".

### Bab Ezzouar (east, business district)

- Feel: about 15 km east of the centre, mostly residential estates built since the late 1970s, which Wikipedia calls "dormitory" estates with no real centre [11]. The south has what the page calls Algeria's first business district, with head offices of Mobilis, Ooredoo, Algérie Poste and CMA-CGM, and the USTHB university of about 45,000 students [11]. Lepetitjournal places it with Kouba among business and international-hotel areas closer to the airport [2]. A 2026 investment guide rates it the best balance of yield and demand (city file, ZoomAlgérie 2026-08).
- Rent: F2/F3 median 85,000 DZD (≈ €559) (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23); Bab Ezzouar/Chéraga F3 60,000–130,000 (city file, ZoomAlgérie, low confidence).
- Getting around: tram stops Université de Bab Ezzouar (USTHB), Cité 5 Juillet, Bab Ezzouar–Le Pont and others; an SNTF suburban station near USTHB and the business district [11][16]. The tram runs west to Ruisseau/Les Fusillés, where it meets the metro [16]. Wikipedia expected four metro stations in 2026, but whether they have opened is unconfirmed [11].
- Places:
  - Centre commercial de Bab Ezzouar (shopping and leisure centre, 45,000 m² on three floors per French Wikipedia; a popular evening spot per a blog) — French Wikipedia [11], city file (Cyril Jarnias nightlife)
  - USTHB (university) — French Wikipedia [11]
  - Fitness World Sifaks (gym) — French Wikipedia [11]
  - Baha M'Hmed semi-Olympic pool (opened 2006) — French Wikipedia [11]
  - Stade Mohamed Bentalha (football ground) — French Wikipedia [11]
  - Josip Tito park/square — French Wikipedia [11]
- Fits: Q5 "Salarié·e" (telecoms and logistics head offices on site); Q10 "Voyager facilement" (close to the airport [2]); Q3 "Moyen"; Q7 "Reste à taille humaine" is a poor match (dense estates).

### Chéraga and Ouled Fayet (western family suburbs)

- Feel: Chéraga, about 18 km west of Algiers, had 89,557 residents in 2020 [12]. Guides describe it as a commercial hub with malls and restaurants and often dense traffic, and Ouled Fayet, about 10 km from the centre, as recent gated residences with more space for the money (Algérie Nomades, 2026-07-03) [4]. A blog calls the Chéraga–Draria–Ouled Fayet area quieter and family-oriented [5].
- Rent: Ouled Fayet F2/F3 median 85,000 DZD (≈ €559) and Draria 60,000 (≈ €395) (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23); Chéraga/Draria/Ouled Fayet F3–F4 70,000–120,000 (city file, Cyril Jarnias, low confidence).
- Getting around: ETUSA lines 3, 56, 57 and 120, plus private buses to Alger-Centre and Bab El Oued; two interchanges on the southern ring road [12]. No metro yet; the Rostomia–Ouled Fayet extension is on the plan list without a date [17]. A guide warns that commutes from the western seaside suburbs can be long at peak hours [3].
- Watch: dense traffic [4][12].
- Places:
  - Garden City (shopping mall). Wikipedia puts it in Chéraga [12] and Algérie Nomades in Ouled Fayet [4]; the exact address was not confirmed.
  - El Qods shopping centre — French Wikipedia [12]
  - Parc Dounia and Teri Park (leisure parks) — French Wikipedia [12], Algérie Nomades [4]
  - Kids Land (children's leisure) — Algérie Nomades [4]
  - Cinéma El-Sahel (cinema) — French Wikipedia [12]
  - JSM Chéraga (football club, 1940) — French Wikipedia [12]
- Fits: Q4 "Avec mes enfants" and "Pour fonder une famille" (space, parks, malls); Q8 "À la plage" (the western beaches, see below); Q3 "Moyen"; Q7 "Prend son temps".

### Bab El Oued (affordable, seafront, working class)

- Feel: a working-class quarter of the historic city on the north seafront, below Bouzaréah and next to the Casbah, famous for Place des Trois-Horloges and the Triolet market, with many workshops [13]. Density is about 70,610 people per km² [13]. OpenShores (2026-04-13) describes intense street life, noticeably lower rents, and density and noise [3].
- Rent: F3 45,000 DZD (≈ €296), small sample (city file, Algérie360 2026-10-23, low confidence); one-bedrooms 40,000–65,000 (city file, Cyril Jarnias, low confidence).
- Getting around: the metro extension from Place des Martyrs towards Triolet (1.8 km, 3 stations) was under construction or planned [17], and the dossier saw works in September 2026 (TSA). Until it opens, bus or taxi to the centre; RN11 runs west [13].
- Watch: the 2001 mudslides badly hit the Triolet market [13].
- Places:
  - Place des Trois-Horloges (square) — French Wikipedia [13]
  - Marché Triolet (market) — French Wikipedia [13]
  - Jardin de Prague (Algiers' first public garden, 1832) — French Wikipedia [13]
  - Zawiyas of Sidi Saâdi and Sidi Amar (Sufi lodges) — French Wikipedia [13]
  - Basilica Notre-Dame d'Afrique (Catholic basilica on a 124 m cliff over the bay; daily mass 18:00, Friday 10:30) — English Wikipedia [19], Wikivoyage [1]
- Fits: Q3 "Serré" (the cheapest central rents in the sources); Q7 "Vibre jour et nuit"; Q10 "La mémoire de la diaspora" (old Algiers next to the Casbah); not Q4 "Avec mes enfants" on a first move.

## Living: typical weeks

All weekly totals use only prices in the city file or in the pages read here. Quantities (how many meals, rides, days) are stated assumptions. Groceries use the only single-person figure found: €150–250 a month (city file, lesafriques.com 2026-10-19), which is 22,800–38,000 DZD at 152, or 5,302–8,837 DZD a week. Utilities 5,971 DZD a month (1,389 a week) and ADSL 1,665 (387 a week) are aggregator figures (city file, combien-coute.net 2026-11). A one-year lease paid upfront is the norm (city file, lepetitjournal), and diaspora or expatriate tenants are often asked for 6–12 months upfront (city file, ZoomAlgérie). The weekly rent below is a share of a large upfront payment, not cash leaving the account each week.

### Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "Télétravail" + Q4 "Seul·e"

- Base: El Biar. It costs less than Hydra but is close to the Sidi Yahia coworking spaces, and it has a square, cafés and parks [5][7]. Caveat: no digital-nomad visa exists, and a blog calls remote work for a foreign employer a grey area (city file, Cyril Jarnias 2026-05-14).
- Week (Sunday–Thursday is the working week):
  - Sun: work from home; evening walk to Place Kennedy [7].
  - Mon: coworking day at KeepWork, Sidi Yahia [14]; taxi 300–600 DZD each way (city file); journey time not sourced.
  - Tue: home; groceries in the neighbourhood shops (none named for El Biar); evening in Parc des pins [7].
  - Wed: coworking again; gym after work (aggregator price 3,360 DZD a month; no named gym in El Biar was found).
  - Thu: last workday; dinner at Le Ciloc in Hydra [1].
  - Fri: weekend. Friday prayer at Al Khoulafa Arachidine or El Forkane mosque [7]; afternoon at the Jardin d'essai (DA150) [1], by taxi to the centre, then metro (El Biar has no station).
  - Sat: see Saturday-night options.
- Saturday night:
  - "Danser jusqu'au matin": weak in Algiers. Alcohol licences are limited, and hotel bars and clubs serve alcohol while rooftop lounges often do not (city file, Cyril Jarnias nightlife, low confidence). Seventy-Five Lounge at the El Aurassi hotel (2 Bd Frantz Fanon) [1] or the Marina Palm beach club [1]. Wikivoyage lists no nightclub [1]. No entry prices found.
  - "Un bon dîner entre amis": Le Ciloc, Hydra [1], or Al Boustan's panoramic terrace in the Bois des Arcades, Riadh El Feth [1]. A three-course dinner costs about 1,892 DZD (≈ €12) (city file, combien-coute.net).
  - "Tranquille chez moi": home, or a film at the Cinémathèque d'Alger [3]; cinema ticket about 605 DZD (city file, combien-coute.net).
- Weekend outing:
  - "À la plage": the resorts about 20 km west, Sidi Fredj, Palm Beach, Douaouda and Zéralda (city file, Wikipedia "Algiers"); or the Sablette seafront promenade [1].
  - "En montagne, en forêt": the Baïnem forest, about 600 ha, 8 km north-west of the centre [24], or the Bois des Arcades above the Martyrs' Memorial [1]. The Chréa massif (cedars, ski station [22]) is an **orange zone** for France, "déconseillé sauf raison impérative", over trafficking in the mountains (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-15) [23], so it is not proposed. Djurdjura National Park lies near Tizi Ouzou [25]; no distance, transport or France Diplomatie zoning was found for it, so it is not routed here.
  - "Dans la savane ou le désert": not realistic from Algiers for a weekend. Ghardaïa (M'zab valley, UNESCO) is about 10 hours by bus, or a few Air Algérie flights a week [26]. It would take a long weekend; no fare was found.
  - "Je reste en ville": the Casbah with a local guide (FCDO advice, city file), museums, the Jardin d'essai [1].
- Weekly spend (DZD): rent F2 85,000–115,000 ÷ 4.3 = 19,767–26,744 (Cyril Jarnias) · coworking 9,900 ÷ 4.3 = 2,302 [14] · utilities 1,389 · ADSL 387 · groceries 5,302–8,837 · 3 popular lunches × 300–600 = 900–1,800 (Africarrières) · 1 mid-range dinner 800–1,500 (Africarrières) · all-network transport pass 2,500 ÷ 4.3 = 581 (Cyril Jarnias) · 2 taxi rides × 300–600 = 600–1,200 (Cyril Jarnias) · gym 3,360 ÷ 4.3 = 781 (combien-coute.net) · 5 cappuccinos × 148 = 740 (combien-coute.net). **Total ≈ 33,549–46,261 DZD (≈ €221–304 at the official rate; ≈ €122–168 at 276).** Cross-check: the blogger's "comfortable" single budget of 180,000–250,000 DZD a month is 41,860–58,140 a week.

### Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "J'entreprends"

- Base: Hydra/Sidi Yahia if budget allows (Sonatrach and ministries nearby [6], three coworking spaces [14][15]). Bab Ezzouar is the cheaper base beside the telecom head offices [11].
- Week:
  - Sun: KeepWork, Sidi Yahia [14]; register interest in the state "Startup" label and the Algerian Startup Fund (dossier, startup.dz).
  - Mon: client meetings in Hydra; lunch at Le Ciloc [1].
  - Tue: tram day in the Bab Ezzouar business district (Mobilis, Ooredoo, Algérie Poste head offices [11]); taxi from Hydra to the tram, time not sourced.
  - Wed: bank errands. Accounts are opened in a branch with a residency permit, and a foreign-currency account needs a dinar account at the same branch (city file, Expat.com, lepetitjournal). No crypto: it is banned by law (dossier).
  - Thu: private office day at KeepWork (5,000 DZD) [14]; job platform Emploitic for hiring (city file, low confidence).
  - Fri: prayer, then family time; Hydra's Notre-Dame de Lourdes church for Christians [6]. Non-Muslim proselytising is illegal (city file, Freedom House 2025).
  - Sat: see options.
- Saturday night:
  - "Danser jusqu'au matin": Seventy-Five Lounge, El Aurassi [1]; hotel-bar scene only (city file, low confidence).
  - "Un bon dîner entre amis": Le Ciloc [1] or Al Boustan [1].
  - "Tranquille chez moi": home in Hydra.
- Weekend outing:
  - "À la plage": Sidi Fredj or Palm Beach (city file).
  - "En montagne, en forêt": Baïnem [24]; Chréa is in an orange zone [23].
  - "Dans la savane ou le désert": Ghardaïa by plane over a long weekend only [26].

### Q1 "Tester quelques mois": the first week

- Where to stay first: Alger-Centre, for the metro, banks and agencies on foot. Central Touring Hotel, 9 Rue Abane Ramdane, DA2,000–5,000 a night, or Hôtel Elghanami from DA8,000 (Wikivoyage, listing updated Nov 2025) [1].
- Arrival: the airport is about 20 km from the city (city file). The airport bus runs about every 30 minutes by day for DA25 [1]. A taxi costs about DA1,000–1,500 [1]; the city file's blog figure is 1,200 normal and over 1,500 suspicious (Cyril Jarnias). Yassir and Heetch work [1]. FCDO advises booking taxis through your hotel after dark (city file).
- Visa reality: a stay is generally capped at about 180 days a year without extension (city file, Expat.com). Diaspora members with Algerian citizenship may enter visa-free on a foreign passport, but the current status of that measure is unconfirmed (city file, Wikipedia).
- Day by day:
  - Day 1: check in; buy a SIM. Ooredoo's "500" offer (500 DZD with 5 GB for four weeks, original passport needed) appears only in a search-result summary of an eSIM vendor page dated 2026-10-23 that could not be opened [29]. Verify it in a shop.
  - Day 2: money. "Mobile money" in the African sense was not found; Yassir offers "Yassir Cash" payments (dossier). A bank account generally needs a residency permit and a branch visit (city file, Expat.com). Banxy (Natixis Algérie) is said to allow online opening (city file, Cyril Jarnias, low confidence). In practice a trial stay runs on cash: cards are generally not accepted by taxis (city file).
  - Day 3: flat search on Ouedkniss, DarJadida and Facebook groups (city file, Cyril Jarnias). Rule: never pay before a visit, and check the owner's title deed (city file, ZoomAlgérie).
  - Day 4: visits in Telemly and Kouba [2][10] by metro. No visit fees were found in any source. Customs to budget for: agency fee of about one month and deposit of one month (city file, lepetitjournal). Upfront rent runs 3–6 months for locals but 6–12 months for diaspora and expatriate tenants (city file, ZoomAlgérie, low confidence). A whole year upfront is the norm (lepetitjournal). For a few months, a furnished flat (15–30% premium per a blogger, city file) is the realistic route.
  - Day 5: negotiate. November–March is the low season (city file, ZoomAlgérie), and a blogger advises getting any concession in writing (city file).
  - Day 6 (Friday): rest; Sablette promenade [1].
  - Day 7 (Saturday): sign with a notarised lease and a receipt for any cash advance (city file, Cyril Jarnias); move in.
- Saturday night: "Danser jusqu'au matin": El Aurassi lounge [1]. "Un bon dîner entre amis": Sapori couscous bar, 20 Rue Chasseloup-Laubat (Mon–Sat) [1]. "Tranquille chez moi": the hotel room, or the Cinémathèque [3].
- Weekend outing: as in the first profile; Tipaza (below) is the easiest first escape.
- First-week spend (DZD): hotel 7 nights × 2,000–5,000 = 14,000–35,000 [1] · airport bus 25 or taxi 1,000–1,500 [1] · SIM about 500 [29] · 10 metro rides × 50 = 500 [1] · 2 taxi rides × 300–600 = 600–1,200 (Cyril Jarnias) · 7 lunches × 300–600 = 2,100–4,200 and 7 dinners × 800–1,500 = 5,600–10,500 (Africarrières). **Total ≈ 23,325–53,400 DZD (≈ €153–351; ≈ €85–193 at 276),** before any upfront rent. Upfront rent is the real cost: 6 months at the Alger-Centre median of 85,000 is 510,000 DZD (≈ €3,355), plus one month's fee and one month's deposit.

## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances". The visit is meant to help decide whether to live here. Note the limits: do not photograph official buildings or people without care (city file, France Diplomatie 2026-09-15); taxis are scarce after dark (FCDO, city file); drinking in public is illegal (FCDO, city file).

### One day

- Morning: metro to Place des Martyrs [17] and the Casbah (UNESCO since 1992 [20]) with a local guide, as FCDO advises (city file). See Ketchaoua Mosque [1][20], the Palais des Raïs (DA200) [1] and the Palace of the Dey, Bd Mohamed Taleb (08:30–16:00) [1].
- Midday: couscous at Sapori, 20 Rue Chasseloup-Laubat (11:30–15:00) [1]. Walk down to the Grande Poste (1910, neo-Moorish) [1] and Rue Didouche Mourad (Librairie Le 88) [1].
- Afternoon: metro from Tafourah–Grande Poste to Jardin d'essai [17]. The garden covers 80 ha, created 1832 (city file); it opens 09:00–16:00 and costs DA150 [1]. Then walk up to the Martyrs' Memorial (DA50) [1] and the Bois des Arcades viewpoint [1].
- Evening: dinner at Al Boustan's panoramic terrace in the Bois des Arcades [1]. Hotel taxi back (FCDO, city file).
- Getting between: metro DA50 single, DA400 for ten (buy from an attendant; the machines take coins only) [1].

### One week

- Day 1: the one-day route above.
- Day 2: museums of the centre. National Museum of Fine Arts (178 Place Dar Essalem, DA200, closed Sunday) [1], the Bardo [1][9] and MAMA [3]. Evening in Telemly for the bay views [2].
- Day 3: **trip out: Tipaza**, about 70 km west. Buses leave from the 1er Mai station next to Agha, take about 1.5 h, and the last bus back is around 16:00–17:00 [21]. See the UNESCO Roman ruins (DA130 listed on Wikivoyage, not labelled as a fee) and the Royal Mausoleum of Mauretania [21], then a seafood restaurant by the water [21].
- Day 4: **daily life**. A market morning at Marché Clauzel [3], then the Triolet market and Place des Trois-Horloges in Bab El Oued [13]. Afternoon: tram to Pont El Harrach or Bellevue and a 10-minute walk to Djamaa El Djazaïr in Mohammadia, with an observation deck on the minaret [18]; visitors are let in outside prayer times [1].
- Day 5: **rush hour**. Take the tram east at morning peak from Les Fusillés to Université de Bab Ezzouar [16] to see the business district and USTHB [11]; Centre commercial de Bab Ezzouar in the evening [11]. This is the commute of a salaried newcomer.
- Day 6 (Friday): the heights. Hydra and El Biar (Place Kennedy [7], Handmade Art in Hydra [1], dinner at Le Ciloc [1]). This day shows where most expats live and what a calm residential street feels like on the weekend.
- Day 7: Notre-Dame d'Afrique for the view over the bay (18:00 mass) [19]; or a match: MC Alger, CR Belouizdad or USM Alger, with the Stade du 5 Juillet 1962 seating 64,000 (city file). No fixture list or ticket prices were checked. Alternative trip: the western beaches of Sidi Fredj or Zéralda (city file).
- Not proposed: Chréa (orange zone, France Diplomatie 2026-09-15) [23]; anything near the land borders (red zones, dossier).
