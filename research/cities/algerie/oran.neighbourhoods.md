# Oran: neighbourhoods and routes

> Built 2026-11-14 from the city file and targeted searches. Research material, not published text. Every named place has a source; nothing is invented. Prices are dated asking prices, not what people pay.

Conventions. "City file" means `research/cities/algerie/oran.md`; the source it cites is named after it. EUR uses the city file's rate: Bank of Algeria official rate €1 ≈ 152 DZD (9 Nov 2026, Mediaterranee). Cash euros change at about 276 DZD on the informal market (an Algiers figure), so every EUR figure roughly halves for someone paid abroad (city file). Monthly figures become weekly by dividing by 4.3. Numbers in [n] refer to "Sources read" at the bottom. The shared web-search budget ran out on the first Oran-specific search of this pass, so new places come from pages opened directly (French Wikipedia, Wikivoyage, a coworking directory, one expat blog).

**Rent caveat for every neighbourhood.** No neighbourhood-level asking-rent study for Oran was found. Nothing compares to the Algérie360 study for Algiers. Citywide figures: 1-bed 27,269 DZD (≈ €179) in the centre and 16,868 (≈ €111) in the suburbs; 3-bed 42,816 (≈ €282) centre and 27,362 (≈ €180) suburbs (city file, combien-coute.net 2026-11, low confidence). A 2026 guide gives F3s at about 20,000–80,000 DZD (≈ €132–526), "dearer in El-Madania and Canastel, cheaper in Sidi El Bachir" (city file, ZoomAlgérie, low confidence). El-Madania is also the name of an Algiers commune, so this may be a slip in the source. The rent lines below repeat these figures and add only what is specific.

Calendar note. The weekend is Friday–Saturday, Sunday is a workday, and shops partly close for Friday prayer. The next Ramadan starts in February 2027 (city file, Cyril Jarnias 2026-05-20 / FCDO 2026-09-25).

## Neighbourhoods

### Centre-ville and Front de Mer (Place du 1er Novembre, Bd de la Soummam)

- Feel: the colonial-era centre around Place du 1er Novembre, with the town hall and the regional theatre [2]. The Front de Mer, a seafront boulevard of cafés, restaurants and ice-cream shops, runs along cliffs over 50 m high at Gambetta [1][2]. Walking is the main way to get around the centre [1].
- Rent: centre 1-bed 27,269 DZD (≈ €179), 3-bed 42,816 (≈ €282) (city file, combien-coute.net, low confidence). Front de Mer is listed by a blog among the upscale sea-view areas, where villas or large sea-view flats alone can exceed a USD 3,000 monthly budget (Cyril Jarnias, 2026-05-13) [6].
- Getting around: tram stops Place 1er Novembre, Emir Abd el Kader and Gare d'Oran (SNTF trains). The tram runs about 5:00–23:00, every four minutes at peak, for 40 DZD a trip [3]. Bus ticket 20 DZD (city file, combien-coute.net).
- Places:
  - Front de Mer (seafront boulevard, cafés and ice cream) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Promenade de Létang (garden above the port) — French Wikipedia [2]
  - Théâtre régional d'Oran (theatre) — French Wikipedia [2]
  - Former Sacré-Cœur cathedral, now the regional library (Bd Abderahmane Mira) — Wikivoyage [1], French Wikipedia [2]
  - Musée national Ahmed Zabana (museum, 19 Avenue Ahmed Zabana) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Abdellah Ben Salem Mosque (former Great Synagogue, Bd Maata Mohamed El Habib) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Les Ambassadeurs (restaurant at the Royal Hotel, 1 Bd de la Soummam) and Délices de Yemma (local food, 12 Bd de la Soummam) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Regus Oran Cintra (coworking, Bd de la Soummam, 263 m from Place du 1er Novembre; listed as opening 30 Nov 2026; open desk from 38,900 DZD a month) — workin.space [7]

### Sidi El Houari, La Calère and Médina Jdida (old town, affordable)

- Feel: Sidi El Houari is the historic centre, on the slopes of the Murdjadjo facing the sea, once called "Les Bas Quartiers". La Calère is a former fishermen's quarter, partly demolished in the early 1980s, and Médina Jdida a secondary commercial centre [2]. The city file lists Médina Jedida (big market), El Hamri and Sidi El Houari as historic areas (Wikipedia "Oran").
- Rent: no figure specific to these quarters was found; see the citywide caveat.
- Getting around: tram stop M'dine el Djadida [3]; the cable car runs from the old city up to Santa Cruz, with three stations, 07:30–19:00, DA60 one way and DA100 return [1].
- Watch: the Pasha (Hassan Pasha) mosque is described as in poor repair [1]; no dated safety source for the quarter was found.
- Places:
  - Marché de Médina Jdida (market) — Wikivoyage [1], French Wikipedia [2]
  - Mosquée du Pacha (mosque, visits 8:00–12:00 except Fridays) — French Wikipedia [2], Wikivoyage [1]
  - Mosquée du Bey Mohamed el Kébir (mosque, Bd Tripoli, 1793) — French Wikipedia [2]
  - Mausoleum of Sidi El Houari (the patron saint's tomb) and the Église Saint-Louis — French Wikipedia [2]
  - Restaurant le Corsaire (restaurant, Bd de l'A.L.N., old town) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Fort de Santa Cruz and its chapel (fort, by cable car) — Wikivoyage [1]
- Fits: Q3 "Serré" (by elimination; the quarter is described as working-class and historic, but no rent was found); Q10 "La mémoire de la diaspora" (raï, the old city, the Spanish and Ottoman layers [2], city file); Q7 "Prend son temps".

### Gambetta, Es Seddikia, Choupot-Maraval and Les Castors (central-east, middle)

- Feel: Gambetta sits on the cliffs above the Front de Mer [2]. Choupôt-Maraval is a newer commercial hub known for luxury clothing, and Les Castors for car parts [2]. A blog groups Es Seddikia, El Makkari, Hai Khemisti and Gambetta as more affordable, central or well connected, at the cost of smaller flats or distance from the sea (Cyril Jarnias, 2026-05-13) [6].
- Rent: no figure specific to these quarters. The same blog says USD 1,500–2,000 a month covers comfortable living there (≈ 204,000–272,000 DZD, USD-converted at 136) [6]. That is an all-in budget, not rent, and low confidence.
- Getting around: tram stops Les Castors, Mosquée Ibn Badis, Palais de Justice and Carrefour les Trois Cliniques [3], 40 DZD.
- Places:
  - Mosquée Abdelhamid Ben Badis (mosque, by the tram stop of the same name) — French Wikipedia [2][3]
  - Choupôt-Maraval shopping streets (clothing) — French Wikipedia [2]
  - Les Castors (car-parts trade, tram stop) — French Wikipedia [2][3]
  - Gambetta cliffs over the Front de Mer (viewpoint) — French Wikipedia [2]
- Fits: Q3 "Moyen"; Q4 "En couple"; Q5 "Salarié·e" (the tram runs east to USTO and west to the centre [3]).

### Akid Lotfi, Canastel and Les Falaises (east coast, upmarket)

- Feel: Canastel, Les Falaises, Bel Air and the Front de Mer are the upscale sea-view districts, with recent buildings, villas and hotels. Akid Lotfi is modern and "relatively accessible", with residences, shops and a small community of foreign families and young executives [6] (city file, Cyril Jarnias 2026-05-13, low confidence). Wikipedia lists Akid Lotfi among the newer commercial hubs and names the Canastel cliff and a public garden on the Oran–Canastel axis [2].
- Rent: Canastel is "dearer" (city file, ZoomAlgérie, no figure). Canastel and Akid Lotfi are the dearest areas for purchase (city file, Cyril Jarnias). Villa rentals in Akid Lotfi often require a year paid upfront [6]. No asking rent was found.
- Getting around: no tram stop is named after these quarters [3]; car or taxi. The aggregator taxi rate is 121 DZD pickup plus 44 DZD a km (city file, combien-coute.net).
- Places:
  - Sheraton Oran (hotel, Bd du 19 Mars, Route des Falaises; rooms from DA14,250; free airport shuttle) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Arena (upscale nightclub at the Sheraton) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Regus Oran, Bd des Falaises (coworking, Résidence les 5 Continents; listed as opening 1 June 2027) — workin.space [7]
  - Canastel cliff and the public garden on the Oran–Canastel axis — French Wikipedia [2]
  - Le Méridien Oran Hotel & Convention Center (hotel on a cliff, Les Genêts, Route 75; which quarter it belongs to is not stated) — Wikivoyage [1]
- Fits: Q3 "Le prix compte peu"; Q4 "Avec mes enfants" for families who drive (foreign families named there [6]); Q10 "Retrouver d'autres repats" (the only quarter where a source names a foreign community); Q2 "Danser jusqu'au matin" (Arena).

### Bir El Djir, USTO and Hai Es Sabah (east, affordable, tram)

- Feel: Bir El Djir is the eastern suburb, 171,883 people in 2009. It was agricultural until the late 1980s and is now a major centre of the metropolitan area, with Sonatrach buildings, the EHU hospital, the conference centre, USTO and the Olympic complex [4]. A blog calls it more affordable (city file, Cyril Jarnias, low confidence).
- Rent: suburbs 1-bed 16,868 DZD (≈ €111), 3-bed 27,362 (≈ €180) (city file, combien-coute.net, low confidence); Bir El Djir-specific listings not found.
- Getting around: tram stops Cité USTO, Hôpital 1er Novembre, Université USTO, Cité El Yasmine and Hai Es Sabah, to the Sidi Maârouf bus station (SNTF) [3]. A tram fork into Bir El Djir was planned but stalled as of 2024 [3].
- Places:
  - USTO Mohamed Boudiaf (university, campus designed by Kenzo Tange) — French Wikipedia [4][2]
  - EHU "1er Novembre 1954" (public hospital) — French Wikipedia [4]; city file (Cyril Jarnias)
  - Complexe olympique Miloud Hadefi (stadium, 40,000 seats per French Wikipedia, 50,000 per the city file; pool, athletics track) — French Wikipedia [4]
  - Palais des Congrès (conference centre) — French Wikipedia [4]
  - Makers Lab (coworking, Résidence Mirabelle, Bloc C5; open desk from 8,000 DZD a month) — workin.space [7]
  - The Think (coworking, Bir El Djir; prices on request) — workin.space [7]
- Fits: Q3 "Serré" to "Moyen"; Q5 "Salarié·e" (Sonatrach, the hospital and the university are here [4]); Q5 "Télétravail" (the cheapest coworking found [7]); Q7 "Reste à taille humaine" is doubtful, given rapid growth.

### Aïn El Turk (coastal resort town, 15 km)

- Feel: a seaside resort of 37,010 people (2008), about 15 km north-west of Oran, with hotels and tourist complexes and a plain of orchards behind [5]. A blog says the city's nightlife centres on the Corniche, including a club open until 4 am in Aïn El Turk (city file, Cyril Jarnias nightlife, low confidence).
- Rent: not found.
- Getting around: no bus line or fare was found. A taxi of about 15 km would come to roughly 121 + 44 × 15 = 781 DZD at the aggregator's per-km rate. That is a calculation, not a quoted fare (city file, combien-coute.net).
- Places:
  - Beaches of Aïn El Turk, Saint Roch, Paradis, Bouisseville and Trouville — French Wikipedia [5]
  - Plage des Andalouses (resort beach, about 8 km from Aïn El Turk) — French Wikipedia [2]
  - Cap Falcon lighthouse and a thalassotherapy centre with a pool — French Wikipedia [5]
  - Sun House (club, open until 4 am) — city file (Cyril Jarnias, low confidence)

## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances". General limits: no photos of official buildings (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-15) [8]; drinking in public is illegal (FCDO, city file).

### One day

- Morning: walk through Sidi El Houari: the Pasha mosque (visits 8:00–12:00 except Friday) and the mausoleum of Sidi El Houari [2]. Then the cable car from the old city up to Fort de Santa Cruz and its chapel (DA60 one way, DA100 return, 07:30–19:00) [1].
- Midday: Le Royaume du Couscous, 15 Rue Ho Chi Minh [1], or Délices de Yemma, Bd de la Soummam [1].
- Afternoon: Musée national Ahmed Zabana, 19 Avenue Ahmed Zabana (no fee given) [1]; Place du 1er Novembre, the theatre and the former cathedral, now a library [1][2]; Promenade de Létang above the port [2]. All on foot [1].
- Evening: the Front de Mer for ice cream and the sea view [1]; dinner at Les Ambassadeurs [1].

### One week

- Day 1: the one-day route.
- Day 2: **daily life: a market morning** at Médina Jdida (tram M'dine el Djadida) [1][3], and the Bey Mohamed el Kébir mosque on Bd Tripoli [2]. Afternoon on the Choupot-Maraval shopping streets [2].
- Day 3: **rush hour**. Ride the tram east at morning peak (every four minutes [3]) from Place 1er Novembre to Université USTO and Hai Es Sabah [3]. See USTO, the EHU hospital and the Miloud Hadefi complex [4]; this is the commute of a salaried newcomer.
- Day 4: **trip out: the western coast**. Aïn El Turk beaches, Cap Falcon lighthouse [5] and Les Andalouses [2]. Transport not sourced (taxi, about 781 DZD computed). Out of season, it shows what the resort is like in daily life.
- Day 5: Akid Lotfi and Canastel by taxi [6][2], the residential side where foreign families live; Es Sénia mall in the evening by tram [1][3].
- Day 6 (Friday): Friday prayer time is quiet, and shops partly close (city file). Visit Abdellah Ben Salem Mosque from outside [1]; Oran Zoo (DA100) [1].
- Day 7: a football match: MC Oran at Stade Ahmed-Zabana [2] (no fixture checked). Or **second trip out: Tlemcen**, for the Great Mosque, El Mechouar and Sidi Boumédiène [9][12]. No transport time or fare from Oran was found, and France Diplomatie strongly advises against approaching the closed Moroccan border [8]; Tlemcen itself is not named in its zoning, so check the current map first.
