# Ouagadougou, Burkina Faso

> Curated 2026-11-14. Raw items for Là-bas — not published text. Testimonies are anecdotes, not data.

Context only: Ouagadougou is flagged `unsafe` and is not proposed by the site. This file holds only
what is needed to explain why (official advice, conflict, civil liberties, dated sources). No lifestyle
or price curation was done on purpose. Items marked "(national)" describe the whole country and are
filed here because the capital is where their effects are most visible.

## Items

- [housing.areas] Displacement camps are not recognised in the capital; displaced people settle in unplanned "non-loti" fringe areas such as Pazani, Zongo and Kamboinsé, where basic services are thin or absent. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus "Situation sécuritaire", 2026-01-30 (citing Degorce et al. 2024 and PASAS 2025), official, confidence: medium
- [housing.areas] The same report says authorities fear a mass influx of displaced people into Ouagadougou, so official counts of displaced residents are contested. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30, official, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] FCDO reports armed attacks on the Ouagadougou–Bobo-Dioulasso road, rising use of landmines and explosive devices on major routes, vehicle hold-ups increasing outside the capital, and buses stopped by armed men, especially at night. — UK FCDO safety and security page, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [daily.transport] A US-based think-tank analysis cited by the Belgian asylum office says armed groups encircle about 130 towns and cut the main roads linking Ouagadougou to Togo, Benin, Niger, Mali and Côte d'Ivoire. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30 (citing Africa Center for Strategic Studies, 2025-09-09), official, confidence: medium
- [health.care] Canada says quality medical care is limited, "even in major cities such as Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso". — Government of Canada travel advice, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] The UK FCDO advises against all travel to the whole of Burkina Faso, capital included, citing terrorist attacks, terrorist kidnapping and an unstable political situation; travelling against this advice may void insurance. — UK FCDO travel advice, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] The FCDO says the terrorism threat is present "increasingly in the capital, Ouagadougou"; possible targets include shopping centres, transport hubs and religious sites. — UK FCDO safety and security page, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] The UK has no embassy in Burkina Faso: consular help comes from Accra with no in-person assistance, and the FCDO warns it may not be able to evacuate British nationals. — UK FCDO travel advice, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] France formally advises against all travel to Burkina Faso; Burkinabè authorities unilaterally broke off diplomatic relations, and the French embassy and the consulate general in Ouagadougou are closed. — France Diplomatie, updated 2026-09-10, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] France Diplomatie says French residents and travellers face possible curfews and checkpoints and a risk of arrest and detention, and asks them to stay well away from gatherings in Ouagadougou. — France Diplomatie, updated 2026-09-10, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Canada advises avoiding all travel (its highest level); it places Ouagadougou within the terrorism threat and lists hotels, restaurants and markets used by foreigners as possible targets. — Government of Canada travel advice, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Canada reports pickpocketing, bag and phone snatching near the UN roundabout and the central market, notes petty-crime risk is higher after dark in general (not stated for those spots specifically), plus violent criminal groups and attacks near withdrawal points. — Government of Canada travel advice, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high ✎ spot-check 2026-11-14: corrected
- [safety.general] The FCDO separately says thieves near the UN Circle and the former Central Market are often armed. — UK FCDO safety and security page, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] FCDO: protests are common, government buildings have been targeted, crowds "can gather quickly and may become violent", and further coups are possible. — UK FCDO safety and security page, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] ACLED data for 1 Jul 2024–14 Nov 2025 show no conflict deaths in the Centre region (Ouagadougou); recorded incidents were abductions of magistrates and gendarmes firing on a truck at the Paspanga camp. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] A Jeune Afrique mapping of ACLED data shows VDP militias deployed everywhere except the Ouagadougou region, where the army keeps dominant control and jihadist presence is described as more limited. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30 (citing Jeune Afrique, 2025-10-25), official, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] The same report says JNIM operated across large areas from January to July 2025, "progressively encircling" Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30, official, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] Some diplomats and analysts now call a JNIM takeover of Ouagadougou plausible; Le Monde (June 2025) judged it improbable in the short term, and ISS said the groups lack the firepower and logistics for a lasting siege. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30, official, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] ADF magazine reports extremists control up to 60% of the country, "essentially surrounding the capital"; ISS analysts it quotes say they lack the personnel and weapons to take the city. — ADF (Africa Defense Forum), 2026-03-03, intl-media, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] An Atlantic Council analysis says armed groups move relatively freely across as much as 80% of the territory (a figure Ouagadougou disputes) and that independent security reporting is increasingly scarce. — Atlantic Council AfricaSource, 2026-02-23, academic, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] On the night of 3–4 January 2026 a major security alert hit Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso; authorities called it an attempted coup and several soldiers were arrested. — Timbuktu Institute, 2026-01-09, academic, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] After "unprecedented coordinated attacks" in neighbouring Mali, the security ministry launched operation WIBGA-2, posting police at strategic intersections in the capital and asking residents to report suspicious behaviour near barracks and airports. — AFP via Seneweb, 2026-04-29, intl-media, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] On 18 November 2025 hundreds of police and gendarmes searched the Sankar Yaaré market, seizing explosives, cyanide and narcotics; the security minister said armed groups sourced explosive materials in such markets. — RFI via allAfrica, 2025-11-20, intl-media, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] HRW says security forces have turned dozens of villas in Ouaga 2000 and other properties on the outskirts into unofficial detention sites where hundreds, including critics, have reportedly been held incommunicado. — Human Rights Watch, 2026-06-22, intl-media (NGO), confidence: high
- [safety.general] Journalist Atiana Serge Oulon was taken from his Ouagadougou home in June 2024 by men claiming to be intelligence agents and remained disappeared two years on; several dozen critics have been unlawfully conscripted and sent to combat zones. — Human Rights Watch, 2026-06-22, intl-media (NGO), confidence: high
- [safety.general] (national) Attacks continue in the north: on 1 September 2026 JNIM killed 24 civilians at the Soubo artisanal gold site (Yatenga), and an explosive device killed three more near Mera on 5 September. — Human Rights Watch, 2026-11-04, intl-media (NGO), confidence: high
- [safety.general] (national) HRW documented 57 incidents killing more than 1,800 civilians in 11 regions (Jan 2023–Aug 2025) by the army, VDP militias and JNIM, and counts more than 2.3 million displaced. — Human Rights Watch Q&A, 2026-04-02, intl-media (NGO), confidence: high
- [safety.women] (national) A sociologist at Université Joseph Ki-Zerbo quoted by the Belgian asylum office says displacement has reinforced patriarchal dynamics and increased gender-based violence; no Ouagadougou-specific data. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30, official, confidence: low
- [climate.daily] Canada notes the June–October rainy season can bring floods that disrupt services in the capital. — Government of Canada travel advice, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [rights.lgbt] (national) Same-sex sexual activity and the "promotion" of same-sex relationships are criminal offences with sentences of up to 5 years; the FCDO reports little to no public acceptance and a higher risk of harassment or violence. — UK FCDO safety and security page, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [rights.lgbt] (national) Canada says LGBTQ+ people face violence and discrimination and advises against public displays of affection. — Government of Canada travel advice, updated 2026-10-14, official, confidence: high
- [rights.racism] (national) HRW says hate speech, stigmatisation and violent attacks against Fulani (Peul) communities have intensified since the current leader took power, with Fulani civilians often treated as collectively aligned with armed groups. — Human Rights Watch Q&A, 2026-04-02, intl-media (NGO), confidence: high
- [other] (national) The death penalty was reinstated in October 2026 for terrorism, espionage, treason, parricide and importing expired or banned products that cause death. — UK FCDO safety and security page, updated 2026-10-26, official, confidence: high
- [other] (national) Burkina Faso has published no updated official count of displaced people since March 2023 (just over 2 million); diplomatic and humanitarian sources cited by the Belgian asylum office estimate 3–5 million. — Belgian CGRA/Cedoca COI Focus, 2026-01-30, official, confidence: medium
