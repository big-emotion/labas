# Santo António, São Tomé and Príncipe

> Curated 2026-11-14. Raw items for Là-bas — not published text. Testimonies are anecdotes, not data.

Currency note: EUR equivalents use the dossier rate **€1 = 24.5 STN** (fixed euro peg), rounded. USD figures are converted at **US$1 ≈ 21.4 STN** (Wikivoyage, rate dated August 2026), so US$1 ≈ €0.87. Research-depth caveat: the session's shared web-search budget was exhausted, so this file relies on pages opened directly: dossier sources, Téla Nón articles found through its on-site search, Wikipedia and Wikivoyage. No price source specific to Santo António or Príncipe exists in the aggregators reached (livingcost.org covers only São Tomé and Trindade). Items marked (national) apply to the whole country.

## Items

- [cost.living] Consumer goods on Príncipe cost three to five times more than in São Tomé, according to island business owners asking the central government to restore subsidies; the cause is an unreliable sea link and chronic fuel shortages. — Téla Nón, 2021-02-08, local-media, confidence: medium (older)
- [cost.living] An opposition leader said in the 2026 campaign that the island no longer grows enough food and imports bananas, matabala and even brooms from São Tomé (a campaign claim, not verified). — Téla Nón, 2026-09-25, local-media, confidence: low
- [cost.living] Construction is constrained: sand extraction and tree felling are restricted to protect the environment, so sand has been imported from the African mainland coast. — Téla Nón, 2026-09-25, local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Getting to the capital: a domestic flight of about 30 minutes costs US$180–220 return (≈ 3,850–4,710 STN, €157–192); the ferry costs about US$90 (≈ 1,930 STN, €79), takes about 10 hours and runs twice a week. — Expat.com guide, 2025-10-21, company, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Locals often take cargo boats, a roughly 10-hour overnight crossing the guide says is not considered particularly safe; charter boats do it in about 3.5 hours. — Wikivoyage São Tomé and Príncipe, accessed 2026-11-14, blog, confidence: low
- [daily.transport] Príncipe Airport is 3 km north of town, with a 1,750 m runway extended in 2015; its only commercial flights go to São Tomé (STP Airways, Afrijet), and it is not certified for instrument flight. — Wikipedia "Príncipe Airport" and "Santo António", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] On the island there are few roads and many are unpaved; people hire a driver with a car or motorbike, or a boat. — Wikivoyage, accessed 2026-11-14, blog, confidence: low
- [daily.transport] On 26 August 2026 the inter-island cargo ship Rehoboth sank while docking at the island's only port and blocked it; fuel stations had long queues and people bought fuel in drums. — Téla Nón, 2026-08-26, local-media, confidence: high
- [daily.transport] The regional president promised to start building the island's first mooring port, in Santo António bay, during the next term. — Téla Nón, 2026-09-25, local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.water] An opposition leader claimed more than 80% of Príncipe's population has no drinking water and that only part of Santo António gets water, every other day; the ruling party said only some localities lack water. — Téla Nón, 2026-09-25, local-media, confidence: low
- [daily.power] In March 2024 the island lived "one of the worst" power crises: only two of the three generator units at the regional plant were working, at reduced output, and spare parts were not due until late April. — Téla Nón, 2024-03-17, local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.power] In October 2026 the infrastructure ministry sought a firm to supervise construction of a solar photovoltaic park at Azeitona, Príncipe. — Téla Nón (tender notice), 2026-10-27, local-media, confidence: high
- [daily.power] Residents linked a 2026 wave of break-ins to night-time power cuts that leave several areas in the dark. — Téla Nón, 2026-10-29, local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.internet] Healthcare, schooling and internet are all weaker on Príncipe than on São Tomé; the island is called the least practical place for long-term living. — Immigrant Invest, 2026-09-06, company, confidence: medium
- [daily.money] (national) No ATM accepts overseas cards; cash is best withdrawn at the main bank in the capital, so cash must be brought from São Tomé. — UK FCDO and Wikivoyage, accessed 2026-11-14, official, confidence: medium
- [health.care] Hospital de Príncipe in Santo António is the only hospital and is very limited; complex cases need evacuation via São Tomé. — Expat.com guide, 2025-10-21, company, confidence: medium
- [health.care] Health investment under way: a UNDP tender (Oct 2025) to upgrade the Porto Real health post into a full health centre, and a May 2026 tender for a steam autoclave for Príncipe. — Téla Nón (tender notices), 2025-10-21 and 2026-05-29, local-media, confidence: high
- [health.care] Malaria: 3 cases recorded on Príncipe in Q1 2025, against 1,293 on São Tomé island. — Immigrant Invest, 2026-09-06, company, confidence: medium
- [family.schools] No international school on the island; school works are tendered in Praia Abade (2026) and vocational equipment was delivered to Príncipe schools by the Portuguese-funded ERGUES project (Oct 2025). — Expat.com 2025-10-21 and Téla Nón 2025-10-09 and 2026-06-16, local-media, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] A growing wave of burglaries of homes and shops worried residents in October 2026; stolen items included phone credit cards, cash and school bags. One suspect was detained, and residents asked for more police patrols. — Téla Nón, 2026-10-29, local-media, confidence: high
- [safety.general] (national) Crime is generally low but burglaries and armed robberies happen; the whole country is under France's yellow "heightened vigilance" level. — UK FCDO accessed 2026-11-14; France Diplomatie updated 2026-09-15, official, confidence: high
- [social.culture] The Auto de Floripes street play, staged on 15 August, is the highlight of Príncipe's culture month, which also has talks and art and food workshops. Dozens of emigrants come home for August. — Téla Nón, 2026-08-18, local-media, confidence: high
- [social.culture] The town is known for colonial architecture (it was the colonial capital 1753–1852); in 2024 the regional president sought joint funding with the Lusophone capitals' union UCCLA to restore it. — Wikipedia "Santo António" accessed 2026-11-14; Téla Nón 2024-10-26, local-media, confidence: medium
- [social.sport] The Estádio Regional 13 de Junho is the island's only sports facility, home to Sporting Clube do Príncipe and GD Os Operários. — Wikipedia "Santo António", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [social.pace] Town population was 2,620 at the 2012 census (Wikipedia) or about 1,500 (Expat.com 2025); the whole island had about 8,420 people in 2018. Listed separately because the figures conflict. — Wikipedia "Santo António" and "Príncipe", accessed 2026-11-14; Expat.com guide 2025-10-21, academic, confidence: medium
- [community.diaspora] A Cape Verdean sports paper's donation to the São Francisco elderly day centre in Santo António (9 residents, 67 daily meals) says people of Cape Verdean descent form most of the local population. — Téla Nón, 2026-10-14, local-media, confidence: low
- [other] Regional elections on Príncipe drew four parties and more than 6,000 registered voters in late September 2026; water, the port and jobs were the main issues. — Téla Nón, 2026-09-25, local-media, confidence: high
- [climate.daily] Very warm, humid all year (average 24.8 °C, humidity 86%); about 1,872 mm of rain over 188 rainy days a year; July driest (21 mm), October wettest (398 mm). — Wikipedia "Santo António", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [nature.access] Príncipe has been a UNESCO biosphere reserve since 2012, and the Obô do Príncipe natural park dates from 2006; plantations have largely reverted to forest. — Wikipedia "Príncipe", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: high
- [rights.lgbt] (national) Same-sex activity is legal; attitudes are less tolerant than in the UK and public affection may draw negative attention. — UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14, official, confidence: high
