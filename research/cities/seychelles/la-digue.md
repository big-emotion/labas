# La Digue, Seychelles

> Curated 2026-11-14. Raw items for Là-bas — not published text. Testimonies are anecdotes, not data.

Extra city (the dossier's "La Digue (extra, optional)": the third inhabited island, a mostly car-free life by bicycle). Currency note: SCR→EUR conversions use the dossier rate **1 EUR ≈ 16 SCR** (implied by Expat.com, Nov 2025). USD→EUR conversions use **1 EUR ≈ 1.10 USD** (implied by Expat.com FR vs EN salary figures, Nov 2025). The shared web-search budget ran out after the first three searches of this pass, so the file rests on direct fetches (see Gaps).

## Items

- [cost.rent] Simple houses on La Digue: €600–1,200/month (≈ 9,600–19,200 SCR), with limited supply. — Expat.com FR guide, 2025-11-26, intl-media, confidence: medium
- [cost.rent] Room or 1-bed rentals around USD 490–850/month (≈ €445–773); housing is scarce, which makes the search complex. — Cyril Jarnias, "Living in Seychelles", 2026-07-06, blog, confidence: low
- [cost.living] An aggregator puts a single person's monthly costs at: rent USD 850 (≈ €773), food USD 620 (≈ €564), electricity USD 45, internet USD 85; it does not explain its method. — Living Cost Index, "La Digue in 2026", accessed 2026-11-14, company, confidence: low
- [cost.salary] The same aggregator gives an average full-time salary of USD 1,500/month (≈ €1,364), range USD 450–3,800, without sources. — Living Cost Index, accessed 2026-11-14, company, confidence: low
- [business.invest] La Digue sale prices: average about 7,280 SCR/m² (≈ €455/m²), the lowest of the three islands; a 584 m² waterfront plot at Anse Réunion listed at USD 345,000 (≈ €314,000) and a 2,500 m² plot at Grosse Roche at USD 395,000 (≈ €359,000). — Cyril Jarnias, "Seychelles real estate prices", 2026-07-06, blog, confidence: low
- [housing.areas] About 2,800 people (2014) live mostly in the west-coast villages of La Passe (the administrative centre, with the ferry jetty) and Anse Réunion. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [work.jobs] Tourism is the main industry; copra and vanilla were the mainstays before 1960. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] There is no airport; residents reach Mahé by ferry, usually via Praslin. The bicycle is the main way around; a few private vehicles exist, but most cars and buses belong to hotel companies, and ox-carts are still used. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Cat Cocos ferry times: Mahé–La Digue direct 1 h 10, via Praslin 1 h 45; Praslin–La Digue 15 min. — Cat Cocos schedule page, accessed 2026-11-14, company, confidence: medium
- [daily.internet] Internet on La Digue is described as sometimes unreliable. — Expat.com EN guide, 2025-11-26 (as checked in the dossier verification log), intl-media, confidence: low
- [daily.power] The island has a PUC power plant at L'Union; residents of the nearby elderly home are exposed to its smoke and noise, and PUC is taking over that site to expand its installations and improve service to La Digue. — Seychelles Nation, 2026-11-14, local-media, confidence: high
- [health.care] La Digue has a small hospital, but some residents prefer Praslin or Victoria, and women usually give birth in Victoria. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [health.care] Conflicting description: La Digue has "a dispensary only". — Expat.com EN guide, 2025-11-26, intl-media, confidence: medium
- [health.care] Medical facilities are limited on isolated islands, some with no doctor; insurance covering evacuation is advised. — Government of Canada travel advice, 2026-06-09, official, confidence: high
- [family.schools] La Digue has a primary and a secondary school; there is no international school on Praslin or La Digue. — Seychelles Nation (elderly-home article) 2026-11-14; Expat.com EN guide 2025-11-26, local-media/intl-media, confidence: medium
- [other] Government services are limited, so many residents travel to Praslin for them; the post office closes on Sundays; a small police station was set up mainly for tourists. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [other] A new 10-unit residential care home for the elderly (about R15 M, ≈ €940,000) had its foundation stone laid on 13 Nov 2026, due December 2027; the old site is flood-prone. — Seychelles Nation, 2026-11-14, local-media, confidence: high
- [social.religion] Residents (Diguois) are mostly Catholic; the 15 August feast is a major local holiday. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [nature.access] The Veuve Nature Reserve protects the rare black paradise flycatcher (about 100 birds); Belle Vue / Eagle's Nest, the highest point, rises above 300 m with guided hikes. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [climate.daily] Temperatures usually run 24–32 °C; heavy rain is common but short (an hour or less), heaviest October–March. — Wikipedia "La Digue", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] Neither the UK nor the Canadian advisory has a La Digue section; the national picture is mostly non-violent petty crime, with warnings for beaches, trails and holiday lets. — UK FCDO accessed 2026-11-14; Government of Canada 2026-06-09, official, confidence: medium

- [health.care] St Mary's Hospital replaced the old Logan Hospital and has handled all services since 23 October 2023: consultations, emergency care, dialysis, surgery, X-ray, ultrasound, laboratory, pharmacy, a labour room, dental care and a hospice. It cost about US$4 M from the UAE (≈ €3.6 M) plus R2 M from a local fund (≈ €125,000). This settles the earlier hospital-vs-dispensary conflict. — Seychelles Nation, 2023-10-23, local-media, confidence: high
- [health.care] The La Passe pharmacy opens Monday to Friday 9:00–18:00 and is closed at weekends; emergency numbers are 999, and 151 for the free A&E line. The same guide still lists Logan Hospital, which has closed. — SeyVillas health guide, © 2026, company, confidence: low
- [daily.power] La Digue gets its power from Praslin by undersea cable. After the cable was damaged, two standby generators (first installed in 2019) were moved in February 2025 to a new substation at L'Union Estate, to keep power on whenever supply from Praslin fails. — Seychelles Nation, 2025-02-22, local-media, confidence: high
- [daily.power] On 21 April 2026 tree-felling at Cap Samy on Praslin cut power to La Digue as well, from about 1:50 p.m. until about 4 p.m. — Seychelles Nation, 2026-04-22, local-media, confidence: high
- [daily.water] Night-time water cuts from 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. covered La Digue in November 2025, and new restrictions followed in July 2026 after two months with very little rain. — Seychelles Nation, 2025-11-21 and 2026-07-09, local-media, confidence: high
- [daily.water] A new sewage plant at L'Union Estate (SCR 320 M, ≈ €20 M, 1,000 m³ a day) had 60 properties connected in May 2026 and aims to cover 95% of household wastewater; households had relied on septic tanks and soak pits. Connection is free, but a monthly charge based on water use will follow. — State House, 2026-05-21, official, confidence: high
- [daily.transport] From 1 June 2026 Seychellois pay half the port fee: Mahé–La Digue R25 → R12 (≈ €1.56 → €0.75), Praslin–La Digue R15 → R8; visitors' fees are unchanged. — Seychelles Nation, 2026-06-01, local-media, confidence: high
- [daily.transport] Getting around in 2019: bike hire about US$11 (≈ €10, the length of hire is not stated), a full day by ox cart US$160–200 (≈ €145–182), a half day by taxi about US$90 (≈ €82). There were only a handful of taxis and no car rental. — Seychelles News Agency via SBC, 2019-05-09, local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] An old ferry tariff (about 2013) put the Praslin–La Digue crossing at 70 SCR one way (≈ €4.40 at today's rate) after a 5 SCR rise. — ATC News, ~2013-01, local-media, confidence: low
- [business.invest] La Passe sale prices: small traditional houses about €2,500/m² (≈ 40,000 SCR), luxury seafront homes above €5,000/m² (≈ 80,000 SCR). This conflicts with the same author's 7,280 SCR/m² island average. — Cyril Jarnias, 2026-04-27, blog, confidence: low
- [cost.negotiation] One guide advises opening property offers about 10–15% below the asking price and lists La Digue among the most sought-after areas in 2024. — Cyril Jarnias, 2025-10-02, blog, confidence: low
- [safety.women] No La Digue data. Nationally, 26% say women often or always face sexual harassment in public spaces, and 75% want police and courts to do more. — Afrobarometer dispatch AD1105, 2026-01-07 (2025 survey), academic, confidence: medium
