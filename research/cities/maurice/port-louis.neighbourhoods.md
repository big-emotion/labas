# Port-Louis: neighbourhoods and routes

> Built 2026-11-14 from the city file and targeted searches. Research material, not published text. Every named place has a source; nothing is invented. Prices are dated asking prices, not what people pay.

Rate: the dossier's **1 EUR ≈ 52.5 MUR** (`research/countries/maurice.md`, implied by Numbeo, November 2026). "[city file]" means `research/cities/maurice/port-louis.md`; bracketed numbers refer to "Sources read" at the end. The session's shared web-search budget was already spent when this pass started, so everything below comes from the city file, the dossier and pages fetched directly (Wikipedia, Wikivoyage, Expat.com, OpenShores, an investment blog, school and hospital sites). Many Wikivoyage listings carry no date and some links on them are dead; they are used only for a place's existence and street.

Shared caveats for every neighbourhood below:

- Where people actually live: the dossier says most middle-class and expat households live on the Plaines Wilhems plateau (Quatre Bornes, Rose Hill, Ebène, Moka) or on the coast and commute into the capital [dossier]. Ebène / Quatre Bornes has its own city file; Beau Bassin–Rose Hill and Moka are kept here because they are the nearest commuter bases.
- Safety: France rates the whole country at normal vigilance, but says assaults have risen since 2025 and names town centres and outskirts at night, beaches, parks and public car parks as risk spots; burglaries are frequent (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-15) [city file]. Island-wide curfews ran 12–18 Oct 2026 after a police shooting (FCDO; Le Mauricien) [city file].
- Water: Mare-aux-Vacoas reservoir at 33% on 13 Nov 2026, possibly falling to 20% (Le Mauricien) [city file]; whether Port-Louis taps are cut was not found.
- Up-front cash: deposit usually 1–2 months; agency commission one month's rent plus 15% VAT, shared between owner and tenant; leases usually one year, most rentals furnished (Expat.com, 2026-02-17) [city file][3].
- Moving around: Metro Express Port Louis Victoria – Curepipe, Rs 32–52 a ride (Expat.com, 2026-02-24) [city file]; buses Rs 17–47 by distance, cash (Expat.com, 2026-02-20) [4]; two bus stations, Immigration Square (Gare du Nord) for the north and east and Victoria Square for the south and west [1][2]; taxis have no meters, agree the fare first [city file]; ride-hailing apps DodoGo, Yugo and MoKabb show fares before booking, and Uber runs through a local taxi franchise (Wikivoyage, undated) [1].

## Neighbourhoods

### City centre: Caudan, Place d'Armes, Chinatown

- Feel: the business district; an investment blog describes a captive clientele of executives, banks and service firms around the Caudan Waterfront and centre, and retailers, restaurants and liberal professions along Chinatown, rue Royale and Edith Cavell (2026-06-09) [6]. Place d'Armes is busy by day and peaceful in the evening; Chinatown is "surprisingly quiet at night" (Wikivoyage, undated) [1].
- Rent: 1-bed in the centre Rs 24,300/month (≈ €463), Numbeo 2026-11-12 [city file]; studio Rs 12,000–22,000 (≈ €229–419) and 1-bed Rs 18,000–35,000 (≈ €343–667), OpenShores 2026-03-10 [city file][5]; renovated city-centre 2-bed Rs 25,000+ (≈ €476+), OpenShores 2026-04-29 [city file]. Apartment sale average about Rs 54,000/m² (≈ €1,029) [city file].
- Getting around: on foot; Metro from Port Louis Victoria (and a temporary Place d'Armes station) towards Rose Hill and Curepipe [7]; both bus stations are in or next to the centre [1][2]. Street parking is limited to two hours [1]; city-centre parking Rs 200–500 a day (≈ €3.81–9.52) [city file]. A diversion on rue Mgr-Leen runs 16 Nov–21 Dec 2026 [city file].
- Watch: FCDO advises against walking alone at night in the backstreets of the business district (read 2026-11-14) [city file]. Wikivoyage warns to keep belongings close in the Central Market and calls the Citadelle "unsafe to visit on your own" (undated) [1].
- Places:
  - Central Market, "Bazar" (fruit, snacks, handicrafts; cheapest food in the capital; full meal Rs 150–250) — Wikivoyage [1], OpenShores [5]
  - Winner's Supermarket (supermarket, Sir Célicourt Antelme Street) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Caudan Waterfront (shops, cinemas, craft market, casino; opened 1996) — Wikipedia [8], Wikivoyage [1]
  - Le Calife (Creole and Indian, halal biryani, Edith Cavell Street) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Jummah Mosque (1853, block bounded by Royal, Queen and Jummah Mosque streets) — Wikipedia [9], Wikivoyage [1]
  - St Louis Cathedral (Catholic, Sir William Newton Street) — Wikivoyage [1]
  - Jardin de la Compagnie with Travellers' Lane, and the Natural History Museum on Chaussée Street — Wikipedia [2], Wikivoyage [1]
  - National Library of Mauritius (Edith Cavell Street, opened 2000) — Wikivoyage [1]
- Fits: Q5 "Salarié·e" (banks and offices on foot [6]); Q4 "Seul·e"; Q8 "Je reste en ville"; Q2 "Un bon dîner entre amis" (Caudan restaurants [1]); Q7 "Reste à taille humaine" (quiet evenings [1]). Not Q3 "Serré" for a flat here.

### Champ de Mars and Tranquebar

- Feel: intermediate-range housing for executives and families who work in Port-Louis or on the plateau; older buildings that owners are renovating (investment blog, 2026-06-09) [6]. Tranquebar is listed by French Wikipedia among the near suburbs served by city transport [2].
- Rent: no area figure found. City-wide asking 2-bed Rs 25,000–55,000 (≈ €476–1,048) and 3-bed Rs 40,000–80,000 (≈ €762–1,524), OpenShores 2026-03-10 [city file]. Estimated average flat sale price in Tranquebar about Rs 3.5 M (≈ €66,700) [6].
- Getting around: walking distance or minibus to the centre is not sourced; the area is served by the city bus network [2]. Taxi Rs 400–800 for an urban ride (OpenShores) [city file].
- Watch: the Citadelle (Fort Adelaide, Sebastopol Street) above the city is "unsafe to visit on your own" (Wikivoyage, undated) [1].
- Places:
  - Champ de Mars racecourse (Destaining Street; free; racing May–September, usually Saturdays) — Wikivoyage [1], Wikipedia [10]

  - Hotel Le Champ de Mars (22 Frère Félix de Valois Street, next to the racecourse) — Wikivoyage [1]

### Moka (Helvétia, Telfair, Réduit)

- Feel: a village of 8,846 people (2011) on the other side of the Moka Range from Port-Louis, at 203–425 m, in a real-estate boom driven partly by Ebène CyberCity about 2 km away (Wikipedia) [13]. Expat.com calls it a planned, family-oriented hub that "feels more professional than lively", with no direct sea access (20–40 minutes' drive to beaches) (2026-02-17) [3]. The French embassy is in Moka (France Diplomatie) [dossier].
- Rent: no figure; Expat.com says prices have risen and can exceed other inland areas [3].
- Getting around: 10–15 minutes to Ébène [3]; the Metro's Réduit branch lists an Ebène Cybercity station [7]; time to Port-Louis not sourced. A car is the realistic choice (inference).

## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances".

### One day

- Morning: Central Market (fruit, dholl puri, goni baskets; keep belongings close) [1][city file]; walk past Jummah Mosque (1853) [9] to Aapravasi Ghat, the UNESCO-listed immigration depot [1].
- Midday: Le Calife for biryani [1] or a market meal (Rs 150–250) [5].
- Afternoon: Place d'Armes and Government House [1]; Jardin de la Compagnie and Travellers' Lane, the Natural History Museum next door [1]; then the Blue Penny Museum at the Caudan, where the two rare stamps are lit for 10 minutes on the half hour (entry fee not listed) [1].
- Evening: Sunset Café or Black Steer at the Caudan, with port views [1]. Everything is walkable; take a taxi after dark rather than the backstreets (FCDO) [city file].

### One week

- Day 1: the one-day route above.
- Day 2 (daily life): Metro at the morning rush (exact peak hours not sourced) from Port Louis Victoria through Rose Hill Central, among commuters, to judge a daily ride (Rs 32–52) [7][city file]; on to Curepipe (end of the line, about 40 minutes from Port-Louis) for the Trou aux Cerfs crater and the botanic gardens (daily 6:00–18:00) [16][24].
- Day 3: Pamplemousses, about 10 km northeast: SSR Botanical Garden (giant water lilies, palms; fee not found) [19][20] and L'Aventure du Sucre in an old sugar mill (daily 09:00–18:00) [20].
