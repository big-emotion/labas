## Neighbourhoods

### Centre-ville (Lumumba arrondissement)
- Feel: Lumumba is the oldest arrondissement and the administrative and commercial centre [4][P housing.areas]. Expat.com says the centre is convenient for business, restaurants and services (2025-12-15) [5]. Le Mag Voyage calls it the best-connected area, with easy taxis and public transport (2026-01-19) [7]. en.Wikivoyage says walking is fine in the main parts of town [2].
- Rent: Expat.com: furnished 1-bed USD 250–400, about 150,000–240,000 XAF USD-converted (≈ €229–366); 3-bed USD 400–650, about 240,000–390,000 (≈ €366–595) [P cost.rent]. Undated ads: a "magnificent" centre flat at 850,000 XAF (≈ €1,296), unclear whether monthly [P cost.rent].
- Getting around: shared "cent-cent" taxi 150 XAF on fixed routes; private taxi 700–1,000; airport 1,000–2,000 [P daily.transport]. STPU buses run the main routes [5]. During the Sept 2026 fuel shortage, bus fares on the Patra–centre-ville route went from 200 to 500 XAF (ADIAC, 2026-09-29) [P daily.transport].
- Watch: France advises alertness downtown at night, citing dangerous driving (2026-10-30) [P safety.general].
- Places:
  - Gare de Pointe-Noire, avenue Charles de Gaulle (1936 colonial railway terminus with a small cafeteria) — en.Wikivoyage (Aug 2020) [2]; en.Wikipedia [4]
  - Marché central, between avenue Monseigneur-Derouet and avenue Schœlcher (central market) — fr.Wikipédia [3]
  - Cathédrale Notre-Dame-de-l'Assomption (Catholic cathedral) — fr.Wikipédia [3]
  - Musée du Cercle Africain (masks, sculpture and painting; 09:00–20:00) — en.Wikivoyage (Aug 2020) [2]; fr.Wikipédia [3]
  - La Citronnelle, avenue Charles de Gaulle (bakery-pâtisserie) — fr/en.Wikivoyage [1][2]
  - Kactus, Charles de Gaulle crossing (pizza and burgers, 10:00–22:00; Oct 2017 listing) — en.Wikivoyage [2]
  - La Source du village, avenue Agostino Néto, opposite Clinique Guénin (Ethiopian and African food) — fr.Wikivoyage (Feb 2018) [1]
  - Clinique Netcare, avenue Georges Dumond, and Clinique Guénin, near the Kassaï roundabout (private clinics) — [P health.care]

### Côte Sauvage and the port-side seafront (Wharf)
- Feel: the beach quarter. Expat.com says Atlantic-coast areas are popular with expats and close to international schools, and that beachfront homes cost a premium [5]. Restaurants and hotels line the shore [1][2]. Weekends revolve around beaches, barbecues, restaurants and bars [P social.food].
- Rent: undated ad for a villa at the Wharf at 2,000,000 XAF (≈ €3,049); houses with pool and garden can exceed 2,800,000 (≈ €4,269) [P cost.rent]. No flat prices were found for the seafront.
- Getting around: private taxi 700–1,000 XAF from the centre [P daily.transport]. No distance or time found.
- Watch: France advises against walking on the beach south of the former wharf at any hour (criminal groups, 2026-10-30) [P safety.general]. Some Côte Sauvage swimming spots are dangerous, with no lifeguards [P nature.access]. The coastline recedes 2–3 m a year [3].
- Places:
  - Côte Sauvage beach (main city beach; swimming and surfing) — Expat.com [5]; en.Wikivoyage [2]
  - Le Derrick, Port Side Beach (brasserie; daily menu about 5,500 XAF, 11:30–15:00 and 19:00–22:00) — en/fr.Wikivoyage [2][1]
  - Cercle Naval / Club nautique, near the artisan market (yacht-club restaurant over the water; 7,000–9,000 XAF) — en.Wikivoyage [2]
  - Majestic, opposite the Cercle nautique (full meal about 20,000 XAF; Feb 2018) — fr.Wikivoyage [1]
  - Artisan village, Port Side Beach (crafts; prices negotiable) — en.Wikivoyage [2]
  - Pyramide (restaurant-bar on the Côte Sauvage) and Le Sea Club (modern French, facing the sea) — fr.Wikivoyage (Feb 2018) [1]
  - Hotel Twiga, avenue Dr Jacques Bouity (beachfront hotel with bar-restaurant) — en.Wikivoyage [2]
  - Hotel Palm Beach (beach hotel with pool; rooms from 80,000 XAF, undated) — en.Wikivoyage [2]
- Fits: Q8 "À la plage"; Q3 "Le prix compte peu"; Q2 "Un bon dîner entre amis"; Q10 "Retrouver d'autres repats" (expat-run restaurants [P cost.living]).

### Tié-Tié (with Fond Tié-Tié)
- Feel: an arrondissement on the railway, with the first CFCO station at kilometre 6 [3][4]. Its market and bus terminus make it a transport hub: ADIAC names the Fond Tié-Tié–Thystère bus route (2026-09-29) [P daily.transport].
- Rent: no figure found. The city-wide editorial 1-bed estimate is 150,000–400,000 XAF (≈ €229–610) [Dos].
- Getting around: buses 200 XAF in normal times; 500 during the Sept 2026 shortage on Fond Tié-Tié–Thystère and Grand marché–Thystère. The bus strike that followed boosted moto-taxis and taxis [P daily.transport].
- Watch: water mains: many neighbourhoods lacked LCDE water and private boreholes spread to every arrondissement, raising quality worries (ADIAC, 2020; old) [P daily.water].
- Places:
  - Fond Tié-Tié market (market) — OpenShores [6]
  - Tié-Tié CFCO station (railway station) — fr.Wikipédia [3]; en.Wikipedia [4]
  - Marché Thystère (market; the Thystère bus terminus is named by ADIAC) — en.Wikivoyage [2]; [P daily.transport]
- Fits: Q3 "Serré" (on the unsourced assumption that rents are lower away from the coast; no figure found); Q7 "Vibre jour et nuit".

### 5. Q1 "Tester quelques mois": first week of a trial stay
- Before landing: online visa before travel, none on arrival, yellow-fever certificate mandatory; France has a consulate general in Pointe-Noire [P admin.visa]. The visa needs an accommodation certificate or a paid hotel booking [B admin.visa].
- Where to stay first: Hotel Palm Beach (rooms from 80,000 XAF, undated) [2] or Hotel Twiga (price not listed) [2]. en.Wikivoyage warns hotels are expensive for their standard and few take cards [2]. Malonda Lodge, a few km south (bungalows, 2018 listing, no price) [1].
- Day 1: airport taxi 1,000–2,000 [P daily.transport]; bring cash. International-card ATMs are limited [P daily.money]; Crédit du Congo has some that accept foreign cards [2].
- Day 2: SIM and data. Operators MTN and Airtel are named nationally; mobile plan 5,000–15,000 a month [B daily.money][B cost.living]. Mobile-money tariffs were not found.
- Day 3: flat hunting. An old Africanews report (2016, updated 2024) says agents charged a flat 5,000 XAF to show several flats, with notice boards at intersections, and some rentals found within 24 hours [P cost.rent][P housing.search]. Landlords usually want several months' deposit [P cost.rent]. Ask 10–20 % off for leases of six months or more outside the centre [P cost.negotiation].
- Day 4: ride a cent-cent at rush hour to test the commute (150) [P daily.transport].
- Day 5: banking: account-opening rules were not found; see Gaps.
- Day 6: residence registration at the commissariat or mairie within 15–30 days in some areas [B admin.residency]; morning at the Marché central [3].
- Day 7: Mass at the cathedral [3]; Côte Sauvage by day [5].
- Rough first-week spend: Palm Beach 7 × 80,000 = 560,000; airport taxi 1,000–2,000; mobile plan 5,000–15,000; one agent showing 5,000; 14 meals between La Cité shawarma (2,000) and Le Derrick (5,500) = 28,000–77,000; 10 cent-cent rides × 150 = 1,500; 6 taxis × 700–1,000 = 4,200–6,000 → **≈ 604,700–666,500 XAF (≈ €922–1,016)**, before any rent advance. ## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances". Visa before departure, none on arrival; yellow-fever certificate required [P admin.visa]. Use trusted taxis arranged through the hotel at night [6]. Carry cash: cards are rare outside big hotels [P daily.money].

### One day
- Morning (centre): Gare de Pointe-Noire, a 1936 colonial terminus, on avenue Charles de Gaulle [2]; Musée du Cercle Africain (09:00–20:00; fee not given) [2]; breakfast at La Citronnelle [1].
- Midday: taxi (700–1,000) [P daily.transport] to Port Side Beach: lunch at Le Derrick (about 5,500) [2]; artisan village next door [2].
- Afternoon: Côte Sauvage by day: stay on the busy stretches, not south of the former wharf, and swim only where it is safe (no lifeguards) [5][P safety.general][P nature.access].
- Evening: dinner at Cercle Naval (7,000–9,000) or Majestic (about 20,000) [2][1]; back by trusted taxi [6].

### One week
- Day 1: the one-day route.
- Day 2 (daily-life moment): early morning at the Marché central or Grand Marché [3][5], then a cent-cent ride at rush hour (150) [P daily.transport].
- Day 3 (trip out): Diosso gorges, about 35 km and an hour away, by taxi hired at 3,000 XAF an hour [9][5][2]. Pay about 2,000 a person at the lookout [2]. Add the Mâ-Loango regional museum, in a former royal palace (2,500 XAF) [2][5]. Its exact location is not stated in the sources read. Loango, the former kingdom's capital, lies about 15 km from the city [3].
- Day 4 (trip out): Tchimpounga chimpanzee sanctuary, about 50 km, tour booked in advance [5][2].
- Day 5: Pointe Indienne for calmer water [5]; evening at Pyramide [1].
- Day 6 (daily-life moment): a league match at the Stade de Pointe-Noire (Stade Municipal) in Mvoumvou: clubs include AS des Cheminots and JS les Bougainvillées [3][4][P social.sport]. Fixtures were not found.
- Day 7 (daily-life moment): Sunday Mass at the Cathédrale Notre-Dame-de-l'Assomption [3]; afternoon at the Centre culturel Jean-Baptiste Tati-Loutard in Mpita (programme not found) [3].
- Longer option: Conkouati-Douli national park, about five hours north in the dry season, needs at least an overnight [5][2]. Wikivoyage calls it the country's most biodiverse park, with its only marine protected area [10]. France calls the Mayombe road to Dolisie risky in rain [P nature.access].
