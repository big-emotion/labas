# Djibouti: neighbourhoods and routes

> Built 2026-11-14 from the city file and targeted searches. Research material, not published text. Every named place has a source; nothing is invented. Prices are dated asking prices, not what people pay.

Rate: the dossier's **1 EUR ≈ 205 DJF** (`research/countries/djibouti.md`, an unchecked estimate built from the 177.721 DJF/USD peg); Wikivoyage gives €1 ≈ Fdj 203 in July 2026 [2]. USD amounts are converted at the peg (1 USD = 177.72 DJF). "[city file]" means `research/cities/djibouti/djibouti.md`; bracketed numbers refer to "Sources read" at the end. The web-search budget ran out after six queries in this pass; everything else came from direct page fetches.

Shared caveats for every neighbourhood below:

- Security: the whole capital is in France Diplomatie's yellow "enhanced vigilance" zone; there has been no attack since the 24 May 2014 bombing (La Chaumière, Place du 27 Juin), and vigilance is urged in the historic centre, at religious sites, near ports, airports and military sites, and in restaurants and bars (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-15) [city file]. FCDO advises against walking alone late at night (2026-07-27) [city file].
- Photography: photographing ports, public buildings, airports, military sites and bridges can lead to arrest (FCDO 2026-07-27; France Diplomatie 2026-09-15) [city file]. This matters in Gabode, Haramous and on the airport road, which sit next to the airport and the bases.
- Alcohol: public sale is banned in the capital's popular neighbourhoods except in hotels (France Diplomatie 2026-09-15) [city file].
- Heat: often above 40 °C from May to October; November to April is the pleasant season (France Diplomatie) [city file]. Electricity for an air-conditioned flat is the hidden cost: 40–80 USD/month for a 2-bed (Africarrières), 150–200 USD as a realistic minimum and 400–500 USD for heavy AC (blog testimonies) [city file].
- Up-front cash: deposit one to three months (Africarrières 2026-02-25), agency fee about one month (Expat.com 2025-11-04) [city file].
- Getting around: green taxis from 500 Fdj (Wikivoyage) [1] or 600 FDJ per zone (national tourism agency, ANT) [11]; minibuses from 50 FDJ per zone (ANT) [11] or 100–200 FDJ, often 150 (blog, 2026-03-08) [4]; night fares about 50 % higher [4]. No meters; agree the fare first [city file]. Ride-hailing apps Velox and VGO Taxi appear only on Wikivoyage [1].
- Weekend: the tourism office is closed on Friday and Saturday [15]; that matches a Friday–Saturday weekend, an inference not stated by a source.

## Neighbourhoods

### Haramous (with Lotah Village)

- Feel: the high-end area near embassies and international schools; the US Embassy and most of its housing sit there, and residents report no traffic (Talesmag post report 2025-08-11) [24]. A relocation blog calls it a standing residential sector with guarded streets, private guards and NGO 4x4s, villas with views over the Gulf of Tadjourah and secured compounds with generators and often pools (Cyril Jarnias, 2026-03-08) [3]. Diplomats describe walking or biking to work in five minutes [25].
- Rent: "expat-standard" studio or 1-bed 1,000–2,000 USD (≈ 177,720–355,440 DJF, ≈ €867–1,734); a 3-bed villa in Lota–Haramous listed at 265,000 DJF (≈ €1,293); a furnished 5-room villa 450,000 DJF (≈ €2,195) — Jarnias blog 2026-03-08 [city file][3]. Expat.com also names "Hérouale", near the airport and embassies, with family villas up to 400,000 DJF (≈ €1,951) [city file]; it is probably the same area spelled differently, but no source confirms that.
- Getting around: The Villa Haramous is about 3.85 km from the centre; walkability 41/100, so a car is close to essential; an 8 km taxi ride about 7.5 USD (≈ 1,330 DJF) [3]. Diplomats recommend a 4WD [city file].
- Watch: near the airport and military sites, where photography is banned (FCDO, France Diplomatie) [city file]. Power cuts often take the internet down with them (diplomat testimony, Talesmag 2025-02-22; blog, not an official source) [city file].
- Places:
  - QSI International School of Djibouti (American-curriculum school; about 70 pupils per a 2025 diplomat report; 13,400–16,200 USD in 2024–25, amounts not shown on QSI's own page) — Talesmag [24], Jarnias [3], [city file]. Expat.com places QSI in the Hérons quarter instead [26]; the location conflict is unresolved.
  - Kurry (Indian restaurant, Lootah Village Compound) — ANT [6]
  - Pizzaiolo Haramous (pizzeria; a diplomat calls it a pizza place "with a pool" for children) — ANT [6], Talesmag [24]
  - The Villa Haramous (hotel with restaurant, bar, pool, sauna and hammam) — ANT [7], Jarnias [3]
  - Casino supermarket, Haramous branch (supermarket) — Wikipedia's supermarket list cites a "Haramous Casino Supermarket" page (seen in a search excerpt only) [32]; diplomats shop at Casino and Al Gamil and find imported goods expensive [24][25]
  - Embassy of the United States (Haramous estate, Lot 350-B) — Jarnias [3], Talesmag [24]
- Fits: Q4 "Avec mes enfants" (international school and quiet streets [24]); Q3 "Le prix compte peu" (the costliest area [3]); Q2 "Tranquille chez moi"; Q7 "Prend son temps".

### Héron, Îlot du Héron and Plateau du Marabout

- Feel: historically a suburban villa area that gets more prestigious towards the seafront; wealthier expatriates and business clients; newer residences such as the "Djiboutiana" project with rooftop terraces, fibre and hybrid solar (Jarnias, 2026-03-08) [3]. Expat.com calls the "Quartier des Hérons" the most popular area for expatriate families: calm, greener, with gated residences (2025-11-04) [26][city file].
- Rent: 3-bed villa 300,000–450,000 DJF/month (≈ €1,463–2,195), Expat.com 2025-11-04 [city file]; a villa in a residential zone 1,500–3,500 USD (≈ 266,580–622,020 DJF, ≈ €1,300–3,034), Africarrières 2026-02-25 [city file]. Furnished studios of 20–45 m² are offered around Héron Marabout and Gabode, no price given [3].
- Getting around: short taxi rides to the centre within the 500–1,000 FDJ centre-to-centre range (blog) [4]; no distance figure found.
- Watch: the Kempinski and the seafront hotels are places "frequented by expatriates" where France Diplomatie asks for vigilance (2026-09-15) [city file]. Nothing area-specific found.
- Places:

  - Melting Pot (fusion restaurant, Rue Bernard, Héron; 11:00–23:00, about US$4–30) — ANT [6], Wikivoyage [1]
  - Pizzaiolo Héron (pizzeria) — ANT [6]

### Plateau du Serpent

- Feel: one of the four original plateaus of the colonial town, with Héron, Marabout and the Djibouti plateau (Jarnias) [3]; home of the city's big hotels and a search area for furnished flats [3][7]. Lonely Planet describes the Serpent and Héron area as residential, with embassies, villas and upscale hotels (search excerpt only; the page itself returned no text) [36]. Africarrières calls it "slightly cheaper" (2026-02-25) [city file].
- Rent: no Serpent-specific figure. City-centre benchmark: 1-bed 500–800 USD (≈ 88,860–142,176 DJF, ≈ €433–694), Africarrières 2026-02-25 [city file]; aggregator 1-bed centre 100,978 DJF (≈ €493), combien-coute.net 2026-11-14, low confidence [city file].
- Getting around: next to the centre; walkable to Place du 27 Juin for those who tolerate the heat; centre taxi 500–1,000 FDJ [4].
- Watch: hotels and restaurants used by foreigners fall under France Diplomatie's vigilance advice [city file]. Nothing area-specific found.
- Places:

### City centre: Place du 27 Juin, European Quarter and Quartier 1 (African Quarter)

- Feel: the commercial and administrative heart, "sometimes noisy", busy day and night; buildings of one to three storeys with shops at street level; expatriates who want city life, short-stay workers and digital nomads (Jarnias) [3]. Expat.com: lively, noisy and less green, with shops, banks and restaurants; local dishes in the Quartier africain (2025-11-04) [26]. Place du 27 Juin has Moorish-inspired arches; Hamoudi Mosque's minaret overlooks the central bus terminal (Wikipedia) [16]. Africarrières names Hamoudi among slightly cheaper areas [city file]; it is likely the quarter around the mosque, but no source says so.
- Rent: 1-bed centre 500–800 USD (≈ 88,860–142,176 DJF, ≈ €433–694), Africarrières 2026-02-25 [city file]; 2-bed 150,000–250,000 DJF (≈ €732–1,220), Expat.com 2025-11-04 [city file]; a blog average of 255,000 DJF for a 1-bed (range 120,000–390,000; ≈ €585–1,902), low confidence [city file]. Hotel rooms from about €50 a night [3].
- Getting around: many workplaces are within walking distance; minibuses leave from Place Mahmoud Harbi (also called Place Rimbaud), the central hub, roughly 05:00–22:00 [4][11]. Airport to Place Ménélik about 15 minutes, 3,000–4,000 FDJ by taxi [4].
- Watch: France Diplomatie names the historic centre, religious sites and restaurants/bars as places for vigilance; the 2014 bombing hit La Chaumière on Place du 27 Juin (2026-09-15) [city file]. A blog says some central pockets have flimsy sheet-metal and wood construction and were formerly subject to flooding (Jarnias, low confidence) [3]. Money changers work around the market area (Wikivoyage) [city file].
- Places:
  - Marché central (central market: spices, textiles, crafts, produce; khat arrives around 13:00) — Expat.com [26], Wikivoyage [2]
  - Hamoudi Mosque (Sunni mosque, built around 1906 per Wikipedia, which notes other records give 1913–1920; about 1,000 worshippers) — Wikipedia [18][16]
  - Notre-Dame-du-Bon-Pasteur Cathedral (Catholic cathedral, Boulevard de la République, dedicated 1964) — Wikipedia [19]
  - Ryad Market (market, city centre) — Wikipedia [16]
  - Café de la Gare (restaurant, Rue Nasro Houed), La Chaumière and Time Out (restaurants, Place du 27 Juin) — ANT [6]
  - Hamdani (local and Yemeni restaurant, Avenue 13), Kokeb (Ethiopian, Rue de Marseille) and Le Santal (Indian and Chinese, Rue Bender) — ANT [6]
  - Hôtel Menelik with Club Menelik (hotel and club, Place du 27 Juin) — ANT [7], Wikivoyage [1]
  - Agence Nationale du Tourisme office (tourist office, Place du 27 Juin; Sun–Thu 08:00–13:00 and 14:00–17:00) — ANT [15]
- Fits: Q7 "Vibre jour et nuit" (as far as the city goes); Q2 "Danser jusqu'au matin" (Club Menelik [1]); Q5 "J'entreprends" (banks and administration [26]); Q4 "Seul·e"; Q6 "Arabe" (Yemeni and local eateries [6]).

### Gabode (Gabode 3 and 5) and the airport road

- Feel: estates built for civil servants; expatriates, civil servants and upper-middle-class workers; walled houses, shaded courtyards, very uneven quality with plumbing, electrical, AC and generator repairs (Jarnias 2026-03-08) [3][city file].
- Rent: a 4-bed flat listed at 170,000 DJF/month (≈ €829), marked open to negotiation, posted around July 2026 (Expat.com listings) [city file]. Blog benchmark "outside the centre": 1-bed about 85,000 DJF (range 40,000–130,000; ≈ €195–634), low confidence [city file].
- Getting around: well connected to the main roads and close to the airport [3]; the airport is about 6 km from the centre [16].
- Watch: next to the airport and the Gabode industrial zone; photography ban near airports and military sites (FCDO, France Diplomatie) [city file]. Generator and power problems reported in this housing stock (blog) [3].
- Places:
  - Lycée Français de Djibouti, Kessel campus (French AEFE-partner school, CM1 to Terminale, Route de l'aéroport, Gabode; about 1,400 pupils across both campuses) — LFD [23], Wikipedia [22]
  - Al Gamil hypermarket (hypermarket, Z.I.S. Gabode) — Al Gamil brochure, search excerpt only [33]; named by diplomats as a grocery store [24]
  - Jules Verne (European restaurant, Route de l'aéroport) and Pizzaiolo Aviation (pizzeria) — ANT [6]
  - Red Sea hotel (Gabode 3), Auberge Sharaf (Gabode) and Waafi Residence Gabode 3 (Route de l'Aéroport) — ANT [7]
  - Golden Airport hotel (Route de l'Aviation; doubles listed at €105, dead listing) — ANT [7], Wikivoyage [1]
- Fits: Q5 "Salarié·e" (airport, port and free zone employers [city file]); Q4 "En couple" and "Avec mes enfants" (French school on the doorstep [23]); Q3 "Moyen"; Q7 "Reste à taille humaine".

### Ambouli and Versailles

- Feel: transitional, partly informal areas with new two- to three-storey buildings of 70–110 m² 2- or 3-bed flats, some near the sea or the Oumousalama mosque; young professionals, couples and short-stay workers; apart-hotels with cleaning and internet (Jarnias 2026-03-08) [3]. Ambouli was a village about 3 km south of the original town, known for date palms and orchards (Wikipedia) [16].
- Rent: no figure; land and rents described as "somewhat cheaper" than Haramous or Héron [3]. City benchmark for the outskirts: 1-bed 300–500 USD (≈ 53,316–88,860 DJF, ≈ €260–433), Africarrières 2026-02-25 [city file].
- Getting around: on the airport side of the city (Djibouti–Ambouli airport [16]); taxis and minibuses; no time figure found.
- Watch: nothing area-specific found.
- Places:
  - Jano House aparthotel (26 Avenue Guelleh Batal, Ambouli) — ANT [7], Jarnias [3]
  - Waafi Corniche Hotel Apartments (apart-hotel) — Jarnias [3]
  - Oumousalama mosque (mosque) — Jarnias [3]
  - La Mer Rouge (seafood restaurant, Rue Nelson Mandela, 11:00–23:00, "pricey") — Wikivoyage [1], Jarnias [3]
  - La Pergola (European restaurant, Route Nelson Mandela) and Al Janaten (local restaurant, Rue Nelson Mandela) — ANT [6]
- Fits: Q1 "Tester quelques mois" (apart-hotels [3]); Q4 "En couple"; Q3 "Moyen"; Q5 "Salarié·e".

### Balbala (with Hayableh and PK12)

- Feel: the southern suburb west of the Ambouli river, 554,350 residents at the 2024 census; over 80 % of housing is corrugated sheet, planks and metal panels, with limited piped water and electricity, and much of it "an area of extreme poverty" (Wikipedia) [20][city file]. Mostly Djiboutian low- and middle-income families; more stable parts include Cité Barwaqo, Cité Hodan and parts of Quartier 11 near the Arta road (Jarnias) [3]. PK12 on the edge is a fast-growing area with secured compounds and modern villas for staff of logistics projects [3][city file].
- Rent: no figure; described as lower than the centre (Jarnias) [3]. Africarrières says peripheral districts can save 30–40 % (2026-02-25) [city file]. Expat.com: a 3-bed in newer outer districts 200,000–300,000 DJF (≈ €976–1,463), far from services [city file].
- Getting around: Hayableh and PK12 are minibus terminals; average door-to-door minibus trip 43–44 minutes [4]. Long-distance minibuses to Arta, Ali Sabieh and Dikhil leave from the Parc à Bétail in Balbala [10].
- Watch: limited piped water and electricity (Wikipedia) [20]. A blog says Balbala is less exposed to floods than the lower town (Jarnias, low confidence) [3]. No official safety remark specific to Balbala was found.
- Places:
  - Université de Djibouti, Balbala campus (public university, opened October 2016) — Wikipédia [17]
  - Parc à Bétail (long-distance minibus station) — ANT [10], Jarnias [4]
  - Escale Djibouti International Hotel (hotel, PK 13) — ANT [7]
- Fits: Q3 "Serré" (the only area with lower rents, at the cost of services [3][20]); Q6 "Français" is less useful here than Somali (street languages per the dossier); not suited to Q4 "Avec mes enfants" for an international school. A returnee with family in Balbala may still settle there; no testimony was found.

### 1. Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "Télétravail" + Q4 "Seul·e"

- Base: Plateau du Serpent. Central, close to the hotels and cafés [3][7], and "slightly cheaper" than Héron or Haramous [city file]. Remote work is the hard part: France Diplomatie calls the internet expensive and unstable and says some social networks need a VPN (2026-09-15) [city file]; a diplomat reports the internet dropping with power cuts [city file]. Unlimited fixed internet at 50–60 Mbps is quoted at 10,500 DJF/month (≈ €51) [city file].
- Monday: work from home in the morning; lunch near Place du 27 Juin (Café de la Gare [6]); walk or a 500–1,000 FDJ taxi [4].
- Tuesday: work; early-evening run on the corniche, the seafront running route [26].
- Wednesday: work; groceries at Casino (Haramous branch [32]) or the Marché central [26], by taxi.
- Thursday: work; dinner at Sings on the Serpent [6].
- Friday: weekend day (inferred, see caveats); morning at the Marché central before the khat trucks at 13:00 [2]; afternoon beach at the Kempinski, Îlot du Héron [3].
- Saturday: day trip (see Q8 below); evening out (see Q2 below).
- Sunday: working week starts; work from a hotel lobby if the power drops (Sheraton [7]; no coworking space was found).
- Saturday night:
  - "Danser jusqu'au matin": Club Menelik at Hôtel Menelik, Place du 27 Juin; Club Hermes, La Galette, La Oasis and Le Maries are also listed (addresses not given) [1]; the Aden Bay Casino at the Sheraton stays open until 04:00 [1]. Beer about 1,000 Fdj, mixed drink 1,500 Fdj [1].
  - "Un bon dîner entre amis": Melting Pot, Héron [1][6], or La Chaumière, Place du 27 Juin [6].
  - "Tranquille chez moi": a food-delivery service exists (unnamed) [25]; cinema at Bawadi Mall on another evening (Rue de Venise; ticket about 1,500 DJF) [city file][1].
- Weekend outings (Q8):

### 3. Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "J'entreprends"

### 4. Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q4 "Avec mes enfants"

- Base: Haramous. It has QSI, quiet streets near the embassies, and children who bike to school [24][3]. The Lycée Français de Djibouti's Kessel campus is on the nearby airport road at Gabode, and its Dolto campus (nursery to CE2) is in the southern port zone [23][22]. Expat.com's "Hérons" is the alternative [26].
- Monday: school run to Kessel or QSI (a "short and easy" commute, per a diplomat at QSI [24]); groceries at Casino [32].
- Tuesday: work; after school, a swim at Pizzaiolo Haramous [24].

### 5. Q1 "Tester quelques mois": the first week

- Where to stay first: an apart-hotel in Ambouli (Jano House [7]) or Gabode (Waafi Residence Gabode 3 [7]), or a central hotel. No room rates were found on the tourism agency's list; a blog gives about €50 a night as the minimum for hotel rooms in the centre [3].
- Day 1 (arrival): the airport is 6 km from the centre [16]; a taxi to Place Ménélik costs 3,000–4,000 FDJ [4]. Tourist e-visa (14 or 90 days, single entry): 26.65 USD (≈ 4,736 DJF), about 10 working days to process (Expat.com 2025-11-04) [city file]. After three months you need a temporary residence card; overstays cost 40,000–80,000 FDJ (France Diplomatie) [city file].
- Day 2: SIM card from Djibouti Telecom, the only mobile operator. Phones need a physical SIM because eSIMs are not available (diplomat report) [city file]. No SIM price was found; a 10 GB plan costs about 20 USD/month [5]. Open a D-Money wallet: free, and it works by USSD *111# [city file].
- Day 3: housing search. Agencies (for off-market listings and ownership checks), expat groups and Airbnb/Flatio [city file]. Expat.com housing listings [city file]. The Facebook group "DJIBOUTI EXPATS" carried no rentals on 2026-11-14 [city file].
- Day 4: visits in Gabode, Ambouli and the centre by taxi (500–1,000 FDJ a ride [4]). Viewing fees: not found.
- Day 5 (Friday): Marché central and the Quartier africain for prices and food [26].
- Day 6: compare offers. Plan for the first month plus a 1–3 month deposit (Africarrières) plus an agency fee of about one month (Expat.com). Agree in writing who pays the agency (blog) [city file]. For a 1-bed at 500–800 USD that is 3–5 months, or 1,500–4,000 USD (≈ 266,580–710,880 DJF, ≈ €1,300–3,468). Ask for receipts and a signed inventory [city file].
- Day 7: bank account. The French school takes cash payments at "la BOA" (Bank of Africa) [23], so the bank exists locally. Account-opening rules for non-residents were not found. Card payments work and supermarket ATMs were used without problems (diplomat report) [city file].
- Saturday night:

  - "Un bon dîner entre amis": Melting Pot, Héron [1].
  - "Tranquille chez moi": an apart-hotel with a pool [3].

  - "À la plage": Moucha by boat, 45 minutes [13].

## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances". Go from November to April [city file]. Whale sharks show up from November to January (sightings 70–80 % likely) [2]. Do not photograph the port, the presidential palace or uniformed staff [city file][16].

### One day

- Morning: Place du 27 Juin and the tourist office for maps (open from 08:00) [15][16]. Walk to Hamoudi Mosque (from the outside; non-Muslims should ask before entering, though no source sets rules) [18], then the Marché central [26]. Notre-Dame-du-Bon-Pasteur Cathedral is on Boulevard de la République [19]. Everything is on foot.
- Midday: lunch at Hamdani (Yemeni and local, Avenue 13) or Kokeb (Ethiopian, Rue de Marseille) [6]. A modest local meal costs 5–8 USD [city file]. The khat trucks reach the Central Market around 13:00 [2].
- Afternoon: taxi (500–1,000 FDJ [4]) to Siesta Beach [16], or a boat to Moucha, 45 minutes each way [13]. No entry fees were found for these.
- Evening: the corniche at sunset [26], then dinner at Melting Pot, Héron (US$4–30) [1], or La Mer Rouge [1]. For a late drink, Club Menelik [1]. Night taxis cost about 50 % more [4].

### One week

- Day 1: the city day above.
- Day 2: Moucha and Maskali by boat. The islands have beaches, mangroves, birds and reefs [13][24]. Package tours combine Moucha with Arta Plage and Sables Blancs (The Abroad Guide lists 1,000 USD per person for three days) [31].
- Day 3: whale sharks from Arta Plage (November to January) [2][29][31], then up to Arta town (755 m) for the view over the gulf [28]. A taxi round trip costs about 8,000 FDJ [4].
- Day 4: Lac Assal, about 1.5 hours away, 120 km west on a paved road [12][27]. You can float on the salt water and walk with salt caravans [12]. Sleep at a Ghoubbet campement such as Le Campement du Rift Afar or Dankalelo [8]. You need a driver or a hired car (40–60 USD a day) [2][city file], and France Diplomatie advises against night driving [city file].
- Day 5: ferry to Tadjourah. It leaves on Tuesday at 08:00, Thursday at 10:30, Friday at 08:30 and Saturday at 08:00, costs 700 FDJ and takes 2.5–4 hours [10][4]. Afternoon at Sables Blancs (see `tadjourah.neighbourhoods.md`).
- Day 6: back by minibus (1,500 FDJ, from behind the UNFD building [10]). Daily-life moment: a weekday morning at Place Mahmoud Harbi and the Marché central. The minibuses leave "when full" and the market fills before the 13:00 khat trucks [4][2]. Or catch a national-championship match at Stade El Hadj Hassan Gouled Aptidon [21] (no fixture list found).
- Day 7: Lac Abbé at sunrise, with the limestone chimneys up to 50 m high [30][26]. Allow an overnight at a campement (Koutabouya or As Bole) [8]. It needs a 4x4 [26]. If you would rather not do the trip, take a slow morning in Haramous or Héron to see where families actually live [3].
