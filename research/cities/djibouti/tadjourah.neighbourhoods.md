**Scope warning.** Tadjourah is a town of 18,808 people (2024 census) [city file]. No source opened gives a rent. The 2026-11-15 pass added OpenStreetMap data [16][17], which names seven quarters (Galato, Saharito, Gablassan, Marsaki, Harat, Flaa, Élamo), the hospital, mosques, a primary school, two ATMs and a seafood restaurant. OSM shows each quarter as a single point, not an outline, so the groupings below are mine and follow the point positions. The four areas are: the old town and seafront (Galato, Saharito, Gablassan, Marsaki), the ferry quay and new port, the western quarters and hotel strip (Harat, Flaa, Élamo), and the Sables Blancs coast. Everything about daily life here is thinner than for the capital, and the living weeks say so.

**Distances** are straight-line, worked out by me from OSM coordinates [16][17] and measured from the Rond-Point de l'Indépendance in the centre. They are rough and are not road distances.

Shared caveats:

- Security: Tadjourah is in France Diplomatie's yellow "enhanced vigilance" zone. North of the Balho–Adaïlou–Ouaddhi–Godoria axis, army checkpoints may control access. The Eritrean border to the north is red, and Oued Kalou and Mont Moussa Ali are closed (France Diplomatie 2026-09-15; FCDO 2026-07-27) [city file]. Boaters are told to postpone trips in the southern Red Sea and Bab-el-Mandeb (France Diplomatie 2026-11-05) [city file].
- Roads: outside the capital, France Diplomatie advises against night driving and recommends a 4x4 with spare tyres. For the north it recommends two-vehicle convoys with a guide [city file].
- Phone and internet: the network is "often faulty" in the regions, and internet is expensive and unstable (France Diplomatie 2026-09-15) [city file]. About 350 km of fibre is being laid towards the north; the towns are not named (ADI 2026-11-12) [city file].
- Health and schools: there are no private clinics or international schools in regional towns, so serious care means going to the capital (Expat.com 2025-11-04) [city file]. The town does have a public hospital (Hôpital de Tadjourah on OSM [16]). Dawan Africa reports a new 50-bed regional hospital, built with China, with internal medicine, intensive care and mother-and-child care. That report is undated, and its page did not load, so this rests on a search excerpt only [23]. There is also a free dialysis centre run by a foundation (Présidence, 2025-08-21) [22].
- Alcohol: public sale is banned in the inland regions outside hotels and gîtes (France Diplomatie) [city file]. The Gadileh Resort is described as alcohol-free (traveller note on Petit Futé, search excerpt only) [9].
- Getting there:
  - Ferry: from Djibouti on Tuesday at 08:00, Thursday at 10:30, Friday at 08:30 and Saturday at 08:00; 700 FDJ (children and students 350); a car costs 5,000–6,000 FDJ and a 4x4 7,000 FDJ. The ferry waits at the quay for about an hour, then returns. It is often suspended from mid-July to late August and does not run on public holidays (national tourism agency, ANT) [3].
  - Minibus: 1,500 FDJ, from behind the UNFD building in Djibouti [3][4].
  - Road and air: about 130 km by road; a flight takes about 30 minutes [5]. Domestic flights cost 60–150 USD [city file].

## Neighbourhoods

### Old town and seafront: Galato, Saharito, Gablassan, Marsaki (centre)

- Feel: "the White City", with whitewashed coral-stone houses and seven mosques. The historic Togori eela well is in the centre (Wikipedia) [city file][6]. The town was the seat of the Afar sultanate of Ad-Ali until the last sultan died in 2019 [city file]. The dossier calls it quiet, conservative and mostly Afar [dossier]. OSM places the Galato and Saharito quarters on the seafront, Gablassan just inland, and Marsaki to the north-east [16]. The administrative core sits around the Rond-Point de l'Indépendance: the Préfecture, the regional council, the police and the hospital [16]. In 2015 the seafront corniche was rebuilt with antique-style street lamps, palm trees, a long stone wall and paving, and shops were built further along the waterfront (ADDS 2015-10-19) [19].
- Rent: none found. Expat.com says regional towns lack housing to expatriate standards (2025-11-04) [city file].
- Getting around: a small town. The quarters lie within about 1 km of the central roundabout (my calculation from OSM [16]). No taxi or tuk-tuk fare was found. Wikivoyage lists taxis only for the capital, Ali-Sabieh, Dikhil, Dorale and Arta [7].
- Watch: no area-specific source. The shared caveats apply. Government buildings and police stations are mapped here [16], so the capital's photography rule applies [capital file].

### Ferry quay and new port

- Feel: the historic port of the Ethiopian trade, redeveloped in 2000. A new port of about 90 M USD handles livestock, sesame, frankincense, fertiliser and grain; it was first planned for Ethiopian potash (Wikipedia) [5][city file]. It opened on 15 June 2017 with a potash system rated at 2,000 t/hour and a 190 m ro-ro berth (Baird Maritime 2017-06-17) [24]. The ferry terminal is in town, about 450 m south-west of the central roundabout. The new port is about 2 km to the west, past the hotel strip (my calculation from OSM [16]). Most people in the region work in marine trade and fishing [city file].
- Rent: none found.
- Getting around: the ferry quay links the town to Djibouti on the days above [3]. Tadjoura Airport (IATA code TDJ) is about 3.5 km east of the centre, towards Sables Blancs (OSM [16]; Wikipedia [5]).
- Watch: photographing ports is banned and can lead to arrest (FCDO, France Diplomatie) [capital file]. Ferry service stops in rough summer seas [3].
- Places:
  - Ferry terminal, Djibouti–Tadjourah ferry (passenger and vehicle ferry, on the western edge of the old town) — ANT [3]; OpenStreetMap [16]
  - Port of Tadjourah (new cargo port, opened 2017-06-15) — Wikipedia [5]; Baird Maritime [24]
  - Rond-Point du Nouveau Port de Tadjoura (roundabout at the port entrance, about 2 km west) — OpenStreetMap [16]
  - Centrale Thermique de Tadjourah (the town's thermal power station, run by Électricité de Djibouti, by the port road) — OpenStreetMap [16]
  - Sémaphore (a six-storey signal building on the shore near the port) — OpenStreetMap [16]
  - Aérodrome de Tadjoura (airstrip, east of town) — Wikipedia [5]; OpenStreetMap [16]

### Western quarters and hotel strip: Harat, Flaa, Élamo

- Feel: the quarters west of the ferry terminal, where the coast road (RN-9) heads towards the new port. OSM places Harat about 0.5 km, Flaa about 0.8 km and Élamo about 0.9 km west to north-west of the centre [16]. The two long-standing hotels sit on the shore about 1.3–1.6 km west of the centre (my calculation from OSM [16]). This is the town's fuel and services edge, with a fuel station, the public-works depot, an adult vocational centre and a barracks [16].

### Sables Blancs coast (about 4 km from town)

- Feel: the town's beach strip. "Plage des Sables blancs" (Wikipédia) [6] and a hotel and campement about 4 km from Tadjourah (ANT) [11][8]. OSM places the beach about 4.3 km east of the central roundabout and maps a row of eight simple shade huts on it [17]. A second, unnamed sand beach lies about 2.4 km east of the centre, on the way [17]. Whale sharks visit the Gulf of Tadjourah from November to January (Expat.com) [city file].

- Getting around: 4 km from town [11]. An unpaved track, the "Piste des Sables Blancs", leads there [16]. The airstrip is about 1 km inland of the beach [16]. No transport option was found apart from your own car or a hotel transfer.

## Living: typical weeks

**Realism note.** Without rents, a located market or a supermarket, these weeks are sketches. The hospital, a primary school, the mosques and two ATMs are now mapped [16][17]. Living here as a newcomer means planning for regular trips to the capital (2.5–4 hours by ferry, on four days a week [3][4]) for health care, schooling beyond public school and imported goods [city file]. Food and utility prices come from Djibouti-city sources (Africarrières 2026-02-25) and may not apply to the town; that is my assumption. Weekly figures are monthly ones divided by 4.33.

### 1. Q1 "M'installer pour de bon" + Q5 "Télétravail" + Q4 "Seul·e"

- Base: the old town, close to the hotels [8] and the ferry [3]. Remote work is risky: the regional network is "often faulty" (France Diplomatie) [city file]. A fixed line is cheap, at 550 FDJ/month (installation 3,300 FDJ, Djibouti Telecom) [city file], but no regional data-plan price or speed was found.
- Monday–Thursday: work from home. Lunch at La Brise de Mer on the seafront [16] or at Le Golfe's restaurant [8][16]. Bread from the seafront bakery [17]. Evening walk along the 2015 corniche to the Togori eela well [19][6].
- Friday: weekend (inferred from the capital's office hours [capital file]). For Muslim readers, Friday prayer at the Grande Mosquée de Tadjourah or Mosquée Cheikh Isaac [16].
- Errands: cash from the Salaam Bank or BCIMR ATM on the seafront [17]; SIM and line questions at the Djibouti Télécom office [16].
- Saturday: Sables Blancs [6][11].
- Sunday: work.
- Saturday night:
  - "Danser jusqu'au matin": not realistic in Tadjourah. No bar or nightlife source exists, and alcohol is restricted [city file]. The nearest option is the capital's clubs [capital file], and there is no night ferry.
  - "Un bon dîner entre amis": seafood at La Brise de Mer [16], or the hotel restaurants (Le Golfe, Corto Maltese, Sable Blanc) [8][16].
  - "Tranquille chez moi": the town's default.
- Weekend outings (Q8):
  - "À la plage": Sables Blancs [6].
  - "En montagne, en forêt": the Day Forest and the Goda mountains. Day Forest is the wettest part of the country, with junipers above 950 m [city file][12]. Campements at Bankoualé, Dittilou (Monts Goda) and Forêt du Day [11]. Guided walks run from one hour to a full day [13]. Check the checkpoint rule before going north [city file].
  - "Dans la savane ou le désert": Lac Assal, which is in the Tadjourah Region [14]; distance from town not found. Sleep at the Ghoubbet campements (Dankalelo, Le Campement du Rift Afar) [11]. You need a 4x4 and no night driving [city file].
  - "Je reste en ville": the old town, the Rimbaud house [6] and the corniche with the Vieux Boutre wreck [19][16].
- Rough weekly spend (one person, rent unknown):
  - food 300–500 USD ÷ 4.33 = 12,313–20,522 DJF [capital file: Africarrières]
  - fixed line 550 FDJ ÷ 4.33 = 127 DJF [city file]
  - mobile plan 10–25 USD ÷ 4.33 = 410–1,026 DJF [capital file: Africarrières]
  - one round trip to the capital: ferry 2 × 700 = 1,400 DJF, or minibus 2 × 1,500 = 3,000 DJF [3]
  - **Total ≈ 14,250–24,675 DJF/week (≈ €70–120) without rent and electricity**, neither of which could be sourced for the town.

## Visiting: discovery

Q1 "D'abord des vacances". Go from November to April. Daily highs are lowest in January (29.0 °C) and peak in July (41.7 °C) [city file]. The ferry often stops from mid-July to late August [3]. Whale sharks visit the gulf from November to January [city file].

### One day

A day trip from the capital is not practical by ferry, because it turns round after about an hour at the quay [3]. Plan at least one night.

- Morning: take the ferry from Djibouti (Tuesday or Saturday at 08:00, Friday at 08:30, Thursday at 10:30), arriving after 2.5–4 hours [3][4]. Or drive the 130 km road [5].
- Midday: check in and have lunch at Hôtel Le Golfe or Hôtel Corto Maltese, both on the shore west of the centre [8][16].
- Afternoon: walk east along the corniche [19] past the ferry terminal into the old town, with the whitewashed houses, the Togori eela well and the house said to be Rimbaud's [6][city file]. On the seafront, the Vieux Boutre wreck and Mosquée Cheikh Isaac [16]. Then inland to the Grande Mosquée [16]. See the mosques from the outside. Do not photograph the port or government buildings [capital file]. No entry fees were found.
- Evening: Sables Blancs, about 4 km east, at sunset [6][11][17]. Then dinner back on the seafront at La Brise de Mer (seafood) [16], at the Sable Blanc holiday village [8] or at the Gadileh Resort [10][25].

### One week

- Day 1: arrival, and the old-town day above.
- Day 2: Sables Blancs beach and swimming [6]. In November–January, ask locally about whale-shark boats. Tour packages run them from Arta Plage on the capital's side [capital file].
- Day 3: up to the Goda mountains. Overnight at the Bankoualé or Dittilou campement [11], with guided walks to canyons and waterfalls (the Bankoualé waterfall is named by Wikipédia) [13][6].
- Day 4: Day Forest National Park, the juniper stand above 950 m [12]; overnight at the Forêt du Day or Bare-Bare campement [11]. You need a 4x4 and must avoid night driving [city file].
- Day 5: Lac Assal and the Ghoubbet. Float on the salt lake and walk with salt caravans (ANT) [14]. Overnight at Le Campement du Rift Afar or Dankalelo [11].
- Day 6: back to Tadjourah. Daily-life moment: the port and the ferry quay when the ferry arrives from the capital, in the hour it spends at the quay [3]. On Friday, the town around the Grande Mosquée at prayer time, from a respectful distance [16][city file]. In the evening, the football pitch and multisport court east of the centre [16].
- Day 7: return to the capital by minibus (1,500 FDJ) or the next ferry day [3].
- Not proposed: Godoria mangroves and the Sept Frères. Godoria lies on the checkpoint axis, and the Sept Frères (Sawabi) are red except on authorised agency trips (France Diplomatie) [city file].
