# Tadjourah, Djibouti

> Curated 2026-11-14. Raw items for Là-bas — not published text. Testimonies are anecdotes, not data.

> Conversion note: EUR figures use the dossier's rate of 1 EUR ≈ 205 DJF (unchecked estimate from the 177.721 DJF/USD
> peg), so 1 USD ≈ €0.87. Very little Tadjourah-specific material exists online; items marked "national" apply to the
> whole country and are included only where they shape daily life in a regional town.

## Items

- [cost.rent] No rent, sale price or listing for Tadjourah was found; Expat.com says the regional towns lack "logements adaptés aux standards expatriés" and that the few who move there face precarious lodging. — Expat.com Djibouti guide, 2025-11-04, company, confidence: medium
- [cost.living] Djibouti Telecom's monthly prepaid fixed-line plan costs 550 FDJ in the regions (≈ €2.70, with 60 minutes) against 1,595 FDJ in the capital; line installation is 3,300 FDJ in the regions (≈ €16) against 11,000 FDJ in Djibouti-ville. — Djibouti Telecom website, accessed 2026-11-14, company, confidence: high
- [cost.negotiation] Dhow crossings across the gulf cost about 500 FDJ (≈ €2.45) and the price is negotiable and weather-dependent. — Cyril Jarnias blog "Les transports en commun à Djibouti", 2026-03-08, blog, confidence: low
- [housing.areas] Tadjourah, "the White City", is known for whitewashed coral-stone houses and seven mosques; the historic Togori eela well sits in the centre. — Wikipedia "Tadjourah Region" / Wikipédia "Tadjoura (Djibouti)", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: medium
- [work.jobs] A new port of about 90 M USD was built for general cargo (livestock, sesame, frankincense, fertiliser, grain), first planned to ship Ethiopian potash; no traffic or jobs figures were found. — Wikipedia "Tadjoura", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: medium
- [work.jobs] Most people in the region work in marine trade and fishing; salt from Lake Assal is exported. — Wikipedia "Tadjourah Region", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: low
- [business.startup] National: an extraordinary 1.8 billion FDJ (≈ €8.8 M) youth-entrepreneurship envelope lists fisheries, tourism and renewables among priority sectors — relevant to a fishing and tourism town, though no regional quota is stated. — ADI, 2026-10-27, local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.power] National: rural electrification was 1 % against 54 % urban in 2020; no figure was found for Tadjourah town. — Wikipedia "Economy of Djibouti", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: low
- [daily.internet] France Diplomatie says the phone network is "often faulty" in the regions and internet is expensive and unstable. — France Diplomatie "Informations utiles", 2026-09-15, official, confidence: high
- [daily.internet] About 350 km of fibre is being deployed towards the north of the country under the regional EARDIP project; towns are not named. — ADI, 2026-11-12, local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.water] French Wikipedia says water is relatively abundant in Tadjourah, linking the town's name to it; no utility data was found. — Wikipédia "Tadjoura (Djibouti)", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: low
- [daily.transport] Ferry Djibouti → Tadjourah: about 700 FDJ (≈ €3.40), 3–4 hours; minibus about 1,500 FDJ (≈ €7.30), leaving when full. — Cyril Jarnias blog, 2026-03-08, blog, confidence: low
- [daily.transport] Daily ferries leave L'Escale in Djibouti for Tadjourah and Obock (about three hours), and a new highway links Djibouti to Tadjourah. — Wikivoyage "Djibouti", accessed 2026-11-14, blog, confidence: low
- [daily.transport] Wikipedia gives the ferry at about 2.5 hours, the road at about 130 km, the RN-11 to Balho, and a 30-minute flight from the capital to the airstrip. — Wikipedia "Tadjoura", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Domestic flights to Obock or Tadjourah cost 60–150 USD (≈ €52–130). — Africarrières, 2026-02-25, company, confidence: low
- [daily.transport] Outside the capital, France Diplomatie advises against night driving, recommends a 4x4 with spare tyres, and convoys of two vehicles plus a guide for the north. — France Diplomatie "Informations utiles", 2026-09-15, official, confidence: high
- [health.care] Regional towns have no private clinics or international schools, per Expat.com, so serious care means travelling to the capital. — Expat.com Djibouti guide, 2025-11-04, company, confidence: medium
- [health.care] National: malaria, dengue and chikungunya circulate all year; insurance covering repatriation is essential. — France Diplomatie "Santé", 2026-09-15, official, confidence: high
- [family.schools] No international school in regional towns; public schooling follows the French model. — Expat.com Djibouti guide, 2025-11-04, company, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] Tadjourah lies in France Diplomatie's yellow "enhanced vigilance" zone; north of the Balho–Adaïlou–Ouaddhi–Godoria axis, army checkpoints may control access. — France Diplomatie "Sécurité", 2026-09-15, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] The Eritrean border to the north is red for France and the FCDO advises against all travel to that border area; Oued Kalou and Mont Moussa Ali are closed. — France Diplomatie 2026-09-15 / UK FCDO 2026-07-27, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Boaters are told to postpone trips in the southern Red Sea and Bab-el-Mandeb indefinitely (military activity, rising Somali piracy). — France Diplomatie "Dernières minutes", 2026-11-05, official, confidence: high
- [social.nightlife] France Diplomatie says public sale of alcohol is banned in the inland regions (outside hotels and gîtes); no bar or nightlife source for Tadjourah was found. — France Diplomatie "Informations utiles", 2026-09-15, official, confidence: medium
- [social.culture] Tadjourah was the seat of the Afar sultanate of Ad-Ali from the 16th century until the last sultan's death in 2019; Rimbaud stayed there in 1886 and a house said to be his can be visited. — Wikipédia "Tadjoura (Djibouti)" / Wikipedia "Tadjoura", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: medium
- [social.religion] Seven mosques in a town of 18,808 residents (2024 census); the region had 60,645 residents in 2024. — Wikipedia "Tadjourah Region" / "Tadjoura", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: medium
- [community.expat] A diplomatic family in the capital names Gadileh Resort in Tadjourah (pools, water slides) as a family weekend outing. — Talesmag post report, 2025-08-11, blog, confidence: low
- [climate.daily] Mean daily maximum peaks at 41.7 °C in July and is lowest at 29.0 °C in January; about 186 mm of rain a year, heaviest in November (35 mm), almost none in June–July. — Wikipedia "Tadjoura" (climate table), accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: medium
- [climate.daily] The western highlands of the region are about 11 °C cooler, with night lows around 10–15 °C. — Wikipedia "Tadjourah Region", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: low
- [nature.access] Day Forest National Park (est. 1939), the wettest part of the country at about 500 mm/year, with junipers above 950 m; it has lost 88 % of its area over two centuries. — Wikipedia "Day Forest National Park", accessed 2026-11-14, academic (encyclopaedia), confidence: medium
- [nature.access] Sables Blancs beach is near town; the Gulf of Tadjourah hosts whale sharks from November to January and kite/windsurfing. — Wikipédia "Tadjoura (Djibouti)" / Expat.com 2025-11-04, academic/company, confidence: medium
- [rights.lgbt] National: same-sex activity "not illegal" per the FCDO but public affection can draw police attention under "good morals" laws; other sources call it de facto illegal. — UK FCDO 2026-07-27 / Wikipedia "LGBTQ rights in Djibouti" citing Outright International 2026-03-12, official, confidence: medium
