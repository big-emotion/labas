# Bubaque, Guinea-Bissau

> Curated 2026-11-14. Raw items for Là-bas — not published text. Testimonies are anecdotes, not data.

Currency note: EUR equivalents use the fixed peg stated in the dossier, **1 EUR = 655.957 XOF**. Research limitation: the shared web-search budget was nearly exhausted, so items come mostly from pages fetched directly. Almost all price data comes from one travel-company article (Scoot West Africa, first published 2021, updated to Feb 2024) and is tourist-facing; no long-term rent figure exists for the island.

## Items

- [cost.living] Lodge les Dauphins: 47,000 XOF/night single with full board (≈ €71.65), 2022–2023 prices. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [cost.living] Cajou Lodge: 120,000 XOF/night with full board (≈ €182.94), 25 % off Sunday–Thursday. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [cost.living] Mango Lodge: 20,000 XOF per room with breakfast (≈ €30.49), 20 % off Sunday–Thursday; Saldo Mar rooms from 20,000 XOF. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [cost.living] At Restaurant Nuna a whole fish with rice and salad cost 1,500 XOF (≈ €2.29) and a large Super Bock beer 750 XOF (≈ €1.14). — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [cost.living] Bike hire on Bubaque costs about 5,000 XOF/day (≈ €7.62); bikes can be in poor condition. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [cost.negotiation] Travellers can tag along on a hotel's private boat transfer for about 30,000–35,000 XOF (≈ €45.73–53.36). No source on negotiating rents or market prices on the island. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [housing.search] No long-term rental listing found (a search returned only short-stay platform pages). Most of the nicer accommodation is run by Europeans, a blogger notes. — Trailing Ivy (Paula Dear), 2025-02-23, blog, confidence: low
- [work.jobs] Most families live from subsistence farming and fishing; tourism is limited, mostly boat charters from Senegal. — Wikipedia "Bissagos Islands", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Local canoes (canoas/pirogues) from Bissau cost about 3,500 XOF one way (≈ €5.34) and take about 3.5 hours on a good day; Fridays are the most crowded. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Wikivoyage gives about 3,000 XOF (≈ €4.57) for the pirogue and calls it overcrowded and sometimes unpleasant. — Wikivoyage "Bissagos Islands", accessed 2026-11-14, blog, confidence: low
- [daily.transport] A blogger describes a 4–5-hour pirogue crossing from Bissau, loaded with cargo and needing regular bailing; life jackets were provided. — Trailing Ivy (Paula Dear), 2025-02-23, blog, confidence: low
- [daily.transport] The Consulmar ferry costs 16,500 XOF one way for foreigners (≈ €25.15), about 4 hours; it runs once a week in each direction. — Scoot West Africa (2024-02) and Wikivoyage "Bissagos Islands" (accessed 2026-11-14), company, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Fast boats run when demand allows in the dry season at 30,000 XOF each way (≈ €45.73), 90–150 minutes; boats do not run in rough seas. — Wikivoyage "Bissagos Islands" and Scoot West Africa, accessed 2026-11-14, blog, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Air Bijagos (the only airline as of 2024) advertises flights on most days for about 90,000 XOF each way (≈ €137) onto a dirt runway; in Nov 2026 its site said online booking was suspended but flights continue (booking via WhatsApp). — Wikivoyage "Bissagos Islands" and airbijagos.com, accessed 2026-11-14, company, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] On the island, people travel by motorbike or on foot; there is one basic road and few vehicles, and no car ferry. — Wikipedia "Bubaque" and Trailing Ivy (2025-02-23), academic, confidence: medium
- [daily.transport] Between islands, arranging transport can take up to 30 days (Uno–Orango example), though the hop is seven minutes by fast boat. — Lusa via AMAN Alliance, 2025-07-07, intl-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.power] A blogger says there is no mains electricity on the islands; villagers use small solar lamps and panels. — Trailing Ivy (Paula Dear), 2025-02-23, blog, confidence: low
- [daily.power] Lodge les Dauphins runs 24/7 on solar panels and two generators; Cajou Lodge powers its air-conditioning by generator in the evenings. — Scoot West Africa (2024-02) and Wikivoyage, company, confidence: low
- [daily.power] Bubaque's health centre runs on solar panels that cannot guarantee 24-hour power; staff switch devices and the water pump off during shortfalls. — O Democrata GB, 2025-07-05, local-media, confidence: high
- [daily.internet] MTN and Orange both work on the islands; the author found Orange fine for internet and calls on Bubaque, while locals consider MTN more reliable. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [daily.water] Villages use a communal well and carry water by hand; the Bubaque health centre reports drinking-water and sanitation problems, especially in the rainy season. — Trailing Ivy (2025-02-23) and O Democrata GB (2025-07-05), local-media, confidence: medium
- [daily.money] No ATM or card information found for Bubaque; on remote Unhocomo island a Malian trader barters food for fish because of a cash shortage. — O Democrata GB, 2026-04-29, local-media, confidence: low
- [health.care] Bubaque's health centre has five doctors who also cover nearby islands; X-ray was not working; malaria, malnutrition and hypertension are common cases. — O Democrata GB, 2025-07-05, local-media, confidence: high
- [health.care] In the 11-area Bijagós health region only Bubaque has doctors; most areas lack midwives, pharmacies, labs, X-ray and ultrasound, and several have no boat for emergency evacuations. — O Democrata GB, 2026-05-10, local-media, confidence: high
- [health.care] Canada states there are no clinics or trained medical staff on the islands, which conflicts with local reports of a staffed centre on Bubaque. — Government of Canada, 2026-10-13, official, confidence: medium
- [health.care] A Chinese medical team held free consultations on Bubaque in April 2026. — O Democrata GB, 2026-04-27, local-media, confidence: medium
- [family.schools] Bubaque has a high school; bilingual Kriol–Portuguese schooling in the archipelago ended in 2022. — Wikipedia "Bissagos Islands", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] Canada advises reaching the islands by chartered flight or tour-operator boat and avoiding pirogues, which it calls unsafe; emergency consular help comes from Dakar. — Government of Canada, 2026-10-13, official, confidence: high
- [safety.general] Criminal networks use isolated Bijagós islands for clandestine migration to Spain because of a "total absence" of security forces; a Bubaque-sector fisherman says he was approached to pilot a boat. — O Democrata GB, 2025-04-06, local-media, confidence: medium
- [safety.general] Several Bijagós islands have served as narcotrafficking transit depots since the early 2000s. — Wikipedia "Bissagos Islands" (undated claim), accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: low
- [safety.general] Watch for Nile crocodiles and saltwater hippos in the marshes. — Wikivoyage "Bissagos Islands", accessed 2026-11-14, blog, confidence: low
- [social.food] Commerce is concentrated around the port; the largest supermarket is Super Mercado Agoueinitt, ~100 m up the main road; shops stock only what locals buy, and restaurants generally need booking. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [social.food] Much food comes from the mainland; a blogger lived on basics (biscuits, bread, tinned sardines) from the market and a small supermarket. — Trailing Ivy (Paula Dear), 2025-02-23, blog, confidence: low
- [social.culture] Bijagó society is often called matriarchal, but other authors say it is fundamentally patriarchal with strong female roles; contested. — Wikipedia "Bissagos Islands", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [social.culture] Languages: Kriol in towns, Bijagó in the countryside; Portuguese is not often used, even in schools (c. 2024). — Wikipedia "Bissagos Islands", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [social.culture] Visitors are told to greet people, ask permission before leaving usual paths and respect restricted (sacred) areas. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: medium
- [social.culture] The island has a museum and hosts the headquarters of the UNESCO biosphere reserve. — Wikipedia "Bubaque", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [social.pace] Town population 4,299 and island 6,427 at the 2009 census. — Wikipedia "Bubaque", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: medium
- [social.pace] The French Wikipedia gives 9,244 inhabitants for the island, with no reference year. — Wikipédia (fr) "Bubaque", last modified 2025-03-16, academic, confidence: low
- [climate.daily] The author advises avoiding the rainy season, when many hotels close. — Scoot West Africa, updated 2024-02, company, confidence: low
- [nature.access] The Bijagós archipelago was inscribed as a UNESCO natural World Heritage Site on 13 July 2025; it has been a Biosphere Reserve since 1996 and a Ramsar site since 2014. — O Democrata GB (2025-07-13) and Wikipedia "Bissagos Islands", local-media, confidence: high
- [nature.access] Bubaque has many beaches largely untouched by mass tourism, e.g. Bruce beach (about 35 minutes' drive from the centre). — Wikivoyage "Bissagos Islands" and Wikipedia "Bubaque", accessed 2026-11-14, blog, confidence: medium
- [nature.access] The island covers 48 km², about 18 km² of it marsh flooded at high tide (English Wikipedia gives about 75 km²). — Wikipédia (pt) and Wikipedia (en) "Bubaque", accessed 2026-11-14, academic, confidence: low
