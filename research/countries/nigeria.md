# Nigeria

> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

Exchange rate used throughout: **1 EUR ≈ 1,500 NGN** (mid-market, 7 Nov 2026 [S5]; the naira crossed below ₦1,500/€ in early October 2026 [S6]). Bank/transfer rates are worse. Nigerian landlords usually demand **1–2 years of rent upfront**, plus agency, legal and caution fees that add roughly 25–30 % [S9][S12]. Plan cash flow around that, not around a monthly figure. Legally, the Lagos Tenancy Law 2011 caps advance rent at one year for a yearly tenancy (six months for a monthly one), but it **does not apply in Apapa, Ikeja GRA, Ikoyi or Victoria Island**, and a 2025 bill to extend it statewide was not confirmed as enacted [S47]; in Abuja, residents report landlords demanding up to two years although tenancy law allows 12 months (Oct 2025) [S12].

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: English (official, business, admin), Nigerian Pidgin (street lingua franca, especially Lagos), Yoruba (Lagos, Ibadan), Hausa (Abuja's markets, the north), Igbo (Enugu, the south-east). French is marginal: the Franco-Nigerian Chamber runs a yearly French-language competition precisely because French is little used in an English-speaking country [S30]. A French-only speaker will struggle more here than anywhere in francophone Africa.
- **Currency**: Naira (NGN). Very volatile: it lost a lot of value in 2023–2024 and has firmed in 2026 (~₦1,500/€) [S5][S6]. Inflation eased to 16.05 % by October 2025 from 33.88 % in late 2024 [S8].
- **Payments**: largely cashless. Fintech wallets dominate: OPay (≈39 M monthly transacting users in 2025), PalmPay (≈40 M users, June 2026), Moniepoint (millions of businesses) [S27]. Bank transfers and POS are the default way to pay almost everything.
- **(a) French / EU passport holders**: a visa is required. Since **1 May 2025**, Nigeria moved short visits to an **e-Visa** (13 categories; replaces visa on arrival; e-Visas cannot be extended in-country) [S14][S15]. To live and work: employer sponsorship under an **Expatriate Quota** (portal-only since 1 May 2025, stricter reviews focused on skills transfer), then a **residence permit (CERPAC)**, now applied for online (an e-CERPAC launch date of 1 August 2025 rests on a single secondary source — unverified; 1 Aug 2025 is confirmed as the end of the overstay amnesty) [S14][S15]. FCDO confirms visa on arrival is no longer available and that CERPAC holders need no visa (page current 14 Nov 2026) [S1b]. Self-employed / remote-worker routes: not found (no digital-nomad visa identified). An Expatriate Employment Levy was announced and then suspended in March 2025 [S11]. International travellers must complete an online Nigeria Health Declaration Form before travel, as part of Ebola screening; FCDO gives no start date (the earlier "13 July 2026" date could not be confirmed) [S1b].
- **(b) Diaspora with Nigerian roots**: anyone **born abroad with at least one Nigerian parent is a Nigerian citizen by birth** (Constitution s.25(1)(c)) [S16]. Citizens by birth may hold another nationality; the dual-nationality restriction in s.28 only bites on citizens by registration/naturalisation [S17]. So a French person with a Nigerian mother or father can claim a Nigerian passport. **Grandchildren only**: no automatic route (s.25 refers to parents, not grandparents) [S16]; no right-of-return / DNA / Afro-descendant scheme comparable to Ghana's or Sierra Leone's was found. A foreign **wife** of a Nigerian can register as a citizen (s.26); a male-spouse equivalent was not found [S17]. NiDCOM (Nigerians in Diaspora Commission) runs a diaspora registry and engagement programmes, but no concrete returnee package (tax, customs, housing) was found as of 2025 [S18][S19]. Lagos and Ogun were reported to be *considering* returnee estates/startup villages — unconfirmed [S19].
- **Overall security picture (Nov 2026)**: UK FCDO (updated 10 Nov 2026) advises against **all travel** to Borno, Yobe, Adamawa, Gombe, Katsina, Zamfara, Sokoto and riverine Niger Delta areas, and against **all but essential travel** to Bauchi, Kaduna, Kano, Kebbi, Jigawa, Niger, Kogi, Plateau, Taraba, Kwara, Benue, Abia, Anambra, Imo and non-riverine Rivers/Delta/Bayelsa. **Abuja is ambiguous**: the FCT, "including Abuja", appears in the heading of that section, but the bullet list beneath it names only six North Central states — check the FCDO map and your insurer before relying on either reading [S1]. **Lagos, Ogun, Oyo, Osun, Ondo, Ekiti, Edo, Enugu, Ebonyi, Cross River (non-riverine), Akwa Ibom (non-riverine)** are not on those lists [S1]. France Diplomatie (updated 10 Nov 2026) says the terrorist threat is high and "tend à s'étendre plus au sud", citing the abduction of 39 pupils and their teachers in Oyo State in May 2026 (alert published 18 Mar 2026, updated 3 Aug 2026) [S3].
- **Health alert**: an **Ebola (Bundibugyo strain) case was confirmed in Abuja on 20 Oct 2026** in a traveller from Uganda; 44 contacts traced; France Diplomatie (10 Nov 2026) reports no widespread community transmission [S3][S4].

## Cities

### Lagos

- **Cost of living**: The most expensive city in the country for housing. Average 2025 one-bedroom rents (Lagos Residential Market Report 2025, Edala Development): **Ikoyi ₦8 M/yr (~€445/month)**, **Victoria Island ₦7.5 M**, **Lekki Phase I ₦4.5 M (~€250/month)**, Ikeja ₦3.2 M, **Yaba ₦2.2 M (~€120/month)**, Ajah and Gbagada ₦2 M, Surulere ₦1.5 M; Eko Atlantic tops at ₦20.9 M [S8]. A 2026 consumer guide gives lower Island ranges (₦1.5–4 M for a 1-bed) — treat as optimistic [S9]. Meals: neighbourhood *buka* ₦1,500–3,000 (~€1–2), mid-range restaurant ₦8,000–20,000 per person (~€5–13). Single professional in central areas: ₦500,000–800,000/month (~€330–530) excluding big-ticket Island rent; daily ride-hailing alone can exceed ₦300,000/month [S9]. Expats mostly live on the Island (Ikoyi, VI, Lekki, Banana Island) near offices and international schools [S11][S10].
- **Work & money**: Nigeria's commercial and financial capital: banking, telecoms, oil & gas HQs, FMCG, media, Nollywood and the music industry. Multinationals cited: Nestlé, Chevron, KPMG [S11]. Remote work is feasible if you secure power (inverter/solar) and dual internet. Salaries: not found from a reliable source.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Africa's busiest startup market by deal count in 2025, though Nigerian funding fell 17 % to $343 M in 2025 (Africa: The Big Deal) [S20]; it rebounded to $635 M in January–September 2026 (77 startups raising ≥$100k, but ~69 % of it went to four companies: Moove, Flutterwave, Jumia, Yellow Card) [S46]. Dealroom's 2025 index called Lagos the world's fastest-growing tech ecosystem (value ×11.6 since 2017, five unicorns), while StartupBlink ranked it only 76th in 2025 [S22]. Hubs: **CcHub (Yaba, since 2011; added private startup offices in June 2026)** [S21][S22], **Leadspace (Yaba)**, **Venia Hub** [S22]. Lagos State announced a ₦31 bn innovation fund (Aug 2025) and is domesticating the Nigeria Startup Act [S22]. Fintech home of Paystack, Flutterwave, Moniepoint, OPay. **Crypto**: Nigeria ranked **6th worldwide** in Chainalysis's 2025 Global Crypto Adoption Index (down from 2nd in 2024), ~$92 bn on-chain value received, nearly half of sub-Saharan Africa's total [S23] (a "2nd at grassroots level" claim circulates but is unverified); the **Investments and Securities Act 2025** (signed March 2025) classifies digital assets as securities under SEC oversight [S24].
- **Daily infrastructure**: Grid power is rationed by "bands": **Band A** feeders promise ≥20 h/day at ~₦209–225/kWh, but Ikeja Electric repeatedly apologised for missing the promise [S25]. Generators remain common; petrol is ~₦1,275–1,370/litre in 2026 [S25]. Budget ₦30,000–70,000/month for power and ₦30,000–50,000 for fibre or Starlink [S9]. An expat who lived there 2023–2026 says home internet worsened, with frequent outages [S10]. Traffic is notorious (FCDO cites long delays [S2]). **Ride-hailing: Uber stopped operating in Nigeria on 2 Sept 2026**; Bolt, inDrive, LagRide, Rida, Shuttlers remain [S28]. The **Blue Line** rail carried ~3.5 M passengers in 2025 (~15,000/day); the Red Line runs Agbado–Oyingbo [S29]. Water: most homes rely on boreholes and tanks (not found from a dated source).
- **Health & schools**: Private hospitals used by expats: **Reddington (Victoria Island)**, **Evercare Lekki (165 beds)**, **Lagoon Hospitals**, Duchess International (Ikeja), St. Nicholas (Lagos Island); a specialist consult at Reddington/Evercare/Lagoon VI ≈ ₦25,000 (late-2025 benchmark) [S31]. An expat 2023–2026 rated medical care "limited" [S10]; international health insurance with evacuation is advised [S31]. **Lycée Français Louis Pasteur** (AEFE, ages 3–18): ~₦16 M/yr (~€8,850) for grades 7–10, ~€10,600 for 11–13 (May 2025) [S32].
- **Safety**: Not under FCDO or France travel restrictions [S1][S3], but FCDO notes high violent crime nationwide (muggings, armed robbery, kidnap), higher outside major cities and at night, and risk of robbery in taxis/public transport [S2]. Lagos police warned of a resurgence of **"one chance" robberies** (fake commuter vehicles) on Oworo, Alapere, Ogudu, Ketu axes (undated page, verify) [S34]. Periodic protests in Lagos [S2]. Island gated estates are where most expats live; the 2023–26 expat report says local transport is "not usable" for them and most hire a driver [S10].
- **Social life & vibe**: Africa's music capital (Afrobeats). **Detty December**: a month of concerts and parties as the diaspora flies home; the 2025 Detty December Fest ran 7–29 Dec at Ilubirin, with Wizkid, Davido, Olamide performing around the city [S35]. Cowrywise sized it as a ~$300 M December economy [S35]. Fast, loud, transactional, very social; religion (church and mosque) is visible but Lagos is the most cosmopolitan city. The 2023–26 expat calls Lagos best suited to singles [S10].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Large "returnee"/"japada" crowd in Lekki/VI; reverse migration is covered by local press as small but growing, with some stigma [S19]. **Franco-Nigerian Chamber of Commerce (FNCCI/CCIFN)** inside the French Consulate in Ikoyi — the largest bilateral chamber in Nigeria, 40th anniversary Nov 2025; a 2026 "French Week" was held [S30]. Consulate community ~300 people plus a larger expat scene [S10]. Expat.com Lagos forum [S11]. Facebook/WhatsApp groups: not verified without login.
- **Climate in practice**: Hot and humid all year; rainy season roughly late March to early December, peaks June–July and Sept–Oct [S36]. **Flooding** paralysed Lekki, Victoria Island, Ikoyi and the Mainland in August 2025 and again around 1 July 2026 [S36]. Check a flat's flood history before signing.
- **Watch out**: 1–2 years' rent upfront; flooding on the Island; generator noise and fumes; construction dust; traffic eating 2–4 h a day; a 2023–26 US-diplomatic expat reported "considerable prejudice", workplace bullying and poor air quality [S10]; Uber's exit leaves fewer ride options [S28].

### Abuja

- **Cost of living**: One-bedroom up to **₦3 M/yr (~€165/month)** in Maitama, Wuse, Jabi, Gwarinpa, Guzape, Asokoro (Oct 2025), excluding legal/agency/caution fees; Kubwa 1-bed ₦1–1.2 M; Apo Resettlement ₦1.5–2.5 M; Life Camp self-contained ₦1.2 M [S12]. 2025 prime 2-bed (Garki/Wuse) ~₦3.5 M [S13]. Listings in 2026: 1-bed Maitama ₦2.5 M, Wuse 2 ₦3 M; furnished units ₦4.5–5.5 M [S13b]. Cheaper than Lagos for housing; imported groceries expensive [S37].
- **Work & money**: Federal government, ministries, embassies, UN agencies, NGOs, consultancies. Strongest city for development-sector and diplomatic jobs. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Smaller than Lagos. **Abuja Start-up Hub** (NITDA + NSIA, ~$11.2 M from Japan's JICA, 2025–2030) [S26]. Ventures Platform originated in Abuja; CcHub has had a presence (older source, flag) [S26]. Company registration is national (CAC), same as Lagos.
- **Daily infrastructure**: Planned city, more orderly and less congested than Lagos; commutes typically 15–30 min outside rush hour; power still inconsistent, generators common [S37]. Abuja Light Rail with limited coverage [S37]. Ride-hailing: Bolt and others (Uber left Sept 2026) [S28]. Police warn against unpainted "kabu-kabu" taxis because of **"one chance" robberies**: 263 cases in the FCT in 2024; a lawyer was killed in January 2026 [S34].
- **Health & schools**: **Cedarcrest Hospitals** (founded Abuja 2008, Gudu flagship), **National Hospital Abuja** (public flagship) [S31]; Nizamiye Hospital not verified. **École Française Marcel Pagnol** (AEFE, Kaura District, preschool to lycée), fees ~€3,075–3,560 (undated, likely outdated) [S32]. Institut Français du Nigeria is in Abuja [S30].
- **Safety**: **FCDO (10 Nov 2026) places "the Federal Capital Territory, including Abuja" in the heading of its all-but-essential-travel section, though the FCT is not in the bullet list itself** — treat Abuja as restricted for insurance purposes until clarified [S1]; it notes kidnappings in Abuja's outer suburbs and terrorist attacks linked to the FCT that could target hotels, malls, bars [S2]. Local press: repeated kidnappings in **Bwari Area Council outskirts — Peze community, Byazhi area of Kubwa (at least 16 abducted, early March 2026)**, Kagini (family abducted, 3 Nov 2026); residents moving deeper into the city [S33]. Central districts (Maitama, Asokoro, Wuse II, Jabi) are where expats live, mostly in gated compounds [S37][S38]. Ebola case confirmed in Abuja 20 Oct 2026 [S4].
- **Social life & vibe**: Quieter, administrative, "stability, structure, predictability" versus Lagos's energy [S38]. Wuse II is the lively district for restaurants and nightlife; **Jabi Lake Mall**, **Millennium Park**, Bature Brewery, rooftop lounges in Wuse II/Maitama [S37]. More religiously mixed, with a stronger northern/Muslim presence than Lagos (editorial). Family-friendly central districts, Gwarinpa family-oriented [S37].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Embassy/NGO expat community; Expat.com Abuja forum (~98 members) [S37]; Institut Français. Diaspora returnee groups specific to Abuja: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: Savanna climate on a plateau: rainy April–October, dry season with dusty **harmattan** Dec–Feb, hot March–April (general knowledge, no dated source found).
- **Watch out**: Probably under FCDO "all but essential" advice (FCT named in the section heading; can affect insurance) [S1]; kidnapping on the periphery, so cheaper outer districts (Kubwa, Bwari, Kuje) carry a real trade-off [S33]; Ebola alert [S4]; landlords can demand up to two years upfront [S12].

### Ibadan (additional — cheap, close to Lagos, university city)

Why it matters: Nigeria's third-largest city, ~2 h from Lagos, home of the University of Ibadan and University College Hospital (UCH); much cheaper housing; Oyo State is not under FCDO restrictions [S1].
- **Cost of living**: 2-bed in an "emerging" area (Akala Way) ~₦800,000/yr in 2025 (~€45/month), forecast ₦1.2 M in 2026; Bodija and Jericho saw ~50 % rises [S13]. Single person budget ~$335/month excluding rent (aggregator, low confidence) [S39].
- **Work & tech**: Education, health, public sector, trade; a growing tech scene (promotional source only) [S39]. Remote work feasible.
- **Safety**: Not under FCDO restriction [S1], but France Diplomatie cites the **May 2026 abduction of 39 pupils and their teachers in Oyo State** as a sign the threat is spreading south [S3].
- **Watch out**: thin data; fewer international schools and private hospitals than Lagos (not verified in detail).

### Enugu (additional — calmer south-eastern capital, Igbo diaspora base)

Why it matters: capital of Enugu State, not under FCDO restrictions (unlike neighbouring Abia, Anambra, Imo) [S1]; home city of many Igbo diaspora families; described by a travel guide as one of Nigeria's calmer state capitals (no statistics) [S40].
- **Cost of living**: 2-bed in GRA ~₦1.5 M/yr in 2025 (~€85/month), forecast ₦1.9 M in 2026 [S13]. Cheaper than Lagos/Abuja [S40].
- **Watch out**: the **IPOB Monday "sit-at-home"** still disrupts business across the South-East; the governor has banned observance and claims ₦10 bn lost per Monday; a reported February 2026 cancellation was disputed [S41]. Thin data on tech, schools, hospitals.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of city conditions, not measurements. No question in the quiz measures a personality trait. Academic anchors: Hofstede scores Nigeria high on Power Distance (80) and Indulgence (84), low on Individualism (30) and Long-Term Orientation (13) [S42]; critics note national scores hide Nigeria's ethnic diversity [S42]. A large factor-analytic study of educated urban Nigerians exists (Ohaeri & Lewis, 2004) but no reliable comparison of Big Five profiles between Nigerian cities or ethnic groups was found [S43].

**Lagos**
- Openness: rewards people hungry for novelty — music, fashion, startups, nightlife running all December [S35][S22].
- Conscientiousness: you need to be highly organised to survive rent paid 1–2 years upfront, power bands, generators, and traffic [S8][S25].
- Extraversion: networking drives business; the social calendar is dense; best suited to singles according to one expat [S10].
- Agreeableness: hard-bargaining, transactional city; an expat reported prejudice and workplace bullying [S10].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): high requirement — noise, floods, crime vigilance, outages [S36][S2][S10].

**Abuja**
- Openness: fewer cultural scenes than Lagos; suits those content with malls, parks, lounges [S37].
- Conscientiousness: an orderly, planned city with shorter commutes rewards routine-lovers [S37][S38].
- Extraversion: quieter; social life clusters in Wuse II and diplomatic/NGO circles [S37].
- Agreeableness: hierarchical, government-centred environment, consistent with Nigeria's high Power Distance [S42].
- Emotional stability: daily life calmer than Lagos, but you must accept a security context (FCDO advice, periphery kidnappings) [S1][S33].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: Same-sex sexual activity is illegal, up to 14 years in prison federally; some northern states apply Sharia law, which can prescribe the death penalty; the Same Sex Marriage (Prohibition) Act also punishes attending perceived same-sex weddings; high risk of violence and blackmail (FCDO, page current 14 Nov 2026) [S2]. Mass arrests at alleged "gay" parties have occurred (76 arrested, 2023) [S44]. Afrobarometer Round 8 (Jan–Feb 2020): only **9 %** of Nigerians would welcome homosexual neighbours [S45]. A US diplomat (2023–26) described Lagos as unwelcoming for LGBT expats [S10].
- **Racism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners**: no systematic study found. A 2023–26 expat report mentions "considerable prejudice" and prevalent hate-group propaganda (single testimony) [S10]. Reverse-migrants report stigma attached to "japada" and reverse culture shock [S19].
- **Conflict zones**: jihadist insurgency and banditry in the North-East and North-West (Borno, Yobe, Adamawa, Gombe, Katsina, Zamfara, Sokoto — FCDO "against all travel"); separatist violence and Monday sit-at-homes in the South-East; militancy in riverine Niger Delta (FCDO 10 Nov 2026) [S1][S41]. France Diplomatie (alert dated 18 Mar 2026, updated 3 Aug 2026) says the threat is spreading south [S3].
- **Women's daily experience**: FCDO contains no women-specific advice [S2]. FIDA (women lawyers) demanded a state of emergency on "one chance" robberies in Abuja after a woman lawyer was killed in Jan 2026 [S34]. No reliable dated source on street harassment found — not found.

3. « Passer 2 à 3 heures par jour dans les bouchons, ça te paraît supportable ? » (Lagos vs Abuja/Ibadan)
4. « Ton budget logement, c'est plutôt moins de 100 € par mois ou plus de 300 € ? » (Ibadan/Enugu vs Lagos Island)

6. « Tu veux être à moins de deux heures d'une grande ville, mais payer beaucoup moins cher ? » (Ibadan)
