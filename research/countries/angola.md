## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: Portuguese is the working language of administration, business, schools and
  universities. Instruction at the main public university, UAN, is "mainly in Portuguese" [3]. Umbundu,
  Kimbundu, Kikongo and other national languages are widely spoken, but this pass found no fetched
  source with figures. **French is not a daily-life language.** It helps only in some Congolese and
  Bakongo circles in the north (unsourced, low confidence). English is used inside the oil sector.
  For a French speaker with no Portuguese, this is the main barrier.
- **Currency**: kwanza (AOA). On Numbeo's 2026-11-12 data, 570,553 AOA ≈ €563.8, which means about
  **1,000 AOA ≈ €1** [5]. Inflation was 28.2% in 2024 and 20.2% in 2025, with an IMF projection of
  12.9% for 2026 [12]. Landlords often want **US dollars and several months' rent up front** [3].
- **Entry and residence, French/EU passport holders**: visa-free stays of **up to 30 days per visit and at
  most 90 days per calendar year**. The list grew to 98 visa-exempt countries on 2023-09-28 [7]. The
  exemption covers tourism only. Work, study or business needs a visa obtained in advance, and working
  or living without the right visa brings fines and deportation [2b]. Work visas are typically 12 months,
  renewable up to twice, with 1–3 months of processing [3]. The France Diplomatie entry page could not
  be fetched (404) [1b], so check it before relying on this.
- **Diaspora with family roots**: dual nationality has not been banned since the 1991 revision of the
  Nationality Law [8]. A child born abroad to at least one Angolan parent is Angolan at birth [8].
  **Grandchildren**: not found. Naturalisation takes 10 years from the date of a permanent residence
  permit, and marriage to an Angolan national needs at least 5 years of union [8]. **No diaspora visa,
  right-of-return or Afro-descendant scheme was found** (unlike Ghana or Benin).
- **Context**: Greater Luanda has 8.8 million people (2024 census), about a third of the country [11].
  Oil is about 90% of exports [12]. Informal employment is 94.1% (2025) [12]. A new international
  airport, Dr. António Agostinho Neto, opened in November 2023 near Viana [11].

## Cities

### Luanda

- **Cost of living**:
  - Two very different markets.
    - *Local market* (Numbeo, 14 contributors, updated 2026-11-12): 1-bed in the centre 350,160 AOA
      (~€345); outside the centre 183,112 AOA (~€180). Cheap meal 5,000 AOA (~€5). A single person spends
      about €564 a month without rent [5].
    - *Expat-standard market* (Expat.com, 2025-12-11): 1-bed in an expat area USD 1,000–2,000
      (~€860–1,720). A villa in a gated compound costs USD 3,000–5,000+. A single professional spends
      USD 1,150–2,150 a month without rent [3].
  - **Neighbourhoods**:
    - Talatona is the premier expat district, with international schools and Belas Shopping.
    - Miramar is chosen for safety, embassies and sea views.
    - Luanda Sul has gated communities.
    - Alvalade, Maianga and Vila Alice are "more affordable, relatively safe" [3].
  - Luanda once ranked as the world's most expensive city for expatriates (Mercer). The 2014 import
    tariffs pushed prices up further [11]. This is older data, but the reputation persists.
- **Work & money**:
  - Oil and gas dominate formal well-paid jobs. Around 2010, oil workers earned about USD 5,400 a month
    against an average salary of about USD 260. This is old data (2010) and shows the inequality [11].
  - Other employers named in fetched sources: telecoms (Unitel, Movicel, Angola Telecom) [13].
  - Banking and public administration: not sourced in this pass.
  - Remote work is technically feasible (several submarine cables land here, see below). But a
    euro/dollar income meets kwanza inflation [12], and the visa rules give no nomad route [7].
  - Local salaries for 2024–2026: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - **Startup law**: parliament unanimously passed a start-up law that gives startups a legal
    definition, with an annual revenue cap of USD 3.5 million and no minimum (ITWeb, 2026-03-23) [19].
    The article names no specific tax incentive and no Luanda hub. It mentions the AfDB-backed Crescer
    youth-employment project (USD 125 million) and DIGITAL.AO [19]. Law number and gazette date: not
    confirmed.
  - **Crypto**: Law No. 3/24 (in force 2024-04-10) **bans cryptocurrency mining** and possession of
    mining equipment, with prison terms of 1–12 years for individuals; foreigners may be expelled [20].
    The stated reason is pressure on the power grid. It also places the issue of any legal tender,
    crypto included, under the central bank (BNA) [20]. Anyone drawn by crypto should treat Angola as
    restrictive, not as a hub.
  - Still to research: named hubs and incubators, Multicaixa Express / Unitel Money / Afrimoney.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Internet: Angola is linked by the WACS, SAT-3 and SACS cables. SACS is a direct cable to Fortaleza,
    Brazil [13]. Fixed broadband was concentrated in Luanda, but that figure dates from 2012 [13].
  - Power cuts and water: not quantified in fetched sources.
  - Transport:
    - *Candongueiros* (blue-and-white Hiace minibuses) carry most commuters. The fare was 100–200 AOA per
      trip, an undated figure that is probably outdated [11].
    - The FCDO advises **against taxi apps where possible** and against minibuses. It recommends
      pre-arranged cars [2].
    - Ride-hailing apps present in Luanda: not confirmed by a fetched source.
- **Health & schools**:
  - Health: "several competent private clinics in Luanda" charge high fees in advance. Outside Luanda,
    care is "very limited". Ambulance: 112 [2c]. Complex cases may need evacuation to South Africa or
    Europe, so international insurance with evacuation cover is essential [3]. Named clinics: not found.
  - Schools [3]:
    - **Lycée Français Alioune-Blondin Bèye** (French curriculum, €211–877 per quarter per Expat.com;
      this looks low for an AEFE school and was not confirmed on the school's own fee schedule, so
      treat it as unverified)
    - Luanda International School (IB, Talatona, USD 28k–64k a year)
    - ESCOLA (British/South African curriculum)
    - American Schools of Angola
    - Escola Portuguesa de Luanda
    - Waiting lists can last 2+ years.
  - Cholera: hotspots active in Luanda in November 2026 [1].
- **Safety**:
  - FCDO (updated 2026-05-28, still current 2026-11-14): "high level of crime in Luanda, some of it
    violent". The advice covers armed muggers, people followed after withdrawing cash, theft from
    slow-moving cars downtown and staged minor accidents. Do not walk between bars on **Ilha do Cabo**
    (Ilha de Luanda), nor anywhere alone after dark, and do not use a phone in traffic [2].
  - Protests over fuel prices and the cost of living led to **three days of violent riots in July 2025**,
    and curfews can be imposed at short notice [2].
  - HRW (World Report 2025, on 2024) reports police killings, arbitrary detention of protesters, and a
    2024 law providing up to 25 years in prison for protest-related vandalism [6].
  - Neighbourhood nuance: Miramar and Talatona are perceived as safer [3]. The musseques (informal
    settlements) ring the city [11].
- **Social life & vibe**: Ilha de Luanda (beach bars, Clube Náutico, 1924) is the nightlife strip [11],
  and the FCDO's crime warning applies there [2]. Carnival takes place in February [3]. Hofstede gives
  Angola a high Indulgence score (83): leisure and celebration are valued [10]. Kizomba, semba and
  kuduro come from Angola (general knowledge; no fetched source in this pass). Basketball and football
  clubs: not sourced.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: the Expat.com Luanda forum has about 1,600 members, 115 topics and
  76 listed businesses, with threads on Talatona, cars and Halal food [3]. Angolan returnee associations,
  Facebook/WhatsApp groups and Alliance Française activity: not found in this pass. Afrobarometer
  (2024-11-20) reports that **most Angolans have considered emigrating, mostly to Europe** [9]. The flow
  runs mainly outward, not inward.
- **Climate in practice**: hot semi-arid climate with only 405 mm of rain a year, mostly in March–April.
  May–October is dry and grey ("cacimbo" fog), with highs around 24.6 °C in July and 31 °C in March [11].
  Expat.com gives a rainy season of November to May [3]. Heavy rain floods low-lying roads.
- **Watch out**:
  - Very high expat-standard costs, with dollar rents paid in advance.
  - Inflation [12] and the language barrier.
  - Violent street crime [2] and political tension around protests [2][6].
  - Cholera [1], and health care that is costly and needs evacuation insurance [2c][3].

### Benguela

- **Cost of living**: not found as Benguela-specific data. It is assumed cheaper than Luanda (low confidence).
- **Work & money**:
  - Historic trade hub; fish processing and sugar milling [14].
  - The main driver is the **Lobito Corridor**: the Benguela Railway was concessioned in 2022 to Lobito
    Atlantic Railway (Mota-Engil, Trafigura, Vecturis). An EU–US–Angola–Zambia–DRC MoU was signed in
    October 2023. The EU gives €116 million (Global Gateway) and the US hundreds of millions through PGI.
    The first DRC copper shipment reached the US in August 2024 [15].
  - Lobito port lies 29 km north and handles about 2 Mt and 370 ships a year [14][16].
  - Logistics, mining transit and port jobs are the realistic career angle.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Airports: Benguela airport, plus Catumbela airport between Benguela and Lobito [14][16].
  - The ADONES domestic fibre network links coastal cities [13].
  - Power, water and apps: not found.
- **Health & schools**: Universidade Katyavala Bwila, founded in 2009 [14]. Hospitals and international
  schools: not found. The FCDO says care outside Luanda is "very limited" [2c].
- **Safety**: no city-specific FCDO line. The general Angola advice applies: road travel in convoy and
  not at night, landmines on verges, frequent checkpoints [2]. Local press not reached.
- **Social life & vibe**: smaller coastal provincial capital. The municipality had about 692,000 people
  in 2022 [14]. Beaches: Baía Azul near Baía Farta, and Caotinha [14]. Restinga beach is in Lobito [16].
  Nightlife and culture: not sourced.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: desert climate (BWh) [14]. Months: not found; it is presumably similar to the
  Luanda pattern of a cool, grey dry season.
- **Watch out**:
  - Very thin information, with healthcare far below Luanda [2c].
  - Portuguese is required.
  - The job market depends on a corridor whose effects on daily life are not yet documented.

### Lubango (additional: the cool highland alternative)

Why it matters: Lubango is Angola's **second city**, with 890,384 people in the 2024 census. It sits at
about 1,720–1,760 m (the source gives both), with an **18.6 °C average** and rains in December–March [17]. It is the only Angolan city
with a temperate climate. Its economy is agricultural, with food processing. It has a university
(Mandume ya Ndemufayo), the Escola Portuguesa do Lubango, an airport and a railway [17]. Cost, safety,
community and health data: not found. Candidate for the product: yes, but research it first.

### Lobito (additional, optional)

Twin city of Benguela, 29 km north, with a port that is "one of Africa's finest natural harbours". It is
the terminus of the Benguela Railway, and its population was about 393,000 in 2014 [16]. For a mover it
works as part of a **Benguela–Lobito–Catumbela** area rather than a separate choice.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of cited context. Neither the cities nor the quiz measure personality.
Country-level cultural data: Hofstede/Culture Factor gives Angola Power Distance 83, Individualism 18,
Achievement 20, Uncertainty Avoidance 60, Long-term Orientation 15 and Indulgence 83 [10]. In practice
this points to hierarchy, family and network-based life, and a culture of leisure and celebration.

**Luanda**
- Openness: suits someone curious about a fast-changing megacity of 8.8 M [11] and an Afro-Lusophone
  culture, who will learn Portuguese.
- Conscientiousness: helps with dollar budgeting, advance rents [3], visa paperwork and its fines [2b],
  and a hierarchical administration (PDI 83) [10].
- Extraversion: rewarded. Life runs through networks (Individualism 18) and celebration (Indulgence 83)
  [10], with the Ilha nightlife [11].
- Agreeableness: deference to hierarchy and relationship-building help [10].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): important, given violent crime advisories [2], inflation [12],
  protest tension [6] and high costs.

**Benguela / Lobito**
- Openness: an emerging logistics hub [15] rather than a cultural capital.
- Conscientiousness: needed to cope with thin services and healthcare [2c].
- Extraversion: lower-key than Luanda (inferred, low confidence).
- Agreeableness: a smaller community, so relationships matter more (inferred).
- Emotional stability: less urban pressure, more isolation (inferred).

**Lubango**
- Suits a calmer, nature-oriented temperament: cool highland climate [17], an agricultural town.
- Low confidence overall.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex activity has been legal since the new Penal Code took effect on 2021-02-09 [18].
  - Discrimination based on sexual orientation is outlawed in employment and in goods and services
    (Penal Code art. 214), as well as in the 2015 Labour Law [18].
  - No marriage and no recognition of couples [18].
  - FCDO (2026): public attitudes are less tolerant than in the UK, and public affection may draw
    negative attention [2].
  - A 2017 ILGA-RIWI survey found 61% agreeing on equal rights [18]. This is old data.
  - Editorial reading (not from a source): on paper, this is one of the more protective legal
    frameworks in Africa. Law on paper is not daily acceptance; see the FCDO line above.
- **Racism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners**: no reliable source found in this pass. Afrobarometer
  2025-03-13 says Angolans hold "divergent views on migration" [9]. Not investigated: colourism,
  perceptions of *retornados* and *mestiços*, and attitudes toward West African and Congolese migrants.
- **Conflict zones**: the FCDO advises against all but essential travel to **Cabinda Province (except
  Cabinda city)** because of separatist attacks on foreigners and kidnapping risk. It also advises against
  the area **within 1 km of the DRC border in Lunda Norte** (2026-05-28) [2][2a]. HRW reports police
  firing live rounds at protesters in Lunda Norte diamond areas (March 2024) [6]. Landmines remain on
  roads and verges [2]. The FCDO notes an Ebola outbreak in DRC's Ituri Province, which brings health
  screening at borders [2c].
- **Political climate**: riots in July 2025 over fuel and the cost of living [2]. 2024 laws allow up to
  25 years in prison for protest vandalism and broadcast shutdowns without a court order [6].
- **Women's daily experience**: the FCDO reports rapes in nightlife areas and private homes and advises
  never travelling alone at night [2]. HRW reports police sexual assault and harassment of women street
  vendors (2024) [6]. Data on women's professional life: not found.
