## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** English is the only official language. Mandinka, Pulaar (Fula), Wolof, Serer and Jola are national languages [9]. In the Banjul–Serrekunda area, Wolof is the lingua franca of streets and markets [7]. French is **not** widely used. The country is surrounded by francophone Senegal, so some Wolof/French bilinguals exist, but no source quantified this.
- **Religion:** about 95.7% Muslim and 4.2% Christian (2024) [9]. Friday prayer and Ramadan structure the week and the year.
- **Currency:** Gambian dalasi (GMD) [9].
- **Population:** 2,422,712 (census of 1 May 2024) [9]. Greater Banjul Area: 405,809 (2024) [8]. The Kanifing–Serrekunda urban area holds roughly half the population (2018 estimate) [7].
- **Entry for (a) French/EU passport holders:** not confirmed. France Diplomatie's "Entrée / séjour" sub-page could not be read [2]. For British nationals, UK FCDO (updated 11 Nov 2026) gives a 28-day stay stamped on arrival with no visa. This can be extended twice by 28 days at the Immigration Office in Banjul or at the tourist police stations in the Tourism Development Area. Longer stays need a residence permit [3]. Whether the same rule applies to French/EU nationals was **not found**. Verify with the Gambian embassy.
- **Residence permit:** required beyond the visitor period [3]. Fees, categories and the "alien ID card" process were **not found**. The Gambia Immigration Department site was unreachable on 2026-11-14.
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:**
  - **Citizenship by descent:** a child is Gambian at birth, wherever born, if at least one parent is a Gambian national of Gambian origin. Since the 1997 Constitution this works through either parent. A child born abroad cannot automatically pass nationality on to their own children [10].
  - **Dual nationality:** allowed since 2001 for Gambians *born in The Gambia*. Naturalised citizens must renounce their other nationality [10].
  - **Naturalisation:** generally 15 years of residence. Spouses of Gambians can apply after 7 years [10].
  - **Afro-descendant right of return:** none in force. In 2010, President Jammeh announced that Black African descendants willing to settle could get nationality, but no legal provision followed. About 100 people relocated and were unable to obtain nationality. The 2020 draft constitution would have covered people with one Gambian grandparent, but it was **not adopted** [10]. The "Roots" heritage-tourism link with African Americans is real [11], but it is not a legal route to settle.
- **Political calendar:** a presidential election is set for **Saturday 5 December 2026**. The campaign runs 16 Nov–3 Dec. FCDO expects "large gatherings and increased activity across the country" and, in general, advises avoiding large gatherings and protests (updated 11 Nov 2026) [3]. During the 2016 election, internet and phone networks were shut down nationwide [12].

## Cities

### Banjul

Banjul is the capital on St Mary's Island. It is small (26,461 people in the city proper, 2024) and mostly administrative: ministries, the Central Bank and the port [8]. Few people who move here actually live inside Banjul. Most live along the coast in Kanifing/Serrekunda and nearby areas.

- **Cost of living:** basic apartments cost **GMD 15,000–25,000/month (≈ EUR 180–305)**, rented mostly by people working in the administration (Expat.com, Nov 2025) [5]. Numbeo's national figures put a 1-bedroom in a city centre at **GMD 7,589 (≈ EUR 90)** and an inexpensive meal at **GMD 489 (≈ EUR 6)** (updated 7 Apr 2026, only 13 contributors, so low reliability) [4]. The gap between the two sources is large. Numbeo probably reflects local-standard housing, while Expat.com reflects expat-standard housing.
- **Work & money:** jobs are in government, the Central Bank, the port and groundnut processing [8]. Nationally, agriculture employs about 75% of the labour force (undated figure) [11]. 2025 GDP per capita was **USD 987 nominal**, with growth of 5.9% (2025) and 5.0% forecast for 2026 [11]. Local salaries were not found. Remote work is possible, but internet reliability was not documented.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** see Serrekunda, where the ecosystem sits (Kanifing).
- **Daily infrastructure:** a road crosses the Denton Bridge to Serrekunda. The Banjul–Barra ferry crosses the river [8]. FCDO says the ferry is "often overcrowded" and lacks life-saving equipment, and recommends the Senegambia Bridge near Farafenni instead (Nov 2026) [3]. The city sits at about 2 m elevation, and a government minister has warned it could be submerged by a 1 m sea-level rise [8]. Power cuts, water and ride-hailing: **not found**.
- **Health & schools:** Marina International School, British curriculum (Expat.com, Nov 2025) [5]. The reference public hospital (Edward Francis Small Teaching Hospital) is **not confirmed** by the sources fetched.
- **Safety:** see the country-wide FCDO notes under Serrekunda [3]. No Banjul-specific data.
- **Social life & vibe:** quiet after office hours (inference, not sourced). Arch 22 (the 1994 coup monument) and Albert Market are the landmarks [8].
- **Diaspora & expat community:** not found specifically for Banjul.
- **Climate in practice:** rain from June to September/October, peaking in August. Mean highs run from about 30°C (Aug) to 34°C (Mar), and mean lows from about 16°C (Jan) to 24°C (Jul) [9]. FCDO warns of localised flooding June–October [3].
- **Watch out:** small and administrative, with little living stock for newcomers. Flood-prone and low-lying [8]. Access depends on the Denton Bridge.

### Serrekunda (Kanifing urban area, incl. Kololi, Kotu, Fajara, Bakau)

This is the country's real urban centre. Kanifing Municipality had 382,096 people in 2013 and about 40% of all business establishments [7].

- **Cost of living (Expat.com, Nov 2025)** [5]:
  - Serrekunda proper: **GMD 10,000–25,000/month (≈ EUR 120–305)**, with few expats.
  - Kololi/Senegambia: studios from **GMD 20,000 (≈ EUR 245)**, large flats up to GMD 50,000. Lively and noisy.
  - Kotu: **GMD 25,000–50,000**, family-friendly.
  - Fajara: **GMD 40,000–80,000** for 3–4 bedroom houses. The most sought-after area.
  - Cape Point (Bakau): from **GMD 60,000**.
  - Electricity runs GMD 2,000–5,000/month, internet GMD 1,500–4,000/month and a guard GMD 1,000–3,000/month. The deposit is usually 1–2 months' rent, on 6–12-month leases.
  - Monthly budget for a single person is about **GMD 76,500 (≈ USD 1,224)**, based on Numbeo data from Sept 2025. Imported Western goods cost 2–3 times more [5].
  - Numbeo (Apr 2026) lists utilities at GMD 2,250, a mobile plan at GMD 1,050 and 60 Mbps+ broadband at **GMD 3,875/month** [4].
- **Work & money:** commerce (Serrekunda Market, also called Sandika, is the largest market in the country), tourism on the Kololi strip, NGOs and research (the MRC Unit at Fajara) [5][7]. GIEPA's focus sectors are agribusiness, ICT, light manufacturing, solar energy, transport and tourism (GIEPA site, 2026) [13]. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - **Startup Incubator Gambia** describes itself as the country's first incubator, with 50+ startups backed. It runs a 6-month incubation programme, coworking space and design-thinking bootcamps from the MSTE Building, Kanifing Institutional Layout (site viewed 2026) [14].
  - **GIEPA** (the investment agency, founded 2010) offers a Special Investment Certificate. A GIEPA Amendment Bill was under review in May 2026 [13].
  - **Wave (mobile money) does operate in The Gambia.** It started operations in September 2022 (licence obtained Nov 2021) [15]. The FinScope Consumer The Gambia 2025 survey, presented 8 July 2026, puts formal financial inclusion at 82% in 2025 against 19% in 2019, driven mainly by mobile money; the press credits Wave for much of this (Financial Afrik, 8 Jul 2026) [23].
  - Other hubs, crypto adoption, investors and company-registration delay: **not found**.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - Telecom operators: Africell, QCell, Comium and state-owned Gamtel/Gamcel [12].
  - The country has been on the ACE submarine cable since 2011 [12].
  - Power-cut frequency, water quality (tap water is not drinkable per Expat.com [5]) and ride-hailing apps: **not found**.
  - FCDO: poor driving standards, potholed roads, poor lighting at night, some unroadworthy taxis. ATMs are concentrated in the Senegambia area and can be unreliable (Nov 2026) [3].
  - The Bakoteh open dump burns waste on a main road into Serrekunda [7].
- **Health & schools (Expat.com, Nov 2025)** [5]:
  - Private clinics: MRC Unit (Fajara), Westfield Clinic (the guide places it in both Serrekunda and Fajara), SOS Clinic (Kololi). Kanifing General Hospital is the public referral hospital.
  - A consultation costs GMD 500–2,000. Insurance runs USD 1,200–3,600/year for an individual. Medical evacuation cover to Dakar, Accra or Europe is "strongly recommended".
  - Schools: Banjul American International School (Fajara, USD 8,000–15,000/year), SBEC International School (Brufut, British curriculum), Gambia Maarif Schools.
  - **French school:** École Française de Banjul, Atlantic Road, Bakau, founded 1984, preschool to *première*, with CNED distance courses from *sixième* [24]. Its current AEFE status and enrolment were not confirmed. A French government page from around 2017 gave about a hundred pupils (unverified for 2026). The Alliance Française de Banjul also operates [24].
- **Safety (UK FCDO, updated 11 Nov 2026)** [3]:
  - Petty theft in cities and tourist areas. Take extra care at night on isolated beaches and in markets. There have been thefts from hotel rooms.
  - "Bumsters" approach people on beaches and may offer unofficial tours into Senegal.
  - Corruption and bribe requests are reported "at all levels".
  - "No recent history of terrorism", but attacks cannot be ruled out.
  - Tourist police control the entrance to the Kololi Strip [16].
  - France Diplomatie (updated 10 Sept 2026): global "vigilance renforcée" alert and measles alert. The zone map details could not be read [2].
  - Neighbourhood nuance from residents: the Kololi strip is associated with sex tourism, which locals criticise [7].
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - The Kololi/Senegambia Strip is lined with restaurants and is busy with European tourists, mainly British, Belgian, German and Dutch [16].
  - Wrestling and football are popular. Serrekunda is a regional centre of the Tablighi Jamaat Islamic movement [7].
  - Weekly life follows Islamic practice (95.7% Muslim) [9].
  - Music scene and specific venues: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community:**
  - The Expat.com Gambia forum has about 1,791 members (Nov 2025) [5].
  - Retirees mention Fajara Golf Club, expat dinners and volunteering [5].
  - Returnee associations and Facebook/WhatsApp groups: **not found** (no search possible).
- **Climate in practice:** same as Banjul [9]. Flooding in the rainy season reduces customers at Serrekunda Market [7].
- **Watch out:**
  - Noise and sex tourism on the Kololi strip.
  - Open-air waste burning at Bakoteh.
  - Rents for Western-standard housing are far above local incomes.
  - English-speaking environment. A francophone gets by through English or Wolof; French is limited to niches such as the École Française and the Alliance Française in Bakau [24].
  - Bribe requests [3][5][7].

### Other cities that matter for living

- **Brikama:** headquarters of the West Coast Region (Brikama LGA had 730,895 people in 2013). Locally called "Satey Ba" ("big town"). Described as the centre of the Gambian music industry and known for wood carving [17]. A cheaper inland alternative. Rents and services: not found.
- **Brufut / Kotu–Brusubi coastal belt:** a developing, quieter area with gated compounds. 3–4 bedroom houses run GMD 30,000–60,000. A car is recommended. SBEC International School is here (Expat.com, Nov 2025) [5]. The guide presents it as a family option; whether families are actually moving here in numbers is not sourced.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These describe who may feel at ease in each place. No city or quiz question measures a personality trait. No Hofstede or GLOBE scores for The Gambia were found.

**Serrekunda / Kololi–Fajara coast**
- **Openness:** suits people curious about a mix of Wolof, Mandinka and Fula cultures and a busy market life. The country's largest market and an ethnically diverse population back this up [7].
- **Conscientiousness:** helps with administration. You need to plan around unreliable ATMs, potholed roads and possible bribe requests [3]. Leases are short with deposits up front [5].
- **Extraversion:** the Kololi strip has restaurants and crowds. Wrestling and football are social anchors [7][16]. Someone who wants quiet would pick Brufut or Fajara instead [5].
- **Agreeableness:** a small, conservative, 95.7% Muslim society [9]. People who adapt easily to religious norms and neighbourhood ties will find it easier. Approaches by beach "bumsters" call for polite firmness [3].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** helps with rainy-season flooding, uncertainty around the December 2026 election [3] and economic pessimism. Afrobarometer (Oct 2026) reports that three-quarters of young Gambians see the country going in the wrong direction [18].

**Banjul**
- **Conscientiousness:** useful for an administrative, office-hours rhythm [8].
- **Extraversion:** suits people who want a quieter base, since the city is small (about 26,000 people) [8].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - Same-sex acts are illegal for men and women. The Criminal Code allows up to 14 years. The 2014 "aggravated homosexuality" amendment allows up to life imprisonment. Since 2013, "men dressing as women" can bring 5 years [19].
  - In September 2024, the National Assembly raised the minimum penalty for "homosexual acts" from 2 to 5 years (Freedom House 2025) [20].
  - FCDO describes "zero tolerance" (Nov 2026) [3].
  - Afrobarometer (2021–23 survey, published Oct 2024): 93% would dislike a gay neighbour [19].
  - In March 2026, two men were sentenced to 5 years by the Brusubi Magistrates' Court [19].
- **Women:**
  - FGM has been banned since 2015, but 75% of women aged 15–49 have been cut (UNICEF 2022) [21].
  - A 2024 repeal bill was rejected in July 2024 [20][21]. A constitutional challenge (Almamy Gibba and Others v Attorney General) is before the Supreme Court. Hearings ran in the first half of 2026, and no ruling was found as of mid-November 2026; the 2015 ban stays in force until the court rules (HRW, 2 Feb 2026; Al Jazeera, 7 Jul 2026) [21][25].
  - Freedom House calls violence against women "a serious problem" (FIW 2025) [20].
  - Women's day-to-day experience in public space: not found.
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of diaspora:** no reliable source found. The country's own youth emigration pressure is high: Afrobarometer (7 Aug 2026) reports most Gambians have considered emigrating, and (30 Oct 2026) that three-quarters of young Gambians consider leaving [18].
- **Conflict:** no active conflict on Gambian soil. FCDO warns that "bumsters" may offer unofficial tours into Senegal that are unlikely to follow immigration procedures (Nov 2026) [3]. Across the southern border, in Senegal's Casamance, a weakened MFDC rebellion persists: a clash at Kadialock in March 2026 killed one Senegalese soldier, and armed groups linked to cannabis and timber trafficking use the porous Gambian border as a sanctuary (AllAfrica, 13 Mar 2026) [26]. Reports of displacement into Gambian Foni villages exist in local press but were not opened (unverified). This concerns the rural south-west border, not the Banjul–Serrekunda area. France Diplomatie's zone map should still be checked.
- **Governance:** Freedom House rates the country "Partly Free" (50/100, FIW 2025). Corruption safeguards score 1/4 [20]. There was a reported coup attempt in December 2022, which the army denies [9].
