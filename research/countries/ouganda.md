## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** English (official since 1962) and Swahili (official since 2005) [12]. In Kampala, Luganda is the most widely spoken language [12]. Lusoga, spoken around Jinja, is largely understood by Luganda speakers [12]. Schools teach in English at every level [14]. French is barely used. The only French-speaking anchors found are the French embassy and its community pages [15]. No French school was found in Kampala (see Gaps).
- **Currency:** Ugandan shilling (UGX) [13]. Day-to-day payments run largely on mobile money (MTN, Airtel), but I found no dated source for usage figures (not found).
- **Entry and residence: French/EU passport holders.** A visa is required. You apply on the e-visa portal (visas.immigration.go.ug), get an approval letter and are issued the visa on arrival [6][7]. The tourist visa costs about USD 50 for up to 3 months [8], according to Expat.com (Nov 2025). The East Africa Tourist Visa costs USD 100 and allows multiple entries into Kenya, Rwanda and Uganda over 90 days [6]. Your passport must be valid for 6 months [6], and you need a yellow fever certificate [8]. To work, you need a work permit. Expat.com (Nov 2025) says a Class A permit for employees of local firms costs about USD 400 a year and takes 10–14 working days [8]. Another Expat.com guide quotes 4–6 weeks for the employer-sponsored Class G2 permit [9]. Official fees are in the "Uganda Citizenship and Immigration Control Fees, 2021" schedule on immigration.go.ug [7], which I did not open.
- **Entry and residence: diaspora with family roots.** Uganda has allowed dual citizenship since the 2005 constitutional amendment. A 2009 amendment barred senior office-holders from holding it [10]. You are a citizen by birth, wherever you were born, if at least one parent or grandparent was a Ugandan citizen by birth [10]. Fees on the official site: **USD 200** for an "Indigenous Ugandan" dual-citizenship application, and **USD 200 plus USD 5,000 on approval** for a non-Ugandan [11]. I found no diaspora visa or "right of return" scheme similar to Ghana's or Benin's (not found). The Wikipedia summary relays an unverified, undated report from the Ugandan embassy in Berlin: children born abroad who hold their country of residence's citizenship may be refused [10]. **Check this with the embassy before relying on it.**
- **Health context, Nov 2026:**
  - The WHO declared a public health emergency of international concern over Ebola in DRC and Uganda on 17 May 2026 [1].
  - France Diplomatie (updated 2026-11-10) still lists **Kampala and Wakiso districts at very high risk** of new cases. It also says Uganda closed its border with DRC [1].
  - UK FCDO says the WHO declared **Uganda's outbreak over on 27 Aug 2026**, but the risk of spread from DRC remains [5].
  - **The two governments' pages disagree. Check both before moving.**
- **Politics:** The general election was held on 15 Jan 2026. On 17 Jan the Electoral Commission declared Museveni re-elected with 71.65% of the vote, against 24.72% for Bobi Wine. Mobile internet was cut from 13 Jan, deadly crackdowns followed, and Wine was placed under house arrest on 16 Jan [17]. FCDO (checked 2026-11-14) notes the inauguration took place on 12 May 2026 and that elections have led to protests and violence, including fatalities [4]. FCDO advises avoiding demonstrations and political colours (red, yellow) [4].

## Cities

### Kampala

The capital: 1.8 M people in the city proper (2024 census) and roughly 4–6.7 M in the metro area [13].

- **Cost of living**
  - Numbeo (2026-11-06, 47 contributors), 1-bed rent:
    - city centre: UGX ~1.97 M (≈ EUR 430), range 1.0–4.08 M
    - outside centre: UGX ~682,000 (≈ EUR 150), range 500,000–800,000
  - Numbeo, other prices: cheap meal UGX 10,000 (≈ EUR 2.2); mid-range meal for two UGX 100,000 (≈ EUR 22); single person excluding rent ≈ EUR 433/month [3]. Numbeo commenters say these reflect expat rather than local shopping [3].
  - Expat.com (Nov 2025), by neighbourhood [9]:
    - Kololo/Nakasero: 2-bed USD 1,500–2,500
    - Ntinda/Muyenga/Bugolobi: 1-bed USD 600–1,200
    - Kyanja/Kulambiro/Kansanga: 2-bed USD 400–800
    - landlords ask 3–6 months upfront plus a deposit
  - Blog by an 18-month resident (updated Aug 2026) [16]:
    - furnished 1-bed USD 500–600
    - unfurnished 1-bed USD 150–200
    - Kololo luxury flat USD 800–1,000
    - comfortable expat budget USD 1,200–1,600/month
    - local-diet groceries USD 200–250/month
  - Lakeside areas with newer compounds: Lubowa, Bunga, Munyonyo [18].
- **Work & money**
  - Kampala is the economic centre. All licensed commercial banks have their headquarters there, along with the main media groups [13].
  - Heavy industry is moving to the Kampala Industrial and Business Park in Namanve [13].
  - The economy is mostly informal: informal employment was about 90% in 2021 [19].
  - GDP growth was about 6% in 2024–25 (IMF via Wikipedia) [19]. Expat.com (Nov 2025) says commercial oil production was expected in FY 2025/26 [9]. Whether first oil has actually started by Nov 2026: unverified (could not check).
  - Salaries: not found. Remote work is feasible on fibre and 4G, but internet cuts at election time are a real risk [17].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**
  - Hubs:
    - **Outbox**: 14 years old; runs the NSSF Hi-Innovator Program and 10X for young women [20]
    - **Innovation Village**, Bugolobi: venture studios and catalytic funds; claims 5,000+ startups supported [21]
    - MUBS entrepreneurship and incubation centre [22]
  - Company registration goes through **URSB**, which offers online registration. Its business facilitation centre is at Plot 1 Baskerville Avenue, Kololo [23].
  - SafeBoda, a local boda-hailing app, operates in Kampala [9][16].
  - Crypto: Bank of Uganda says virtual assets are "not legal tender" and used at your own risk. In a Nov 2025 keynote at the Kampala Blockchain Summit, Governor Michael Atingi-Ego said Kenya had begun licensing virtual-asset providers while Uganda had "none", and that Uganda was still developing its legislation. He cited a Financial Intelligence Authority finding that 84.5% of Uganda's virtual-asset activity happens on decentralised platforms [33]. So there is grassroots activity but no licensed crypto industry. A "Kampala crypto hub" angle is **not supported**. Whether a law has passed since early 2026: unverified.
  - Investor presence and startup rankings: not found (StartupBlink and TechCabal blocked the fetch).
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - Power: the Umeme distribution concession was due to end in 2025, and the article cites reporting on a UEDCL takeover. Umeme had a long record of "chronic unreliability" and outages [24]. Current reliability after the takeover: not found.
  - Fibre exists in some neighbourhoods [8].
  - Transport: **Uber, Bolt and SafeBoda** [9]; matatu minibus USD 0.5–1.5 [9]. Heavy rush hours run 7–9 am and 5–8 pm [9]. Boda-bodas are fast but often dangerous [13][4].
  - Water: not found.
- **Health & schools**
  - Private hospitals: **Nakasero Hospital, International Hospital Kampala, Kampala Hospital, Case Hospital** [13][8].
  - Public and mission hospitals: Mulago National Referral, Mengo (1897), Lubaga (1899) [13].
  - Private insurance is effectively required. Expat.com quotes USD 1,700–5,600 a year [8].
  - Schools [9][8]:
    - **International School of Uganda** (Lubowa, IB; the two Expat.com pages disagree on 2025–26 fees: USD 18,000–28,000 per [8], USD 22,400–54,650 per [9]. Check with the school)
    - **Kampala International School Uganda** (Bukoto)
    - Kampala Diplomatic International School (Ntinda, USD 3,000–5,400)
    - Lincoln International, Rainbow International
  - French school: **not found**. The embassy site has a "Scolarité" section I could not read [15].
- **Safety**
  - France Diplomatie (2026-09-15): crime is rising, including in residential neighbourhoods such as Kololo, Nakasero and Naguru. It cites a persistent threat from al-Shabaab and the ADF (Islamic State affiliate) and advises extra caution in crowded public places [2].
  - FCDO (checked 2026-11-14): terrorists are "very likely to try to carry out attacks in Uganda" (hotels, restaurants, transport hubs, gatherings). Foreigners on boda-bodas have been robbed, sometimes with violence and weapons. Drink spiking is flagged in bars and on public transport [4].
  - The 2026 blog says the main risks are home break-ins (guards and bars are standard), night robbery and road accidents. Violent crime against foreigners is "relatively rare" [16].
  - Africanvestor (Sep 2026) names Kololo, Nakasero, Naguru, Bugolobi and Muyenga as the safest areas [18].
- **Social life & vibe**
  - Kololo has many bars and clubs (named: The Terrace, Otters, Kush Lounge); club entry costs about USD 2.70 [16].
  - The blog describes Ugandans as loving to party [16].
  - Malls: Garden City, Acacia, Oasis. Markets: Nakasero, Owino [9].
  - Religion is visible: Catholic, Anglican, Pentecostal and Muslim communities; the Bahá'í Mother Temple of Africa is on Kikaya Hill [13]. Martyrs Day (3 June, Namugongo) is a huge pilgrimage [9].
  - Uganda Martyrs and Kabalagala nightlife: details not found beyond the above.
- **Diaspora & expat community**
  - Expat.com Kampala forum: about 1,900 members [9]. Expat population estimated at 30,000–40,000 [9].
  - The French embassy lists French associations and a directory of French-speaking professionals (content not opened) [15].
  - Returnee and diaspora networks, Facebook and WhatsApp groups: **not found** (no search budget).
- **Climate in practice:** about 1,200 m altitude, highs of 25–28 °C and lows of 16–17 °C. Two rainy seasons, roughly Mar–May and Sep–Nov; April is the wettest month [13][9]. Downpours flood roads and make traffic worse (inferred from the rainy-season and traffic sources, unverified).
- **Watch out**
  - Election-time repression and internet cuts [17][4]
  - Ebola alerts in 2026 [1][5]
  - Large upfront rent payments [9]
  - Boda-boda road risk [4][16]
  - The Anti-Homosexuality Act (see below)
  - Crime is rising even in expat areas [2]

### Jinja

About 279,000–300,000 people. City status since 1 July 2020. 81 km east of Kampala, on Lake Victoria where the White Nile begins [25].

- **Cost of living:** Numbeo has no Jinja page [26]. Rent and budget figures: **not found**. Assumed lower than Kampala (unverified).
- **Work & money:** Wikipedia describes Jinja as the "second largest economy in Uganda". **Kakira Sugar Works** employs 7,500+. Other industries: steel, palm-oil refining, brewing; Kiira Motors planned a car plant. Nalubaale, Kiira and Bujagali (250 MW) hydropower are nearby [25]. Adventure tourism (rafting, bungee) is a real job sector [25].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** MUBS has a Jinja campus [22] and Makerere has an Eastern Campus [25]. Hubs and incubators: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:** Power protests over Umeme outages have been reported in Jinja and Bugembe [24]. Ride-hailing: not found. A Kampala–Jinja Expressway is listed, alongside the old highway [25]; its completion status was not checked.
- **Health & schools:** Jinja Regional Referral Hospital [25]. Schools: Busoga College, Jinja College, Kiira College Butiki [25][14]. International or French schools: not found.
- **Safety:** France Diplomatie puts Jinja under "vigilance renforcée", the same level as Kampala and Entebbe, and notes that crime there has recently increased (2026-09-15) [2]. Local-press nuance: not found.
- **Social life & vibe:** The **Nyege Nyege** electronic music festival has been held near Jinja since 2015 (Jinja Golf Course / Source of the Nile in 2023–24). It generated about UGX 6.6 bn in 2024 but faces regular religious backlash [27]. Life revolves around the river and lake, and the pace is slower than Kampala (editorial inference).
- **Diaspora & expat community:** not found.
- **Climate in practice:** lakeside and similar to Kampala (inference; no Jinja-specific source).
- **Watch out:**
  - Thin data
  - A smaller job market outside sugar, industry and tourism
  - Medical care beyond the regional referral hospital means going to Kampala; FCDO says facilities are limited outside Kampala [5]

### Entebbe (extra: the quiet lakeside alternative to Kampala)

- 81,000 people (2024 census), 34–36 km from Kampala via the Entebbe–Kampala Expressway. Home to the **international airport**, State House, the 1898 Botanical Gardens, the 1900 golf club and the Uganda Virus Research Institute [28].
- The expat blogger moved from Kampala to Entebbe [16]. It suits people who want calm by the lake while staying close to the airport.
- France Diplomatie (2026-09-15) puts it under "vigilance renforcée" and reports thefts and violence against expatriates; it advises avoiding isolated areas, even by day [2]. Rent: not found.

### Mbarara (extra: the second city of the south-west)

- About 221,000 people (2020 est.). City since July 2020, 270 km south-west of Kampala [29].
- A dairy and commercial hub: Nile Breweries, Coca-Cola, Lato Milk. Home to **Mbarara University of Science and Technology** and the 600-bed Regional Referral Hospital [29].
- Of interest to diaspora with roots in the south-west, or people in agribusiness and health. Cost of living, internet and expat community: not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of cited facts. They describe who *tends to be comfortable* in a place. They do not say any city or quiz question measures a trait. Hofstede country scores for Uganda: not found (the page did not load). Afrobarometer Round 10 (2024) finds that 92% feel free to vote as they wish and 78% back equal political chances for women. It also finds concerns about corruption and unemployment [30]. No academic study of regional personality in Uganda was found.

**Kampala**
- **Openness:** Suits people curious about a busy music and nightlife scene (Kololo bars, Ugandan party culture [16]) and a mix of churches, mosques and the Bahá'í temple [13]. Less suited to anyone who needs to live openly as LGBT [4].
- **Conscientiousness:** Helps with upfront rent, work-permit paperwork and private insurance [8][9]. People who plan and budget cope better.
- **Extraversion:** A strong social and party scene [16] suits sociable people.
- **Agreeableness:** Bargaining and overcharging are routine (agree prices first [16]), so a mix of friendliness and firmness helps.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** Rush-hour congestion [9], boda-boda road risk [4], election-time unrest and internet cuts [17], and 2026 Ebola alerts [1] call for a high stress tolerance.

**Jinja / Entebbe**
- **Openness:** Jinja draws people into rafting, river life and the Nyege Nyege scene [25][27].
- **Conscientiousness:** In a small town you have to organise your own healthcare access to Kampala [5].
- **Extraversion:** A quieter fit; the social scene is smaller (inference).
- **Agreeableness:** Smaller communities, so relationships carry more weight (inference, unsourced).
- **Emotional stability:** Calmer than the capital, with the same national political and health context [2].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**
  - The **Anti-Homosexuality Act 2023** has been in force since 30 May 2023.
  - Penalties: life imprisonment for homosexuality, the **death penalty for "aggravated homosexuality"**, and up to 20 years for "promotion" [31][4].
  - The Constitutional Court (April 2024) upheld most of the law. It struck down only the clauses on renting premises and failing to report. An appeal was filed on 11 Jul 2024 [31].
  - In Aug 2023 the World Bank halted new lending. In Oct 2023 the US announced Uganda's removal from AGOA trade benefits [31].
  - France Diplomatie (2026-09-15) says intolerance can sometimes turn violent and advises great caution in public [2]. FCDO (checked 2026-11-14) warns LGBT+ people, or people perceived as such, of a greater risk of harassment, imprisonment, blackmail and violence [4].
- **Conflict zones**
  - France Diplomatie (2026-09-15) formally advises against the DRC border area and Bwera town, and the South Sudan border. It advises against non-essential travel to Semuliki NP and Karamoja (the Moroto–Kotido–Kaabong road) [2].
  - FCDO (checked 2026-11-14) lists recent attacks in the west: a school in Mpondwe (Kasese), Jun 2023, 42 dead; two tourists and their driver killed in Queen Elizabeth NP, Oct 2023; a bar and farm in Kyabandara (Kamwenge), Dec 2023, 10 dead [4]. FCDO does not name the group on that page; France Diplomatie names the ADF as a persistent threat [2].
  - FCDO notes violence in Karamoja but does not advise against travel there [32].
- **Political repression:** The Jan 2026 elections brought a mobile internet shutdown (from 13 Jan), at least 7 people killed overnight on 15–16 Jan (Reuters), about 2,000 detentions (a figure attributed to the army chief), and Bobi Wine under house arrest [17].
- **Treatment of diaspora and foreigners, racism, colourism:** No reliable dated source found. FCDO notes that people who show signs of wealth are targeted by criminals [4].
- **Women's daily experience:** Not found beyond Afrobarometer's 78% support for women's political participation [30] and FCDO's general drink-spiking warning [4]. FCDO links to generic advice for women travellers [5].
