## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Population:** ~220,000 (2023 estimate) [8]. It is Africa's second-smallest country, made up of two islands. São Tomé island holds almost everyone. Príncipe had ~8,400 people (2018 estimate) [10].
- **Languages used day to day:** Portuguese is official and is spoken by about 98% of people. The creoles Forro, Angolar and Principense are also spoken [3][8]. About 6.8% speak French, which schools teach as a foreign language and which is useful in trade with Gabon, Cameroon and Congo [8]. Outside upscale lodges, English is rare [2]. **A French speaker without Portuguese will struggle with administration, schools and the rental market.**
- **Currency:** the dobra (STN), pegged at about €1 = 24.5 STN since 2010 [3]. Inflation ran at 16.1% in 2024 and 10.7% over January–July 2025 [2]. Cash is king: cards are rarely accepted and ATMs can be offline [2][3]. VAT has been 15% since 1 June 2023 [2].
- **Entry, (a) French/EU passport:** visa-free for **15 days** (EU exemption since 2015, per Wikipedia citing Timatic and a 2017 notice, so possibly dated) [6][5]. The FCDO page for British passports states a €20 entry fee paid on arrival, also payable in US dollars or dobras (FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14; no update date shown on re-fetch) [5]. The €20 fee is confirmed for UK passports only; check with the SMF for French/EU passports. Longer stays require a visa or entry authorisation through the SMF e-Visa portal. Processing takes 7 working days, the fee is paid at the border, and proof of €100/day in funds is required [7].
- **Residence:** the legal framework is Law 5/2008 and Decree-Law 11/2009, administered by the SMF. A residence visa (2 entries, 2 months) leads to a 1-year renewable temporary residence certificate. Accepted grounds are employment, self-employment, family reunification, study, research and similar [3]. Permanent residence requires 5 years plus proof of Portuguese [3]. Work visas need a local employer sponsor and take 4–6 weeks [2]. (Source [3] is a commercial agency, Sept 2026, which summarises the law. Verify with the SMF.)
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:** dual nationality is generally allowed for nationals from birth under the 2003 constitution [9]. Children born abroad to São Toméan parents can claim nationality at majority if their birth was registered with a consulate [9]. Naturalisation requires 5 years' residence and Portuguese, and generally renunciation of other nationalities [9]. **No Afro-descendant "right of return" scheme was found** comparable to Ghana's or Benin's. A **citizenship-by-investment** programme exists instead: Decree-Law 07/2025 (1 Aug 2025), a minimum donation of US$90,000 for a single applicant and US$95,000 for a family of two to four, plus US$5,000 per extra dependant and a non-refundable US$5,000 due-diligence fee per application [9][3]. According to [3], applications from people with three or more citizenships were paused in April 2026.
- **Politics:** President Carlos Vila Nova dismissed Prime Minister Patrice Trovoada on 6 Jan 2025 [11]. Américo Ramos was appointed prime minister on 12 Jan 2025, after Ilza Amado Vaz's brief tenure [8]. A coup attempt on 24–25 Nov 2022 left four dead [12]. The FCDO notes that economic strain and disputes over investment can occasionally lead to civil unrest (FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14) [4].

## Cities

### São Tomé (capital, Água Grande district)

- **Cost of living:**
  - Rent for a 1-bed downtown is about **€233/month** (range €100–300, roughly 2,450–7,350 STN), or about €163 outside the centre (Expat.com citing Numbeo, Apr 2025) [2]. Immigrant Invest quotes ~US$200 (citing Numbeo) in its text and US$250+ in its comparison table (6 Sept 2026) [3].
  - A 3-bed costs ~€575 downtown (range €400–750) and ~€450 outside the centre [2], and a house with a pool from ~US$1,300 [3].
  - GlobalCostData quotes far higher figures (~US$1,810 for a 1-bed in the city centre) [13]. That source looks unreliable. Treat it as an outlier.
  - Neighbourhoods expats name: downtown, the waterfront (baía Ana Chaves) and **Guadalupe** [2]. Areas listed by Wikipedia include Almeirim, Pantufo, Riboque and São Marçal [14].
  - Monthly budget for a single person: €750–950 (Numbeo, Apr 2025) [2] or US$700–1,000 [3]. A family of four needs €1,700–2,700 excluding school fees [2].
  - Price of a meal: not found.
  - The rental market is informal. Housing is found through word of mouth and Facebook expat groups, the deposit is 1–3 months' rent, and it is often paid in cash [3].
- **Work & money:**
  - The average local net salary is ~€460/month, and senior local roles rarely exceed US$1,000 [2][3]. Expat.com's own conversion of that figure ("USD 1,682") is inconsistent with €460, so treat the salary figure as approximate.
  - Employers include NGOs, donor projects, telecoms, tourism, cocoa and coffee exports, energy and construction [2][3]. Unemployment was 14.2% in 2023 according to Expat.com [2] and 9.1% according to IMF 2025 figures cited on Wikipedia [15]. The two figures disagree.
  - Remote income from abroad is called "the most realistic model" for expats [3].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** a small, donor-driven ecosystem, not a startup scene.
  - **REINA** (Rede de Incubadoras e Aceleradoras de STP) is promoted by the Ministry of Youth and Sports with UNDP funding [22] (listing undated).
  - **SOW Hub / Startup São Tomé**, run by the consultancy Represe, is listed by AfriLabs as an incubator for technology-based small businesses [21] (listing undated; activity level not confirmed).
  - The Rise Up programme for women entrepreneurs, launched in STP in early 2025, was cancelled in March 2025 [23] (unverified: based on the press-release title only).
  - **Crypto/Web3:** on 12 Oct 2025 the country's Citizenship and Investment Unit signed an MoU with Atlas Sovereign Smart Solutions (an IOPn joint venture) that mentions a future "Digital Treasury and Web3 Free Zone" and AI projects. It is a memorandum and a plan, with no operational status reported [24]. Do not read it as an existing crypto hub.
  - On 13 Nov 2026 the Central Bank publicly denounced illicit financial activity ("Banco Central denuncia exercício de actividade financeira ilícita") [16].
  - Ease of registering a company: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - Power cuts are frequent. In August 2025 they reached up to 12 hours a day after a private producer stopped generating (IMF via [3]). A generator or UPS costs US$500–1,500 [3].
  - 4G covers ~90% of the country (operator CST). Fibre exists only in parts of the city [3].
  - Tap water is not drinkable [2].
  - Transport means taxis and moto-taxis ("motoqueiros"), the only public transport, which the FCDO describes as often unsafe and in poor condition (accessed 2026-11-14) [4].
  - Ride-hailing apps and mobile money: not found.
- **Health & schools:**
  - The reference hospital is **Hospital Ayres de Menezes**. It handles minor illness and emergencies, but serious cases are evacuated to Lisbon (~7 h flight) or Libreville [2][3]. Medevac insurance is essential [2][3]. Malaria is endemic: 1,293 cases on São Tomé in Q1 2025 [3].
  - Schools follow the Portuguese curriculum: **Escola Portuguesa de São Tomé e Príncipe** and **Escola Internacional de STP**. There are no IB, British or US schools [3], and no recognised international school according to [2]. **No French (AEFE) school found**; France Diplomatie's bilateral page (updated 29 Oct 2026) mentions none [25].
  - For French speakers: the **Alliance française de São Tomé-et-Príncipe** spreads French and runs cultural events, under the French embassy in Libreville; France has no consulate in the country (the Libreville consulate general covers it, with an honorary consul in São Tomé) [25].
  - Higher education: the Universidade de São Tomé e Príncipe [14].
- **Safety:**
  - The FCDO calls the crime rate generally low but notes that burglaries and armed robberies can happen. It advises against wearing expensive jewellery, carrying large amounts of cash or taking valuables to the beach (FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14) [4].
  - OSAC rates crime and terrorism threats as low, and the Organised Crime Index score is 1.70/10 (via [3], Sept 2026).
  - France Diplomatie's page was updated on 10 Sept 2026, but its security section was not retrieved [17].
  - Neighbourhood-level detail: not found.
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - Expats describe a "leve-leve" (slowly, slowly) pace, and nightlife and dining are limited [3].
  - Music: **ússua** and **socopé** rhythms, plus the **tchiloli** dramatic dance-theatre [8][14].
  - Food: fish, seafood, breadfruit, banana, and coffee used as a spice [8].
  - Football is the main sport [8].
  - The population is ~81% Christian (2020) [8]. Religion is present in daily life but not dominant.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** the expat community numbers an estimated 3,000–5,000, mostly Portuguese, Angolan, Cape Verdean, Brazilian and French [2]. The Expat.com forum has about 602 members [2]. Only 30 French nationals were on the consular register in 2022 [25]. Facebook expat groups are used for housing [3]. Specific group names or returnee associations: not found.
- **Climate in practice:**
  - The rainy season runs from October to May.
  - The dry season ("gravana") runs from June to September and is cooler, drier and more comfortable.
  - Temperatures stay around 22–30 °C [14].
- **Watch out:**
  - Portuguese is mandatory in practice.
  - Power cuts are long.
  - Imports arrive by ship, so they are expensive and stock runs out [3].
  - Serious healthcare means evacuation.
  - There are no French or international-curriculum schools.
  - The job market is tiny and wages are low.
  - Political instability recurs.

### Santo António (Príncipe)

- **Profile:** capital of the Autonomous Region of Príncipe, with 2,620 inhabitants (2012) [18] or about 1,500 according to Expat.com (2025) [2]. Príncipe has been a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 2012, and the designation was extended to the whole country in 2025 [10].
- **Cost of living:** no city-specific rent data found. Expat.com and Immigrant Invest describe services as very limited. Basics are probably cheaper, but imports cost more (?) [2][3].
- **Work & money:** jobs are in ecotourism, conservation and the public sector (inferred from the biosphere status [10]). Specific employers: not verified in this pass.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:** the airport is 3 km away, with flights to São Tomé on STP Airways and Africa's Connection [18]. A return flight costs US$180–220, and the ferry takes about 10 hours [2]. Healthcare, schools and internet are weaker than in São Tomé [3].
- **Health & schools:** Hospital de Príncipe only, and serious cases are evacuated [2]. No international school.
- **Safety:** no specific source. Country-level advice applies [4].
- **Social life & vibe:** a very small town with colonial churches and the *Auto de Floripes* street play [18]. Príncipe has its own **dêxa** rhythm [8]. Sport centres on the island's only stadium, the Estádio Regional 13 de Junho [18].
- **Diaspora & expat community:** not found.
- **Climate in practice:** about 1,872 mm of rain a year, much wetter than the capital. July is the driest month and October the wettest [18].
- **Watch out:** Immigrant Invest calls it "the least practical option" for long-term living [3]. The island is isolated, a medical emergency means a flight and then an evacuation, and the internet is weak.

### Other places that matter for living (from [2][3])

- **Neves** (north of São Tomé island): the fuel and fishing port. Costs are lower than in the capital, but services are limited [2].
- **Mé-Zóchi** (Trindade, inland): the rural option closest to the capital, green and cheaper. It suits remote workers with their own power backup [3].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

The traits below describe who tends to settle in easily. Neither the city nor any quiz question measures them. **No Hofstede or GLOBE scores exist for São Tomé-et-Principe (not found).** Afrobarometer has surveyed the country (2015, 2018, 2022, 2024): 62% of respondents say corruption has risen, and 61% say the country is heading in the wrong direction (Round 9) [19].

- **São Tomé**
  - **Openness:** suits people curious about a Lusophone and creole culture (tchiloli, ússua) who are willing to learn Portuguese [8][14].
  - **Conscientiousness:** helps with an informal rental market, cash-only payments and paperwork at the SMF [2][3]. You need to plan stock, medicines and backup power yourself [3].
  - **Extraversion:** party-lovers will find it quiet, because nightlife is limited [3]. Social life runs through word of mouth and the small expat circle [2][3].
  - **Agreeableness:** the "leve-leve" pace rewards patience and politeness over insistence [3].
  - **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** you must cope with power cuts of up to 12 h [3], distant serious healthcare [2] and occasional political tension [4][12]. In return, crime pressure is low [4].
- **Santo António**
  - **Openness:** suits nature lovers and people seeking isolation (biosphere reserve) [10].
  - **Extraversion:** a very small social circle [18].
  - **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** isolation and a 10 h ferry mean you need a high tolerance for depending on others [2][3].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - Same-sex activity has been legal since the Penal Code took effect in Nov 2012.
  - The 2019 Labour Code bans workplace discrimination based on sexual orientation, and the Penal Code provides aggravated sentences for homophobic crimes.
  - There is no recognition of couples [20] (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14).
  - The FCDO notes that attitudes towards same-sex relationships are less tolerant than in the UK and that public displays of affection may draw unwanted attention (FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14) [4].
  - Survey (2016, reported by Gay Star News via Wikipedia): 46% would like or not mind an LGBT neighbour [20]. That figure is old.
- **Racism, colourism, treatment of the diaspora:** no reliable source found.
- **Conflict:** no conflict zone. The Nov 2022 coup attempt left four dead [12], and the government was dismissed in Jan 2025 [11]. The FCDO mentions occasional unrest (accessed 2026-11-14) [4].
- **Women:** Afrobarometer Round 9 found 52% saying gender-based violence is "somewhat" or "very" common in their community [19]. Afrobarometer dispatch AD1239 (2 Oct 2026) covers gaps in women's empowerment [19]. The FCDO has no section on women [4].
