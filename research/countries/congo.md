## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages:** French is the official language and the language of administration, schools and business. All the official, school and business sources consulted are in French [5][6][9][10]. Day to day, people also speak Lingala (dominant in Brazzaville) and Kituba/Munukutuba (dominant in Pointe-Noire and the south). *This language split is general knowledge; no source was opened for it in this pass.*
- **Currency:** Central African CFA franc (XAF), pegged to the euro at a fixed rate (1 EUR = 655.957 XAF). Exchanging money outside bureaux de change and ATMs is not allowed [4].
- **Entry for French/EU passport holders:** A visa is required before departure: "Aucun visa n'est délivré à l'arrivée". You apply and pay online through a Congolese embassy. Visas last 15–91 days and take about a week. You need a passport valid 6 months, a return ticket, repatriation insurance, an accommodation certificate (from the mairie, certified by the DDST) or a paid hotel booking, and yellow-fever vaccination (mandatory). France Diplomatie, updated 30 Oct 2026 [3]. The UK FCDO also says a visa through the embassy and a yellow-fever certificate are required, as of 27 Oct 2026 [13].
- **Residence:** After you arrive, you apply for a residence permit and register at the commissariat or mairie, within 15–30 days in some regions [7]. To work, you need a work visa plus an ACPE work authorisation (temporary or provisional), then a work permit. Hiring a foreigner is allowed only when the skill is unavailable locally [8].
- **Diaspora with family roots:** Dual nationality has been allowed since the 2002 Constitution. Law 32-2011 removed the requirement to renounce another nationality on naturalisation. A child of at least one Congolese-by-birth parent is Congolese wherever they are born. Former nationals can reacquire nationality if they reside in Congo, are not under arrest and were not previously expelled [15]. *The source is Wikipedia's summary of the nationality code, which itself gives a confusing effective date (20 Jan 2002) for the 2011 law; check it against the official text.* **No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found.**
- **Political context:** In the 15 Mar 2026 presidential election, Denis Sassou Nguesso was re-elected (about 95%) amid opposition boycotts, activist arrests and a nationwide internet blackout on election day [18]. Previous internet shutdowns were recorded in 2011, 2015 and 2016 [19]. These blackouts matter for remote workers.

## Cities

### Brazzaville

Capital. Population was about 2.14–2.15 million at the 2023 census, more than a third of the country [16].

- **Cost of living:**
  - **Expat-standard rents (lepetitjournal, updated Jul 2025) [5]:**
    - Furnished 3-room flat on the Plateau des Quinze Ans: about 750,000 XAF/month (≈1,150 EUR) after negotiation.
    - 2-bed flat in Camp Clairon / Les Flamboyants: about 1,500,000 XAF (≈2,300 EUR).
    - Villas in OCH, La Glacière, Bacongo: 1.5–2M XAF.
    - Deposit is 2 months plus 1 month agency fee. Agency viewings are often charged at about 10,000 XAF.
  - **Expat.com (Dec 2025) [6]:** 1-bed in the centre is USD 500–1,000 (≈300k–600k XAF, ≈460–920 EUR).
  - **Africarrieres (Feb 2026) [9]:** T3 in a sought-after area costs USD 600–1,200; on the periphery, USD 300–600.
  - **OpenShores (May 2026) [11]:** 2-bed is USD 300–500 in the centre and USD 150–250 in the suburbs. This is a local-market rather than expat figure.
  - **Middle-class 1-bed estimate:** about 150k–350k XAF (≈230–530 EUR). This is an editorial synthesis of [6][9][11]. The low end applies to areas like Moungali and Poto-Poto, the high end to the centre-ville.
  - **Meals and budget:** A simple local meal costs USD 2–5, mid-range USD 8–15 and an international restaurant USD 20–40. Monthly budget for comfortable expat living is USD 800–1,500 [9]. Lepetitjournal (Oct 2024) puts the cost of living at about 36% higher than France [7].
  - **Minimum wage:** 70,400 XAF/month since Jan 2025 [9].
- **Work & money:**
  - The city hosts ministries, embassies, bank HQs and the BEAC [11], plus the WHO Africa regional office [16].
  - Most job offers on emploi.cg were in Brazzaville: 56 of 75 when lepetitjournal checked (Nov 2024) [8].
  - Networks and word of mouth matter more than job boards [8].
  - 200k–300k XAF/month is cited as a "decent urban" salary [9].
  - Remote work is feasible in principle but exposed to power cuts and internet blackouts [5][18].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - Companies register through the ACPCE guichet unique. Expect queues and reports of irregular practices; no official delay or cost was found [10].
  - The **Startup Act** label (2023) requires majority-Congolese capital. Benefits include a free .cg domain and financing guarantees [10]. This favours diaspora founders with Congolese nationality.
  - **OSIANE** is the Central African tech show. Its 10th edition ran 2–5 Jun 2026 at the Grand Hôtel de Kintélé (Brazzaville) and included a "Challenge Startup Bassin du Congo" with OBAC Capital [17].
  - The Maloukou special economic zone targets digital services [10].
  - Lepetitjournal calls the ecosystem "embryonnaire" with no reliable data [10].
  - Named incubators and crypto adoption: **not found**.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - Power cuts are frequent. A generator is a common expat purchase; the USD 300–800 price comes from a search snippet only and is not on the Africarrieres page (unverified) [1]. Some areas use prepaid E2C meters, and water comes from SNE [5].
  - Utilities cost USD 50–150/month and home internet USD 30–80 [9]. The WACS submarine cable and fibre are expanding [10].
  - Mobile money is in use, and the ACPCE lists MoMo numbers [10]. Operator market shares were not found.
  - There is no organised public bus system. Shared taxis and minibuses cost USD 0.5–2 [9][16].
  - Ride-hailing apps: **not confirmed**.
  - Ferries and fast boats cross to Kinshasa [16].
- **Health & schools:**
  - **Health:** CHU de Brazzaville (Av. Lyautey), Clinique Netco (Av. Lyautey), Clinique Les Clairons (Plateau des 15 ans), Clinique Elikia (Poto-Poto), Hôpital Blanche Gomez (Bacongo), and the French embassy's centre médico-social [7]. Medical evacuation is likely for anything beyond basic care, and the FCDO says there is no central emergency-services number (page read 2026-11-14) [14]. France Diplomatie does list **117** for the police (updated 30 Oct 2026) [2].
  - **Schools:** Lycée International Français Saint-Exupéry (AEFE, about 735–850 pupils, about 1,600–3,500 EUR/yr) [6][12]. American International School of Brazzaville (US/IB, about USD 15–25k/yr) [6]. Clé Phénix, Anne-Marie Javouhey, and Turkish, Chinese and Lebanese schools [12]. Université Marien Ngouabi has about 25–26k students [6][16].
- **Safety:**
  - France Diplomatie (30 Oct 2026): insecurity is low in the central neighbourhoods where foreigners live; be careful at night and in outlying districts. Checkpoints appear in tense periods such as elections [2].
  - FCDO (summary updated 27 Oct 2026; safety page undated, read 2026-11-14): opportunistic crime and pickpocketing, especially in Brazzaville and Pointe-Noire; avoid walking after dark and isolated areas including beaches; demonstrations can turn violent [13].
  - The neighbouring **Pool** department has a history of the Ninja insurgency, with a ceasefire since Dec 2017. France Diplomatie (30 Oct 2026) says ex-rebels who have not fully disarmed remain, and advises intercity travel by day only, in a convoy of at least two 4x4s, sticking to the RN1 [2]. The FCDO says clashes could resume with little notice (read 2026-11-14) [13].
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - UNESCO City of Music since 2013 [16]. Congolese rumba has been on UNESCO's intangible-heritage list since 2021, jointly with the DRC, and Brazzaville was one of its cradles [20].
  - Poto-Poto is lively with markets, nightlife and the Poto-Poto painting school [6][16].
  - France Diplomatie describes society and religious practice as "fairly open" [4].
  - Expect church and bar noise near housing [5].
- **Diaspora & expat community:** The Expat.com Brazzaville forum has about 1,281 members [6]. Named diaspora or returnee associations, Facebook groups and WhatsApp groups: **not found** (search budget exhausted).
- **Climate in practice:**
  - Rains fall mainly from October to May. July and August are the driest months [16].
  - France Diplomatie gives the long rainy season as Oct–Dec and the short one as Mar–May [4].
  - Rain degrades roads [4][13].
- **Watch out:**
  - Expat rents are expensive for what you get [5].
  - Power and water are unreliable [5][9].
  - Internet blackouts around elections [18].
  - Bureaucracy and informal fees [10].
  - Healthcare limits that mean evacuation [14].
  - Road accidents are frequent [2].

### Pointe-Noire

Second city. Population was about 1.42 million at the 2023 census. It is the oil capital and has the Atlantic port [21].

- **Cost of living:**
  - **Rents:** T3 at USD 400–900 [9]. OpenShores says rents are similar to or slightly above Brazzaville, with overall costs 10–15% higher because of expat-driven prices [11]. Africarrieres instead calls them "slightly lower" [9]. Treat the two as roughly equal.
  - **1-bed middle-class estimate:** 150k–400k XAF (≈230–610 EUR), editorial.
  - Neighbourhood rent data was **not found**. The centre-ville and the Kassaï roundabout area are cited for clinics [7].
- **Work & money:**
  - The oil industry is the core: TotalEnergies, Eni and Perenco, plus services [11][21][22]. The Port Autonome de Pointe-Noire is the other main employer [21].
  - Most ACPE job offers are in oil and gas [8].
  - The economy is highly exposed to oil cycles. Public debt is about 93% of GDP (2024) [22].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - There is an ACPCE guichet in Pointe-Noire [10].
  - OSIANE 2026 held pre-events there [17].
  - Hubs: **not found**.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - Agostinho-Neto airport had direct flights to Paris, Abidjan, Istanbul, Addis Ababa, Douala, Libreville, Luanda and Cotonou, among others, as of Aug 2023 [21]. Air France flies direct [4].
  - Passenger rail to Brazzaville is "formellement déconseillé" until rehabilitation work is finished [4].
  - The road to Dolisie through the Mayombe is dangerous in rain [2].
  - Power and internet: the same national issues as Brazzaville. City-specific data was **not found**.
- **Health & schools:**
  - **Health:** Clinique Netcare (Av. Georges Dumond) and Clinique Guénin (near the Kassaï roundabout) [7].
  - **Schools:** Lycée Français Charlemagne, Connie's Academy, École Supérieure de Technologie du Littoral, Institut UCAC-ICAM, and the University of Loango [21].
- **Safety:** France Diplomatie (30 Oct 2026) says crime downtown is limited. It advises against walking on the beach south of the former wharf, against going into peripheral districts, and against driving downtown at night [2].
- **Social life & vibe:** It is a beach city on the Atlantic with a fishing culture [21] and an expat-oriented restaurant scene [11]. Specific nightlife and music information: **not found**.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** **Not found** in this pass.
- **Climate in practice:** The wet season runs Oct–Apr, wettest in Feb and Nov. Jun–Sep is dry, and July has almost no rain [21].
- **Watch out:**
  - The economy depends on oil cycles [22].
  - Prices are pushed up by oil-sector expats [11].
  - Beach-area crime [2].
  - Isolated by land: the rail line is advised against and the Mayombe road is risky [2][4].

### Dolisie (additional city)

Third city, with 178,172 inhabitants (2023 census). It is a rail junction on the Congo-Ocean line and a commercial centre [23].

- It is the cheapest named city: a 2-bed costs USD 100–150 and street food USD 1–2. However, OpenShores (May 2026) says access to services, including international private healthcare and education, is "quasi nul", and the public hospital is rudimentary. It mostly draws humanitarian workers and consultants [11].
- There is an ACPCE guichet [10].
- It is only relevant for someone with family there or an agricultural or forestry project.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of cited facts. No city "measures" a personality trait, and no quiz answer does either. **No Hofstede or GLOBE scores exist for the Republic of Congo** (none were found). Afrobarometer has surveyed the country since 2023 [24].

**Brazzaville**
- **Openness:** Suits people drawn to music and art. The city is a UNESCO City of Music and a cradle of rumba, and has the Poto-Poto painting school [16][20].
- **Conscientiousness:** Rewards people who plan ahead for paperwork (visa before arrival, residence registration, ACPCE queues) and for backup power and water [3][5][7][10].
- **Extraversion:** Social life runs through bars and music, and Poto-Poto is lively [6]. Networks matter more than job boards for finding work [8], which favours outgoing people.
- **Agreeableness:** 91% would like having neighbours of other ethnicities, or not mind (Afrobarometer Round 10) [24]. But a 2026 Afrobarometer dispatch says most Congolese do not want the country to take in more foreigners [24]. One editorial reading: tolerance of difference at neighbour level can coexist with guardedness towards "étrangers" as a category. No source measures this for diaspora returnees.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** Helps with power cuts [1][9], election-period checkpoints and internet blackouts [2][18], and limited emergency care [14].

**Pointe-Noire**
- **Openness:** A coastal, port and oil-industry city with an international mix [11][21].
- **Conscientiousness:** Suits people with a structured corporate career, since the oil sector is the core employer [8][22].
- **Extraversion:** The expat-restaurant and beach scene favours sociable people [11]. Night movement is restricted downtown [2].
- **Agreeableness:** No city-specific data.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** Oil-cycle job insecurity [22] and land isolation [4] call for tolerance of uncertainty.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - Same-sex activity has been legal since 1940. The age of consent is unequal: 21 for same-sex acts versus 18.
  - There is no recognition of same-sex unions and no anti-discrimination protection.
  - Police have used the penal code to extort bribes from young men perceived as gay.
  - Violence rose in 2022–2023, mostly within families.
  - Afrobarometer 2021–2023: 73% in Brazzaville would dislike a homosexual neighbour.
  - An NGO was refused registration in 2020 until it removed its LGBTQ+ focus.
  - Source: Wikipedia summary citing ILGA, the US State Department, Outright and Afrobarometer, read 2026-11-14 [25].
  - France Diplomatie and the FCDO pages read do not address it [4][13].
- **Treatment of foreigners and diaspora:** Afrobarometer (6 Oct 2026, AD1241) finds most Congolese have considered emigrating, but most oppose taking in more foreigners [24]. The FCDO notes criminals may target foreigners (Oct 2026) [13]. Reports on colourism or racism towards Afro-descendant returnees: **not found**.
- **Conflict zones:**
  - **Pool department:** a former Ninja insurgency, with a ceasefire since Dec 2017. Disarmament is incomplete, carjacking is higher, and you should travel in daylight convoys [2][13].
  - **Borders:** France Diplomatie classes as orange a 30 km strip along the Central African Republic border and a 10 km strip along the DRC and Cabinda borders; the whole country is yellow (map dated 5 Mar 2026) [2]. The FCDO advises against all travel within 50 km of the CAR border in Likouala (27 Oct 2026) [13].
- **Political:** Demonstrations sometimes turn violent [13]. Activists were arrested before the March 2026 election, and there was an internet blackout [18].
- **Women:** Afrobarometer Round 10 (2023 fieldwork; country page read 2026-11-14): 87% say police and courts must do more against discrimination and harassment of women and girls. A news release (26 Mar 2025) says citizens back equal access to jobs, but women face obstacles [24]. Street-level harassment reporting: **not found**.
