## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** English is the official and working language. Chichewa is the everyday lingua franca, especially in the Centre and South, and Chitumbuka is used in the North. Chiyao is spoken in the South [5]. **French is practically absent**: the nearest French consulate is in Johannesburg, with an honorary consular agency in Blantyre [3].
- **Currency:** Malawian kwacha (MWK). Reserve Bank of Malawi rates on 13 Nov 2026: USD ≈ 1,717–1,751 MWK, EUR ≈ 1,958–1,996 MWK. Policy rate 24% (Mar 2026), inflation 19.4% y/y (Sep 2026) [9]. Annual inflation was 28.8% in 2023 and 30.6% in 2024 [12]. Arrivals must declare cash, and holding more than US$1,000 needs Reserve Bank permission (FCDO, updated 20 Oct 2026) [1].
- **Chronic forex shortage:** NOCMA told Parliament the country earns less foreign currency than its imports need. An IMF report notes international banks have stopped confirming letters of credit for fuel imports (Nyasa Times, 5 Oct 2026) [15]. In practice that means fuel queues, import delays and difficult transfers abroad.
- **(a) French/EU passport holders:** for British passports, the FCDO (20 Oct 2026) says a visa is required: on arrival (cash US$50 single entry, $150 six-month multiple, $250 one-year multiple) or an e-visa (evisa.gov.mw) [1]. For French passports, France Diplomatie (updated 15 Sept 2026) says a visa has been required since 3 Jan 2026: e-visa at evisa.gov.mw (photo, passport, host's invitation letter, card payment) or, since Feb 2026, a visa on arrival at the airport with the same documents. Same fees as above (as of 25 Feb 2026). A visa covers up to 3 months: 30 days at first, extended to 3 months by reporting to immigration within those 30 days [26]. To live there, the Department of Immigration lists a Temporary Employment Permit, Business Residence Permit, Temporary Residence Permit, Student Permit and Permanent Residence Permit, with online applications at epermit.gov.mw. Requirements and fees were not visible on the home page [10]. One Expat.com thread is titled "Malawi Business Visit Permit caps at 90 days" [5]. Business registration and permits also go through the One Stop Service Centre at the Malawi Investment and Trade Centre (MITC) [21].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:** dual nationality has been **allowed since the 2019 amendment to the Citizenship Act**, provided the authorities are notified [11]. The immigration site has a "Dual Citizenship" form and a "Renunciation and Restoration of Citizenship" heading [10]. A child born abroad to a parent born in Malawi is Malawian, **but that does not pass to the next generation born abroad**. Grandchildren must go through discretionary registration [11]. **No right-of-return scheme or diaspora visa was found** (unlike Ghana or Sierra Leone).
- **Political context:** Peter Mutharika (DPP) won the presidential election on 16 Sept 2025 with 56.8%. Lazarus Chakwera conceded on 24 Sept 2025, and Mutharika was sworn in on 4 Oct 2025. Before the vote there were incidents, including a June 2025 rally attacked by men armed with machetes. The new president promised a "tough and painful" recovery amid inflation and fuel and food shortages [13].

## Cities

### Lilongwe

Political capital, about 989,000 inhabitants in 2018, growing about 3.9–4.3% a year (the source gives both figures) [6]. Most expats live here [5].

- **Cost of living:** *1-bed rent by neighbourhood: not found* (Numbeo returned an error). Expat.com estimates about **US$700/month for one person** (≈ 1.21 M MWK ≈ €615) and US$2,000 for a family of four, undated [5]. Neighbourhoods are numbered "Areas". In the city's 2030 land-use plan, Area 10 is earmarked partly for low-density housing and Areas 43 and 47 for medium/high-density housing (a plan, not a description of today's rents or demand), and Old Town is the commercial centre with markets [6]. Bishop Mackenzie school is in Area 3 [20]. Prices for a meal: not found. Inflation runs at 19.4% (Sep 2026) [9], so any budget goes out of date quickly.
- **Work & money:** the economy is dominated by government and public institutions. The civil service holds about 27% of formal jobs, alongside embassies and international organisations [6]. Kanengo is the industrial area (food processing, tobacco and maize storage) [6]. Salaries: not found. Remote work depends on a generator or solar setup and a backup connection (see power cuts).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** **mHub**, presented as "Malawi's first technology hub", is in Lilongwe. It runs an incubator and accelerator (launched 2018 with GrowthAfrica and UNDP Malawi), the "Jobs for Youth" programme and K20 M challenges with Standard Bank [16][17]. Draper Startup House opened in Malawi in 2022 (old source) [17]. E-health startup Ocliya won Seedstars Malawi in 2019 (old source) [17]. Crypto: Yellow Card ran an awareness campaign, and a 2022 article links Bitcoin interest to the falling kwacha and cheaper remittances [18]. The **central bank's position was not found**. Few investors: almost all coverage dates from 2018–2023.
- **Daily infrastructure:** structural **load shedding**. In July 2025, ESCOM reported a deficit of up to 40 MW against 190 MW of demand, with cuts in blocks of 3.5 hours and peak hours at 5:30–7:30 and 16:30–20:30. A 50 MW supply from Mozambique (cut from 120 MW) was promised for end-2025 [14]; whether it arrived was not verified in this pass. Only 15.6% of the population has electricity (World Bank 2023) [12]. **Fuel**: recurring shortages, still severe in Oct 2026 [1][15][19]. Transport: on 31 Oct 2026, Sky Energy launched 100 electric taxis in Lilongwe with its **Sky E-Ride** app (bookings from 1 Dec 2026) [19b]. Presence of Uber, Bolt or Yango: not found. Mobile money (Airtel Money, TNM Mpamba): widely cited but not verified in a source opened in this session. Kamuzu International Airport is 7 km away, with daily buses to Blantyre, Mzuzu, Lusaka, Johannesburg and Dar es Salaam [6].
- **Health & schools:** France Diplomatie lists doctors (Dr Arturo Muyco), clinics and pharmacies in Lilongwe (15 Sept 2026) [3]. **Bishop Mackenzie International School**: IB PYP/MYP/DP in English, founded 1944, Area 3 [20]. A French school was not found. The FCDO says facilities are basic outside the main areas, with payment upfront and full insurance essential [1].
- **Safety:** FCDO (20 Oct 2026): **"increase in break-ins in Lilongwe, Blantyre and Limbe, including violent assaults on residents"**. The FCDO reports occasional kidnappings for ransom, robberies of people who ate drugged food offered by strangers, and a methanol-in-alcohol risk [1]. France Diplomatie (15 Sept 2026): the whole country is "vigilance renforcée" (yellow). It names Lilongwe and Blantyre for night-time theft, car-jacking and assault, advises against walking alone in working-class neighbourhoods, and says to stay away from demonstrations; thefts concentrate in bus stations and markets [2]. Protests in central Lilongwe on 4–5 July (year to be confirmed) turned violent (unverified: seen only in search summaries) [1-search].
- **Social life & vibe:** a city spread out among trees, with a "village-like" administrative feel. Old Town has the markets, cafés and restaurants [6]. Detailed sources on nightlife and music: not found. Society is socially conservative and religious: the churches organised national demonstrations in 2017 [8].
- **Diaspora & expat community:** the expat population is estimated at 15,000–20,000, mostly South Africans, British, Indians, Chinese and Americans (Expat.com, undated) [5]. The active Expat.com Malawi forum has 2026 threads, including "Returning Home after leaving Malawi as a teenager" [5], a source of community, not of facts. Named diaspora associations or WhatsApp groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice:** rains December to March (~870 mm/year), a mild dry winter April to August (highs ~22 °C in July, lows ~9 °C), then hot September to November (~29.5 °C in October) [6]. Cholera rises in the rainy season [1].
- **Watch out:** power cuts plus fuel queues plus forex shortage form one interlocking daily problem [14][15]. Inflation is still high. Residential break-ins are rising [1]. Very little French spoken. Same-sex relations are criminalised [1][8].

### Blantyre (with Limbe)

Commercial and financial capital, about 800,000 inhabitants (2018) [7]. Expat.com calls it "more Malawian" than Lilongwe [5].

- **Cost of living:** *rents not found*. Residential neighbourhoods named: **Sunnyside, Mandala, Namiwawa, Mount Pleasant**, plus Kabula and Limbe (merged in 1956) [7]. An Expat.com forum thread lists "Websites with Homes for rent in Blantyre" [5]. National budget estimate: US$700/month for one person (undated) [5].
- **Work & money:** manufacturing (8 industrial zones), services, the **Malawi Stock Exchange**, Carlsberg, David Whitehead (textiles), Cold Storage, and the international trade fair in May [7]. Universities: MUBAS (former Polytechnic), Kamuzu University of Health Sciences, and a MUST city campus (2024) [7]. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** a Blantyre-specific hub was not found. MUBAS and KUHeS are potential talent pools [7].
- **Daily infrastructure:** the same national load shedding [14]. In 2026 the mayor of Blantyre spoke publicly about the fuel shortage ("People are suffering") [19]. Chileka airport is 14 km away, with the Sena railway to the Mozambican ports and coaches from Wenela [7].
- **Health & schools:** **Queen Elizabeth Central Hospital** is the country's largest referral hospital (~1,000 beds). Private options are Blantyre Adventist Hospital and Mwaiwathu Private Hospital. Beit CURE does orthopaedics [7]. France Diplomatie lists Dr Christian Jaggi and pharmacies in Blantyre and Limbe [3]. School: **St Andrew's International** (primary and high school) [7]. The **French honorary consular agency is in Blantyre** [3].
- **Safety:** FCDO (Oct 2026) names Blantyre and Limbe among the cities with rising break-ins [1]. France Diplomatie (Sept 2026) gives the same night-time advice [2].
- **Social life & vibe:** a historic colonial city (Mandala House 1882, Chichiri Museum, Carlsberg brewery) [7]. Hillier and more commercial than Lilongwe. Detailed nightlife and music: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** smaller than Lilongwe's [5]. Groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice:** tropical savanna, rains November to April (~1,120 mm in the article text; the 1961–1990 Chileka table gives ~900 mm) [7], hotter than Lilongwe. The FCDO (20 Oct 2026) says cyclones and floods are common November to April across Malawi, often closing roads with landslides [1]; the southern region is the most exposed (editorial reading, not separately sourced).
- **Watch out:** same structural problems as Lilongwe. Fewer international NGOs and embassies, so fewer jobs for foreigners. The Mulanje massif nearby: in 2025, British tourists were attacked in a hotel there, and the FCDO advises asking the Mountain Club of Malawi for advice before a climb [1].

### Mzuzu (extra city, why it matters)

Northern capital, 221,000 inhabitants (2018), a university town (Mzuzu University, Mzuzu Academy), with a cool highland climate (~24 °C max, ~1,290 mm of rain). It sits near Lake Malawi, in a region of tea, rubber, coffee and the Viphya plantation [22]. It suits someone wanting a quieter, cooler, Tumbuka-speaking life close to the lake. France Diplomatie lists Mzuzu among sites where thefts occur [2]. Rents and infrastructure: not found.

### Zomba (extra city, why it matters)

Former capital, 105,000 inhabitants (2018), home to Chancellor College (University of Malawi), with colonial architecture and the **Zomba Plateau** (~1,800 m, hiking) right at the edge of town. Humid subtropical, rains November to March [23]. A small academic city, about an hour from Blantyre (distance not verified). Theft flagged by France Diplomatie [2].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

*These are editorial readings drawn from the facts above. No city, and no quiz question, measures a personality trait.*

**Lilongwe**
- Openness: an international crowd of NGOs and embassies alongside a conservative society [5][6][8]. Suits curious people at ease in a small diplomatic expat world.
- Conscientiousness: power cuts, fuel and forex make you plan everything (generator, fuel, cash) [1][14][15]. Planners do well.
- Extraversion: a spread-out city with a calm pace. Little documented nightlife (not found). Suits introverts and family life.
- Agreeableness: a dense expat community and an informal network are central [5].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for scheduled outages, queues and inflation near 20% [9][14].

**Blantyre**
- Openness: "more Malawian", less expat-heavy [5]. Suits people who want to blend into local life.
- Conscientiousness: commerce, the stock exchange and industry [7] reward a business mindset.
- Extraversion: a busier commercial hub than Lilongwe (interpretation, low confidence).
- Agreeableness / Emotional stability: the same daily constraints, plus a climate that is hotter and more exposed to cyclones [1][7].

Academic sources: Afrobarometer Round 10 shows 76% of Malawians think the country is going "in the wrong direction" (related release 5 Nov 2026), 63% trust the courts "somewhat" or "a lot", and 58% put food insecurity in their top 3 priorities for government action (related release 4 Aug 2025) [24]. These are opinion survey figures about the country, not personality measures. Hofstede/GLOBE scores for Malawi: not verified (page not readable).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:** same-sex relations are **illegal** (Penal Code s.153: up to 14 years for men; s.137A: up to 5 years for women) [1][8]. On **28 June 2024**, the Constitutional Court rejected the challenge to sections 153, 154 and 156 and sent the question back to Parliament. HRW called it a "dark day" (HRW, 11 Jul 2024) [25]. The 2012 and 2015 moratoria on arrests are fragile: a Mzuzu High Court injunction in Feb 2016 let arrests resume pending review, and a man was arrested in Mzuzu in April 2018 and charged under s.156 [8]. Strong religious hostility: national church demonstrations in 2017 [8]. FCDO 2026: a socially conservative society where public displays of affection draw attention [1].
- **Racism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners:** no reliable source found on colourism or on how the African diaspora is received. Documented risk is criminal rather than discriminatory: break-ins with violent assaults on residents, car-jacking at night and occasional kidnappings for ransom (FCDO, 20 Oct 2026; France Diplomatie, 15 Sept 2026) [1][2].
- **Conflict zones:** none. No zone is red or orange; the whole country is yellow, mainly for road risk (France Diplomatie, 15 Sept 2026) [2]. Political and economic demonstrations recur [1][2]. Election tension in 2025 [13].
- **Women's daily experience:** no specific section in the FCDO advice [1]. The FCDO warns of robberies after drugged food or drink offered by strangers (20 Oct 2026) [1]. mHub runs digital programmes for reporting rape (Mtandire, undated) [16]. Reliable data: not found.
