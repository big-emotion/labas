> **Bottom line for the product.** Every Western government consulted (France, Belgium, UK, Switzerland) formally advises against **all** travel to Niger as of October–November 2026 [1][4][5][6]. This includes Niamey: Diori Hamani airport was attacked on 29 January and 18 June 2026, and there was gunfire at the airport and in the city centre on 29 August 2026 [4], and kidnap risk is rated high countrywide, including in the capital [1][4][5]. The French embassy is closed and some French nationals have reportedly been turned back on arrival [1][2][3; search snippet only]. The project rule is that `unsafe` cities are never proposed. **Recommendation: flag both Niamey and Agadez as `unsafe`**, so the quiz never suggests them while this advice stands.

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages in daily use**: French is the administrative language. Hausa is the street and market lingua franca in the south and centre (Maradi, Zinder, much of Niamey). Zarma-Songhai is spoken in Niamey and the west, Tamasheq in the Agadez/Aïr region, and Fulfulde (Peul) widely. Expat.com lists French, Hausa, Zarma, Tamasheq and Peul [12; guide last updated 2018-04-27, flagged old].
- **Currency**: West African CFA franc (XOF), pegged at 1 EUR = 655.957 FCFA. Cash is king. Niamey ATMs take international cards but often run out of cash [13; Belgian MFA, 2026-10-14]. In Maradi the Nigerian naira is also widely accepted because of trade with Kano [16].
- **Entry and residence for French/EU passport holders**: a visa is required before travel, with no visa on arrival at the airport. Single entry costs EUR 90 and multiple entry EUR 150, via the Niger embassy in Paris. Your passport must be valid for more than 6 months [2; France Diplomatie, updated 2026-11-10]. The French page does not cover residence or work permits. Belgium notes that a work permit or work visa is mandatory [3; 2026-10-14]. A France Diplomatie search snippet mentions French nationals being turned back (*refoulés*) on arrival. The opened security page does not repeat this, so treat it as unconfirmed but plausible given the diplomatic break [1]. France has had no ambassador or military presence since end-2023. The embassy is "fermée jusqu'à nouvel ordre", with a consular section at boulevard de la Jeunesse, Niamey [1][17].
- **Diaspora with family roots**: Niger has allowed **dual nationality since 2014** (Loi n° 2014-60). A child born anywhere to at least one Nigerien parent is Nigerien at birth, and mothers have passed nationality on equal terms since 1999. A foreign spouse can naturalise after 3 years of residence; other foreigners need about 10 years [7; Wikipedia, undated revision, legal text 2014]. **Diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme: not found.** We found nothing like Ghana's or Benin's Afro-descendant programmes.
- **Political context**: a military junta has ruled since the July 2023 coup. Niger left ECOWAS in January 2024 and formed the Alliance des États du Sahel (AES) with Mali and Burkina Faso. ECOWAS sanctions in 2023 caused shortages and price rises [18]. Soldiers attempted a coup near the Presidential Palace in **August 2026** [8][5]. The Benin border is still closed, with a roadmap to reopen it agreed in June 2026 [14; World Bank, 2026].
- **Economy**: the World Bank estimates 2026 growth at 7.0%, driven by oil, agriculture and mining. Poverty is about 47.7%, and more than 1 million people were displaced as of 2026-08-31 [14].

## Cities

### Niamey

- **Cost of living**: Numbeo was unreachable and no recent rent listings were found. **Rent range: not found.** Expat.com's dated 2018 estimate is about EUR 800/month for a single person and EUR 2,200 for a family of four [12; flagged old]. **Regulated social-housing cap**: a decree published on 22 April caps rent for F3 social housing in Niamey at 80,000 FCFA/month (about EUR 122); it covers F2/F3 social housing only, not the open market, and enforcement is unclear [23; Le360 Afrique, 2026-06-03]. Neighbourhoods: Commune I (Plateau side) is the wealthiest area and home to most embassy staff. Koira Tégui and Talladjé are poorer, and Terminus is the old colonial core. The right bank (Commune V: Karadjé, Banga Bana) hosts the university [8]. Belgium tells residents to avoid the right bank, and notes that petty crime is common in the northern districts [4; 2026-10-14].
- **Work & money**: the economy is dominated by informal commerce [8]. Formal jobs are mostly the state, NGOs and UN agencies (more than 1M displaced people [14]), oil, mining and telecoms. **Salaries: not found.** Remote work is technically possible but fragile: mobile internet is "sometimes unstable" [13], and only 23.2% of the population used the internet in January 2025 [15].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: CIPMEN calls itself the "1er incubateur de PME au Niger". It runs pre-incubation, incubation, acceleration and coworking, plus the SAHEL-INNOV forum, the E-takara competition and the CODE LOCCOL coding academy, with activity listed in Niamey, Zinder, Agadez, Maradi and other cities [11]. Its website shows no dated news after 2021, so current activity is unverified. A local operator (NINETEC) launched a Starlink distribution campaign in Niamey on 2025-04-30 [9]. Airtel Niger launched an AI anti-scam tool on 2025-08-01 [9]. **Investor presence, crypto adoption and company-registration timelines: not found.**
- **Daily infrastructure**: there were 16.5M mobile connections (60.1% of the population) in early 2025 [15]. The power network is fragile. Nigeria cut its electricity supply after the 2023 coup [18], and **current load-shedding data was not found**. There is a rail link to Dosso, but the Benin extension failed and disassembly began in 2024 [8]. **Ride-hailing apps: not found.**
- **Health & schools**: the reference hospitals are Hôpital National de Niamey (the largest), Hôpital National de Lamordé and Maternité Issaka Gazoby [8]. France Diplomatie names the private clinics Gamkalley and Kaba, calls medical infrastructure "limitées", and strongly advises repatriation to France for any surgery. Meningitis epidemics (including W135) occur from February to July, so vaccination is strongly advised [10; 2026-11-10]. Abdou Moumouni University sits on the right bank [8]; its 2022 enrolment figure (29,605) was not found on re-check (unverified). **French school: suspended.** The AEFE-network Lycée La Fontaine in Niamey was put *en veille* (activity suspended) from the 2024–2025 school year; the French government told families to use an approved school outside Niger, a French-curriculum school in Niamey, or CNED distance learning [24; Sénat, question 2024-04-04, answer 2024-05-30]. No source found on a reopening as of 2026-11-14.
- **Safety**: all travel is formally advised against by France (whole country red, updated 2026-11-10) [1], the UK (updated 2026-10-23) [5] and Belgium (2026-10-14) [4]. Diori Hamani airport in Niamey was attacked on 29 January and **18 June 2026**, and gunfire was exchanged in the city centre and at the airport on 29 August 2026; Tahoua airport (not Niamey) was attacked on 9 March 2026 [4; 2026-10-14]. The UK also reports attacks near the Presidential Palace [5]. A Western national was kidnapped in central Niamey in October 2025 [4]. The FCDO reports a rise in kidnappings of foreigners in 2026 [5]. France advises avoiding public events and unsecured or out-of-the-way hotels, bars, restaurants and nightclubs in Niamey, and keeping travel times discreet [1; 2026-11-10]. Belgium says that if Belgians must reside in Niger, they should reside **only** in Niamey [4]. The UK offers consular help only remotely from Lagos and warns: "Do not rely on the UK government to evacuate you" [5].
- **Social life & vibe**: a tea culture of *fadas* (young men's tea circles) and Hausa *hira* street gatherings. Nigerien hip-hop and the Tal National orchestra, the Musée National Boubou Hama, Grand Marché and Petit Marché. The national traditional wrestling championship (*lutte traditionnelle*) and Stade Général Seyni Kountché [8]. Islam dominates daily life, with active Sufi orders and the reformist Izala movement. There is a Catholic cathedral [8]. Alcohol is legal [3], but France's advice to avoid bars and nightclubs makes going out risky [1]. (Editorial impression, unsourced: daily life is described as family-centred.)
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com's Niger forum has about 2,536 members and 58 discussions [12; 2018 snapshot]. The expat mix was historically French, Chinese, Nigerian, Malian and Lebanese [12]. **Facebook/WhatsApp groups, returnee networks and associations: not found.**
- **Climate in practice**: Niamey is very hot. Mean daily highs run about 32°C in January and 41–42°C in April, with records above 45°C. Rain falls June to early September (about 554 mm) and is almost absent October to April [8]. Rains cause flooding from June to October [1]. November–February is the comfortable season, with night lows of 14–18°C [8]. March–May is brutal, and the heat combines with power cuts.
- **Watch out**: real attacks inside the capital in 2026, kidnap risk for Westerners and possibly diaspora members with foreign passports, a closed French embassy and possible refusal of entry for French nationals, an August 2026 coup attempt, fuel prices rising (ActuNiger, 2026-10-18) [9], cash shortages at ATMs [13], and limited healthcare.

### Agadez

- **Cost of living**: not found.
- **Work & money**: a market and transport town for nearby uranium mining. It is known for its camel market, silverwork, leatherwork and the Agadez cross [19]. Tourism, once larger than Niamey's, "essentially came to an end" after the 2007 violence [19]. Migrant transit towards Libya has been a major part of the local economy despite a 2016 crackdown [19].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: CIPMEN lists regional activity in Agadez [11; no date]. Nothing else found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: not found specifically. Outside Niamey, France says mines, IEDs and frequent attacks rule out road travel [1].
- **Health & schools**: France says medical evacuation capacity outside Niamey is very limited [10]. Nothing else found.
- **Safety**: the whole country is red [1]. Belgium advises living only in Niamey, which implicitly excludes Agadez [4]. Kidnap risk is high countrywide [1][5]. The former US drone base (Air Base 201) was here [19]. **Unsuitable for relocation.**
- **Social life & vibe**: a Tuareg cultural heartland with UNESCO-listed old-town architecture. The Cure Salée nomad festival takes place nearby in In-Gall in the last two weeks of September [20]. Recent editions were not verified.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: hot desert, up to 49.1°C, about 111.5 mm of rain mostly in July and August, and about 3,500 sunshine hours a year [19].
- **Watch out**: isolation, road travel to Niamey judged too dangerous [1], an economy built on mining and migration, and almost no consular reach.

### Maradi (additional — the commercial hub of the Hausa south)

- It is the second-largest city: 267,249 people in 2012, with about 270,000 estimated now. It is the main market and transport hub of south-central Niger, oriented towards Kano in Nigeria. CFA francs and naira both circulate [16]. It would matter for traders and for diaspora with Hausa roots.
- **Safety**: France reports kidnappings for ransom by criminal groups coming from Nigeria in southern Niger [1]. The whole country is red [1].
- Rent, internet and schools: not found.

### Zinder (additional — historic capital, refinery town)

- Third-largest city (235,605 in 2012). It was the former capital of the Sultanate of Damagaram, and the old town of Birni holds the Sultan's palace and the Great Mosque [21]. The SORAZ oil refinery is about 50 km north [21]. ActuNiger reported a large drug seizure there on 2026-11-12 [9].
- Safety: the whole country is red [1]. Other details: not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This section is interpretive. It describes who might feel at ease in each city's conditions. Neither the cities nor any quiz question measure personality. **Hofstede and GLOBE scores for Niger were not found.** We also found no Afrobarometer Niger round newer than the coup.

**Niamey**
- Openness: life is shaped by Islam, conservative dress expectations ("cover arms and legs") and limited nightlife [3][8]. It suits people curious about Sahelian and Hausa/Zarma culture, tea-circle sociability and wrestling, more than people seeking cosmopolitan novelty.
- Conscientiousness: cash shortages, fragile power, visa-first entry and a work-permit requirement [2][3][13] reward people who plan ahead and keep backups.
- Extraversion: social life happens in *fadas*, *hira* street gatherings and family circles [8]. Official advice also says to avoid bars, nightclubs and gatherings [1], so outgoing people who need a public night scene will feel constrained.
- Agreeableness: respect for religious and social codes, and caution with photography (never photograph soldiers or police) [3], is a daily necessity.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): the conditions include attacks on the airport and near the palace, kidnap risk, a coup attempt in 2026 [4][5][8] and 40°C+ heat in spring [8]. Only someone with very high stress tolerance, an employer's security framework and an exit plan should consider it.

**Agadez**: the same conditions in a more extreme form: isolation, desert heat [19] and roads deemed too dangerous [1]. Today this is not an environment for a voluntary move.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: criminalised. In March 2025 the junta's *Charte de la Refondation* (art. 25) banned same-sex relations. The new penal code (enacted 2026-02-16, in force 2026-06-11) sets **5–10 years** in prison for same-sex acts or public displays, **10–20 years** for organisations, venues or same-sex marriage (including officiants and witnesses), fines of 10M–100M FCFA for acts and 50M–500M FCFA for organisations, and no suspended sentences or mitigating circumstances [22; Wikipedia, citing June 2026 press]. The Belgian MFA confirms 5–10 and 10–20 years and says homosexuality is "not socially tolerated" [3; 2026-10-14].
- **Treatment of foreigners and diaspora**: the junta expelled France's troops and ambassador in 2023 [18]. France reports high kidnap risk for foreigners, especially Westerners [1]. The UK says the US embassy has warned Americans and Britons of kidnap risk everywhere, including Niamey [5]. **Specific reports on how diaspora members with French or Western passports are treated: not found.** Assume a foreign passport makes you a potential target. **Racism and colourism reports: not found.**
- **Conflict**: jihadist insurgency, mines and IEDs on roads outside Niamey [1]. Attacks on Niamey airport on 29 January and 18 June 2026, on Tahoua airport on 9 March 2026, and gunfire at Niamey airport and city centre on 29 August 2026 [4; 2026-10-14]. Attempted coup or mutiny on 29 August 2026 [8]. Pipeline sabotage by armed groups in Dosso [14]. Banditry and kidnapping for ransom in the south from Nigeria [1]. More than 1M displaced people [14].
- **Women's daily experience**: Belgium advises modest dress [3]. Since 2014 women can pass nationality to a spouse, and since 1999 to their children [7]. **Recent dated reporting on women's daily safety and freedom of movement: not found.**
