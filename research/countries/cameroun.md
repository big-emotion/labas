## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: French dominates in Douala, Yaoundé and Kribi; English in the North-West
  and South-West (Buea, Limbe, Bamenda). In Yaoundé, people also speak "Ewondo Populaire", a Beti-based
  pidgin [12]. Camfranglais and Cameroonian Pidgin English are widely heard in the street. (Editorial
  observation, no source opened in this pass.)
- **Currency**: CFA franc BEAC (XAF), pegged to the euro. France Diplomatie gives 1,000,000 FCFA ≈
  €1,524.49, so **€1 ≈ 656 FCFA** [3]. Amounts over 1M FCFA must be declared when you enter or leave [3].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**: you need a visa, applied for online (e-visa platform evisacam.cm).
  There is **no visa on arrival** at Yaoundé or Douala airports, and you cannot use the e-visa at land
  or sea borders [3][4]. Stays over 30 days or for work need a temporary visa (up to 6 months) or a
  long-stay visa (over 6 months), arranged through the Cameroonian consulates in Paris or Marseille [3].
  You must show a **yellow fever vaccination** certificate [3][4]. Residence permit (carte de séjour)
  procedure: not found. France Diplomatie says to ask the consulate [3].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**: Cameroon **does not recognise dual nationality**. The 1968
  Nationality Code (Art. 31) says an adult Cameroonian who acquires or voluntarily keeps another
  nationality loses Cameroonian nationality [23, law of 11 June 1968]. A 2020 bill was never
  passed. In June 2024 the question was raised again in Parliament: an MP said the rule is "applied
  arbitrarily", and the minister did not answer that point [7, 26 June 2024]. A 2025 law-firm note says
  the position "has evolved, but significant legal uncertainties remain". It says some dual nationality
  is tolerated (for example, a nationality acquired automatically rather than sought), and that getting
  citizenship back is "possible in some circumstances" but complex [8, 30 May 2025]. Diaspora members
  describe the online passport process as "smoother now" but resent not being "full citizens" [9,
  c. Jan 2024]. **There is no diaspora visa, right-of-return or Afro-descendant scheme comparable to
  Ghana, Sierra Leone or Benin: not found.** In practice, a French citizen of Cameroonian origin enters
  as a foreigner, on an e-visa.

## Cities

### Douala

The economic capital: about 3.8M people in the city proper (2025 est.) and the country's main port
and airport [11].

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bed rent: Numbeo (12 contributors, updated 6 Nov 2026) gives about **337,000 FCFA (≈ €514)** in
    the centre and **187,000 FCFA (≈ €285)** outside [5]. A 2026 local guide gives **100,000–200,000
    FCFA (≈ €150–305)** for a 2-room flat in Bonanjo, Bonapriso or Bonamoussadi, and 150,000–350,000
    FCFA for 3 rooms. Villas run 300,000–1,000,000 FCFA [1, May 2026].
  - Intermediate areas (Akwa, Deido, Bépanda): 60,000–100,000 FCFA for 2 rooms. Working-class areas
    (Bonabéri, New Bell, Nylon, PK): 30,000–50,000 FCFA [1].
  - Expat.com listings (undated): Bonapriso 2-bed at 500,000 FCFA; Bonamoussadi 2-room at
    250,000 FCFA [search results, see 6].
  - Upfront costs: deposits of **2–6 months' rent**, sometimes non-refundable, plus 1 month's agency
    fee [1]. Older forum advice (c. 2011, outdated) mentions 6–12 months paid in advance [6].
  - A meal in a cheap restaurant costs about **2,500 FCFA (≈ €3.80)** [5]. Basic utilities run about
    95,000 FCFA a month [5].
  - Monthly budget for one person: not published by Numbeo [5]. Editorial estimate: 450,000–700,000
    FCFA (≈ €690–1,070) for an expat-standard flat in Bonapriso or Bonamoussadi (?).
- **Work & money**:
  - Douala handles most major exports (oil, cocoa, coffee, timber) and its airport is the busiest in
    CEMAC [11]. The main sectors are port logistics, trade, industry, banking and telecoms.
  - About 30% of residents live in poverty [11].
  - Salary data: not found.
  - Remote work is feasible, but home broadband is expensive. Numbeo gives about **103,000 FCFA a
    month (≈ €158)** for 60 Mbps+ unlimited [5].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - **ActivSpaces**: a non-profit hub and incubator based in Douala (Immeuble TECNO, boulevard de la
    Liberté), with spaces in Buea and Bangangté; its AfriLabs profile says coworking is free for
    founders actively building a startup [24]. TechCabal (1 Jul 2025) names Douala and Yaoundé as
    attracting new accelerators (search summary; the article returned 403) [25].
  - Mobile money (MTN MoMo, Orange Money) is ubiquitous by editorial observation. No dated source was
    opened.
  - Crypto adoption, investor presence and company registration time: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Douala has a completed urban fibre loop (older data) [16].
  - Power cuts (Eneo délestages): widely reported, but no dated source was opened. Not found.
  - Bus rapid transit was planned for 2024–25, with status unconfirmed [11].
  - Heavy flooding is common in the rainy season, so visit a flat before renting [6].
  - The FCDO advises **not hailing yellow taxis in the street** and booking trusted taxis instead [4].
  - Ride-hailing apps available: not verified (Yango is reported locally, unconfirmed).
- **Health & schools**:
  - Reference hospitals and clinics: not found in this pass.
  - French schools: **Lycée français Dominique Savio** (AEFE network, conventionné), in Bonapriso,
    from maternelle to terminale [26]. It was still recruiting staff for the 2026 school year [26].
- **Safety**:
  - Douala is **not in a red or orange zone** for France [2, updated 5 Nov 2026].
  - The FCDO warns of armed robbery and mugging, and names **Nkololun, New Bell, Akwa, Bonabéri and
    Village** as areas to avoid when isolated or at night [4]. It also says terrorist attacks "could
    occur anywhere, including Douala" [4].
  - France advises against taxis after dark, against showing valuables, and in favour of a day and
    night guard for rented homes [2].
  - After the **12 Oct 2025 presidential election**, there were protests at the Elecam headquarters in
    Douala. Douala was among the cities hardest hit by the 3-day opposition lockdown from 3 Nov 2025 [13].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Waterfront bars and bistros [11]. Cultural venues include Doual'art, the Maritime Museum and the
    sculpture *La Nouvelle Liberté* [11].
  - Makossa music, the Ngondo festival (Sawa people), and dishes such as ndolé and grilled fish
    (soya, braisés) are editorial knowledge, unsourced in this pass.
  - The pace is commercial, loud and fast (editorial).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com has a Douala forum and real-estate section [6]. Named
  associations, Facebook or WhatsApp groups and returnee events: not found.
- **Climate in practice**:
  - Hot and humid all year: 27 °C average, 83% humidity, about 3,600 mm of rain a year. **August is the
    wettest month (≈ 700 mm) and December the driest** [11].
  - Expect flooded streets and traffic paralysis from July to September [6][11].
- **Watch out**:
  - Large upfront rent payments and fake "agents": use a registered agency and never pay in cash [6].
  - France says French nationals are "très fréquemment la cible d'arnaques en tous genres", including
    romance scams that start online [22, updated 2026-11-05].
  - Expensive internet [5], flooding [6], and post-election tension [13].

### Yaoundé

The political capital: civil service, embassies (Bastos), presidential palace (Etoudi). Population
3.76M (2025 infobox) [12].

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bed rent: Numbeo (26 contributors over 18 months, updated 2 Oct 2026, partly estimated) gives
    **82,800 FCFA (≈ €126)** in the centre and **60,000 FCFA (≈ €92)** outside [10]. This is
    **probably underestimated for expat-standard housing (?)**.
  - The 2026 local guide gives **150,000–400,000 FCFA (≈ €230–610)** for 3 rooms in **Bastos, Golf or
    Quartier du Lac**; 50,000–90,000 FCFA for 2 rooms in Mvog-Mbi, Essos or Nlongkak; and
    30,000–50,000 FCFA in Melen, Biyem-Assi or Emana [1]. Villas run 300,000–1.5M FCFA [1].
  - A meal costs about **2,000 FCFA (≈ €3)** [10]. Internet runs about 30,000 FCFA a month at the median,
    but with a wide range [10].
- **Work & money**: The economy is mostly civil service and the diplomatic corps, with tobacco, beer,
  dairy and timber industries [12]. NGOs and international organisations also matter (editorial).
  Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Not found in this pass (no hub verified).
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - The Yaoundé fibre loop was "under construction" in older data [16].
  - France flags the **Yaoundé–Douala road as dangerous** [2]. It takes about 3 hours by road; trains
    run to Douala and Ngaoundéré [12].
  - Power and water reliability: not found.
- **Health & schools**: The Central Hospital (650 beds) is the largest [12]. Universities include
  Yaoundé II, UPAC and UCAC [12]. French school: **Lycée Fustel de Coulanges** (AEFE conventionné),
  named in a France Alumni brochure (c. 2020) [26].
- **Safety**:
  - Yaoundé is not in a red or orange zone for France [2].
  - The FCDO names **Briqueterie and Mokolo market** as areas to avoid when isolated, says improvised
    explosive devices "have been seen in Yaoundé", and advises not hailing yellow taxis [4].
  - Yaoundé saw a partial shutdown during the Nov 2025 lockdown [13].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Churches predominate and mosques are also present [12]. Sights include the Municipal Lake and the
    Mvog-Betsi zoo [12].
  - The city is built on hills, with a slower, more administrative pace than Douala and a strong
    student population (editorial).
  - Bikutsi music (Beti) is editorial knowledge, unsourced.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Not found.
- **Climate in practice**: Wet from March to November, with a dip in July [12]. It is cooler than
  Douala thanks to the altitude (editorial; altitude not in the fetched text).
- **Watch out**: The city runs at the pace of the administration. For anyone who is not a civil
  servant, diplomat or NGO worker, the private job market is thinner than in Douala (editorial).

### Kribi

A beach town on the South coast, about 150 km south of Douala. It has Cameroon's first **deep-sea port**
(first container terminal opened March 2018; a second terminal from 2019 brings capacity above 1M TEU a
year; run by KCT, a CMA CGM / China Harbour / Bolloré (now AGL) consortium) and a 216 MW gas power station (2013) [14]. Population figures conflict: 55,000–81,000 [14].

- **Cost of living**: Rents run **20,000–100,000 FCFA a month (≈ €30–150)**, more in tourist areas [1,
  May 2026]. Neighbourhood breakdown: not found. Numbeo: no page found.
- **Work & money**: Port logistics, energy, tourism and fishing [14]. Few jobs outside these sectors.
  Remote work depends on mobile data (fibre not verified).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: A planned 130 km rail link to Edéa [14]. Road to Douala about 150 km [14].
  Other infrastructure: not found.
- **Health & schools**: Not found. Expect to go to Douala for serious care (editorial (?)).
- **Safety**: Not in a red or orange zone. The South region only has yellow-zone sites [2, Nov 2026].
- **Social life & vibe**: Beach life, the Lobé falls where the river meets the ocean [14], seafood.
  Quiet outside weekend and holiday peaks (editorial).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Not found.
- **Climate in practice**: Tropical monsoon, humid all year, about 2,870 mm of rain. **September is the
  wettest month (≈ 483 mm) and December the driest (59 mm)** [14].
- **Watch out**: A small town with limited healthcare and work, and dependence on Douala (editorial).

### Bafoussam (additional: West region hub, cooler highlands)

- The third city economically. The urban community has about 1.1M people (2023 est.) [15].
- Bamiléké commerce, coffee, potatoes, a brewery and a BEAC branch [15].
- Altitude about 1,500 m, with temperatures of **15–27 °C**. Dry season mid-November to mid-March [15].
  This matters for people who struggle with coastal humidity.
- Rents run 15,000–80,000 FCFA, from studio to 3 rooms [1].
- **Safety nuance**: France classifies **parts of the West region orange**: border areas with the
  North-West and South-West, the Lake Bamendji area and the D66 axis [2].
- Many diaspora members have family roots in the West region (editorial (?)).

### Limbe / Buea (additional: anglophone coast and "Silicon Mountain", currently not recommended)

- Limbe has black-sand beaches, botanical gardens, the SONARA refinery and the CDC head office [17].
- Buea hosts "Silicon Mountain", a tech community named in 2013 by Rebecca Enonchong, with the
  University of Buea and an annual conference of 500+ attendees [18]. Businesses there were hurt by the
  **2017 internet shutdowns** in the anglophone regions [18].
- Rents in Limbe: 20,000–100,000 FCFA [1].
- **Not proposed for Là-bas now**: the South-West region is **orange for France, with red zones**
  (Bakassi, Korup, west of Kumba and Mamfe) [2, Nov 2026], and the Anglophone conflict is ongoing [19].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No academic regional-personality study on Cameroonian cities was found in this pass. Hofstede, GLOBE
and Afrobarometer data for Cameroon were not verified. These are editorial readings of the cited facts,
not measurements. No quiz question measures a trait.

**Douala**
- Openness: suits people curious about a dense, mixed port city with a waterfront arts scene
  (Doual'art) [11].
- Conscientiousness: rewards people who plan, because of large upfront rent payments, agency checks
  and flood-season constraints [1][6].
- Extraversion: a commercial, noisy city with waterfront bars [6][11]. Energy suits outgoing people.
- Agreeableness: negotiation is normal, since prices are often negotiable by 10–20% [1].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for traffic, floods, scams [2][6] and episodic
  political tension (Nov 2025 lockdown) [13].

**Yaoundé**
- Openness: suits people curious about institutions, campuses and museums more than street buzz
  [12].
- Conscientiousness: suits people comfortable with institutions and procedures, since the economy
  depends on the civil service [12].
- Extraversion: quieter than Douala. Social life runs through networks, churches and campuses [12].
- Agreeableness: hierarchy and relationships matter in an administrative capital (editorial).
- Emotional stability: lower day-to-day stress than Douala (editorial). The FCDO still names crime
  hotspots [4].

**Kribi**
- Suits people who want calm and nature over a career (Openness to slow living, low Extraversion
  need) [14]. Emotional stability is needed to accept isolation from specialised care (editorial).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex acts are criminalised under **Art. 347-1 of the Penal Code (since 1972)**: up to 5 years'
    prison and/or a fine. Under the 2010 cybercrime law (Art. 83), online same-sex "propositions" carry
    1–2 years [20].
  - On 8 Feb 2021, two trans women were arrested in a Douala restaurant and later sentenced to 5 years.
    A judge ordered their release pending appeal [20].
  - The FCDO says "same-sex partners have been arrested and prosecuted" [4, accessed 2026-11-14].
  - On 30 June 2024 the president's daughter Brenda Biya came out publicly, which drew backlash [20].
    There has been no legal change.
- **Conflict zones**:
  - **Far North** (Boko Haram/ISWA, kidnappings) and the **whole North-West**: red for France [2, Nov
    2026]. **South-West**: orange, with red pockets [2].
  - The FCDO advises against all travel within 40 km of the Nigerian border (except Garoua), and near
    the CAR border and Bakassi [search summary of FCDO snapshots, Oct 2025] (unverified on the live page).
    France puts a **30 km strip along the Nigerian, CAR and Chad borders** in red, plus the Mayo-Louti
    department of the North region [2, updated 2026-11-05].
  - The **Anglophone conflict**, ongoing since Sept 2017: 6,000+ civilians killed (as of Jan 2023) and
    about 700,000 internally displaced [19].
  - Switzerland reported **at least 174 people kidnapped in the Far North from Jan–Aug 2025** [search
    summary of Swiss FDFA page].
- **Political tension**: After the **12 Oct 2025** election (Biya 53.66%, Tchiroma 35.19%), protests
  followed. Reported deaths were 16 (authorities), 48 (UN data) or 55 (opposition). More than 800 people
  were arrested (HRW: about 2,000 detained). Biya was inaugurated on 6 Nov 2025 [13].
- **Treatment of diaspora/foreigners**:
  - Scams target French nationals "very frequently" [22, updated 2026-11-05].
  - Diaspora members feel like second-class citizens because dual nationality is not recognised [9].
  - Racism/colourism reports specific to diaspora returnees: not found.
- **Women's daily experience**: Not found in a dated source. The FCDO page has no specific note [4].

   doucement ? (Douala vs Yaoundé)

   une ambassade ? (Douala vs Yaoundé)

6. Tu veux surtout faire carrière, ou surtout ralentir ? (Douala vs Kribi)
