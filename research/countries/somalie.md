## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Bottom line for Là-bas.** France Diplomatie (updated 2026-11-04) puts the whole centre and
  south, Mogadishu included, in the **red zone** ("formellement déconseillé"). Hargeisa and
  Berbera are **orange** ("déconseillé sauf raison impérative"). The UK FCDO (updated
  2026-06-04) advises against all travel to Somalia. Its only exceptions are three Somaliland
  regions (Awdal, Maroodi Jeex, which contains Hargeisa, and Sahil), and even there it advises
  against all but essential travel [1][2][3]. Under the product rule, **Mogadiscio should be
  flagged `unsafe` and never proposed.** Hargeisa needs an explicit warning wherever it appears.
- **Languages.** Somali everywhere. Arabic is the second language and English is widely used in
  Somaliland, especially in business and universities [7]. French is practically absent. No
  source was found showing any French-speaking community.
- **Currency.**
  - Somalia: the Somali shilling (SOS), about 26,000 per USD in May 2025 [11]. In practice the
    **US dollar is the main currency**, mostly paid through mobile money such as EVC Plus [11].
  - In April 2026 many Mogadishu shops stopped accepting worn shilling notes and moved to
    mobile payments [11].
  - Somaliland uses its own Somaliland shilling, which cannot be exchanged outside the
    territory [12]. In Hargeisa, ATMs pay out US dollars on international cards. Elsewhere,
    bring cash [2].
- **(a) French or EU passport.** A visa is required for all of Somalia, Somaliland included [4].
  - Mogadishu: apply online in advance through etas.gov.so. The FCDO warns of a reported data
    leak on that platform [4].
  - Hargeisa: buy a one-month single-entry visa on arrival. A federal eVisa is not valid for
    entry there [4].
  - You may need an invitation letter, usually from an employer [4].
  - Passport must be valid for 6 months after arrival [4].
  - Long-term residence or work permit procedures: not found.
- **(b) Diaspora with Somali roots.**
  - The 1962 Citizenship Law bans dual nationality. The 2012 Provisional Constitution allows
    it [10].
  - A 2016 bill to resolve the conflict had still not passed as of 2021, the latest date in the
    source [10]. Any reform in 2022–2026: not found.
  - Nationality passes mainly through the father [10]. Former nationals can apply to regain it
    after 3 years of residence [10].
  - Both Somalia and Somaliland treat British citizens of Somali descent as dual nationals,
    which limits consular help [1]. The same probably applies to French citizens, but this was
    not verified.
  - No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found.
- **Somaliland status.** Somaliland is a self-declared state in the north-west, and most
  countries do not recognise it. Israel became the first UN member state to recognise it, on
  2025-12-26. Somalia rejects its independence [7]. Its residents mostly travel on Somali
  passports [10].

## Cities

### Mogadiscio (Mogadishu)

**Status: red zone (France, 2026-11-04). The FCDO advises against all travel (2026-06-04). Not
suitable for the product.**

- **Cost of living.**
  - Aggregator model (livingcost.org, updated 2026-06-21): one person about USD 721/month
    (≈ EUR 620) with rent [13].
  - 1-bed flat (40 m²): USD 164 central (≈ EUR 140), USD 70 for the site's "cheap" option
    (≈ EUR 60; location not stated). Cheap lunch about USD 3 [13].
  - Low confidence. These are modelled figures, not expat-grade housing. Foreigners and many
    returnees live in guarded compounds or hotels near the airport zone, at much higher prices.
    No dated price for that was found.
  - Named districts: Hodan, Wadajir, Abdiaziz, Yaqshid, and the old quarters of Hamar Weyne and
    Shangani [6].
- **Work & money.**
  - The city is the seat of the federal government, central bank, Hormuud Telecom and most
    banks [6].
  - The building sector is booming: over 6,000 new buildings went up between 2020 and 2025, and
    property prices have risen sharply [6].
  - Aid and NGOs are major employers (inferred from the HRW context [5]; not quantified).
  - Salaries: not found. Remote work: technically possible thanks to 4G/5G, but limited by
    security.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech.**
  - Mobile money is everywhere: about 3 million Hormuud EVC Plus users, and over 67% of Somalis
    reportedly use mobile money as their only way to pay [8].
  - Hormuud received the first central-bank mobile money licence in February 2021 [8].
  - **iRise Hub** opened in 2017 in Hodan district as Mogadishu's first co-working and
    incubation hub, with early support from the World Bank Youth Innovation Fund and UNDP [20][21].
    Its current activity was not verified.
  - iRise Hub, SIMAD iLab and Hargeisa's HarHub signed a memorandum to form a national startup
    network at the BIC Somali Innovation Forum (page last updated 2026-08-03; event date not
    shown) [22].
  - Investors and company registration: not found (the SomInvest site did not resolve).
- **Daily infrastructure.**
  - Hormuud launched 5G in over 30 towns in 2024. 4G reached about 70% of the population in
    2021 [8].
  - Internet costs about USD 67/month (modelled) [13].
  - Aden Adde International Airport is served by Turkish Airlines and regional carriers (the
    source's airline list dates from January 2015) [6].
  - Power cuts, water and ride-hailing: not found.
  - Road travel is dangerous: checkpoints are run by various armed groups [2].
- **Health & schools.**
  - Universities: Somali National University (reopened in 2014), SIMAD, Mogadishu University,
    Benadir University and City University [6].
  - Reference hospitals: not found in the pages that could be fetched.
  - French or international schools: none found.
- **Safety.**
  - France (2026-11-04): attacks happen daily in the main towns of the south and centre,
    Mogadishu included, and kidnap risk is "extrêmement élevé" [3].
  - FCDO (2026-06-04) [2]:
    - rocket attacks on the airport in 2025;
    - a bombing of the presidential convoy in March 2025;
    - an attack on Lido Beach in August 2024 that killed more than fifty civilians and injured
      over 200;
    - clashes between security forces and the opposition on 2026-06-03.
  - HRW (2025): hundreds of civilians killed in 2024, and tens of thousands forcibly evicted in
    Mogadishu [5].
  - No neighbourhood can be described as safe.
- **Social life & vibe.**
  - Islam shapes daily life: alcohol is banned, and Ramadan rules apply in public [2].
  - Beaches (Lido, Gezira) are popular but have been attack targets [2][6].
  - Konis Stadium (renovated in 2013) and Banadir Stadium are the two main sports venues [6].
  - Nightlife, music scene and cafés: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community.** The large returnee flow is documented anecdotally, but no
  named association or group was found.
- **Climate in practice.** Semi-arid, about 27 °C on average, about 429 mm of rain a year,
  high humidity (about 79%). June–August is the coolest and wettest period [6].
- **Watch out.** Terrorism, kidnapping, armed checkpoints, very limited consular help (French
  interests are handled from Nairobi [3]), cash shortages, and the eVisa data leak [4].

### Hargeisa (Somaliland, self-declared and not recognised by most states)

**Status: orange zone (France, 2026-11-04). Outside Hargeisa and Berbera, Somaliland is red [3].**

- **Cost of living.**
  - Aggregator model (livingcost.org, updated 2026-06-21): one person about USD 682/month
    (≈ EUR 585) with rent [14].
  - 1-bed flat (40 m²): USD 199 central (≈ EUR 170), USD 131 for the site's "cheap" option
    (≈ EUR 110; location not stated). Lunch about USD 5 [14].
  - Low confidence. Named neighbourhoods: not found.
- **Work & money.**
  - The city is a hub for trade, livestock, construction, import and export, and remittance
    finance [9][12].
  - Diaspora remittances flow through Dahabshiil and other transfer firms. Analysts say up to
    half of the roughly USD 1 bn/year reaching Somalia goes to Somaliland [12].
  - Banks: Dahabshiil Bank (first commercial bank, 2014) and Premier Bank (2017) [12].
  - Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech.**
  - Telesom runs the **ZAAD** mobile money platform, plus mobile and fixed broadband [15].
  - Many universities: Hargeisa, Gollis, Admas, Frantz Fanon, New Generation and others [9].
  - **HarHub** is named as Hargeisa's member of a national startup network with Mogadishu's
    iRise Hub and SIMAD iLab [22]. Its programme and address were not verified.
- **Daily infrastructure.**
  - ATMs pay out USD on international cards [2]. Internet costs about USD 23/month
    (modelled) [14].
  - Since August 2024, Ethiopian Airlines and flydubai have listed Hargeisa as being in
    Somalia [9].
  - Local flooding can cut roads [2].
  - Power cuts, water and ride-hailing: not found.
- **Health & schools.**
  - Edna Adan Maternity Hospital is known regionally and runs nursing training [9].
  - Other reference hospitals and international schools: not found.
- **Safety.**
  - France (2026-11-04): Somaliland is "précaire", though no terrorist attack has been recorded
    for twelve years. Visits to Hargeisa require escorts, short stays and secure housing [3].
  - FCDO (2026-06-04): attacks are less frequent than in the south but still "very likely", and
    kidnap risk remains [2].
  - Somaliland lost control of part of its east after the Las Anod conflict that began in
    2022 [7].
- **Social life & vibe.**
  - Conservative Muslim society, and alcohol is banned [2].
  - Cultural life: the Hargeysa International Book Fair (its site was unreachable, so dates and
    details were not verified).
  - Nightlife and music: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community.** Strong ties with the diaspora in the UK, Nordic countries and
  the Gulf are implied by remittance flows [12]. Named groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice.** Mild for the region thanks to altitude: average 18 °C in
  December–January and 24 °C in June. About 412 mm of rain a year, mostly April–September, with
  a peak in August [9].
- **Watch out.**
  - Contested political status: Somali passports, an unrecognised currency, and airline
    listings changed for political reasons [9][10][12].
  - Consular cover is handled from Nairobi (France) or Addis Ababa (UK) [1][3].
  - Restrictions on press freedom [5].
  - Effectively no French spoken.

### Berbera (extra city: Somaliland port, the only other orange-zone town)

- Port city of about 242,000 (2019). DP World runs the port (DP World 51%, Somaliland 30%,
  Ethiopia 19%). It is the gateway of the Berbera Corridor to Ethiopia, and an Ethiopia access
  deal was signed on 2024-01-01 [16].
- Beaches (Batalale) and reefs [16]. The heat is extreme: highs above 40 °C from June to
  September, with high humidity [16].
- Relevant only for logistics and port jobs. Same orange-zone status as Hargeisa [3].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These describe who might cope with each city's documented conditions. They do not claim that the
city or any quiz question measures a trait. No Hofstede, GLOBE or Afrobarometer data for Somalia
could be fetched in this run (status not verified).

**Mogadiscio**
- Openness: rewards people at ease with a very conservative religious frame. Alcohol is banned
  and Ramadan rules apply in public [2]. Cultural life is barely documented.
- Conscientiousness: daily life runs on security routines (convoys, curfews, compounds) [2][3].
- Extraversion: public gathering places such as beaches and hotels are exactly where attacks
  have happened [2].
- Agreeableness: clan and family networks shape access to housing and work (general context,
  not sourced here).
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): extreme. Daily attack risk and very high kidnap
  risk [3].

**Hargeisa**
- Openness: conservative codes and little nightlife [2]. The book fair and universities give an
  intellectual outlet [9].
- Conscientiousness: cash plus mobile money, and paperwork shaped by the unrecognised
  status [12].
- Extraversion: mostly family and social life rather than a bar scene [2].
- Agreeableness: close-knit, remittance-linked diaspora ties [12].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): high. The context is calmer than the south but still
  orange zone, with escorts recommended [3].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT.**
  - Federal penal code art. 409: 3 months to 3 years in prison [17].
  - Death penalty under Sharia in al-Shabaab-held areas and Jubaland [17].
  - The EUAA (May 2025) records reported al-Shabaab executions for same-sex relations in early
    2017 and November 2018 [23]. Wikipedia's claim of two executions "in 2024" cites a 2017
    article, so that date is unverified and is not repeated here.
  - Somaliland: 1964 penal code, with reported penalties up to death and reports of mob
    violence [18].
  - HRW 2025: consensual same-sex conduct is criminalised [5]. FCDO 2026: illegal, and no public
    affection [2].
- **Religion.** FCDO (2026-06-04): for Somali Muslims, converting or promoting another religion is illegal. Alcohol
  is banned [2].
- **Women.**
  - UNICEF figure cited by HRW (2025): 99% of women aged 15–49 have undergone FGM [5].
  - Conflict-related sexual violence mostly hits displaced women [5].
  - Somalia has not ratified CEDAW or the Maputo Protocol [5].
  - Conservative dress norms, with the hijab widespread since 1991 [19].
- **Treatment of diaspora and foreigners.**
  - FCDO (2026-06-04): both Somalia and Somaliland treat British nationals of Somali descent as
    dual nationals, which limits consular help [1].
  - The FCDO (2026-06-04) says terrorists see British nationals, including tourists, aid workers,
    journalists and business travellers, as legitimate kidnap targets [2].
  - No reliable source on racism or colourism was found.
- **Conflict.**
  - Al-Shabaab controls large parts of the centre and south [3].
  - Piracy is active offshore, and Puntland has a local Islamic State branch [3].
  - Las Anod conflict since 2022 in eastern Somaliland [7].
  - About 350,000 people were displaced between January and October 2024 [5].
- **Press.** A journalist was killed in March 2024, and journalists were arrested in
  Somaliland [5].
