# Sénégal

> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

Conversion used throughout: 1 EUR = 655.957 XOF (fixed CFA franc peg). Figures from commercial blogs (OpenShores, Cyril Jarnias, Kolonell) are flagged as such: they are indicative, not surveys.

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: French is the official and administrative language; Wolof is the street, market and taxi language in Dakar, Thiès, Saly/Mbour and Saint-Louis; Pulaar dominates the north (Fouta, around Saint-Louis/Podor), Diola and Mandinka in Casamance. Daily life without any Wolof works in offices and expat areas but is harder in markets and with landlords. (General knowledge, consistent with all French-language sources below; no single dated source opened.)
- **Currency**: CFA franc (XOF), pegged to the euro, issued by the BCEAO. The peg means Senegal's 2024–2026 debt crisis has not produced a currency collapse or runaway inflation [41].
- **Entry and residence — French passport holders** (the source covers French nationals; other EU passports: check with the Senegalese consulate):
  - Visa-free for stays under 3 months; passport valid more than 6 months. For more than 3 months a *carte de résident* is mandatory, requested in Senegal at the préfecture of residence / Direction de la Police des étrangers et des titres de voyage (France Diplomatie, updated 2026-09-29) [8].
  - A French driving licence is valid for one year after settling, then must be exchanged [8].
  - **Watch**: PM Sonko announced on 2024-12-27 a "visa reciprocity" policy that could target France, the US and all European countries. As of the latest source found (Ndarinfo, 2024-12-28) no official decision had been taken [14], and France Diplomatie still lists visa exemption in late 2026 [8]. Sonko was dismissed as PM on 2026-05-22 [40], so the policy's future is uncertain. Not found: any 2025–2026 decree implementing it.
- **Entry and residence — diaspora with Senegalese roots**:
  - People with a Senegalese parent can claim Senegalese nationality; Senegalese abroad ("Sénégalais de l'extérieur") have dedicated deputies in the National Assembly (15 seats per one source (?)) [17] and a Secretary of State (Amadou Chérif Diouf announced new diaspora measures in March 2026) [19].
  - **Dual nationality is legally murky**: commentators cite the 1961 Nationality Code (loi 61-70) and an Article 18 under which an adult who voluntarily takes another nationality may lose Senegalese nationality; in practice many dual nationals exist and the point is debated, mainly around presidential candidacy (which requires exclusive Senegalese nationality) [16]. Low confidence: verify with the Senegalese consulate before relying on it.
  - No right-of-return or Afro-descendant citizenship scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found for non-Senegalese diaspora. Not found.
  - **Returnee finance**: FAISE (Fonds d'appui à l'investissement des Sénégalais de l'extérieur) lends to diaspora entrepreneurs; it granted 750 million FCFA to 150 diaspora members (AllAfrica, 2025-12-16) [18].
- **Macro context to know before moving**: hidden-debt scandal (debt revised to ~100% of GDP at end-2023), stalled then renegotiated IMF programme, tax increases and subsidy cuts that critics say push up fuel and electricity costs (Al Jazeera 2025-11-28; Africanews 2026-03-14) [41][42]. Political shake-up: President Faye dismissed PM Sonko by decree on 2026-05-22; banker Ahmadou Al Aminou Lô named PM; Sonko was elected President of the National Assembly on 2026-05-26 with 132 votes, the opposition largely boycotting [40][40b].
- **Big event**: Dakar hosted the Youth Olympic Games, 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-13, at Dakar, Diamniadio and Saly [8].

## Cities

### Dakar

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bed flat: Numbeo (updated 2026-11-12) gives ~475,000 XOF (~724 EUR) in the centre and ~252,600 XOF (~385 EUR) outside [3]. Per-m² rents (au-senegal.com, 2026-04-02): Almadies/Ngor 7,000–18,000 XOF/m²; Plateau 6,000–15,000; Point E 4,500–8,000; Mermoz 3,000–7,000; Sacré-Cœur/Liberté/Sicap 3,000–6,500; Ouakam/Mamelles 2,800–8,000; Yoff 3,000–6,000 [1]. So a 50 m² 1-bed runs roughly 150,000–350,000 XOF (230–535 EUR) in Sacré-Cœur/Yoff/Mermoz and 350,000–900,000 XOF (535–1,370 EUR) in Almadies/Ngor.
  - Expensive: Fann Hock, Almadies, Mermoz; cheaper: Yoff, Ngor, Virage, Mamelles, Sacré-Cœur (Lepetitjournal, 2024-05-22) [2]. Expect monthly charges of up to ~40% of rent [2], a deposit of 2–3 months plus one month of agency fees (Cyril Jarnias, commercial blog, undated) [58].
  - Cheap restaurant meal ~3,000 XOF (4.60 EUR); single person's monthly costs excluding rent ~484,000 XOF (~738 EUR); utilities for 85 m² ~119,000 XOF (~181 EUR, air-con heavy) [3]. Editorial monthly budget for a single person: 750,000–1,200,000 XOF (1,150–1,800 EUR) with a 1-bed in a mid-range area.
  - Context: one 2024 article reports Dakar rents up to 15x the average Senegalese salary (Le360) [59].
- **Work & money**: Capital and almost the whole formal economy: public administration, banks, telecoms (Sonatel/Orange, YAS — formerly Free), international organisations and NGOs, oil-and-gas services since Sangomar oil (2024) and GTA gas (2025) [47]. Remote work is feasible in the city given fibre (below). Local salaries: not found in a reliable dated source.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - Home of **Wave**, francophone Africa's first unicorn (TechCabal, 2025-06-16) [25]; Wave holds ~80% of Senegal's e-money outstanding (CFA459bn of CFA571bn at 2026-08-21) and joined the BCEAO's PI-SPI instant-payment system on 2026-09-30, conceding interoperability (Launchbase Africa, 2026-10-01) [28].
  - **CTIC Dakar** (2011, first francophone West African tech incubator, backed by Sonatel/World Bank/infoDev) — profile undated [26b]. Other hubs commonly named (Jokkolabs, Impact Hub Dakar): not confirmed in 2025–2026 sources — not found.
  - **Startup Act** (loi 2020-01) relaunched on 2025-11-20 as "Écosystème Startup" with a label and an online one-stop shop; Launchbase calls it the third launch in four years (2025-11-21) [26][27]. Public funders DER/FJ and FONSIS act as early-stage de-riskers (TechCabal 2025) [25].
  - **Crypto**: used informally via mobile money on local platforms (Ejara, BarkaChange) and Binance P2P; BCEAO working on a regional crypto framework and an e-CFA — no legal crypto adoption (Osiris, 2025-10) [29].
  - **Company registration**: APIX one-stop shop, 24–72 h on paper, about a week in practice including bank account (Kolonell, commercial blog, 2026) [51].
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Power: cuts came back in September 2026 after years of relative calm: August demand hit 1,400 MW against 1,250 MW forecast, the Cap des Biches plant's shutdown on 6–7 Sept removed 240 MW, and Senelec scheduled daytime works cuts 21–26 Sept in named Dakar neighbourhoods (Senego, 2026-09-18) [33][34]. Budget for an inverter.
  - Internet: fibre from Orange (entry 14,900 XOF/month, ~30 Mbps) and YAS (40 Mbps for 14,900 XOF, 150 Mbps for 22,900 XOF, but only a few Dakar neighbourhoods) (Osiris, 2026) [35]. Starlink's authorisation remains suspended after a Supreme Court decision on 3 November 2026 (Seneweb) [36].
  - Mobile money: Wave and Orange Money are used everywhere, including for the BRT [25][28].
  - Transport: TER (Dakar–Diamniadio; the Diamniadio–AIBD airport section opened on 2026-09-28, a train every 24 min on that leg) [54b]; electric BRT opened 2024 and won the 2025 Sustainable Transport Award (ITDP/CETUD, 2025-10-24) [32]. Ride-hailing: Yango (most used), Heetch, Yassir; a Dakar court confirmed on 2025-09-24 that they may operate despite taxi protests [30][31]. Traffic jams on the Dakar–Rufisque axis are severe.
- **Health & schools**: Hôpital Principal (military, open to civilians), private Clinique de la Madeleine, Clinique du Cap, Clinique Pasteur, SOS Médecins home visits (Expat.com, Cyril Jarnias, undated) [37][38]. Lycée français Jean Mermoz (AEFE, directly managed, maternelle to terminale, with an American international section (SIA) leading to the Baccalauréat Français International; 2026 welcome booklet) [39]; the Lepetitjournal directory lists other French-curriculum schools [39b].
- **Safety**: FCDO (page cites changes up to 2026-03-30): robbery, burglary and assault increasing in Dakar, sometimes armed; armed robberies at hotels popular with foreigners; frequent political protests that can turn violent and block the airport road [5]. France Diplomatie (updated 2026-09-29): muggings day and night; pedestrian attacks on the **Corniche** after dark [8]. US: not opened (403). Neighbourhood nuance: Almadies/Ngor/Mermoz/Point E have guards and are where expats concentrate; the Corniche at night and crowded markets (Sandaga, HLM) are the cited hotspots [8].
- **Social life & vibe**: The densest nightlife in the country; nights start around midnight; live mbalax and Afro-jazz at Just 4 U, Chez Iba (Plateau), Bideew Bou Bess; club scene in Almadies (travel guides, partly older — flagged) [52]. Wrestling (*lutte*), football, surf at Ngor/Ouakam, beach workouts on the Corniche. Islam (Sufi brotherhoods) structures the calendar (Magal, Tabaski, Ramadan) but Dakar is socially plural; Afrobarometer 2025: 92% fine with neighbours of another ethnic group, 80% with another religion, 75% with immigrants [11].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Facebook group "Expats in Dakar" (housing, tips) [49]; InterNations Dakar, UFE, Club des Expatriés (welcome service) [50]; Expat-Dakar.com classifieds [49]; Expat.com Senegal forum [49]. Returnee networks: FAISE, Forum Diaspora Impact (2026) [18][19]. A Senegalese daily ran a series on "repats" (Le Soleil) [57b].
- **Climate in practice**: Dry, breezy and mild from November to May (sea-cooled, best Dakar months). Rainy season (*hivernage*) roughly late June to October, heaviest in August–September; September–October is the most humid and hot; flash floods in low-lying suburbs when drains block — avoid ground-floor flats in flood-prone areas (WeatherSpark; NextCity, older) [48].
- **Watch out**: rent and service-charge inflation in expat areas; returning power cuts; traffic; protest-related disruption; tax hikes from the debt crisis; harassment of LGBT people (see legal section); noise (mosques, *sabar* ceremonies, building sites).

### Saint-Louis

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed on the island ~80,000–150,000 XOF/month (120–230 EUR); monthly budget 200,000–400,000 XOF (305–610 EUR) (OpenShores, commercial, 2026-03-10) [20]. Neighbourhoods: the UNESCO-listed **Île** (colonial houses), Sor (mainland, cheaper), Langue de Barbarie / Guet Ndar (fishing district, flood-prone) [20].
- **Work & money**: The expat job market is "almost non-existent"; remote work for a foreign employer is the norm [20]. Local economy: fishing, administration, Université Gaston Berger, tourism, and supply contracts for the offshore GTA gas project (local-content workshops December 2025; EITI 2024 report says benefits are growing but local content remains weak — Le Soleil, 2026) [47].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: No named incubator found. Not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: Power and water cuts more frequent than Dakar; generator/inverter and water tank strongly advised [20]. Internet: not found specifically. Transport: no rail; ~4–5 h by road to Dakar (340 km) [20]. Ride-hailing coverage: not found.
- **Health & schools**: Hôpital Régional for routine care; serious emergencies go to Dakar. No AEFE French lycée [20].
- **Safety**: OpenShores says a foreigner can walk alone without constant solicitation [20]; no dedicated official advisory for the city. Main physical risk is flooding and coastal erosion (Langue de Barbarie breach since 2003) [20]. Northern/eastern border areas (Mauritania, Mali) carry caution in FCDO and an "orange" zone in France Diplomatie (Matam department, not Saint-Louis city) [6][8].
- **Social life & vibe**: Slow, calm, intellectual (university), music: the **Saint-Louis Jazz Festival** (33rd edition 28 May–1 June 2025; 34th 13–17 May 2026) [46]. Fish-based cuisine (*thiéboudienne* claims its origin here — general knowledge). Strong Pulaar/Wolof mix and conservative religious tone in daily life (editorial).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Small; mostly retirees, NGO staff, university staff. No named group found. Not found.
- **Climate in practice**: Extreme heat March–June, often ~40°C, peaks 45–47°C with harmattan; many expats leave in those months [20]. Rainy season July–September with floods in low areas [20].
- **Watch out**: Isolation from Dakar healthcare; erosion and flooding; few schooling options; tensions between artisanal fishers and the gas project [46][47].

### Saly (Saly-Portudal, Petite Côte)

- **Cost of living**: Villa with pool, garden and guard ~200,000–400,000 XOF/month (305–610 EUR) (OpenShores, commercial, 2026-03-10) [21]; rents rising since 2023 per the same publisher. A guard costs 30,000–50,000 XOF/month [21]. Property tax and household-waste tax increases (TOM 72,000 → 108,000 XOF; foncier bâti 120,000 → 180,000 XOF for some residents in 2025) reported by Dakaractu (2025-03-03) [22]. Neighbourhoods: Saly Portudal residential zones with private security, Saly Niakh Niakhal, Saly Village; nearby Mbour for cheaper local housing (editorial).
- **Work & money**: Tourism, hospitality, real estate, retiree services. Local salaries 80,000–200,000 XOF/month; skilled jobs rare [21]. Tourist economy in decline: per the Petite Côte hoteliers' union, 60% of independent hotels closed or for sale and direct jobs down 54% since 2019 (Sud Quotidien via AllAfrica, 2025-11-15 — union figures, not independently verified) [23].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Not found. Small-business opportunities in services for retirees (editorial).
- **Daily infrastructure**: Cuts can last hours to days; generator and water reserve recommended [21]. Roads full of potholes, secondary roads turn to mud in the rains [23]. TER extension towards Mbour under study [54].
- **Health & schools**: Clinique de Saly and Mbour hospital for routine care; advanced care in Dakar (80 km); medical-evacuation insurance indispensable [21]. Franco-Senegalese social security does not automatically reimburse French retirees [24]. Schools: not found.
- **Safety**: France Diplomatie names the Somone/Saly-to-Joal coast among places where muggings occur and caution is needed (2026-09-29) [8]. Dakaractu (2025-03-03): series of burglaries and murders worried residents, a German resident robbed in daylight, "Jakarta" motorbikes ridden at speed on roads and beaches at weekends, endangering bathers (a traffic hazard, not described as gangs) [22]. Sud Quotidien (2025-11-15): pickpockets and aggressive soliciting; the resort empties after 8 pm [23]. OpenShores (2026) is more upbeat [21] — sources conflict.
- **Social life & vibe**: Quiet, retiree-heavy, European; restaurants, golf, beach clubs mostly seasonal; much livelier during European winter (editorial, consistent with [21][23]).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: European community since the 1990s, WhatsApp groups, associations and lists of trusted tradespeople [21]; Expat.com "Petite Côte" forum threads [49]. Diaspora returnees: not specifically found.
- **Climate in practice**: Mild October–May (25–32°C, sea 23–28°C); hivernage June–September 33–38°C, 85–95% humidity, storms, high malaria risk; many expats return to Europe then [21].
- **Watch out**: Documented decline of the resort, rising local taxes, villa burglaries, beach pollution, and distance from serious healthcare [21][22][23].

### Thiès (additional city — why: Senegal's third agglomeration, 70 km from Dakar, on the future TER line, cheaper base for people working in Diamniadio/AIBD)

- **Cost of living**: Not found in a dated source. OpenShores says Dakar is 40–60% more expensive than other regions (commercial) [55].
- **Work & money / infrastructure**: Rail revival: GTS service Thiès–Diamniadio (~50–60 min), TER phase 3 to Thiès (~25 km) funded for studies by the EU (EUR 6m, March 2026) and an AFD/EU memorandum (July 2026) [54]. Diamniadio new town and AIBD airport are 30–45 min away (editorial).
- **Safety / health / schools / community**: Not found in dated sources beyond national advice.
- **Watch out**: Thin data — scores not proposed; treat as a candidate for future research.

### Ziguinchor (additional city — why: Casamance capital, greener and cheaper, tied to the 2025 peace process and to PM-era politics; relevant for Diola/Casamance diaspora)

- **Cost of living**: Rents 60–70% lower than Dakar per OpenShores (commercial) [55]. Specific figures not found.
- **Safety**: A peace accord was signed with an MFDC faction in Bissau on 2025-02-23; other factions have not signed (AllAfrica 2025-09-12; ADF) [43]. 2025 was "broadly calm" in Ziguinchor with a few incidents (AllAfrica, 2026-01-02) [44]; a November 2025 assessment describes "neither peace nor war" with mined areas remaining (Senego) [45]. France Diplomatie (zones updated 2026-03-12): Casamance's borders with Gambia (off main roads) and parts near Guinea-Bissau are "déconseillées sauf raison impérative"; the coastal triangle Ziguinchor–Cap Skirring–Casamance river is accessible with heightened vigilance [8]. FCDO lifted its advice against travel on the N4/N5 Gambia–Ziguinchor roads (snapshot 2025-04-30) [7][6].
- **Watch out**: Landmines in rural areas, land disputes with returning displaced people, distance to Dakar healthcare.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This is editorial interpretation of cited facts about each city's pace and social codes. It does not claim that a city or a quiz question measures a personality trait. Hofstede figures for Senegal could only be found in secondary documents (power distance ~70, i.e. hierarchical), not on Hofstede's own site, so they are low confidence [56]. An individualism figure could not be confirmed and is not used. Afrobarometer (2025) shows high tolerance across ethnicity and religion but near-unanimous rejection of homosexual neighbours (97%) [11].

**Dakar**
- Openness: rewards curiosity — the densest live music, art (Biennale), startup and international scene in the country [25][52].
- Conscientiousness: helps a lot — bureaucracy (residence card at the préfecture, leases checked by a notary, charges negotiation) and traffic reward planners [2][8].
- Extraversion: suits people who enjoy crowds — networking happens at events, *teranga* hospitality, late nights from midnight to dawn [52].
- Agreeableness: helpful — social life runs on greetings, family visits and contributions to ceremonies; hierarchy and elders matter (high power distance, low confidence) [56].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed — noise, power cuts back in 2026, protest disruption, rising petty crime [5][33].

**Saint-Louis**
- Openness: suits those drawn to heritage, jazz, river and university life rather than nightlife [20][46].
- Conscientiousness: helpful for self-organised remote workers in a near-absent job market [20].
- Extraversion: low-key social life; suits introverts and small-circle people [20].
- Agreeableness: direct, simple social relations reported; patience with slower services [20].
- Emotional stability: heat waves up to 45–47°C, flooding and distance from specialised care require resilience [20].

**Saly**
- Openness: limited cultural offer; suits people seeking routine, beach and golf over discovery (editorial) [21][23].
- Conscientiousness: managing guards, generators, local taxes and villa security is part of life [21][22].
- Extraversion: social life is in a closed expat/retiree circle; quieter after 8 pm [21][23].
- Agreeableness: community help via WhatsApp groups [21].
- Emotional stability: needs tolerance for reported burglaries and an uncertain local economy [22][23].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: Same-sex relations criminalised. Loi n° 2026-08 (adopted 2026-03-11 by 135 votes to 0 with 3 abstentions, promulgated and published in the Journal officiel 30 March 2026) raises the penalty to **5–10 years in prison** and a 2–10 million FCFA fine, adds a 10-year loss of civic rights, creates offences of "apology" and "funding/support" of homosexuality, bisexuality and transsexuality (3–7 years), and applies the maximum where a minor is involved; it followed the February 2026 arrest of 12 men [9][10]. FCDO: the law is enforced, arrests occur [5]. France Diplomatie (2026-10-27): "a simple approach or attempted homosexual relationship" can lead to prosecution; arrests regularly occur [8]. Afrobarometer 2025: 97% would not want homosexual neighbours [11]. **Practical**: Senegal is very unsafe for openly LGBT residents, and supporters (including foreigners) are also exposed under the new "promotion" offence.
- **Treatment of foreigners/diaspora**: 75% of Senegalese like or are indifferent to immigrant neighbours (Afrobarometer, 2025-07-29) [11]. Returnees face family financial expectations and reintegration difficulties even when well-prepared (Université de Fribourg policy brief, 2023 — older) [57]. A mixed-race testimony on au-senegal.com describes being seen as "white" in Senegal (undated) [57c]. Foreigners have been prosecuted for joining unauthorised protests (FCDO) [5]. Racism/colourism data: skin-lightening (*xeesal*) is a known social issue, but no dated reliable source opened — not found.
- **Conflict zones**: Casamance — low-intensity separatist conflict since 1982, partial peace accord 2025-02-23, mines remain [43][45]; France Diplomatie orange zones: parts of Casamance's borders, the Mali border and the south of the Mauritania border (Matam) [8]. FCDO: jihadist groups (JNIM, ISSP) active close to the Mali border; terrorism "cannot be ruled out"; Sahel kidnapping threat [5][6].
- **Political climate**: Violent 2021–2024 protests; May 2026 rupture between President Faye and Sonko [40]; protests can block the airport road [5].
- **Women's daily experience**: 31.9% of women surveyed reported at least one form of violence in the previous 12 months (ANSD national reference survey, data 2023, published 2024) [13]. 31% of Senegalese think women are sexually harassed in public places (markets, streets, transport) (Afrobarometer, 2025-11-28) [12]. Harassment is described as systemic from streets to public transport, with very few complaints filed (Maison des Reporters via SenePlus) [53]. A Dakar ride-hailing driver was arrested for sexual harassment in a car in November 2025 (Dakaractu) [53b]. FCDO advises modest dress, mainly in religious contexts [5].
