# Côte d'Ivoire

> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

Currency note: the CFA franc (XOF/FCFA) is pegged at 655.957 FCFA = 1 EUR, so EUR figures below are rounded conversions at that rate.

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: French is the language of administration, school and business. Nouchi (Abidjan street slang mixing French and local languages) is everywhere in daily speech and music. Dioula is the trade language in markets and in the north; Baoulé, Bété and others are spoken at home. Arriving French speakers can manage from day one. (General knowledge; no single dated source collected. Low priority to verify.)
- **Currency**: CFA franc (XOF), shared across WAEMU and issued by the BCEAO (general knowledge; [S20] covers BCEAO crypto policy, not the currency itself).
- **Religion**: 2021 census figures quoted by Global Voices (Sept 2024): Islam 42.5%, Christianity 39.8% [S11].
- **Entry for French/EU passport holders**: a visa is required; French Diplomatie lists it under "Entrée / séjour" [S3]. In practice it is an e-visa through the SNEDAI portal, with stays of up to 90 days according to agency pages [S8][S9]. **To live there**: the main route is a work-based *carte de séjour*, which needs an employment contract approved by the labour ministry and a clean criminal record, usually valid 1–3 years and renewable (OpenShores agency guide, 2026, commercial source) [S10]. The ECOWAS free-movement residence card does not apply to EU nationals [S10]. Naturalisation requires about five years of continuous legal residence (same commercial source) [S10]. Treat fees and timelines as unverified; check SNEDAI and the French embassy.
- **Diaspora with Ivorian roots**:
  - Nationality passes by descent under the 1961 Nationality Code (Loi n° 61-415/416) [S15].
  - **Dual nationality is not recognised**: Article 48 of the Nationality Code says an Ivorian who voluntarily acquires another nationality automatically loses Ivorian nationality. The rule was being publicly challenged in December 2025 (opinion piece, Connection Ivoirienne, 18 Dec 2025) and in the political debate around Tidjane Thiam, but no reform allowing dual nationality was found as of Nov 2026 [S13][S14]. **Practical consequence**: a French-born person of Ivorian parentage who holds French nationality from birth (not acquired voluntarily later) is in a different position from an Ivorian who naturalised in France. This is a legal grey area; get advice from a lawyer or the consulate.
  - There is **no diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme** comparable to Ghana's or Benin's. Instead the state courts diaspora *investment*: the DGIE (Direction générale des Ivoiriens de l'extérieur) runs entrepreneurship workshops, and a diaspora investment support fund was announced in Sept 2025 [S28]. CEPICI made the diaspora central to its attractiveness strategy (Jan 2026) [S29]. The government cites diaspora remittances of 938 bn FCFA (≈ EUR 1.43 bn) in 2025 [S30]. A recruitment competition reserved for the diaspora and the FID2A investment forum also exist [S31].
- **Political context**: Alassane Ouattara won a contested fourth term on 25 Oct 2025 with 89.77%, after the two main opposition figures were excluded. Turnout was about half. The National Security Council's official toll, given on 13 Nov 2025, was 11 dead, 71 injured and 1,658 arrests; an earlier government statement had given 3 dead, and Africa Check flagged the confusion over the count [S32][S33][S74]. France Diplomatie notes the 2025 presidential election was "globally calm" across the country [S2].

## Cities

### Abidjan

Economic capital and by far the main destination: roughly half of the country's formal economy, every embassy, the regional HQ of the African Development Bank, the BRVM stock exchange.

- **Cost of living**
  - **Rents vary wildly by commune.** Collected 2026 figures (via The Rio Times, citing The Africanvestor, I am Beezy and IvoireDomi) [S4]:
    - **Cocody** (3 rooms): 250,000–500,000 FCFA (EUR 380–760).
    - **Cocody Riviera Golf** (2 bedrooms, Jan 2026): above 1.2 M FCFA (EUR 1,830+).
    - **Plateau**: 513,000–953,000 FCFA (EUR 780–1,450).
    - **Marcory Zone 4**: 900,000–1.5 M FCFA (EUR 1,370–2,290), high-end stock.
    - **Yopougon** (3 rooms): 80,000–180,000 FCFA (EUR 120–275).
    - **Abobo**: 42,000–78,000 FCFA (EUR 65–120).
  - OpenShores (Apr 2026) gives a 2-room flat at 200,000–600,000 FCFA in Plateau and 120,000–300,000 in Marcory-Treichville [S5].
  - A realistic **middle-class 1-bed** in Cocody (Angré, Riviera 2/3), Marcory or Plateau is about **200,000–500,000 FCFA (EUR 300–760)**. That is editorial synthesis of the sources above.
  - **Deposits**: plan for 2–3 months' deposit plus an agency fee; one guide caps total upfront at 6 months of rent [S6].
  - **Food**: a garba plate (attiéké + fried tuna) costs about 1,000 FCFA (EUR 1.50) (RFI, July 2026) [S4]. Attiéké and fish at a sit-down maquis costs 1,500–4,000 FCFA [S4]. A restaurant meal in Cocody/Zone 4 runs EUR 10–20 [S34].
  - **Monthly budget**: a single person on a local lifestyle needs about 300,000–500,000 FCFA (EUR 460–760) per month (Africarrières, undated) [S7]. An expat-style lifestyle with a car and international school is far higher. The legal minimum wage is 75,000 FCFA (Dec 2022 decree) [S4].
  - **Schools**: Lycée français Blaise Pascal costs 2.87–4.34 M FCFA/year for French and Ivorian families (2026-27). ICSA (American) costs USD 18–27k/year (2025-26) [S4].
- **Work & money**
  - **Main employers**: the port and logistics, banking (BRVM, regional bank HQs), telecoms (Orange CI, MTN, Moov), agro-industry (cocoa, cashew), the AfDB and international organisations, consulting, and construction.
  - **Remote work** is feasible from the main residential communes given fixed broadband (see below), with a backup connection and an inverter for power cuts.
  - **Salaries**: no reliable salary survey found beyond the minimum wage.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**
  - **Company registration**: CEPICI's one-stop shop advertises company creation in 24–48 hours [S16][S17]. Connection Ivoirienne (Aug 2025) quotes 24 h and 10,000 FCFA [S31]. 26,948 companies were created in 2025, up 6% [S18].
  - **Hubs and incubators** (list from erooamba, 2026, low-reliability aggregator): Hub Ivoire Tech (state incubator), Orange Fab CI (launched Sept 2014; 10 M FCFA per selected startup per Digital Mag 2025), Impact Hub Abidjan, Seedspace, Dream Factory (Agence CI PME), Jokkolabs, Akendewa, Babylab, ADN Factory, and university programmes (INP-HB, ESATIC) [S19][S35].
  - **Funding and support**: there is a Startup Act with tax benefits, a PAES programme launched March 2026, and PEPITE 2030. Named funds include Comoé Capital and Teranga Capital [S19].
  - **Maturity**: African Business (Apr 2026) still calls the ecosystem early-stage despite a "startup surge" [S36]. StartupBlink ranks Abidjan #1 in the country and around #575 worldwide [S37].
  - **Fintech** is the star:
    - Almost 50 M mobile-money accounts were opened in 2025 for about 32 M people (Digital Mag) [S21].
    - Wave claims over 21 M accounts, about 60% market share and about 1 M merchants (company figures from a 29 April 2026 press conference, not independently verified; Digital Mag) [S71].
    - MTN removed withdrawal fees in 2025, starting a price war with Wave (Osiris, 5 Sept 2025) [S22].
    - Wave set up a banking entity (Wave Bank Africa SA, Aug 2025) [S23].
  - **Crypto**: no specific law. The BCEAO governor said on 22 July 2026 that crypto "is not a currency" and is not regulated in the Union; a committee (C-CRYPTO) is drafting a WAEMU framework, with no publication date and AMF-UMOA's role still undefined [S20][S25]. Holding or trading crypto is not illegal, but it is not a supervised activity [S20]. Crypto runs in the informal sector (Yellow Card, 2023). Côte d'Ivoire is not a crypto hub like Nairobi or Lagos.
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - **Power**: cuts became more frequent in early 2026. CIE and the state attribute them to grid saturation (heat, urban sprawl), not a lack of generation. A plan of about 700 bn FCFA was announced to reinforce the grid; earlier load-shedding episodes occurred in 2021 and 2024 [S38][S39]. Expect outages in the hot season (Feb–April); a backup inverter is advisable.
  - **Internet**: Ookla data for 2025 put Côte d'Ivoire's **fixed broadband** median at 58.17 Mbps (rank 103 worldwide), among the fastest in Africa; entry-level fixed packages start at 50 Mbps (tech.africa, 2026) [S40]. An Ookla study of H1 2026 gives a 60.99 Mbps median fixed download, the highest of the six African markets studied (Connection Ivoirienne, 17 Aug 2026) [S72]. In H1 2025 Orange led mobile with a median of 35 Mbps (ITWeb; not re-checked) [S40].
  - **Mobile money** (Wave, Orange Money, MTN MoMo, Moov) is used even in shared taxis (wôrô-wôrô) (AllAfrica, Feb 2026) [S21].
  - **Ride-hailing**:
    - Yango dominates.
    - **Uber stopped operating around 24–25 Sept 2025**, announced by messages to users with no official press release (Digital Mag; WeeTracker, 29 Sept 2025) [S26].
    - Heetch and InDrive are available; InDrive lets you negotiate the fare [S26].
    - Yango prices surge at rush hour and in rain [S27].
  - **Public transport**:
    - **SOTRA fares disagree across sources.** The Rio Times (Sept 2026) gives 500 FCFA without a card and a 20,000–25,000 FCFA monthly pass [S4]. A SOTRA communiqué of July 2024, quoted by I am Beezy (2026), gives 200 FCFA for a standard ticket and 500 FCFA for express/shuttle lines; another 2026 guide gives 300 FCFA and an 8,000 FCFA pass for 44 trips [S73]. Plan on 200–500 FCFA a trip; no official 2026 tariff was found.
    - Lagoon boats cost 150–200 FCFA [S4].
    - Gbaka minibuses and wôrô-wôrô shared taxis have irregular fares [S26].
  - **Metro line 1**: 37 km, 18 stations, EUR 1.3 bn, still under construction. Partial opening was targeted for "end of 2026", with 2027 also cited [S41]. Traffic on the bridges and at rush hour is heavy.
- **Health & schools**
  - **Health**: private clinics are the default for expats and middle-class residents; they include Polyclinique Internationale Sainte Anne-Marie (PISAM, Cocody, 24/7 emergency) and Polyclinique Farah [S42][S43]. A GP visit costs 15,000–40,000 FCFA and a specialist 25,000–60,000 (Jan 2026) [S4]. Medical evacuation insurance is advised [S43]. The US State Dept says routine and emergency services "may be limited" [S44].
  - **Schools**: Lycée Blaise Pascal (AEFE-homologated, Riviera 3) and Lycée international Jean-Mermoz (Mission laïque, MLCI) both serve French families [S45]. ICSA offers an American curriculum [S45]. There are many private bilingual schools.
- **Safety**
  - **France Diplomatie** (updated 15 Sept 2026; map revised 5 March 2026) [S1]:
    - Abidjan is in the yellow "vigilance renforcée" zone.
    - Avoid going alone at night in Adjamé, Yopougon, Abobo and the Gonzagueville area of Port-Bouët.
    - Watch out when leaving restaurants, banks and ATMs.
    - Keep car doors locked.
    - Avoid demonstrations.
  - **US State Dept**: Level 2 "Exercise increased caution" nationwide, issued 18 Feb 2026 [S44]. One copy of the page shows a later issue date of 9 July 2026 whose content could not be retrieved (the official page returned 403 on 2026-11-14); check the live page.
  - **UK FCDO** (2024 snapshots): armed burglaries and street hold-ups "not common, but do occur" [S46]. Canada warns of violent crime especially at night [S46].
  - **Online fraud**: the Belgian MFA warns of very frequent online extortion ("brouteurs") (page undated, consulted 2026) [S47].
  - **Neighbourhood nuance**: Cocody, Riviera, Deux-Plateaux, Marcory Zone 4 and Plateau (by day) are where expats and returnees usually live. Yopougon and Abobo are lively and cheap but are flagged at night [S1].
  - **Floods**: rainy-season floods and landslides kill people every year in precarious areas such as Attécoubé and Abobo. In 2025 there were 6 dead in Attécoubé on 21 May (AFP) and 24 dead in 10 days in June. In late June 2026 (reported 30 June), heavy rain killed at least 12 people in Mossikro, between Yopougon and Attécoubé, per the military fire brigade (GSPM); Cocody, Abobo, Treichville, Marcory and Bingerville also flooded [S48].
- **Social life & vibe**
  - **Nightlife**: Abidjan is often described as West Africa's nightlife capital (editorial reputation, not a measure). Marcory Zone 4 is the bar and club district (Thursday to Sunday, music until 3–4 am) [S34]. Yopougon is the "cité de la joie" of maquis; Rue Princesse's current status is contested by sources [S34].
  - **Music**: coupé-décalé, zouglou and Ivorian rap ("rap ivoire") are mainstream.
  - **Food and sport**: food is maquis culture (garba, alloco, attiéké, braised fish). Football is a national passion; Côte d'Ivoire hosted AFCON 2024.
  - **Religion** is visible but daily life is broadly secular in tone. Muslims and Christians are of similar weight [S11].
  - **Pace**: fast, noisy and commercial; "ambiance" is a social value. It is family-friendly for those with budget, international schools and a car.
- **Diaspora & expat community**
  - **Expat networks**: InterNations Abidjan runs events and interest groups [S49]. ExpatExchange mentions the "Abidjan Expat Network" social club (status unverified) [S50]. Expat.com has an Abidjan forum [S51].
  - **Returnee events**: Forum Repats (Paris, April 2025, with an Abidjan stop in May 2025) [S52]. "Le Grand Retour de la Diaspora", Abidjan, 23–30 Oct 2026, organised by Esther Boua Consulting [S53][S30].
  - **Returnee experience**: returnees, often from the US since 2011, cite banks (credit limits, high interest) and family diversion of remitted capital as the real obstacles, more than administration (Connection Ivoirienne, Aug 2025) [S31].
  - No public Facebook/WhatsApp group could be verified without login.
- **Climate in practice**
  - **Seasons**: hot and humid all year (26–32 °C). The main rainy season runs late May to late July, with June the wettest month (≈300 mm). A short rainy season comes around Oct–Nov [S48].
  - **Daily life impact**: in rainy months, flooding paralyses traffic, ride-hailing prices surge, and hillside slums risk landslides [S48][S26]. The hot season (Feb–Apr) brings power cuts [S38].
- **Watch out**
  - High rents in safe communes.
  - Traffic.
  - Rainy-season flooding.
  - Rising power cuts.
  - Online scams.
  - Bank credit hard to get for returnees.
  - Dual nationality not recognised.
  - Political tension around elections (2020 election: 85 dead per ICG, cited in [S33]).

### Grand-Bassam

About 40 km east of Abidjan; former colonial capital, a UNESCO World Heritage site (Quartier France) and a beach town. In practice it is an Abidjan satellite.

- **Cost of living**: cheaper than Cocody for space. Villa listings in Cité Rosier run 700,000–1.5 M FCFA/month for 5–8-room villas with pools (Expat.com listings, undated) [S54]. No reliable 1-bed figure was found; *not found*. Food prices are similar to Abidjan.
- **Work & money**: the local economy runs on tourism, hospitality, crafts and residents who commute to Abidjan. There is little local market for professionals (OpenShores 2026, commercial) [S55]. Remote work is feasible. The village of artists and the creative scene are growing ("new creative hub", Africa.com, undated) [S56].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: no local hub found; *not found*. Entrepreneurs use Abidjan's ecosystem. Opportunities are hospitality, guest houses and art.
- **Daily infrastructure**: the expressway to Abidjan is quick off-peak, but commuting can take 1.5–2 h at peak, especially for school runs (OpenShores, unsourced) [S55]. Power and internet are on the same national grid; *not found* for town-specific data.
- **Health & schools**: limited locally; serious care and international schools are in Abidjan (Cocody, Riviera, Marcory). Town-specific data *not found*.
- **Safety**:
  - Same yellow vigilance zone [S1].
  - **The sea is the main danger.** France Diplomatie says beaches are dangerous and often unsupervised, with frequent drownings [S1]. FCDO singles out Grand-Bassam: swimming there is dangerous "even for excellent swimmers" [S46].
  - No up-to-date dated source on terrorism risk specific to the town was collected.
- **Social life & vibe**: weekend destination for Abidjanais. Calm on weekdays and busy at weekends, with beach bars, the Quartier France and the Abissa festival (N'zima). Slower pace, suited to families who want space and a garden.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: small; mostly connected through Abidjan networks. *Not found* for dedicated groups.
- **Climate in practice**: like Abidjan. Coastal erosion and historic flooding of the Quartier France are documented: weeks-long floods in late 2019 (Quartz, 2019 — older source) [S57]. UNESCO's 2025 decision cites storm surges and erosion, and asks for a conservation report by 1 Dec 2026 [S58].
- **Watch out**: commute to Abidjan, dangerous sea currents, few local jobs, flood risk in the historic quarter.

### San-Pédro

Second port of the country and the world's leading cocoa export port, in the south-west (Bas-Sassandra), about 350 km from Abidjan.

- **Cost of living**: OpenShores (Apr 2026) places it at "intermediate costs between large and small cities", with no figures [S5]. For reference, a 2-room flat costs 60,000–150,000 FCFA in Bouaké [S5]; San-Pédro is plausibly similar to slightly higher (editorial estimate, low confidence). Specific rent data: *not found*.
- **Work & money**:
  - **Port**: the 2024–2026 investment plan was 65% executed by March 2026 [S59].
  - **Economy**: cocoa, wood, agro-industry and logistics. The region is about 5% of GDP (Oxford Business Group, older) [S60].
  - **Planned growth**: a port city ("cité portuaire Hilaire Lamizana") is planned near the stadium [S59]. In 2025 a presidential candidate pitched San-Pédro as the "engine of progress" [S61].
  - Jobs are mainly in logistics, agro-trade and port services.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: no named hub found; *not found*.
- **Daily infrastructure**: the 20,000-seat Laurent Pokou stadium (opened Sept 2023 for AFCON) is still in use in 2026 [S62]. Data on power, internet and ride-hailing: *not found*; Yango coverage is unverified.
- **Health & schools**: a regional hospital exists, but no dated source was collected; *not found*. No French-homologated school found; *not found*.
- **Safety**:
  - Yellow vigilance zone [S1].
  - The orange "advised against unless essential" zone covers the Liberian border area (Tabou, Taï, Grabo), south-west of San-Pédro [S1]. FCDO restricts travel within 20 km of the Liberia border [S46].
  - No city-level crime data found.
- **Social life & vibe**: port and commercial town with beaches nearby (Monogaga, Grand-Béréby) and a much slower pace than Abidjan. Nightlife is modest. Day-to-day life is cheaper and closer to nature.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: small; *not found*.
- **Climate in practice**: equatorial and very wet (south-west forest zone). Rainy months are roughly May–July and Oct–Nov (by analogy with the coast; no city-specific source).
- **Watch out**: isolation from Abidjan (long road trip), limited international schools and specialist health care, and a job market dependent on the cocoa price.

### Yamoussoukro (extra city: political capital, cheapest liveable city)

- **Why it matters**: the official capital; calm, with wide avenues; home of INP-HB, the leading engineering school, which brings students and academics. OpenShores calls it the cheapest city in the country [S63].
- **Cost of living**: a 2-room flat costs 55,000–140,000 FCFA (EUR 85–215) (OpenShores, Apr 2026) [S5]. Food is about 60,000 FCFA/month for one person versus 90,000+ in Abidjan [S5].
- **Work**: civil service, education and agriculture. Few private-sector jobs [S63].
- **Vibe**: quiet and family-oriented; suits civil servants, students and families [S63]. Charles Konan Banny stadium (AFCON 2024).
- **Watch out**: low job market and limited expat services [S63].

### Bouaké (extra city: second city, trade hub)

- **Why it matters**: the main inland trade crossroads with an SME and market economy; rents comparable to Yamoussoukro, a 2-room flat at 60,000–150,000 FCFA (EUR 90–230) (OpenShores, Apr 2026) [S5][S63].
- **Watch out**: few expat services. It is closer to the north, but outside the red zones; the red zones start further north at the Burkina and Mali borders [S1]. Bouaké-specific data was thin.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of documented city traits. No city "selects" a personality, and no quiz question measures one.

**Academic context**:
- Hofstede has no Côte d'Ivoire score. His "West Africa" regional estimate is based on Ghana, Nigeria and Sierra Leone (PDI 77, IDV 20, UAI 54) [S64].
- A study of BRVM institutional investors in Abidjan found collectivism, high uncertainty avoidance, long-term orientation and, unexpectedly, *low* power distance [S64].
- Afrobarometer Round 9/10 finds broad tolerance of ethnic and religious difference across Africa but limited trust beyond family [S65]. In May 2024, 69% of Ivorians said government cares little or not at all about youth problems [S66].

**Abidjan**
- **Openness**: rewards curiosity. It is a cosmopolitan hub where Nouchi, coupé-décalé and new music are born, and Global Voices calls it West Africa's "queer melting pot" [S11]. Its fast-changing startup and fintech scene suits people who like novelty [S36].
- **Conscientiousness**: helps a lot. Rents with 2–3 months' deposit, bank credit hurdles for returnees and traffic planning reward organised people [S6][S31].
- **Extraversion**: a strong fit. Maquis culture, Zone 4 nightlife until 3–4 am and "ambiance" as a social value [S34].
- **Agreeableness**: helpful for navigating family and community expectations (requests for money, diversion of remitted capital) [S31]. Firm boundaries matter as much as warmth.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: needed. Traffic, power cuts in the hot season, rainy-season floods and political tension in election years [S38][S48][S33].

**Grand-Bassam**
- **Openness**: suits people drawn to heritage, art and a creative scene [S56].
- **Conscientiousness**: commuting to Abidjan needs discipline [S55].
- **Extraversion**: suits introverts during the week; weekends are busy.
- **Agreeableness**: small-town social codes; you are known by neighbours.
- **Stress tolerance**: lower daily stress than Abidjan, but the commute and the sea need caution.

**San-Pédro**
- **Openness**: less novelty; you need to like nature and quiet.
- **Conscientiousness**: helpful for relocating with a job (port and agro sectors).
- **Extraversion**: modest nightlife; a better fit for home-centred people.
- **Agreeableness**: community-centred provincial life.
- **Stress tolerance**: less urban stress, more isolation stress (far from Abidjan's services).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex relations are **not criminalised**. However, there are no anti-discrimination protections: when the anti-discrimination provisions were revised in 2021, sexual orientation was left out (Global Voices, 9 Sept 2024, calls it the government's refusal to include LGBTQ+ people) [S11][S12].
  - **Aug–Sept 2024**: an online hate campaign against "woubis" on X and TikTok was followed by reported physical attacks. The government said no law refers to sexual orientation but that marriage is between a man and a woman, and that nobody may take justice into their own hands (Koaci, 5 Sept 2024) [S12][S67].
  - Abidjan serves as a refuge for LGBTQ+ people fleeing neighbouring countries. Violence against trans women is frequent (Global Voices, 9 Sept 2024) [S11].
  - In short: legal, but socially risky. Discretion is the norm.
- **Racism and colourism**:
  - A 2015 decree banned skin-lightening creams (mercury, corticoids, hydroquinone above 2%). Enforcement was described as an "empty shell" in 2015 [S68]. That source is older; no recent enforcement data was found.
  - No dated source specifically on treatment of diaspora returnees, such as perceptions of the "Bengiste" (someone back from Europe), was found. Connection Ivoirienne (Aug 2025) reports frictions as mainly family and financial [S31].
  - Data on racism towards foreigners or non-Black expats: *not found*.
- **Conflict zones**:
  - **Red** (formally advised against) by France Diplomatie: the northern border with Mali and Burkina Faso, northern Zanzan, eastern Savanes and Comoé National Park, because of terrorism and kidnapping (updated 15 Sept 2026) [S1].
  - **Orange**: the Liberian border (Tabou, Taï, Grabo) [S1].
  - The US says do not travel to the northern border region (Feb 2026) [S44].
  - **Elections**: the Oct 2025 presidential election left 11 dead officially [S32]. The 2020 election left 85 dead (ICG via [S33]).
- **Women's daily experience**:
  - The Ministry of Women recorded 9,607 cases of gender-based violence in 2024 (Amnesty) [S69].
  - More than 1,200 rapes and sexual assaults were recorded in 2024, about 12% of them in Abidjan district (AllAfrica, Nov 2025) [S70].
  - Hundreds of women marched in Abidjan in March 2024 against femicide [S69].
  - Women are very visible in commerce and the professions in Abidjan.
  - Street-harassment data: *not found*.

## Questions that would discriminate between this country's cities

1. Tu veux vivre dans une grande ville qui bouge jour et nuit, ou dans une ville plus calme au bord de la mer ? (Abidjan vs Grand-Bassam / San-Pédro)
2. Est-ce que tu acceptes 1 h à 2 h de route par jour pour avoir une maison avec jardin moins chère ? (Grand-Bassam vs Abidjan)
3. Tu cherches un travail sur place dans la banque, la tech ou une organisation internationale, ou tu travailles déjà à distance ? (Abidjan vs Grand-Bassam/San-Pédro)
4. Tes enfants doivent-ils aller dans une école française ou internationale ? (Abidjan only)
5. Le port, le cacao et la logistique, ça te parle pour ton métier ? (San-Pédro)
6. Ton budget logement, c'est plutôt moins de 150 000 FCFA (environ 230 €) par mois ? (Yamoussoukro/Bouaké, outer Abidjan communes)
