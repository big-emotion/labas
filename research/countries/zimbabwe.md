## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: English is the working language of business, government and schooling. Shona dominates in Harare (about 90% of residents are Shona speakers) [9]; Northern Ndebele dominates in Bulawayo, where local radio broadcasts mainly in English and Ndebele [8]. French is essentially absent from daily life: no francophone community or French school was found in this pass.
- **Currency**: Zimbabwe runs a multi-currency system. The ZiG (Zimbabwe Gold) launched on 8 April 2024 and has traded at about 25–27 ZiG per USD officially since November 2024, but the US dollar still handles roughly 70% of transactions after the launch (85% before) [6]. Foreign-currency settlement is legally valid until 31 December 2030 [6]. A new "BiG5" banknote series entered circulation on 7 April 2026 [6]. Parallel-market discounts on the ZiG were large in 2025 [6]. In practice: rents are quoted in USD ("most leases are signed in US dollars") [13]; bring clean, small, post-2013 USD notes because change is scarce [18]; some government services and clinics accept only USD cash [4][6].
- **Entry/residence (a) French/EU passport holders**: a visa is required. Visa on arrival is usually granted for 30 days, or an e-visa can be bought in advance (USD 65.77 for 30 days, around 10 business days to process, per Expat.com, Oct 2025) [3][13]. The KAZA Univisa (USD 50, 30 days, Zimbabwe + Zambia + day trips to Botswana) suits tourists, not residents [3]. Extensions are done in person at the Immigration Department [3]. To live there you need a permit. Expat.com (28 Oct 2025) lists a Temporary Employment Permit (USD 500, up to 12 months, renewable), an Investor Permit (USD 500 + USD 300 per dependant), a Spousal Residence Permit and a Student Permit, with permanent residence possible after 5 years of continuous residence [13]. Volunteers and missionaries also need a temporary work permit [3]. France Diplomatie (updated 15 Sep 2026) confirms that French nationals need a visa, which they can get online at evisa.gov.zw or on arrival. The indicative fee is USD 30 for a single entry or USD 45 for a double entry, paid in USD cash, and the page strongly advises registering online before departure [19]. Expat.com's USD 65.77 e-visa figure is a USD 30.77 government fee plus a USD 35 service charge [13]. FCDO asks for a passport valid 6 months after arrival with 3 blank pages [3].
- **Entry/residence (b) diaspora with Zimbabwean roots**: the 2013 Constitution allows **dual nationality for citizens by birth**. A person born abroad can count as a citizen by birth if a parent or grandparent was a Zimbabwean citizen and the birth is registered in Zimbabwe. Citizens by descent or registration may still be barred from holding two nationalities, and the 1984 Citizenship Act (amended in 2003), which bans dual nationality, had still not been amended to match the Constitution as of 2018, the latest status found [7]. Courts have upheld dual nationality for citizens by birth (*Madzimbamuto*, Constitutional Court, 2014) [7]. FCDO (page current at 14 Nov 2026) still calls the legal position on dual nationality "inconsistent" [1]. **No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme** comparable to Ghana's or Sierra Leone's was found [7]. This matters mostly to the Zimbabwean diaspora (largely in the UK and South Africa), not to francophone diaspora members with no Zimbabwean ancestry.

## Cities

### Harare

- **Cost of living**: Expat.com (Oct 2025) gives monthly rents of USD 1,500–3,500 in **Borrowdale**, USD 1,200–2,800 in **Mount Pleasant**, and often more than USD 4,000 in **Gunhill / Chisipite** (these are expat family houses, not 1-bed flats) [13]. A middle-class 1-bed flat in **Avondale, Belgravia or Milton Park** (inner suburbs that have gentrified [9]) was not priced: not found (Numbeo blocked). Single-person monthly budget: Expat.com contradicts itself on the same page (USD 900 in its key facts, USD 1,200–1,800 in the text, USD 1,330–2,100 in its table) [13]. Treat USD 1,200–2,100 as a rough range (≈ EUR 1,030–1,800 at an indicative ~0.86, to be recomputed). Meal price: not found. Deposits run 2–3 months' rent, and you should budget for a generator or solar backup [13]. Fuel cost USD 1.70–1.90 per litre (Oct 2025) [13].
- **Work & money**: national financial, commercial and communications hub, with tobacco, maize, cotton and citrus trade plus textiles, steel and chemicals manufacturing [9]. Tobacco is the main export crop, and more than half of it goes to China [17]. Most workers are in the precarious informal sector [17]. IMF figures show GDP growth of 2.0% in 2024 and 6.0% in 2025 [17]. Remote work in foreign currency is feasible if you secure power and internet backup, which is an editorial inference from [1][12]. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: mobile money is dominated by **EcoCash** (Econet), which held 99.8% of the market in 2017 (old figure) and has repeatedly been targeted by the government, including a 2020 suspension order [11]. Starlink had been illegal to use since January 2024. The regulator POTRAZ licensed it in late May 2024 through its exclusive local partner, IMC Communications, and the service went live around September 2024 [21]. That matters to remote workers who need a backup connection. Hubs, incubators and investors: not found in this pass. [unverified] Leads: Impact Hub Harare, Hypercube Hub, Econet's Cassava/Vaya ecosystem. Company registration has an Expat.com note on a re-registration deadline of 20 April 2026 [13]. ZIDA's site (the investment agency) could not be read.
- **Daily infrastructure**: frequent and sometimes long power cuts, plus water rationing in some areas (FCDO) [1]. Kariba hydro (~750 MW) and the ageing Hwange coal plant (920 MW installed) cannot cover demand [12]. Expat.com speaks of "daily power cuts" [13]. Internet runs over fibre in Harare, Bulawayo, Gweru and Mutare; national internet penetration was 34.8% in early 2023 [16]. Mobile operators are Econet, NetOne and Telecel [16]. Driving standards are "very poor", and passenger rail is suspended [1]. Ride-hailing: not found ([unverified] Vaya, InDrive). Smash-and-grab robberies on vehicles happen at main intersections on the airport road, on Liberation Legacy Way (Borrowdale Road) and Churchill Road, and on the Masvingo–Beitbridge road (FCDO, Jul 2026) [1].
- **Health & schools**: **Parirenyatwa Hospital** is the main public reference hospital [9]. Public facilities face drug and staff shortages and strikes [4][13]. Private GP visits cost USD 30–80 and specialists USD 80–150, and private clinics may demand cash upfront, often in USD [4][13]. Ambulances are unreliable; the emergency number is 112 [1][4]. **Harare International School** fees ran from USD 5,340 (pre-K) to USD 26,640 (grades 9–12) in 2024-25, and other international schools cost USD 8,000–20,000 a year, with waiting lists [13]. French school: not found ([unverified] a small French school and an Alliance Française exist in Harare; their sites could not be reached).
- **Safety**: FCDO (updated 15 Jul 2026, current at 14 Nov 2026) rates crime as moderate. Mugging, pickpocketing and bag-snatching are common in city centres after dark, and armed robberies of foreign residents happen occasionally [1]. France Diplomatie (15 Sep 2026) rates overall crime as low but reports more burglaries in Harare, after dark and sometimes armed. It advises not resisting, fitting a home alarm linked to a private security company, and not walking in the city centre at night [20]. Demonstrations can turn violent. Photographing police, the military, demonstrations or State House is illegal, insulting the President is a crime, and you should avoid political discussion, including online [1]. France Diplomatie's "vigilance renforcée" (heightened vigilance) alert of 10 Sep 2026 is a worldwide notice covering several regions, not a warning specific to Zimbabwe [5]. Neighbourhood nuance: Borrowdale, Mount Pleasant and Glen Lorne are the affluent northern suburbs [9], and they are where Expat.com prices expat housing [13]. The CBD after dark is the main risk area [1][20].
- **Social life & vibe**: Harare has a lively arts and protest-theatre scene [9]. **HIFA** (the Harare International Festival of the Arts) was cancelled in 2019, and whether it has run since is unclear [9]. Golf: Royal Harare hosts the Zimbabwe Open [9]. Local art and crafts are sold at Avondale Market and Mbare Musika, and galleries in Msasa Park and Umwinsdale produce Shona stone sculpture [9]. Visitors describe locals as kind and resilient (Routard community) [18]. Nightlife, food scene and the weight of religion: not found in this pass.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com's Zimbabwe community has ~5,740 members and about 50,000 expats are estimated in the country (Oct 2025) [13]. Returnee associations and Facebook/WhatsApp groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: at 1,483 m altitude it is mild (about 18 °C on average). Rains fall from November to March/April and peak in January–February. May–September is dry and cool, and September–October is hot and dry [9]. Flooding affected the region in 2026 [1][9].
- **Watch out**: currency confusion (USD/ZiG, scarce change) [6][18]. Daily power cuts and water rationing [1][13]. Political red lines on speech and photography [1]. Costly international schools [13]. Healthcare that is paid upfront in cash [4]. An English/Shona environment with almost no French.

### Bulawayo

- **Cost of living**: Expat.com (Oct 2025) gives USD 800–2,500/month for a 3–4 bedroom house and calls the city "calmer and cheaper" than Harare [13]. 1-bed rent and meal prices: not found (Numbeo blocked). Known suburbs include **Hillside, Burnside, Newton West and Ascot** [8].
- **Work & money**: Bulawayo was once Zimbabwe's industrial heart (vehicles, textiles, building materials, food). It has since declined, with deserted factories and high unemployment as firms closed or moved to Harare, though investor interest has shown some recent revival [8]. It remains the rail hub [8]. NUST, the country's second-largest university, is here [8].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found. [unverified] Lead: a NUST-linked innovation hub.
- **Daily infrastructure**: chronic water shortages because dam capacity has not kept up with population, plus water-quality problems from burst sewers [8]. National power cuts apply here too [1][12]. Fibre is available [16]. Ride-hailing: not found.
- **Health & schools**: reference hospitals not found ([unverified] Mpilo Central Hospital, United Bulawayo Hospitals). Fewer international schools than Harare [13].
- **Safety**: covered by national FCDO advice (Jul 2026), with no Bulawayo-specific warning found [1]. Local-press nuance not found.
- **Social life & vibe**: nicknamed the "City of Kings", Bulawayo has the Natural History Museum, the National Gallery, the Railway Museum and the **Intwasa Arts Festival** each September [8]. Outdoors there are Centenary Park, Hillside Dams and the Matobo Sailing Club [8]. The city is Ndebele-majority, and the memory of **Gukurahundi** (1980s massacres, with first disturbances in Entumbane) still shapes local identity [8]. Expat.com calls it calmer than the capital [13].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: hot semi-arid but tempered by altitude (about 19 °C on average). Rains fall from November to April, heaviest December–February. June–August is dry and cold at night, and October is the hottest month (highs around 30 °C) [8]. Rainfall is only about 594 mm a year, which explains the water stress [8].
- **Watch out**: water shortages [8], a shrinking formal job market [8], and thinner international services than Harare [13].

### Victoria Falls (extra city: tourism-economy town with an international airport)

- Population 35,199 (2022 census) [14]. Tourism is the main industry, and employment grew fastest in the country (10.3% in 2018) [14]. The airport has international flights to Johannesburg, Cape Town, Addis Ababa, Nairobi, Windhoek, Gaborone and Kasane, plus domestic links to Harare and Bulawayo [14]. Property was the second least affordable in the country after Harare (2017, old data) [14]. Rents run USD 700–2,000, with limited schools and medical care (Expat.com, Oct 2025) [13]. The town has a district hospital and a private hospital [14]. It suits a hospitality or tour entrepreneur or a nature lover, not a family that needs international schooling.

### Mutare (extra city: mild highland city on the Mozambique corridor)

- Zimbabwe's third city: 224,804 people (2022 census), ~500,000 metro [15]. Mild highland climate (about 19 °C) [15]. The economy rests on farming, timber, dairy, gold and diamond mining, and trade on the Beira corridor, 290 km to the sea [15]. It is the gateway to the Bvumba and Eastern Highlands [15]. Safety: France Diplomatie (zones updated 13 Mar 2026) advises against the Marange diamond fields, about 50 km south-west of Mutare. Access there is controlled, and buying diamonds from unlicensed sellers is a crime. It also warns of landmines along the northern border with Mozambique and says to stay on main roads [20]. Cost and community data: not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No Hofstede, GLOBE or regional-personality study covering Zimbabwe could be checked in this pass (not found). The notes below are editorial readings of the facts above, not measurements, and no quiz question "measures" any trait.

**Harare**
- Openness: suits people curious about arts and protest theatre and about stone-sculpture culture [9], and willing to live in Shona and English rather than French.
- Conscientiousness: rewards planners. Daily life means managing USD and ZiG and keeping small notes [6][18], backing up power and water [1][13], and handling permits in person [3].
- Extraversion: social life seems spread across the suburbs (golf, galleries, markets) rather than dense street nightlife [9], and the city centre empties after dark [20]. Nightlife detail was not found.
- Agreeableness: social codes reward discretion. Political talk, even online, is risky, and some photography is illegal [1].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for power cuts, currency swings [6] and the occasional unrest [1].

**Bulawayo**
- Openness: suits people drawn to heritage and history (museums, Intwasa, Matobo) [8].
- Conscientiousness: water rationing demands household discipline [8].
- Extraversion: calmer and quieter than Harare [13], which suits lower-key personalities.
- Agreeableness: strong Ndebele identity and a sensitive history (Gukurahundi) call for tact [8].
- Emotional stability: tolerance for economic stagnation and scarce jobs [8].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: sex between men and same-sex marriage are illegal (FCDO, Jul 2026) [1]. Female same-sex relations have been criminalised since 2006, and the Constitution defines marriage as between a man and a woman (2013) [10]. Society is conservative. A small underground scene exists in Harare, and "prosecutions are rare" [1]. Gender-marker changes are not allowed, according to the US State Department's 2023 human rights report as cited by [10]. An intersex-rights legal reform process began in July 2025 [10].
- **Political climate**: insulting the President is illegal. Demonstrations can turn violent, authorities have used force before, and journalism needs accreditation under a broad definition (FCDO, Jul 2026) [1]. France Diplomatie (15 Sep 2026) advises avoiding political activity or public statements, including online, and staying calm at police roadblocks, especially after dark [20]. Its heightened-vigilance alert of 10 Sep 2026 is worldwide, not specific to Zimbabwe [5].
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of diaspora or foreigners**: no reliable dated source found in this pass. Armed robberies occasionally target foreign residents (FCDO) [1].
- **Conflict zones**: no armed conflict and no recent history of terrorism, though attacks "cannot be ruled out" (FCDO, Jul 2026) [1]. Landmines remain along the northern border with Mozambique (France Diplomatie, zones updated 13 Mar 2026) [20]. The 1980s Gukurahundi remains a political wound in Matabeleland [8].
- **Women's daily experience**: FCDO has no women-specific section [1]. No dated GBV or child-marriage data could be read in this pass (not found).
- **Health alerts**: France Diplomatie flagged a rise in measles cases on 13 Mar 2026 [5]. FCDO notes "a number of disease outbreaks" [4].
