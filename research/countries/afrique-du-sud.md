## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: 12 official languages; English is the language of business, administration and most cities. Johannesburg first languages (2011 census): Zulu 23%, English 20%, Sesotho 10%, Setswana 8%, Afrikaans 7% [17]. Cape Town: Afrikaans 36%, Xhosa 29%, English 28% [16]. Durban (city): English 50%, Zulu 33% [18]. **French is essentially absent** outside the French schools, Alliance Française and the consular community (est. 10,000–12,000 French residents nationwide, Expat.com, Dec 2025 [6]).
- **Currency**: South African rand (ZAR). Mature card/contactless banking (ABSA, Standard Bank, Nedbank, FNB); bank fees are charged per operation and international transfers are expensive — Wise/Revolut suggested [6].
- **Entry and residence (a) French/EU passport**: visa-free up to 90 days for tourism/business; any move needs a residence permit applied for through Home Affairs (DHA) or a consulate, 3–6 months ahead: general work visa (job offer), Critical Skills visa (for listed sought-after skills; a points-based scoring system is reported for some work visas — unverified, Expat.com does not mention it), business, study, retirement (proof of ~R37,000/€1,870 monthly pension or passive income per person) and permanent residence [6][9]. Since 1 July 2026, everyone entering or leaving South Africa by air, land, sea or rail must submit an online traveller declaration (linked to SARS) before travelling; the passport needs 2 blank pages and must be valid 30 days beyond departure (FCDO entry requirements, accessed 14 Nov 2026 [1c]). Separately, an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) went live in mid-October 2025 for visa-required nationalities (phase 2 in late Nov 2025 added China, India, Indonesia, Mexico) — it does not concern EU visa-exempt passports [23]. A remote-work visa exists in law; the DHA page could not be fetched and the income threshold was **not verified** in this pass (unverified).
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**: South Africa has **no diaspora / right-of-return citizenship scheme** comparable to Ghana or Sierra Leone (not found). Citizenship by descent requires a South African parent [8]. Naturalisation: 5 years as permanent resident plus 4 of the last 8 years physically resident [8]. Dual nationality: since 6 May 2025 (*DA v Minister of Home Affairs*, Constitutional Court) South Africans no longer automatically lose citizenship when acquiring another one; those who lost it were reinstated [8]. Naturalising foreigners must show their current nationality allows dual nationality, or renounce it [8].
- **Big context for African diaspora readers**: 2026 saw a sharp wave of anti-immigrant violence targeting African nationals (see Legal & sensitive context) [11]. This matters more for a Black African-origin reader than any cost or climate factor.

## Cities

### Johannesburg

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed in centre ~R8,565 (~€460), outside centre ~R6,747 (~€360) per month (Numbeo, 13 Nov 2026 [4]). In the expat belt Sandton / Morningside a 1-bed runs R8,000–14,000 (€430–750); 3-bed in a secure complex R25,000–45,000 [7]. Cheap meal R200 (~€11); mid-range dinner for two R775 [4]. Single person excl. rent ~R13,300 (€712) [4]; Expat.com puts a comfortable single life at ~R25,000/month [7]. Add private security R500–1,000/month (single) [7].
- **Work & money**: economic capital of the country; Johannesburg Stock Exchange (largest on the continent); finance, tech, services; city GDP US$131 bn (2020) [17]. National unemployment 31.9% (2025, cited by Expat.com [7]) — competitive market, work permit required before any paid work [7]. Remote work feasible on home broadband (Numbeo: R651/month for unlimited 60 Mbps+ [4]).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Expat.com/lepetitjournal both carry "Entreprendre en Afrique du Sud" guides (undated) [7][10]. Named hubs, VC presence and crypto adoption figures: **not found in this pass** (search budget exhausted).
- **Daily infrastructure**: national load-shedding ended March 2024, briefly returned Jan–May 2025 (Stage 6 on 22 Feb 2025), none since 16 May 2025 [12]. Johannesburg's own water, electricity and road maintenance are reported as failing; presidential rescue plan launched March 2025; April 2026 warning that its unfunded budget could cost ~R8 bn of national transfers [17]. Car "essentially necessary"; fuel ~R23/l [7]. Gautrain rail links Sandton, OR Tambo airport and Pretoria [19]; FCDO advises not walking to/from Gautrain stations after dark [1b]. Ride-hailing: FCDO warns of fake taxi/ride-hailing drivers [1b] (Uber/Bolt operate — unverified in this pass).
- **Health & schools**: private care strongly preferred, paid upfront; GP R1,000–2,000 [6]. Reference hospitals: not verified this pass. **Lycée Français Jules-Verne** (AEFE, Johannesburg + Pretoria annex), ~R100,000–135,000/year; American International School of Johannesburg [6][7].
- **Safety**: FCDO (fetched 14 Nov 2026) — high crime rate; criminals have followed people from OR Tambo airport to their destinations; express kidnappings rising [1b]. Numbeo perception index 2026 ranks Johannesburg 5th worst in SA [13]. Hillbrow hijacked-building fire killed ≥76 people (31 Aug 2023) [17]. Expats cluster in gated complexes in the northern suburbs (Sandton, Morningside) [7]. CBD, Hillbrow, Jeppestown were the focus of anti-foreigner riots in 2019 [11].
- **Social life & vibe**: not verified in detail this pass. Largest, most diverse Black urban culture in the country (Zulu, Sesotho, Setswana speakers [17]); fast-paced business city.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: lepetitjournal.com Johannesburg edition (French community news, consul general interview) [10]; Expat.com South Africa forum (~24,600 members) [7]. African diaspora associations: not found this pass.
- **Climate in practice**: highland (1,753 m); hot summer days with near-daily afternoon thunderstorms Nov–Mar; dry sunny winters with cold nights and occasional frost (houses are rarely heated) [17].
- **Watch out**: crime and car-dependence; failing municipal services; Johannesburg was the epicentre of the May 2026 anti-immigrant killings (five Ethiopians killed on 3 May 2026) [11].

### Le Cap (Cape Town)

- **Cost of living**: by far the most expensive: 1-bed in centre ~R17,188 (~€920, range R14,000–20,000); outside centre ~R11,098 (~€590) (Numbeo, 13 Nov 2026 [3]). Expat neighbourhoods: Sea Point, Camps Bay [5]. Cheap meal R200; dinner for two R700; single person excl. rent ~R12,054 (€644) [3].
- **Work & money**: diversified economy — technology, finance, real estate, tourism [16]. Broadband ~R689/month for unlimited 60 Mbps+ (Numbeo [3]); "remote-worker magnet" reputation not sourced (unverified). Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: the Silicon Cape Initiative (founded 8 Oct 2009 by Vinny Lingham and Justin Stanford) is the long-standing tech community body [15]; the article itself flags thin angel funding and skills shortages [15]. Current startup counts, VC share and incubators: not found this pass.
- **Daily infrastructure**: same national grid situation [12]. Water: 2015–2018 drought halved daily consumption (1.2 bn → 516 m litres) [16] — residents remain water-conscious. MyCiTi buses; FCDO says not to use them to reach townships such as Khayelitsha [1b].
- **Health & schools**: **Lycée Français du Cap** (AEFE), ~R100,000–135,000/year; Deutsche Internationale Schule Kapstadt, International School of Cape Town, Reddam, Camps Bay School [5].
- **Safety**: FCDO — violent crime and targeted attacks increased on airport routes, especially for hire cars; stick to M3 and N2; attacks on hikers in Table Mountain National Park — hike in groups of 4+; wildfire risk [1b]. Western Cape provincial murder rate 58.3/100k (2025), driven by gang violence in Nyanga, Mitchells Plain, Delft, Bishop Lavis [13]. Strong contrast between the Atlantic Seaboard/City Bowl and the Cape Flats.
- **Social life & vibe**: Expat.com sells it as a life "between sea and mountains" with a lively cultural scene [5] — an expat-platform description, not an independent assessment. Detailed nightlife/music scene: not verified this pass.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: French community via lepetitjournal / Expat.com forum [6][10]. Specific groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: Mediterranean; dry hot summers, rainy windy winters June–September with Atlantic cold fronts [16].
- **Watch out**: rents ~2x Johannesburg/Durban; deeply spatially divided city; Western Cape murder rate is among the highest provinces [13]. (A widely repeated perception that Cape Town is less welcoming to Black residents and African migrants was removed here: no dated source found — to research in a second pass.)

### Durban

- **Cost of living**: cheapest of the three: 1-bed centre ~R7,125 (~€380), outside ~R6,061 (~€325) (Numbeo, 13 Nov 2026 [2]). Cheap meal R140; dinner for two R640; single excl. rent ~R10,425 (€557) [2]. Neighbourhoods: **Umhlanga** (most sought-after, highest rents), **Ballito** (40 km north, beach-town), **Berea / Morningside** (Florida Road restaurants), **Durban North**, **Westville** (families, green, good schools) [5d].
- **Work & money**: Port of Durban is the busiest in South Africa (~60% of national container traffic per Expat.com) [5d][18]; metro = 9.6% of national GDP (2018) [18]; finance, manufacturing, trade, transport, tourism, sugar refining [18]. Average net salary ~R21,211 (~€1,100) (Expat.com, Dec 2025) [5d].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found this pass.
- **Daily infrastructure**: national grid as above [12]. April 2022 floods killed ≥435 in KZN, closed the port and broke water feeders to Durban Heights reservoir [14]. July 2021 unrest started in KZN; up to 45,000 Durban businesses affected; 354 deaths nationally [20].
- **Health & schools**: **no French lycée** — CNED or boarding in Johannesburg/Cape Town; private schools Crawford International (La Lucia), Reddam House (Umhlanga) R84,000–130,000/year [5d].
- **Safety**: FCDO page gives no Durban-specific warnings [1b]. Numbeo perception index 2026 ranks Durban 6th in SA [13]; KZN murder rate 38.8/100k (2025) [13]. Expat.com names Umhlanga, La Lucia, Durban North, Westville, Hillcrest as lower-crime areas, without citing statistics (Dec 2025 [5d]). March 2019 anti-foreigner riots in Sydenham and Overport [11].
- **Social life & vibe**: beach city; large Indian-origin community (18% of metro, 2022) [18] shapes food (curry, bunny chow) and religion mix; Zulu culture dominant. Slower pace than Johannesburg (editorial).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found this pass.
- **Climate in practice**: humid subtropical; hot, humid summers with heaviest rain Oct–Mar; mild frost-free winters (~17 °C) [18]; 300+ sunny days [5d].
- **Watch out**: climate-disaster exposure (2022 floods); history of unrest (2021, 2019); no French school; smaller job market than Gauteng.

### Pretoria (extra city — why it matters)

Executive capital: 134 embassies and high commissions (second-largest diplomatic concentration in the world), University of Pretoria, UNISA, CSIR; 56 km from Johannesburg, linked by Gautrain [19]. Relevant for diplomats, NGO/international-organisation staff and families (Lycée Jules-Verne has a Pretoria annex [6]). Numbeo perception index ranks Pretoria 2nd worst in SA (2026) [13]. Rent/cost data: not fetched this pass.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of city conditions, not measurements. No city or quiz question measures a personality trait.

**Johannesburg**
- Openness: rewards people curious about a very mixed urban culture — multiple languages in daily use [17], Africa's financial capital [17].
- Conscientiousness: a city where planning matters: car-dependent [7], security routines (gated complexes, monthly security fees [7]), and failing municipal water/power that needs backups [17].
- Extraversion: networking-driven business city (JSE, finance [17]); suits people who build careers through contacts.
- Agreeableness: neutral; the city is transactional rather than village-like (editorial).
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): high requirement — FCDO crime warnings, airport follow-home robberies, kidnappings [1b], 2026 anti-immigrant violence [11].

**Le Cap**
- Openness: strong pull for nature/outdoor lovers and creatives — sea, mountain, cultural scene [5][16].
- Conscientiousness: water-saving culture since the 2015–18 drought [16]; hiking safety rules (groups of 4+) [1b].
- Extraversion: suits both — outdoor social life; nightlife not verified.
- Agreeableness: a polished, expat-friendly surface on the Atlantic Seaboard alongside sharp spatial inequality (editorial).
- Emotional stability: moderate — calmer daily life in the City Bowl, but high provincial murder rate concentrated in the Cape Flats [13].

**Durban**
- Openness: Zulu, Indian-South African and English cultures side by side [18].
- Conscientiousness: lower bureaucratic/career pressure than Gauteng (editorial).
- Extraversion: beach and outdoor social life year-round [18].
- Agreeableness: family-oriented suburbs (Westville) [5d].
- Emotional stability: exposure to floods [14] and unrest [20] history.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: legally the most protective country in Africa — constitutional ban on discrimination by sexual orientation (1996), same-sex marriage since 2006 (first in Africa), Hate Crimes and Hate Speech Act signed May 2024 [9b]. Public attitudes are more mixed: 57% support same-sex marriage (Ipsos, 2023) [9b]; historic reports of homophobic violence, including "corrective rape" and unsolved murders of gay men (2010–2013) [9b]. FCDO confirms same-sex activity legal and discrimination illegal (accessed 14 Nov 2026) [1b].
- **Xenophobia / treatment of African foreigners** (critical for this audience): recurring waves — 2008 (62 dead), 2015 (Durban then Johannesburg), 2019 (Durban, Johannesburg; ~640 Nigerians took repatriation flights) [11]. Operation Dudula vigilante campaigns since 2021–22, occupying a school in Jan 2026 [11]. **May 2026**: armed anti-"illegal foreigner" demonstrations; five Ethiopian migrants killed in Johannesburg on 3 May; Ghana evacuated ~300 citizens (announced 13 May, first batch arrived Accra 27 May); Nigeria announced a "voluntary operation" to return 150 citizens (4 May — announcement, completion not confirmed); five Mozambicans killed in Mossel Bay (2 Jun); South Africa reported 50,000+ foreign nationals deported/repatriated by mid-July 2026 [11]. FCDO notes xenophobic and anti-immigration marches have increased, mainly targeting African immigrants [1b]. A French passport-holder of African origin may still be read as an "African foreigner" in daily interactions — editorial inference, no survey found.
- **Racism / colourism**: post-apartheid spatial segregation persists (editorial, visible in neighbourhood crime contrasts [13]). No dated survey on treatment of Afro-European diaspora found.
- **Conflict zones**: none. Risk is crime and civil unrest (July 2021: 354 deaths [20]); FCDO warns protests can turn violent, never cross roadblocks [1b].
- **Women's daily experience**: FCDO reports solo and women travellers receiving unwanted attention and assaults [1b]. Rape recorded at ~63.9 per 100k (2026 figure in Wikipedia infobox, from SAPS data) [13]. Afrobarometer (16 Sep 2026): Southern Africans defend women's marital and reproductive autonomy [21]; South African women more educated than men but more often unemployed (14 Nov 2025) [21].
