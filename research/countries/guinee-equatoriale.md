## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages in daily use**: Spanish is the main official language and the language of school, administration and the street. French and Portuguese are also official (Portuguese since 2010) [4]. The Fang are about 85.7 % of the population and Fang is the majority language, and Bubi is spoken on Bioko [4]. Pichinglis (a Pidgin English) is the everyday lingua franca on Bioko, Malabo included [2]. Expat.com says French speakers "adapt easily" [10, undated]. In practice a French speaker with no Spanish will struggle in offices and administration (editorial inference).
- **Currency**: Central African CFA franc (XAF), pegged to the euro (1 EUR = 655.957 XAF) [4]. Cash dominates. Credit cards work only in a few big hotels (Hilton, Sofitel). ATMs are limited in Malabo and Bata and only some take foreign cards [1, FCDO updated 2026-01-08].
- **Economy**: the economy runs on oil and gas, and it is shrinking. GDP fell by an estimated 5.4–5.8 % in 2025, and a further 3.5 % fall is forecast for 2026–27. Poverty rose from 58.1 % (2022) to 61 % (2025). Unemployment is 13.7 % and only 17 % of jobs are formal [14, World Bank, latest dated item 2026-11-04].
- **Political context**: President Teodoro Obiang has been in power since 1979 and was re-elected in 2022 with 99.7 % of the vote, a result the opposition called fraudulent [4]. Freedom House scores the country 5/100, "Not Free" [12, FIW 2025]. On 2 January 2026 the capital officially moved to Ciudad de la Paz on the mainland [2][6].
- **(a) Entry and residence with a French or EU passport**: a visa is required, including for transit [5]. An e-Visa has existed since 1 July 2023. It is approved in 24–72 h, holders must arrive at Malabo airport, and the fee is reportedly USD 75 (unverified on that page) [5]. Short-stay visas are valid for 90 days [1]. A yellow-fever certificate is required, and the passport must be valid for 6 months after arrival [1]. Longer stays are handled at local police stations, and a one-year residence permit from the Ministry of National Security removes the need for a visa [1, FCDO 2026-01-08]. Make sure your visa is issued and your passport stamped at the border; the FCDO reports travellers delayed or threatened with detention when this was skipped [1]. Citizens of CEMAC countries (Cameroon, Gabon, Congo, Chad, CAR) enter visa-free with their ID card [5]. Even so, Cameroonians were expelled in April 2025 without notice to their embassy [15, Amnesty].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**:
  - A child of a parent who is Equatoguinean by birth is a national wherever the child is born [7].
  - Dual nationality has been allowed since the 2011 regulation (Law 3/2011). However, a national can lose citizenship for holding another nationality "without government authorization" [7, citing Manby 2018 and IRB Canada 2017].
  - Someone with a parent born in the country can naturalise after 1 year of residence [7].
  - Ordinary naturalisation requires 40 years of residence, one of the strictest rules in Africa [7].
  - No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found.
  - Spain's Supreme Court ruled in 2020 that Equatoguineans have no Spanish nationality of origin [7].

## Cities

### Malabo

The former capital and the commercial, financial and oil-services hub, on Bioko island. Population was about 297,000 in 2018 [2].

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bed rent: not found. Numbeo blocked access, and the Expat.com housing thread gives no figures (posts from about 2012–2023) [11].
  - The only general estimate found is USD 1,800 a month for one person and USD 4,500 for a family of four, from Expat.com, undated, with no method given [10]. Treat it as a rough indication.
  - An old forum post (about 2013) notes that "everything seems to be geared towards the oil market" [11]. Malabo is often described as expensive for expats (unverified: no dated source with prices was reachable).
  - Neighbourhoods named in sources: Ela Nguema, Abayak, Sipopo (a luxury resort zone built for the 2011 AU summit), Los Ángeles [2][3]. Malabo II and Caracolas could not be confirmed in fetched sources.
- **Work & money**:
  - Oil and gas services and US oil companies offshore, public administration, trade, fishing, and cocoa and coffee exports [2]. Most expat jobs are in oil [10].
  - The economy is in recession [14], so expect fewer contracts.
  - Remote work is technically possible, but connectivity is limited (see below). Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - No startup hub, incubator or tech ecosystem was found in fetched sources.
  - Agenda 2035 names a "digital economy" as a goal [14].
  - Company registration, investor presence, and fintech or crypto adoption: not found. The government opened an investigation into betting houses and casinos in November 2026 [13].
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Power and water are national problems: 24 % of people lack electricity and 32.4 % lack piped water [14].
  - The government approved an emergency national street-lighting plan in November 2026 [13].
  - Internet users were 60.4 % of the population in January 2025, but only 7.8 % were social-media users [17, DataReportal, published 2025-03-03]. The Africa Coast to Europe (ACE) submarine cable lands in the country [8].
  - Mobile money: not found.
  - Transport is by buses and taxis [2]. Robberies of taxi passengers have been reported, so use pre-booked taxis or taxi apps [1]. No specific ride-hailing app was named.
  - Direct flights to Madrid, Paris, Frankfurt, Casablanca, Istanbul, Abidjan and Addis Ababa [3].
- **Health & schools**:
  - La Paz hospital in Malabo is the one facility the FCDO describes as decent. Elsewhere care is poor, and serious cases are evacuated to Europe. Ambulance: 3101 [1, 2026-01-08].
  - Schools: Lycée Français Le Concorde (French embassy listing) [16]. Colegio Español Don Bosco, Equatorial Guinea Turkish International College and Royal International College [2]. UNGE university [2].
  - Institut Français en Guinée Équatoriale (IFGE) [16].
- **Safety** [1, FCDO updated 2026-01-08]:
  - Crime ranges from petty theft to serious violent offences, and risk is higher in remote parts of town after dark. Robberies and violent assaults on taxi passengers are reported in Malabo and Bata, especially at night; avoid shared taxis with strangers.
  - Police and military roadblocks are common. Refusing ID checks can mean detention, and bribe requests are reported regularly; the FCDO advises asking for a ticket naming the offence and paying it at a local court.
  - Photographing government or military buildings or airports is illegal and can lead to arrest.
  - You must carry your passport or residence permit (or copies) at all times.
  - France Diplomatie has kept its page current (last update 2026-09-10), with a general "reinforced vigilance" alert for French nationals [18]. Its detailed zone map could not be extracted.
  - Neighbourhood-level nuance: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - The city is Catholic-dominated, around the Cathedral of Santa Isabel (damaged by fire in January 2020). There is a mosque (2015) and a modern-art museum [2][3]. Nationally, about 88.7 % of people are Christian (2020 figure) [4].
  - Malabo Stadium (15,250 seats) [2].
  - A new Sofitel Presidencial hotel opens on 1 December 2026 [13].
  - Nightlife and music scene: not found in fetched sources.
- **Diaspora & expat community**:
  - The Expat.com Equatorial Guinea forum has 5,828 members and is active in 2026, with threads on housing, teaching jobs and driving licences [10][19].
  - The largest foreign communities are Cameroonian, Nigerian, Gabonese, Chinese and French [10, undated].
  - French residents are encouraged to register with the consulate [16].
  - No diaspora returnee association was found.
- **Climate in practice**: very wet and cloudy, about 1,850 mm of rain a year. The only dry spell is December–January. It rains from March to November, peaking in September–October [2]. Nights stay at 20–21 °C [2]. Malabo is one of the most lightning-prone capitals [2].
- **Watch out**:
  - Very expensive for what you get.
  - Spanish is essential.
  - The authoritarian state polices photos and ID strictly.
  - Card payments barely work.
  - The oil recession is shrinking jobs.
  - Losing capital status from 2026 [6] may shift ministries away over time.

### Bata

The largest city and port, on the mainland coast (Río Muni). It had 309,000–347,000 people at the 2015 census, and around 800,000 in the wider urban area in 2021 [3b][9].

- **Cost of living**: not found (no Numbeo or forum data reached). Probably cheaper than Malabo (editorial, low confidence).
- **Work & money**: the deep-water port handles timber exports and has an adjoining industrial zone. It was expanded with Chinese loans [3b][9]. It is also a gateway to the Ciudad de la Paz construction projects [13]. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: water was cut for several weeks in September 2019, and power cuts were regular (older source, flagged) [9]. Ferries run to Malabo and Douala, and there is an airport north of the city [3b]. On the mainland, public transport is very limited [1]. Ride-hailing: not found.
- **Health & schools**: École Française de Bata [16]. There are a UNGE medical faculty, a UNED branch and a campus of Colegio Nacional Enrique Nvó Okenve [9]. A Spanish cultural centre has been active since 2001 [9]. Reference hospital in Bata: not found in fetched sources.
- **Safety**:
  - The same FCDO crime and checkpoint advice applies, and night road travel is discouraged [1, 2026-01-08].
  - On 7 March 2021, explosions at the Nkoantoma military barracks on the southeast edge of the city killed at least 107 people and injured at least 615 [3c]. 243 structures were badly damaged or destroyed [3c].
- **Social life & vibe**: the seafront and the Torre de la Libertad, Estadio de Bata, the market, and a nightlife scene that Wikipedia mentions without detail [3b]. Mostly Christian (Catholic, Protestant and Assemblies of God churches) [3b].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found beyond the national Expat.com forum [10].
- **Climate in practice**: tropical monsoon. The rainiest months are April–May and October–November (October averages about 457 mm), and the drier spell is July–August [3b]. This is a different rhythm from Malabo.
- **Watch out**:
  - Less information is available, so plan with less certainty.
  - Infrastructure is fragile (water and power).
  - Public transport inland is limited.
  - French-language schooling options are thinner than in Malabo.

### Ciudad de la Paz (extra, for awareness only)

Ciudad de la Paz has been the official capital since a decree of 2 January 2026, which gives state bodies one year to relocate [6]. It was designed for 160,000–200,000 people but had about 2,000 residents in 2022 [6]. It has a university (AAUCA), the Grand Hotel Djibloho and a golf course [6]. The government approved ministry-building designs in November 2026 [13]. It is not yet a place to "live" for most people, but it may matter for civil-service and construction jobs. Do not add it to the product yet.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No Hofstede, GLOBE or Afrobarometer data for Equatorial Guinea was found; Afrobarometer does not appear to survey the country (not verified, no search available). These notes are editorial readings of the facts above. They describe who may feel comfortable, and they do not measure anyone.

**Malabo**
- **Openness**: suits people curious about living in Africa's only Spanish-speaking country [4], and comfortable working in Spanish and Pichinglis [2]. The arts scene is small (one modern-art museum, IFGE) [3][16].
- **Conscientiousness**: helps a lot. You need to carry ID at all times, keep visa and residence papers impeccable, and handle cash-based payments [1].
- **Extraversion**: social life runs through the oil-expat circuit and big hotels (Hilton, Sofitel) [1][13]. Independent nightlife evidence: not found.
- **Agreeableness**: a conservative, hierarchical society with fast-moving security forces, where public dissent and photography are risky [1][12]. People who adapt to rules more than they challenge them will have an easier time.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: needed for checkpoints, bribe requests [1], the recession [14], and medical evacuation as the fallback for serious illness [1].

**Bata**
- **Openness**: a mainland, Fang-majority port city that is less internationalised than Malabo [3b][4].
- **Conscientiousness**: water and power cuts [9] reward people who plan (tanks, generators).
- **Extraversion**: a seafront and a market life [3b]. Details: not found.
- **Agreeableness**: the same conservative codes as in Malabo [1].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: thinner services and limited transport [1][9] demand tolerance for friction.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex relations are legal, but there is no partnership recognition [1][20]. Wikipedia reports that Article 364(2) of the November 2022 Penal Code bans employment discrimination by sexual orientation [20], while the FCDO (2026-01-08) says there is no legal protection against discrimination by sexual orientation [1]. The two sources disagree; assume no protection in practice.
  - The FCDO says "society is very conservative and discrimination can be a problem for those open about their sexual identity" and that public displays of affection may attract negative attention [1, 2026-01-08]. Wikipedia reports homosexuality is socially seen "as a pathology" [20].
  - In 2014, four men were paraded on TV. In August 2023, activist Trifonia Melibea Obono was detained, and arrests have reportedly risen since [20, Wikipedia, sources dated].
- **Foreigners and diaspora**:
  - In April 2025, Cameroonians were expelled without embassy notice, in breach of CEMAC free-movement rules [15].
  - In May 2025 the vice-president confirmed talks to receive people deported by the US, and the first group arrived at the end of November 2025 [15, Amnesty]. A USD 7.5 million US payment was reported on 10 November 2025 [4].
  - Specific reports of colourism or treatment of the Afro-descendant diaspora: not found.
- **Political repression**: Freedom House scores the country 5/100 [12, FIW 2025].
  - In July 2024, about three dozen (37, per Amnesty) Annobón islanders protesting mining damage were arrested, and internet access on the island was restricted in August 2024 [12]. They were pardoned on 4 June 2025 [15].
  - Activist Joaquín Elo Ayeto (civil-society platform Somos+) was arrested on 1 August 2024 [15].
  - Avoid rallies and demonstrations [1].
- **Conflict**: no armed conflict and no recent terrorism [1, 2026-01-08]. In 2024 Russian Wagner Group personnel were reported to have entered the country at Obiang's request [4, reported 2024].
- **Women's daily experience**: no reliable dated source was found. Freedom House gives 0/4 on equal treatment, without narrative [12].
