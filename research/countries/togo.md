## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: French is the only official language; Ewe and Kabiye are recognised national languages [8]. In practice Ewe (and Mina, a related coastal language) dominates Lomé and the south, Kabiye the Kara region — the second part is editorial knowledge, not verified in a source this pass. Lomé's population is predominantly Ewe [9].
- **Currency**: West African CFA franc (XOF), pegged to the euro (1 EUR = 655.957 XOF) [8]. France Diplomatie notes that only Visa cards work for cash withdrawals and payments (updated 2026-09-15) [3] — Mastercard holders should plan accordingly.
- **Population**: 8,095,498 at the 2022 census; Lomé ~1.5 M (city) / ~2.2 M (metro) in 2022 [8][9]. Religion (2022 census): Christian 49.2 %, Muslim 18.6 %, traditional faiths 17.0 %, no religion 9.4 % [8].
- **Politics (context for daily life)**: a constitution adopted 6 May 2024 switched Togo to a parliamentary system; since May 2025 Faure Gnassingbé, in power since 2005, has been President of the Council of Ministers, with a six-year term renewable indefinitely [8]. Protests in June 2025 were violently repressed and internet / social media were disrupted from late June through September 2025 (Amnesty report 2025/26) [10].
- **(a) French / EU passport holders**: a visa is required, applied for online at voyage.gouv.tg at least 5 days before departure. There has been no express or on-arrival visa since 12 April 2024. Visas issued for 48 hours or 7 days can be extended, up to one month, at the passport and foreigners service in Lomé. For stays over 90 days you need a long-stay visa, then a **carte de séjour** from the Service des passeports et des étrangers (DGDN). A yellow-fever certificate is mandatory (France Diplomatie, updated 2026-09-15) [2]. The UK FCDO page changed its visa-application wording on 2026-11-04 [4]. Work: the employer requests a hiring authorisation and the labour directorate validates the contract; work visas last up to 2 years and can be renewed once (Expat.com, **2017 — old**) [13].
- **(b) Diaspora with Togolese roots**: under the 1978 Nationality Code (amended 1980), a child born abroad to a Togolese father is Togolese automatically. The 1992 Constitution and the 2007 Children's Code also let nationality pass through the mother, which conflicts with the older code. Dual nationality is formally prohibited, but in practice the strict renunciation rule targets mainly naturalised citizens. Naturalisation needs 5 years' residence (Wikipedia summary of the legal texts, accessed 2026-11-14 — verify with a lawyer) [11]. **No Afro-descendant or right-of-return scheme found** (no equivalent of Benin's law or Ghana's citizenship drive). Republicoftogo.com reports a redesigned national diaspora mobilisation strategy (2026-04-25) and "Togolese abroad at the heart of the development strategy" (2026-04-10) [17]. Faure Gnassingbé attended Ghana's Diaspora Summit in Accra in December 2025 [17]. The Haut Conseil des Togolais de l'Extérieur (HCTE) and diaspora-vote details: not found this pass.

## Cities

### Lomé

- **Cost of living**:
  - Meals: an inexpensive restaurant meal is about 1,700 XOF (~2.6 EUR), a mid-range meal for two about 18,000 XOF (~27 EUR) (Numbeo, last updated 2026-10-02, only 8 contributors in 12 months — low confidence) [5].
  - Rent: Numbeo has **no 1-bed rent figure** for Lomé [5]. Expat.com (guide updated 2025-12-08) gives neighbourhood ranges:
    - Tokoin (diplomatic area, secure): villas 800,000–1,500,000 XOF (1,220–2,290 EUR)
    - Nyékonakpoè (family-friendly, near international schools): 500,000–1,000,000 XOF (760–1,525 EUR)
    - Adidogomé (growing, calm): 400,000–800,000 XOF (610–1,220 EUR)
    - Bè: central, lively, cheaper but noisier
    - Agoè: northern outskirts, green and cheaper, car needed [6]

    These are expat villa/house ranges, not 1-bed flats. An older Expat.com page (**2017**) said furnished flats started around 300 EUR and an unfurnished central studio cost about 120 EUR [12]. A realistic middle-class 1-bed figure for 2025–26 was **not found**.
  - Monthly budget: Expat.com is internally inconsistent. Its summary box says ~800 EUR/month for one person and ~2,300 EUR for a family of four, but its detailed tables give ~1,042,500 XOF (~1,589 EUR) for one person (including a 2-bed rent of 400,000 XOF) and ~3,060,000 XOF (~4,665 EUR) for a family of four (guide updated 2025-12-08) [6]. Treat the lower figures as a local-style budget and the higher ones as an expat-style budget.
  - Utilities and internet: utilities for 85 m² are ~85,600 XOF (~130 EUR); 60 Mbps+ broadband is ~25,000 XOF (~38 EUR) [5].
  - Lease terms: 2–3 months' deposit, rent often paid quarterly or half-yearly in advance, 3-month notice [6].
  - Imported goods (cheese, wine, charcuterie) cost 2–3× European prices [6].
- **Work & money**:
  - Economy: the Port of Lomé is the country's only commercial port and a transit hub for Mali, Niger and Burkina Faso, with a free zone dating from 1968 [9]. BOAD and the BCEAO national office are based in Lomé [9]. Ecobank is a major employer — Togo First (2026-11-13) presents it as the first bank to connect business payments to the BCEAO instant-payment system PI-SPI [14]; Ecobank's HQ location could not be re-verified this pass (its site failed to load).
  - Salaries: official unemployment 3.7 % (2023); minimum wage 52,500 XOF/month (~80 EUR) since January 2023; average net salary ~150,000 XOF/month (Expat.com, 2025 data) [6]. Numbeo shows 175,000 XOF (2026-10) [5]. These numbers explain why local salaries rarely fund an expat lifestyle.
  - Remote work: Lomé is a landing point of Google's Equiano subsea cable (landed 2022-03-18) [15]. Lomé-specific remote-work data (coworking spaces, typical speeds): not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - Business creation: 11,978 businesses were created January–September 2026, +7.9 % (Togo First, 2026-10-15) [14].
  - Startups and hubs: Gozem, a super-app for rides, delivery, gas delivery and payments (Gozem Money), lists a company address in Nyékonakpoè (Boulevard du 13 Janvier) and operates in Togo, Benin, Cameroon, Gabon and Congo [16]. It launched in Togo in 2018 and raised a 30 M USD Series B (15 M equity, 15 M debt) in February 2025 (TechCrunch, 2025-02-26) [26]. WoeLab, a Lomé fab lab and incubator founded in 2012 by Sénamé Koffi Agbodjinou, is described as Togo's first incubation lab (TechPoint Africa, 2018-09-26 — old, and known from a search summary only; current activity unverified) [27]. Afric'innov is mentioned as an entrepreneurship-support network (2026-11-07) [14].
  - Mobile money: Mixx (by Yas, the Togocom group) aims to become a financial super-app (2026-11-09) [14]. The Moov Africa (Flooz) figures were not verified.
  - Rules and warnings: the finance minister warned against unlicensed savings and loan structures (2026-11-13) [17].
  - Not found this pass: incubator names, investor presence, crypto adoption and company-registration time and cost (the CFE site did not resolve).
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Power: a 2026-11-14 republicoftogo.com headline, "Puissance installée n'est pas puissance disponible", suggests a live power-supply debate [17]; outage frequency was not found. Expat.com advises checking the backup generator or solar, AC and water pressure before signing a lease [6].
  - Transport: zémidjans (moto-taxis) are more common than cars [9]. France Diplomatie advises avoiding zems (accident risk) and using yellow-plated taxis [1]; Belgium reportedly recommends Gozem car taxis (unverified — search summary only) [0]. A commuter rail line, Blueline, has run since 2014 [9].
- **Health & schools**:
  - Public: CHU Sylvanus Olympio is the reference public hospital, with limited resources [6].
  - Private clinics: Polyclinique Internationale Saint-Joseph, Clinique Biasa, Clinique Soins et Santé and Hôpital International de Lomé [6].
  - Insurance: Expat.com recommends international health insurance (1,500–5,000 EUR/year) or the CFE for French nationals [6]. Afrobarometer (2025-06-20) says most Togolese worry about affording care [18].
  - French-curriculum schools: École Française de Lomé and École Arc-en-Ciel, ~2–4 M XOF/year (3,050–6,100 EUR).
  - English-curriculum schools: American International School, British School of Lomé and EISL, ~3–5 M XOF/year [6].
- **Safety**:
  - France Diplomatie (2026-09-15): Lomé is in the yellow zone (heightened vigilance). Avoid the seafront and the beach from sunset, because knife attacks have been reported near the seafront hotels. Avoid walking or riding a motorbike at night, and keep car doors locked [1].
  - UK FCDO: violent crime, theft and pickpocketing are common, particularly on beaches and in markets; carjackings and staged accidents have been reported [4b].
  - Belgium (2026-10-06): protests in Lomé can turn violent and cut communications [19].
  - Amnesty: seven deaths were reported in Lomé during the 26–30 June 2025 repression [10].
  - Neighbourhood nuance: Tokoin is described as secure (diplomatic area) [6]; neighbourhood crime data were not found.
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Markets: the Grand Marché is tied to the "Nana Benz" cloth traders; the Akodésséwa fetish market is in the east [9].
  - Religion: daily life is mostly Christian, with mosques and a strong traditional-religion presence (17 % nationally) [8][9].
  - Not found this pass: nightlife, the music scene and sport specifics.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: the Expat.com Togo forum is a community source, not a source of facts [6]. Facebook / WhatsApp groups, returnee associations and events: not found (search budget exhausted).
- **Climate in practice**:
  - Lomé is drier than its latitude suggests: ~830 mm of rain a year.
  - Main rains run April–June, with a second rainy season in September–October; December–February is driest.
  - Temperatures average ~27 °C, peaking around 30 °C in February–April [9].
- **Watch out**:
  - Political climate: there is little room for public criticism, and both social-media arrests and internet cuts happened in 2025 [10].
  - Media: RFI and France 24 were suspended from June 2025 and were still suspended at the end of 2025 [10].
  - Safety: the sea has strong currents, and there are knife attacks on the seafront at night [1].
  - Cards: only Visa cards work [3].
  - Paperwork: there are no visas on arrival [2].
  - Local salaries are low compared with expat costs [5][6].

### Kpalimé

- **Basics**: ~120 km north-west of Lomé and ~15 km from the Ghana border, at a crossroads of routes to Ghana and other regions [20][21]. It is Togo's fourth-largest town (75,084 in 2010; 101,088 in 2023 according to French Wikipedia) [20][21].
- **Cost of living**: no figures found. Expat.com only says rural prices are "nettement plus bas" (2017) [12]. A local 1-bed rent was not found.
- **Work & money**: the area around Kpalimé supplies over 80 % of Togo's coffee and cocoa [21]. Crafts are a pillar: 36 artisan workshops and shops plus an artisan training college [20]. Remote workers would depend on mobile data; city-level internet data were not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: not found. It is ~2 hours by road from Lomé (editorial estimate, not sourced).
- **Health & schools**: the only service found is a community psychiatry centre run by the Association Saint-Camille-de-Lellis [21]. Reference hospital and international schools: not found; assume Lomé for serious care (editorial).
- **Safety**: in France Diplomatie's yellow zone (2026-09-15) [1]. Neither France nor Belgium singles out the Plateaux region [1][19]. France Diplomatie (2026-09-15) advises against night driving: poor lighting and signage, and rare but real armed road robberies, especially in the north [1].
- **Social life & vibe**: a nature town — Mont Agou (Togo's highest point), Mont Kloto with views to Lake Volta, the Kpimé, Tomegbé and Kpoeta waterfalls, the Misahöhe forest reserve, a 1913 neo-Gothic cathedral and the Dzogbégan Benedictine monastery [20][21]. Nightlife and food specifics: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: wetter than Lomé — ~1,442 mm a year over ~111 rain days, peaking mid-year and in September; driest in December–January [20]. The forested hills are greener and cooler (editorial — exact temperatures not found).
- **Watch out**: thin data for this dossier; distance from the airport and from specialised healthcare; heavy-rain months.

### Kara (additional — second political pole of the north)

- **Why it matters**: regional capital and home of the Université de Kara (founded 2004). It sits on the Lomé–Ouagadougou road ~413 km north of Lomé, and its growth is linked to the late President Eyadéma [22]. It is relevant for academics, civil servants and families from the Kabiye area.
- **Climate**: ~1,300 mm of rain, June–September; almost no rain November–February; maximums around 38 °C in March [22].
- **Social life**: the July traditional wrestling tournament (an initiation rite, known locally as Evala — name not confirmed in the source) [22]. Population 94,878 (2010) [22].
- **Safety**: Belgium advises vigilance in the Kara region (2026-10-06) [19]. France Diplomatie classes it yellow; the Savanes region further north is orange, with a state of emergency until 2027-03-13 [1]. The US advisory reportedly says "Do Not Travel" north of Kandé, a town in the Kara region north of Kara city (unverified, 2026-03-09) [0b]. The FCDO notes a history of violent demonstrations in nearby Sokodé, Bafilo and Mango [4b].
- **Cost, work, health, internet**: not found this pass.

### Aného (additional — coastal small-town option near Lomé)

- **Why it matters**: 45 km east of Lomé, between the Atlantic and Lake Togo, close to the Benin border. It is a former colonial capital, still an intellectual and Vodun centre [23], and a possible quiet coastal base within commuting distance of Lomé (editorial).
- Population ~25,400 (**2005 — old**) [23]. Other data not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No published regional personality study for Togo was found this pass. Hofstede does not publish a Togo-specific score; Afrobarometer gives values-adjacent data (69 % say police and courts must do more to protect women from discrimination; about half prefer traditional dispute resolution, 2025-06-12) [18]. The lines below describe **who tends to be comfortable** in each place, inferred from cited facts. They are not trait measurements.

**Lomé**
- Openness: rewards people curious about layered religious life (Christian majority, strong Vodun and traditional presence, fetish market) [8][9] and about a cross-border trade culture (port, Nana Benz traders) [9].
- Conscientiousness: helps with paperwork — visa-only entry with no on-arrival option, a carte de séjour, employer-led work permits, Visa-only cards [2][3][13].
- Extraversion: market-and-street city life [9]; nightlife was not documented this pass.
- Agreeableness: social codes favour discretion and conformity — modest dress is advised [3], same-sex affection draws hostility [25], and public political criticism is risky [10].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for zem traffic risk [1], power-supply uncertainty [17], and occasional protests with internet cuts [10][19].

**Kpalimé**
- Openness: suits nature lovers, craft-curious people and people drawn to spiritual retreats (monastery, waterfalls, forest reserve) [20][21].
- Conscientiousness: self-reliance helps with distance from specialised care and services (inferred, low confidence).
- Extraversion: lower-key small town, around 100k people [21].
- Agreeableness: an agricultural and craft community where integration likely runs through local ties (editorial, not sourced).
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): fewer urban stressors, but heavy rains [20].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - The law: same-sex acts are criminal under Article 393 of the 2015 Penal Code — 1–3 years in prison and a 1–3 M XOF fine. Article 392 (public decency) criminalises speech, writing or images seen as offending public decency, and is cited as a tool against LGBTQ advocacy. A TikTok influencer was convicted in Lomé in June 2023 for offending public decency, though the source does not name the article used. LGBTQ groups can register only as health organisations (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14) [24].
  - Official advice: Belgium (2026-10-06) cites up to three years in prison and warns that public affection can provoke violent reactions [25]. The FCDO says penalties are severe [4b].
- **Political freedoms / conflict**:
  - Repression: protests on 5–6 June 2025 led to 81 arrests and reported torture. During the 26–30 June repression in Lomé, civil society reported 7 deaths (bodies found in rivers). Internet was disrupted from late June to September 2025, and RFI / France 24 were suspended (Amnesty 2025/26) [10].
  - The north: jihadist attacks have hit the northern border since November 2021 [1]. The Savanes region is in a state of emergency until 2027-03-13, and foreigners need written authorisation from the Ministry of Armed Forces [1][19].
  - Red zones: France formally advises against the Burkina–Togo–Benin and Burkina–Togo–Ghana tri-border areas (2026-09-15) [1].
  - UK: no travel within 30 km of the Burkina border except Dapaong and the N1, and essential travel only in the rest of the Savanes (updated 2026-11-04) [4]. The FCDO safety subpage still says 5 km — an internal inconsistency [4b]. The US State Department reportedly keeps Togo at Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution) with "Do Not Travel" for areas north of Kandé, which would include the far north of the Kara region (unverified — search summary only, page version 2026-03-09; the page itself returned 403 on two attempts) [0b].
- **Racism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners**: no reliable dated source found this pass. France Diplomatie warns about rising romance and phishing scams (2026-09-15) [1].
- **Women's daily experience**: 69 % of Togolese say police and courts must do more against discrimination and harassment of women. People strongly support keeping pregnant girls in school (Afrobarometer, 2026-07-22) [18]. No women-specific travel advice was found in the FCDO or Belgian pages [4b][19].
