> **Research limitation:** the shared web-search budget for this workflow ran out before this dossier began, so it is built on direct fetches of official pages (France Diplomatie, GOV.UK), Wikipedia, Numbeo and the internal search of the Guinean news site Guineenews. ## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: French is the language of administration, business and the press (all Guineenews sources are in French). On the street, Susu (Soussou) dominates in Conakry and on the coast, Pular (Peul) in the Fouta Djallon (Labé), and Maninka (Malinké) in Upper Guinea (Kankan). Language distribution comes from general knowledge and is not sourced. The Mandinka majority in Kankan is sourced [9].
- **Currency**: Guinean franc (GNF). You may not leave the country with more than 100,000 GNF in cash. Amounts over 10,000 USD/EUR must be declared, and French nationals are "regularly questioned" about this at the airport [3].
- **Political frame**: Mamady Doumbouya took power in the 2021 coup. He won the 28 December 2025 presidential election with 86.72% of the vote and was sworn in as a civilian president on 17 January 2026. The main opposition boycotted the vote, and the UN rights chief cited intimidation and enforced disappearances. The AU lifted sanctions on 23 January 2026 and ECOWAS on 30 January 2026 [10].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**: a visa is required. You apply online in advance on the border police (DCPAF) platform (paf.gov.gn/visa). Upload a passport scan, photo, COVID vaccination certificate, yellow-fever certificate, hotel booking or host attestation (with the host's ID), and for business trips an invitation letter or mission order. The embassy then issues an entry authorisation ("visa volant"), sometimes after an interview. You complete biometrics at the DCPAF counters on arrival, and the visa is issued at Conakry airport. Residents must carry their papers at all times. Foreigners without a residence permit (and a work permit where needed) "can be arrested". France Diplomatie does not describe the residence-permit procedure, so that is not found [3]. GOV.UK confirms the e-visa route, a passport valid 6 months beyond arrival, and a yellow-fever requirement (page undated) [6].
- **(b) Diaspora with Guinean roots**: nationality passes by descent to a child of at least one Guinean parent, born anywhere. Naturalisation normally needs 5 years of residence. The 2019 Civil Code (Loi L/2019/0035/AN) now carries the nationality rules. According to the Wikipedia summary, dual nationality is **not** allowed for people who were Guinean at birth. This needs checking against the Civil Code text before publishing, because practice is reportedly more tolerant [11]. No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found.

## Cities

### Conakry

Capital, about 3.4 million people (3,407,327 at the 2025 census), roughly a fifth of the country. It sits on a peninsula. The five historic communes are Kaloum (the downtown business and ministry district), Dixinn (university, embassies), Ratoma ("known for its nightlife"), Matam and Matoto (the airport). The 2025 census counts 13 communes, after Lambanyi, Sonfonia, Gbessia and others were split off. Addresses and press reports now use these newer names [8].

- **Cost of living**:
  - Guineenews (Jan 2026) quotes a small 2-bedroom flat at **2.5–3 million GNF/month** (about €250–300) in sought-after areas: Kaloum, Dixinn, Lambanyi and Ratoma. A studio in popular neighbourhoods costs about 1.5 million GNF (about €150). The article describes a housing crisis driven by speculation [12]. Euro conversions assume about 10,000 GNF = €1 (approximate, not sourced).
  - Numbeo (last updated 2 July 2025, **only 4 contributors**, so low confidence): a 1-bedroom costs $510/month in the centre and $318 outside. A cheap meal is $4.63. Broadband of 60 Mbps or more costs $185/month [5].
  - The neighbourhoods expats usually pick are Kaloum, Dixinn/Landréah, Kipé, Taouyah, Lambanyi and Nongo. France Diplomatie advises against living **east of Kipé and Taouyah** without strong security measures [2].
  - Monthly budget: not found.
- **Work & money**:
  - Port-based economy (alumina, bananas), plus food processing, cement and fuel [8].
  - The big story is **Simandou**, the iron-ore megaproject. The first ore ship left Morebaya (also spelled Moribaya) port for China on 2 December 2025 with about 200,000 tonnes [17]. It reached China on 17 January 2026, and about 1.6 Mt had been shipped by the end of Q1 2026 [17e]. SimFer is aiming for 120 Mt per year of export capacity [17b]. Rio Tinto SimFer confirmed the project had entered its operational phase in March 2026 [17c]. A "Simandou Mining Summit" was held in November 2026 [17d].
  - Mining, logistics (MSC), telecoms, banks and NGOs are the realistic employers for skilled returnees. Salaries: not found.
  - Remote work depends on a generator or inverter backup (see power cuts below).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - Ecosystem is small and mostly driven by competitions run by large companies:
    - Orange Summer Challenge (Nov 2026 winner: Présence.io) [14a]
    - Orange POESAM social-entrepreneur prize (2026 winner: Solibox) [14b]
    - Rio Tinto "Future Champions" hackathon, co-sponsored by TotalEnergies and Ecobank (2026) [14c]
    - TotalEnergies Startupper of the Year [14]
  - Events:
    - first Startup Week Guinée (Nov 2024)
    - Guinea Fintech Week, 3rd edition, 22–23 Oct 2025. Topics were payments, blockchain, digital lending, regtech and **crypto panels**, with about 25 startups and 1,500 participants expected [15]
    - Conakry hosted Transform Africa Summit 2025 / Smart Africa, with "590 startups" reported [13]
  - State digital hubs: 6 delivered, 20 under construction (as of Jan 2026), for example the ANSUTEN hub in Coyah [13][14].
  - Investor presence and company-registration ease: not found. Crypto adoption figures: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - **Power cuts are the defining daily problem.** In May 2026, EDG (the power utility) announced a maintenance cut from 08:00 to 16:00 across Gbessia, Dixinn, Matam and Ratoma. Residents of Gbessia and Matam also described nearly daily unannounced cuts, with power going off around 10:00–11:00 and returning around 18:00–19:00 (Dabondi). A resident of Kaporo Rail reported nearly a month of nights without power (18:00–08:00) [16].
  - Anti-load-shedding protests blocked the Cosa–Koloma road on 5 Sept 2026 [16b]. The energy minister spoke publicly on the cuts in July 2026 [16c].
  - Water and power cuts are a long-running problem in the city (Wikipedia) [8].
  - Internet: according to the government's 2025 review, the national backbone grew from 50 to 200 Gbps, mobile-internet prices fell 25%, an Internet Exchange Point was added and a Tier III data centre opened [13]. The ACE submarine cable lands in Conakry [18].
  - Mobile money exists (Orange Money, plus Orange Microfinance Guinée), but adoption figures were not found.
  - Ride-hailing apps: not found (no Yango coverage on Guineenews).
  - GOV.UK advises against moto-taxis and shared taxis or tuk-tuks [7].
  - Money in practice: cards are rarely accepted outside banks, and ATMs are few and unreliable. Changing money on the street is illegal and has led to arrests, so use banks or official bureaux (GOV.UK, Aug 2026) [7].
- **Health & schools**:
  - The Ambroise Paré and Pasteur clinics are the reference private clinics, with surgery and ambulances. Guinea has "few hospitals".
  - Full insurance with medical repatriation is essential, because care can be refused without it [4].
  - Measles comes back every year from Feb/March to June. Ebola last broke out in 2021 near Nzérékoré and could return [4].
  - Schools: the Lycée français Albert Camus is in Conakry [8]. Its website could not be loaded (expired certificate), so fees and levels were not found.
  - Universities: Gamal Abdel Nasser and Université Kofi Annan [8].
- **Safety**:
  - France Diplomatie (updated 15 Sept 2026) puts Conakry in the **yellow** zone, "vigilance renforcée" [2]:
    - armed robbery is rising in Conakry and its suburbs
    - pickpockets work the Niger, Madina and Taouyah markets
    - night carjackings and break-ins happen, sometimes by people in military-style uniforms
    - people are sometimes followed from the airport, so take the motorway and avoid the airport road at night
    - avoid the **Route Le Prince** (Hamdallaye – Bambeto – Cosa), where protests are frequent
  - GOV.UK (updated 12 Aug 2026) reports violent carjackings "especially in the outlying suburbs", criminals posing as police, and police extortion of foreigners [7].
  - On the eve of the December 2025 vote, gunfire was reported in Sonfonia and an "armed group neutralised" [10].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Ratoma is the nightlife commune [8]. Bars, maquis and clubs exist. Shisha has been cracked down on since 2022 [19].
  - Good sources on the music scene, restaurants and festivals were not found in this pass. This is a gap: Guinea's musical heritage (Bembeya Jazz, Mory Kanté) is well known but unsourced here.
  - Mostly Muslim, with a Grand Mosque and a Catholic cathedral side by side [8].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Guineenews covers returnee stories and diaspora investment in home villages (Apr 2026) [20]. No named associations, Facebook or WhatsApp groups were verified (social media not reached).
- **Climate in practice**: one of Africa's wettest capitals, at about 3,800 mm/year. The peak is July–August, at about 1,100 mm per month. The dry season runs Dec–April with harmattan dust [8]. Roads flood in the rainy season: GOV.UK gives May–Oct [7], and France Diplomatie warns of floods and landslides from June to November (Sept 2026) [2].
- **Watch out**:
  - power and water cuts
  - housing speculation, with rents high relative to local incomes
  - night crime and fake checkpoints
  - protests on the Route Le Prince
  - police extortion
  - few hospitals
  - an authoritarian political climate with media restrictions [10]
  - rainy-season flooding

### Labé

Capital of Moyenne-Guinée in the Fouta Djallon, 263,186 people (2025 preliminary census), about 1,026 m altitude, about 430 km from Conakry [1].

- **Cost of living**: not found. It is very likely cheaper than Conakry, but this is unsourced.
- **Work & money**:
  - A regional trading hub whose central market draws traders from Senegal, Guinea-Bissau, Gambia and Sierra Leone. Indigo textiles (*léppi*) are a local craft [1].
  - Transporters' strikes in Sept 2026 paralysed traffic towards Senegal and Gambia, in protest at "tracasseries routières" (roadside harassment) [21].
  - A €300m loan for the Labé–Tougué road was ratified in Oct 2026 [21].
  - Remote work: weak (see power below).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: the Université de Labé has been autonomous since 2016 [1]. No hubs or incubators found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Tata airport [1].
  - Reconstruction of the Mamou–Labé road (RN5) was announced in Nov 2025 [1].
  - Load-shedding is documented in nearby Tougué (Apr 2026) [16d]. Labé-specific power data: not found.
  - Rivers are hard to cross in the rainy season on the Labé–Lélouma road (Oct 2026) [21].
- **Health & schools**: not found (a regional hospital exists but is unsourced). Lycée français: not found.
- **Safety**:
  - Labé is in France Diplomatie's general **yellow** zone, with no Labé-specific mention [2].
  - Local press reports isolated violent incidents in 2026: a moto-taxi driver shot in Hafia (Sept 2026), a shooting incident (Sept 2026), and fatal road crashes [21]. (Unverified: seen as search-result headlines only, articles not opened.)
  - Night driving on the Kindia and Mamou routes (the road to Labé) carries a risk of armed roadblocks [2].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Conservative, strongly Muslim Peul city; the great mosque is tied to Karamoko Alpha [1]. Police jailed 16 people in a crackdown on shisha (Oct 2026) [21].
  - Culture: the Musée du Fouta Djallon, the Kolima Jazz orchestra (founded 1963, reviving in 2026), and the Festival des arts et du rire (since 2015) [1][21].
  - Quieter pace than Conakry.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: the Fouta has a large diaspora in France and the US (unsourced). There is strong attachment to villages of origin, and Fouta returnee stories appear in the press [20]. No groups were verified.
- **Climate in practice**: cooler because of the altitude. January lows are about 12 °C. The hottest months are March–April, at about 33 °C. Rain falls May–October (about 1,543 mm), peaking July–September [1]. Of the listed cities, Labé has the mildest temperatures.
- **Watch out**:
  - remoteness (430 km of roads that are hard in the rainy season)
  - conservative social codes
  - FGM is still practised despite the law, a local press report with no figures (Oct 2026) [22]
  - limited healthcare and international schooling
  - transport strikes

### Kankan (suggested addition)

Second city by population, with 410,542 people (2025 census), 555 km east of Conakry. It is Mandinka and Muslim, home to Julius Nyerere University (1964), and a historic trading crossroads [9].

- Hot savanna climate (highs about 34 °C, rain June–Oct) [9].
- **Caution**: France Diplomatie's **orange** zone (avoid unless essential) covers the Mali and Côte d'Ivoire border areas, including Siguiri, Mandiana and the Kankan nature reserve [2]. Kankan town itself is not named.
- Power cuts in Kankan have a long history in local press [16].
- Matters as the hub of Upper Guinea and its gold economy (gold link inferred from historic Bouré; current data not found).

### Kindia (possible addition)

About 135 km from Conakry (distance unsourced). Kindia sits on the Conakry–Mamou–Labé road. Residents started Ramadan 2026 in the dark because of load-shedding [16e]. Armed night roadblocks are reported around Kindia [2]. Not enough data to profile it.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No academic regional-personality study of Guinea was found in this pass. Afrobarometer data exists on attitudes; its finding on gay neighbours is quoted in the legal section below. These lines describe which temperaments may find daily life easier, based on the cited facts. They never measure anyone.

**Conakry**
- Openness: suits people who like cities that improvise. The tech scene runs on hackathons and prize competitions [14] rather than dense coworking networks.
- Conscientiousness: rewards planners. You will need backup power plans [16], careful visa and residence paperwork [3], and cash-export rules to follow [3].
- Extraversion: Ratoma's night scene [8] and big networking events (Fintech Week, Smart Africa) [13][15] suit outgoing people. But night movement is constrained by safety advice [2].
- Agreeableness: daily friction includes police extortion and roadside checks [7], so patience and diplomacy pay off.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): high tolerance needed. Expect long daily power cuts [16], protests [2], night crime [2][7] and a tense political climate [10].

**Labé**
- Openness: less suited to people seeking cultural novelty or nightlife. Conservative codes apply (shisha crackdown) [21].
- Conscientiousness: a slower, community-based life with fewer administrative services.
- Extraversion: community and family life matter more than going out [1].
- Agreeableness: respect for religious and family hierarchies helps (editorial reading of [1][22]).
- Emotional stability: lower urban stress than Conakry, but isolation, transport strikes and road risk [21] add their own strain.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex acts are illegal under Penal Code art. 274 (2016): 6 months to 3 years plus a fine. A morals-police unit was created in 2023. 87% of Guineans would dislike having a gay neighbour (Afrobarometer 2021–2023). A 2024 UPR submission reports that LGBT people are often held for up to five days, beyond the 48-hour legal limit, and pay bribes or fines to be released [23]. GOV.UK (Aug 2026) confirms the criminalisation and says same-sex relationships "are not widely accepted" [7]. France Diplomatie (15 Sept 2026) adds that LGBT people face intolerance that is sometimes violent [2].
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of diaspora**: no reliable source found.
- **Conflict zones**: border areas with Mali and Côte d'Ivoire are orange (France Diplomatie, Mar/Sept 2026), with a risk of terrorist incursions [2]. Border areas are tense and militarised, and the Sierra Leone border can close at short notice [7]. There are road robberies on routes to Mamou, Faranah, Kissidougou, Guéckédou, Macenta and Nzérékoré [7]. Piracy has been reported in territorial waters [7].
- **Political freedom**: the UN rights chief cited intimidation, enforced disappearances and media restrictions around the Dec 2025 vote [10].
- **Women's daily experience**: FGM is still practised in Labé despite the law. It now happens discreetly, in private (Guineenews, 31 Oct 2026, no figures) [22]. **For diaspora families:** France Diplomatie (15 Sept 2026) warns of strong pressure on uncut girls of Guinean origin who visit family from France, and advises women not to walk alone at night [2]. The 2019 Civil Code legalised polygamy [11]. Data on street harassment and women's safety: not found.
