## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** siSwati is the prevalent language, and English is co-official and the language of government and business [3]. French is essentially absent; no francophone infrastructure was found.
- **Currency:** lilangeni (SZL, plural emalangeni, written "E"). It is pegged 1:1 to the South African rand [3], and the rand is accepted in practice. EUR conversion: no dated rate was fetched (not found).
- **Size:** about 1.24 million people (2023 estimate) [3]. It is a small, landlocked absolute monarchy (King Mswati III), wedged between South Africa and Mozambique.
- **Entry and residence, (a) French/EU passport holders:** the France Diplomatie entry page could not be read (only the alerts page loaded) [2]. For comparison, UK citizens are visa-free for 30 days, extendable by 30 days at the Ministry of Home Affairs, and a temporary residence permit is required beyond 60 days [5]. Wikipedia's visa-policy summary lists **all EU member states (France included) as visa-exempt for 30 days** [17]; the official Eswatini Home Affairs list was not read, so confirm before travelling. Work permit process: not found.
- **Entry and residence, (b) diaspora with family roots:**
  - No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found.
  - Nationality passes by descent through the **father only** under the 2005 Constitution, which Wikipedia's summary calls discriminatory [6].
  - Children born abroad to a Liswati father can derive nationality, but "subsequent generations cannot" [6].
  - Dual nationality can be authorised, typically for citizens by birth but not for naturalised persons [6].
  - Naturalisation needs about 5 years' residence, knowledge of English or siSwati, and support from a chief or three citizens. **Khonta** (acceptance by a chief, with a land grant) can also confer nationality [6].
  - In practice, the country is relevant mostly to Swazi-descended people and to Southern African diaspora, not to West/Central African francophone diaspora.
- **Recent political context:** pro-democracy protests in June–July 2021 left at least 20 people killed by security forces, and the internet was shut down [3]. Freedom House 2025 rates the country **Not Free, 17/100**. In 2024, two former MPs were jailed for 25 and 18 years over the 2021 protests, and the Supreme Court upheld the Suppression of Terrorism Act [8].
- **Local media headline worth knowing:** "Illegal foreign-owned businesses given 1 year to close" (Times of Eswatini, 11 Nov 2026) [11]. Only the headline and teaser were read; the teaser speaks of 12 months to regularise operations. Anyone planning to open a small business should check the reserved-sector rules for foreigners.

## Cities

### Mbabane

The administrative capital, at about 1,243 m altitude in the Highveld. Population was about 95,000 (2010 estimate) [4].

- **Cost of living:** not found. Numbeo and Expatistan were unreachable. Named neighbourhoods include Sidvwashini, Kent Rock, Westridge Park, Malunge, Mbangweni and Sandla [4]; Msunduza is an older, denser township [4]. Rent ranges by neighbourhood: not found.
- **Work & money:**
  - Main employers are government, banking, insurance and investment management (described as a regional financial hub), plus tourism [4].
  - The national economy rests on agriculture, manufacturing and sugar / soft-drink-concentrate exports [3]. Exports lifted the trade surplus to E687.9m (Times of Eswatini, 13 Nov 2026) [11].
  - Youth unemployment is a recurring editorial theme in the local press (Nov 2026) [11].
  - Remote-work feasibility and salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - The Eswatini Investment Promotion Authority (EIPA) is in Mbabane. It promotes ICT, mining, manufacturing, agribusiness, tourism and energy [9].
  - EIPA's site has a stale 2019 copyright and unrelated gambling spam in its news feed, so it is poorly maintained [9].
  - The Royal Science and Technology Park exists, but its site did not respond, so its programmes are not verified.
  - Startup hubs, investors and crypto adoption: not found.
  - EIPA cites a World Bank Doing Business 2019 ranking (first in Africa for trading across borders) [9]. That report series has been discontinued, so the claim is old.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - The country has a "relatively well-developed" fibre backbone, but international bandwidth goes through neighbours, which keeps prices high [10]. That source dates from about 2012–2014 and is old.
  - MTN is the historic mobile operator [10]; **Eswatini Mobile** is the second, and both complied with the 2021 internet shutdown [3]. Current mobile-money services (MoMo etc.): not verified (not found).
  - Power cuts and water: not found. Ride-hailing: not found.
  - Bus fares are rising (Times of Eswatini, 10 Nov 2026) [11].
- **Health & schools:**
  - FCDO: "Basic healthcare is available… but there are shortages of common medicines". For serious cases, "it's likely you would need to go to South Africa" [7].
  - Adult HIV prevalence: FCDO cites a 2015 UNAIDS figure of about 28.8% [7]; the US CDC gives **25.1% of adults aged 15–49 (2023)** [18]. Either way, among the highest in the world. Malaria and chikungunya are listed as health risks [7].
  - Named hospitals: not found.
  - Schools: **Waterford Kamhlaba UWC of Southern Africa** (IB and IGCSE, founded 1963 as an anti-apartheid multiracial school) [12]. One of the University of Eswatini's three campuses is in Mbabane; Limkokwing University is near the South Africa border post rather than in town [4].
  - French school: not found. Most likely none.
- **Safety:** FCDO reports "casual theft, pickpocketing and occasional muggings", mainly on city outskirts and at night, and advises to "avoid walking in the downtown areas of Mbabane and Manzini after dark". Crime rises in the pre-Christmas season. Armed robbery "has happened but is not common" [1] (undated page, read 2026-11-14). France Diplomatie publishes a security map of vigilance zones, which was not read [2].
- **Social life & vibe:** a quiet, small, hilly administrative town. The Ezulwini Valley next door ("Valley of Heaven") holds the hotels, casino (Royal Swazi Sun), Gables shopping centre, Mlilwane Wildlife Sanctuary and the national museum at Lobamba [13]. Specific nightlife and music details: not found. Religion's weight: not found (sourced).
- **Diaspora & expat community:** not found (no group pages could be searched).
- **Climate in practice:** mild subtropical highland. It averages about 22 °C in January and 11 °C in July, with about 4 frost days a year. Rain falls mostly in summer, with January the wettest month and June the driest (about 1,040 mm a year) [4]. Expect cool winters (pack heating and warm clothes) and summer thunderstorms.
- **Watch out:** a tiny job market and a weak French-speaking presence. Specialised healthcare means going to South Africa [7]. Political freedoms are restricted (Freedom House 17/100) [8], and the authorities have shut the internet before during unrest [3].

### Manzini

"The Hub", the commercial, agricultural and transport centre, declared a city in 1994. Population figures conflict (78,000 to 110,000, 2008) [14].

- **Cost of living:** not found. Middle-class and upscale areas include **Coates Valley**, **Madonsa** (east of downtown) and **Helemesi Estates** (north). **Fairview** was the first integrated neighbourhood. **Ngwane Park** is lower- to middle-class, and **KaKhoza** is poorer [14].
- **Work & money:**
  - Trade, transport and agro-processing. The **Matsapha** industrial estate (since 1965) is 11 km away, on the South Africa–Mozambique corridor [15].
  - The Swaziland International Trade Fair is held at Mavuso [14].
  - Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - The old Matsapha airport closed around 2014. King Mswati III International Airport is about 57 km east [14].
  - Bus fares are rising (Nov 2026) [11].
  - Power, water and ride-hailing: not found.
- **Health & schools:** same national caveats as Mbabane [7]. Named facilities: not found.
- **Safety:** same FCDO warning about the downtown after dark [1].
- **Social life & vibe:** a busier, more commercial town than Mbabane, with a lower altitude and a warmer climate (inferred from geography; climate figures not found). Details: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** not found.
- **Climate in practice:** not found (sourced). Expect warmer than Mbabane, with summer rain.
- **Watch out:** the same structural limits as Mbabane, and the downtown is not recommended after dark [1].

### Ezulwini / Malkerns (lifestyle corridor between the two cities)

The Ezulwini Valley holds the hotels, casino, shopping and Mlilwane Wildlife Sanctuary, along the MR3 linking Mbabane to Manzini [13]. It matters as where many better-off residents and visitors spend leisure time. The House on Fire / Bushfire festival at Malkerns is a known cultural landmark, but its page could not be verified (not found).

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

Interpretive only; no city or quiz question measures a trait.

**Mbabane**
- **Openness:** suits people who are happy with a small, quiet, nature-framed capital rather than a cosmopolitan scene. Culture is concentrated in the nearby Ezulwini Valley and Lobamba museum [13].
- **Conscientiousness:** suits planners. Residence permits beyond 60 days [5], medical evacuation planning [7] and a thin job market reward organisation.
- **Extraversion:** a low-key town. People seeking big-city nightlife will likely find it limited (unsourced impression, low confidence).
- **Agreeableness:** fits people comfortable with deference to traditional and royal authority, where chiefs even play a role in naturalisation [6].
- **Emotional stability:** you need tolerance for political uncertainty. The 2021 unrest killed at least 20 people and the internet was cut [3], and freedoms remain restricted [8].

**Manzini**
- **Openness:** a commercial and transport hub, more mixed and busier [14].
- **Conscientiousness:** suits people who want trade or industrial work near Matsapha [15].
- **Extraversion:** busier street life than Mbabane (low confidence).
- **Agreeableness:** same social codes as Mbabane.
- **Emotional stability:** the downtown needs street awareness after dark [1].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - Male same-sex acts are illegal under common law that the 2005 Constitution keeps in force. The law is unenforced, with no known prosecutions. Female same-sex acts are not criminalised, and there are no anti-discrimination protections [16] (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-14).
  - On 29 April 2022, the High Court, in a split decision, upheld the refusal to register the group Eswatini Sexual and Gender Minorities [16]. Any later appeal outcome was not verified in this pass.
  - FCDO: "arrests are rare", but LGBT+ people "experience discrimination, and many are not open" [1].
  - Freedom House 2025 also cites discrimination against LGBT+ people [8].
- **Women:** Freedom House 2025 scores women's equal treatment, economic equality and protection from domestic violence each at 1/4 [8]. Nationality cannot pass through the mother [6].
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners:**
  - Nothing reliable was found.
  - The Times of Eswatini headline (11 Nov 2026) on closing illegal foreign-owned businesses signals tightening for foreign small traders [11].
  - Waterford Kamhlaba's founding history shows Eswatini's role as a multiracial refuge under apartheid [12].
- **Conflict / political:**
  - No armed conflict.
  - Political repression: protests in June 2021 left at least 20 dead [3]. In 2024, protest-era MPs were jailed and the anti-terror law was upheld [8].
  - FCDO advises avoiding demonstrations, which security forces have met with force [1].
