# Soudan

> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

**Headline for the product team.** Sudan has been in civil war since 15 April 2023. In November 2026 the UK [1] and France [3] both advise against all travel to the whole country. Neither has a functioning embassy inside Sudan: the French embassy works from Addis Ababa [3], and the British embassy in Khartoum is closed [1]. Drone strikes on power infrastructure, including in Khartoum and Red Sea states, were still being reported in November 2026 [10]. **Recommendation: mark both Khartoum and Port-Soudan as `unsafe` in `cities.json`** so that they are never proposed, under the CLAUDE.md rule. Keep this dossier for when the situation changes, and to answer diaspora readers who ask "et le Soudan ?".

**Research limitation.** The session's shared web-search budget ran out before this dossier began, so no WebSearch queries ran. Every fact below comes from pages fetched directly (official advice, Wikipedia, Radio Dabanga, IOM DTM). Numbeo returned HTTP 429, and US State Dept and Sudan Tribune returned 403. No French-language media (RFI, Jeune Afrique, Le Monde), expat blogs or social media pages could be reached. Many fields are therefore marked "not found". A second pass is needed once the search budget refills.

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: Sudanese Arabic is dominant. English is used in business, NGOs and universities. Beja (To Bedawie) is spoken around Port Sudan. French is essentially absent. *(Common knowledge. No source opened this session; verify.)*
- **Currency**: Sudanese pound (SDG). The official rate was devalued in February 2021 (about SDG 375/USD). The parallel market was about SDG 2,100/USD by July 2024 [8] (older than 2025, flagged). New SDG 1,000 and SDG 2,000 notes were issued in June 2025 [8]. A 2026 analyst estimates the pound has lost about 80% of its value. The IMF projects inflation at 41.3% in 2026, down from 99.9% in 2025, and unemployment at about 59.5% [11] (28 Oct 2026). **Current exchange rate: not found**, so no EUR conversions are given below.
- **Entry: French/EU passport holders**: a visa is required, from a Sudanese embassy. Foreigners must register with the Aliens Department (Ministry of Interior) within 3 days of arrival, with 2 photos and a fee. Other rules [2] (FCDO, current at 14 Nov 2026):
  - A yellow-fever certificate is required.
  - An Israeli stamp means refused entry.
  - A permit is needed to leave Red Sea State (Port Sudan).
  - Children aged 17 and under need the father's consent to enter and exit (the FCDO notes this is "not always enforced").

  No long-stay or residence route for non-Sudanese was found. France "déconseille formellement" all travel (27 Aug 2026) [3].
- **Entry: diaspora with Sudanese roots**: dual nationality has generally been allowed since 1993, except with South Sudanese nationality. Children of a Sudanese father are Sudanese automatically. Since a 2017 Supreme Court ruling and a 2018 amendment, people with a Sudanese mother can also get her nationality [9]. Sudanese nationality was stripped in 2011 from anyone who acquired South Sudanese nationality [9]. No specific diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found.
- **Political geography (late 2026)**: the government is aligned with the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF). It operated from Port Sudan between 2023 and January 2026 [6], and announced on 11 January 2026 that its return to Khartoum was complete [7]. The Central Bank resumed operations in Khartoum on 20 January 2026 [7]. By 27 September 2026, the SAF controlled about 60% of the country, and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) held parts of Darfur and Kordofan. El Fasher fell to the RSF on 26 October 2025 [12]. More than 14 million people have been displaced, 4.5 million of them as refugees (as of 6 July 2026), and about 25 million face severe food insecurity (figure undated in the source) [12].

## Cities

### Khartoum

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bedroom rent: **not found** (Numbeo unavailable; no 2025–2026 rent reporting found).
  - Neighbourhoods historically favoured by expats and the middle class: Riyadh, Al Amarat, Al-Manshiyya, Garden City (Eastern/Southern Khartoum) [5]. Their post-war state is unknown.
  - Meal price and monthly budget: not found.
- **Work & money**:
  - The government, the Central Bank and ministries returned in January 2026 [7]. Reconstruction, humanitarian and NGO work are the likely employers (inference, not sourced).
  - The IMF estimates national unemployment at about 59.5% (2026) [11].
  - Salaries: not found.
  - Remote work depends on internet that is repeatedly cut (see infrastructure).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - Before the war Khartoum had a small tech scene, but no 2023–2026 source on hubs or incubators was found.
  - Mobile banking is common through Bank of Khartoum's **Bankak** app. Fraud cases were documented in October and November 2026 among traders in Kordofan. A local emergency-room volunteer put losses at SDG 18–45 million per victim, and one named case lost SDG 12 million [13].
  - Crypto, investors and company registration: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - The Arab League condemned strikes on energy facilities in Khartoum state (reported 4 Nov 2026). The UN reports that power cuts disrupt water supply and force people onto untreated sources [10].
  - Khartoum International Airport was hit by a drone on 4 May 2026 [7]. Per the FCDO, it runs limited flights and may close again [1b].
  - Nationwide internet shutdowns happened in 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026; the 2026 one was ordered during the national exam period in April 2026 [14]. The operators are Zain, MTN and Sudani [14].
  - Ride-hailing apps: not found.
- **Health & schools**:
  - Hospitals: not found for 2025–2026 (widespread destruction reported after the March 2025 recapture) [5].
  - French or international schools: not found. The French embassy's Khartoum offices are closed [3].
- **Safety**:
  - FCDO (current 14 Nov 2026) advises against all travel because of "ongoing military conflict in Khartoum". It reports widespread violent crime since the war began: looting, muggings, and money demanded at checkpoints [1][1b].
  - The FCDO rates terrorist attacks as likely [1b].
  - Drones struck the airport and military sites in May 2026, and RSF drones were intercepted over the city in June 2026 [7].
  - Neighbourhood-level nuance: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Most residents fled. Khartoum state took 36% of about 2.25 million returnees recorded from November 2024 to August 2025, roughly 810,000 people (our calculation; IOM DTM gives the share, not the count) [15]. Daily life is focused on recovery.
  - Alcohol is legally restricted and drinking can lead to arrest. Modest dress is expected [1b].
  - Nightlife, music, food scene: not found for the post-war period.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found. Social media could not be checked this session.
- **Climate in practice**: hot desert climate. November–February is warm and dry, March–May is very hot, and the short rains peak in August [5].
- **Watch out**:
  - The city remains an active conflict-exposure zone: drone strikes in 2026 [7] and attacks on power infrastructure [10].
  - Large-scale destruction [5], no Western consular presence [1][3], and currency collapse [11].

### Port-Soudan

- **Cost of living**:
  - Rent: **not found**.
  - Prices in November 2026 [16]:
    - bread: SDG 1,000 for 2 pieces;
    - pair of water containers: SDG 3,000;
    - meat: SDG 6,000 per quarter-kilo;
    - cooking gas: SDG 150,000;
    - one egg: SDG 1,700.
  - "Soaring prices, water shortages deepen hardship" (Radio Dabanga, 12 Nov 2026) [16].
  - EUR conversion: not possible, no exchange rate found.
- **Work & money**:
  - The port handles about 90% of Sudan's international trade, and the city has an oil refinery [6].
  - It was the seat of government from 2023 to January 2026 [6][7]. Some activity has moved back to Khartoum since.
  - Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Water shortages (Nov 2026) [16]. A substation in Red Sea state was hit by a drone in early November 2026 [10].
  - The airport runs limited flights and was hit by drones [1b]. RSF drones first struck the city on 4–5 May 2025, hitting an air base and fuel depots [6].
  - A permit is needed to leave Red Sea State [2].
- **Health & schools**: not found.
- **Safety**:
  - FCDO advises against all travel to Sudan, Port Sudan included [1]. France also advises against all travel [3].
  - The French Foreign Ministry has a Red Sea / Bab-el-Mandeb maritime alert for boaters (updated 4 Mar 2026) [4].
  - Neighbourhood nuance: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**: large numbers of internally displaced people live in the city, facing heat and food and water shortages [6]. Beja culture is present (common knowledge, unverified). Nightlife and food scene: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: summers regularly go above 45 °C, and June–August highs average about 40–43 °C. Annual rain is very low: the article text says about 76 mm with over 90% between October and January, but its own 1991–2020 climate table gives 80.6 mm with about 79% in October–January [6]. Either way, rain is rare. Summer heat is the main daily constraint, with power cuts making it worse.
- **Watch out**: extreme heat, water scarcity, high prices [16], drone exposure [6], and travel permits [2].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This section is interpretive. It describes who would cope with current conditions, not who would thrive. No city or quiz question measures a trait. No Hofstede, GLOBE or regional personality data on Sudan was found this session.

**Khartoum (late 2026)**
- Openness: suits people drawn to rebuilding a capital after war. Little cultural life could be documented [5][7].
- Conscientiousness: high demand. Foreigners must register with the Aliens Department within 3 days, permits govern travel, and cash and banking are unreliable [2][13].
- Extraversion: public social life is constrained. Alcohol carries a risk of arrest and conservative codes apply [1b]. Nightlife was not found.
- Agreeableness: daily life depends on family and neighbourhood networks while services are absent (inference from returnee data [15]).
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): extreme demand. Drones, checkpoints, crime, power and water cuts [1b][7][10].

**Port-Soudan (late 2026)**
- Openness: a trade-port city with a large displaced population [6].
- Conscientiousness: a permit is needed to leave the state [2].
- Extraversion: not documented.
- Agreeableness: crowding, with IDPs under strain [6][16].
- Emotional stability: very high demand. Heat above 45 °C, water shortages, price spikes [6][16].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex sexual activity is illegal for both men and women.
  - Under Article 148, penalties are up to 5 years for a first offence, 7 years for a second and life imprisonment for a third. The death penalty and flogging were removed on 9 July 2020 [18].
  - The FCDO says it is "illegal in Sudan and not tolerated in Sudanese society" (current 14 Nov 2026) [1b].
- **Relationships and alcohol**: sex outside marriage is illegal, and unmarried couples may not share housing or a hotel room. Non-Muslims may drink only out of sight of Muslims, and can still be arrested [1b].
- **Women**:
  - The public order law was repealed in November 2019 [19].
  - FGM was criminalised in 2020 [19].
  - Women have not needed male permission to travel since July 2020 [19].
  - Children need the father's consent to cross the border [2].
  - UN Women reports widespread conflict-related sexual violence since 2023 [19].
- **Conflict**:
  - Active war between the SAF and the RSF.
  - El Fasher fell on 26 October 2025, with tens of thousands killed [12].
  - On 7 January 2025 the US determined that the RSF had committed genocide [12].
  - In 2026 there were campaigns in Blue Nile and South Kordofan [12], and drone strikes across many states (Nov 2026) [10].
- **Racism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners**: no reliable 2023–2026 source was found this session. Ethnic targeting in Darfur is documented through the US genocide determination [12]. No source covers the treatment of returning diaspora.
