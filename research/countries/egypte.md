## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages in daily life**: Egyptian Arabic for everything (markets, taxis, administration). English is common in business, tech, private hospitals and upscale Cairo areas *(unverified this session)*. French survives in a francophone elite and school network (four campuses of the Lycée français du Caire, French schools in Alexandria, the Université Française d'Égypte, Université Senghor in Alexandria) [9][12][15][19]. Expect to need Arabic outside expat bubbles.
- **Currency**: Egyptian pound (EGP / E£). After the 2024 float the pound lost about 38–40% against the dollar [14]. The IMF projects 14.3% CPI inflation for 2026 [14]. Prices in EGP move fast, so check budgets in EUR.
- **Entry, French/EU passport holders**:
  - Visa on arrival: US$30, cash only, up to 30 days. A digital visa at Cairo airport has cost US$36 since 1 Aug 2026 [2].
  - Tourist e-visa: valid up to 3 months [2].
  - Sinai-only visa-free stamp for Sharm el-Sheikh, Dahab, Nuweiba and Taba: up to 15 days [11].
  - Residence visa: required beyond 90 days. It is renewable, can be granted for up to 5 years, and is the base for a work permit. Work permits are employer-sponsored, last 1 year and are renewable (Expat.com, updated 2025-12-15) [8].
  - Property: foreigners can buy, usually up to two residential units, but buying does not give residence by itself [8].
  - Naturalisation: generally after 10 years of residence [10].
- **Diaspora with family roots**:
  - Nationality passes through either parent. Law 154 of 14 Jul 2004 made maternal transmission equal, and the 2014 constitution made it a right for children of Egyptian parents [10].
  - Dual nationality has been allowed since 2004, in principle with government authorisation. Holding a second nationality without permission has no automatic penalty [10].
  - Citizenship by investment (amendment ratified 15 Aug 2018): a deposit of E£7 million makes a foreigner eligible to apply after 5 years of residence [10]. Later decrees (from 2019) reportedly added faster US-dollar investment routes (property, business or deposit); their amounts and current status were not confirmed this session *(unverified)*.
  - Egypt has **no right-of-return or Afro-descendant scheme** comparable to Ghana's or Benin's. For sub-Saharan diaspora members without Egyptian parents, Egypt is a "foreigner" destination: there is no diaspora shortcut.
  - Not found this session: whether dual nationals are treated as Egyptian only, and military-service obligations for male dual nationals. The FCDO refers these questions to the Egyptian consulate [2].

## Cities

### Le Caire (Greater Cairo, including Giza, New Cairo and 6th of October)

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bed rent: E£11,800/month in the centre and E£10,230 outside (Numbeo, updated 2026-11-14), about €200 and €175 [3].
  - Expat.com quotes a much lower E£5,000–7,500 for a centre 1-bed (data "as of Dec 2025") [8]. Numbeo's figure is likely closer to expat-area reality. In the neighbourhoods expats actually choose (Zamalek, Maadi, Garden City, New Cairo compounds), expect well above the average *(unverified this session; no neighbourhood-level price found)*.
  - Cheap meal: E£250 (about €4.2) [3].
  - Single person's costs without rent: E£20,340 (about €345) [3]. Expat.com suggests about €750/month all-in for one person and €2,200 for a family of four [8].
  - Neighbourhoods named by sources: Maadi, Zamalek, New Cairo, plus the gated compounds of New Cairo and 6th of October near international schools [8][12]. Heliopolis and Garden City are known expat areas *(unverified this session)*.
- **Work & money**:
  - Cairo is the country's administrative, media, education and corporate hub.
  - Smart Village (est. 2001) is the main tech and business park [14].
  - The New Administrative Capital, announced March 2015 east of Cairo, is drawing government jobs out of the city [15].
  - Remote work is feasible: broadband (60 Mbps+) costs about E£800/month [3].
  - Not found: salary figures.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - Cairo is widely described as one of Africa's "Big Four" startup hubs, with GrEEK Campus, Flat6Labs, the AUC Venture Lab and fintechs such as Fawry, MNT-Halan and InstaPay as markers *(all unverified this session)*.
  - **Crypto**: Article 206 of the Central Bank and Banking System Law (Law 194 of 2020) bans issuing, trading, promoting or running platforms for cryptocurrencies without a Central Bank of Egypt licence, and the CBE repeated the warning in 2021 and Sept 2022 [16][25]. Whether simply holding crypto is illegal is disputed between sources. Either way, this rules out a "crypto city" angle.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - The Cairo Metro opened in 1987, is Africa's oldest and carries over 1 billion rides a year. Two cars are reserved for women [15].
  - A light-rail line has run since 2022 (12 stations over 70 km, as of 2025). The monorail is still in planning [15].
  - Ride-hailing: Uber, Careem, Bolt and DiDi [15].
  - Traffic and pollution are heavy. WHO data puts air pollution at roughly 12× the recommended level [15].
  - Power cuts: rolling cuts were reported in 2023–24 *(unverified this session; current status not found)*.
  - Mobile money: Vodafone Cash and InstaPay are common *(unverified this session)*.
- **Health & schools**:
  - Healthcare is split between public and private. The private sector is better, bilingual, and recommended for expats with international cover and evacuation insurance. Emergency number: 123. 24-hour pharmacies deliver (Expat.com, 2025-12) [8].
  - There is an Italian hospital in Cairo [9].
  - **Lycée français du Caire**: run directly by the AEFE, nursery to lycée. It has campuses in Maadi, Zamalek and New Cairo (primary) and Mearag (secondary) [12], and more than 1,600 pupils according to lepetitjournal.com [9] (the school's own site gives no total, only "over 25 nationalities" [12]). Places fill fast [8].
  - Universities: AUC (1919), Université Française d'Égypte (2002), German University in Cairo (2003) [15].
- **Safety**:
  - FCDO (updated 2026-10-27): crime is "generally low", scams target foreigners, pickpocketing is rare. Terrorists are "likely to try to carry out attacks". Avoid protests, which security forces handle forcefully. Foreigners can be detained for political activity or for social-media posts critical of the government [1][4].
  - France Diplomatie (updated 2026-09-10): its red zones are North Sinai and the Israel border. Cairo itself is not flagged red [5].
  - Not found this session: neighbourhood-level crime data.
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - A huge, dense, 24-hour city of 9.8 million in the city proper and 22.2 million in the metro area (2023) [15].
  - The Cairo Opera House and the Grand Egyptian Museum host francophone and international events, for example Julien Dassin at the Opera on 6 Nov 2026 and the Gala de Danza at the GEM on 5 Nov 2026 [9].
  - Islam shapes the daily rhythm (Ramadan, Friday), and Coptic Christians form a visible minority *(unverified this session)*.
  - A mild magnitude 5.5 earthquake was felt in Aug 2026, with no damage [9].
- **Diaspora & expat community**:
  - **Caire Accueil**: a volunteer, apolitical and non-religious association that helps French speakers of all origins settle in [9].
  - The Institut Français d'Égypte [9].
  - The Expat.com Egypt forum [8].
  - Not found: sub-Saharan diaspora associations or returnee networks in Cairo.
- **Climate in practice**: hot desert. Summer highs often pass 31 °C and rarely 40 °C. Rain falls only in winter, but sudden showers flood streets. Saharan dust storms come between March and May [15].
- **Watch out**:
  - Pollution and traffic [15].
  - Currency volatility [14].
  - A restrictive political climate, including over social-media posts [4][17].
  - Sexual harassment of women [4][17].
  - Anti-Black racism reported against Sudanese and other sub-Saharan Africans (see the legal section) [18].

### Alexandrie

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bed rent: E£7,170 in the centre (about €120) and E£3,560 outside (about €60) (Numbeo, updated 2026-11-10) [6].
  - Cheap meal: E£200 (about €3.4) [6].
  - Costs without rent: E£16,900 (about €285) [6].
  - Clearly cheaper than Cairo.
  - Not found: named neighbourhoods. Raml/Stanley/Sporting along the Corniche are commonly cited *(unverified this session)*.
- **Work & money**:
  - Egypt's main seaport (four ports including Dekhela and Abu Qir), the Alexandria Shipyard, industry and a large financial sector [6][19].
  - The metro economy was worth about $26.7 bn in 2024 [19].
  - Broadband costs E£700/month [6].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: the ecosystem is much smaller than Cairo's. Universities include Alexandria University, E-JUST (New Borg El Arab), the Arab Academy for Science, Technology & Maritime Transport, and a University of Patras branch announced in 2023 [19]. Not found: named hubs.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - The tram (1860) is Africa's oldest [19].
  - A commuter rail runs to Abu Qir, and 103 bus routes operated in 2023 [19].
  - The metro project was due to start in 2020; its current status was not found [19].
  - The airport opened Terminal 2 in 2025 [19].
- **Health & schools**:
  - Many French schools have operated since the 19th century [19].
  - The francophone Université Senghor is here [19].
  - Not found: reference hospitals.
- **Safety**:
  - The FCDO cites terrorist shootings in Alexandria in 2023 (two Israeli tourists and their guide killed) and 2024 (one Israeli-Canadian killed) [4]. Otherwise it gives the same national picture as for Cairo.
- **Social life & vibe**: a Mediterranean Corniche city with a cosmopolitan and francophone past. The Bibliotheca Alexandrina opened in 2002 [19]. Its pace is often described as calmer than Cairo's *(unverified this session)*.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found this session.
- **Climate in practice**:
  - Milder than Cairo: July–August highs are around 30 °C, sometimes humid [20].
  - It is the wettest of the three cities, at about 211 mm of rain a year. Winter storms have caused flooding in the past [20].
- **Watch out**: sea-level rise. Alexandria is among the world's most vulnerable cities. A 2025 study flags more than 7,000 buildings at risk from rising groundwater [20].

### Assouan

- **Cost of living**:
  - Cheap meal: E£250. Broadband: E£500/month [7].
  - Rent: **not found**. Numbeo has no figure (14 entries from 3 contributors, updated 2026-02-01), so this data is very thin [7].
- **Work & money**:
  - Tourism (Nile, archaeology), agriculture (dates, hibiscus), quarrying and the KIMA chemicals plant. City GDP was about $2.0 bn in 2024 [21].
  - Few jobs outside tourism. Remote work is possible but less proven.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: the airport is about 10 km away. Rail and road link the city to Luxor and Cairo [21]. Not found: power, fibre or ride-hailing details.
- **Health & schools**:
  - Aswan Heart Centre (Magdi Yacoub Foundation), Aswan University Hospitals and Aswan General Hospital [21].
  - Aswan University dates from 2012 [21].
  - Not found: French or international schools.
- **Safety**:
  - The FCDO advises against all but essential travel to most of the Western Desert, but lists Luxor, Qena, Aswan and Abu Simbel as explicit exceptions, so Aswan is not under any FCDO travel warning (updated 2026-10-27) [1].
  - Not found: local crime data.
- **Social life & vibe**: a small city of about 370,000 people (2024) [21]. It is the heart of Nubian culture: West Bank villages such as Gharb Soheil, Nubian music (tambour) and the Nubian Museum (1997) [21]. The pace is slow and the nightlife limited.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: extreme heat, with June–September highs above 40 °C and a record of 51 °C. Almost no rain (under 1 mm a year) [21]. Summer life is lived early morning and evening.
- **Watch out**: the heat, a small job market, isolation (about 900 km from Cairo by rail or road), and thin cost data.

### Hourghada (extra: the Red Sea option for remote workers and retirees)

Why it matters: it is the most-documented Egyptian place to live by the sea, with a real resident foreign community.

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed rent E£11,330 in the centre (range 6,000–20,000) and E£6,250 outside; cheap meal E£205; broadband about E£494 (Numbeo, 198 entries from 23 contributors, updated 2026-11-10) [22].
- **Work & money**: tourism with over 9 million visitors a year (2024), diving, and a yacht and cruise port [23].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: a large Russian community (estimated at 20,000 in 2015), Russian schools, a German School and a Russian consulate since 2017 [23].
- **Health**: a private hospital in nearby El Gouna [23].
- **Transport**: Egypt's second-busiest airport, with flights to Cairo and European cities. Cairo is 455 km away [23].
- **Climate in practice**: mild winters, very hot summers, sea at 21–28 °C [23].
- **Safety**: the FCDO notes sexual assaults reported mostly in Red Sea resorts, often by people victims had met, such as hotel or excursion staff [4].
- **Watch out**: a mono-economy built on tourism, and coral reefs reported down about 50% over three decades [23].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of city conditions. No city "measures" a trait, and no quiz question here measures personality. Not found this session: Hofstede, GLOBE or Afrobarometer figures for Egypt. Add them on the re-run.

**Le Caire**
- **Openness**: suits people hungry for density, history, arts (Opera, GEM) and many universities [9][15].
- **Conscientiousness**: helps with residence-visa renewals, employer-sponsored work permits and a volatile currency [8][14].
- **Extraversion**: rewarded in a 24-hour megacity of 22 million [15].
- **Agreeableness**: a cautious agreeableness serves well. Public political expression is risky [4][17].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: high tolerance helps with traffic, WHO-level pollution about 12× the norm, and crowds [15].

**Alexandrie**
- **Openness**: rewards interest in a francophone, Mediterranean heritage (Senghor, French schools, Bibliotheca) [19].
- **Conscientiousness**: moderate needs. The administration is the same as in Cairo, but the city is smaller.
- **Extraversion**: suits people who like a seafront social life without megacity intensity *(interpretive)*.
- **Agreeableness**: n/a beyond the national notes.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: a softer climate than Cairo [20], but people should be comfortable with long-term climate risk [20].

**Assouan**
- **Openness**: suits people drawn to Nubian culture and a slower life [21].
- **Extraversion**: low needs. Social life is small.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: needed for extreme summer heat and distance from Cairo [21].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex activity is not explicitly criminalised, but it is prosecuted under "debauchery" laws. Public acceptance is low, sexual content on social media has led to arrests, and entry can be refused if gender expression doesn't match the passport (FCDO, updated 2026-10-27) [4].
  - HRW World Report 2026 (covering 2025) confirms that LGBT people are imprisoned under "debauchery" provisions [17].
  - Historical record: at least 84 people were arrested between 25 Sep and end Nov 2017, after rainbow flags were raised at a concert [24].
  - Là-bas should treat Egypt as **hostile for LGBT residents**.
- **Racism / treatment of Black Africans and diaspora**:
  - The EIPR documented physical and verbal abuse of sub-Saharan migrants by the public and police, with Sudanese refugees especially targeted (EIPR report dated 6 Mar 2010, via Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14) [18]. Mona Eltahawy described anti-Black racism aimed mainly at Sudanese, Nubian and other darker-skinned people (Washington Post, Mar 2018, via Wikipedia) [18]. In Nov 2020, 14 people were arrested over racist abuse of the Nubian footballer Shikabala [18]. These sources are old; see HRW 2026 below for the current situation of Sudanese refugees.
  - HRW 2026: over 770,000 Sudanese refugees have arrived since April 2023, and thousands were deported in 2025 [17].
  - **For a Black diaspora reader, this is a major point and needs a recent primary source on the re-run.**
- **Conflict zones**:
  - FCDO (2026-10-27): against all travel to North Sinai and within 20 km of the Libyan border. Against all but essential travel to east Ismailia, most of the Western Desert, northern South Sinai and the Hala'ib triangle [1].
  - France Diplomatie (2026-09-10): the Israel border zone and North Sinai are red. It advises against driving at night outside cities [5].
- **Political freedoms**: protests are effectively banned, 23 journalists were jailed as of May 2025, and critics are detained over Facebook posts (HRW 2026) [17]. The FCDO warns that foreigners' critical social-media posts can lead to detention [4].
- **Women's daily experience**:
  - Egypt ranked 139th of 148 countries in the WEF Gender Gap 2025. Authorities targeted at least 29 online content creators in Jul–Aug 2025 and HRW recorded 21 more prosecutions in October, on vague charges such as "undermining family values" (HRW 2026) [17]. Many were women *(share not given in the HRW chapter)*.
  - The FCDO reports sexual assaults, especially at Red Sea resorts, and advises care in taxis at night [4].
  - The Cairo Metro has women-only cars [15].
