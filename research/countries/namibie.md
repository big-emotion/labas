# Namibie

> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

**Method note.** The shared web-search budget for this session ran out after the first query. Most of this dossier comes from pages fetched directly: official sites, The Brief (Windhoek business news), New Era (state daily), Afrobarometer, Expat.com, livingcost.org and Wikipedia. Social-media groups, InterNations and Numbeo could not be checked (blocked or rate-limited). Treat anything marked "not found" as a real gap, not as an absence.

**Currency conversions.** The Namibia dollar (N$, NAD) is pegged 1:1 to the South African rand [7]. EUR figures use an approximate rate of **N$20 = €1** (editorial rounding for 2025–26; check the live rate).

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Population of the main cities (2023 census):** Windhoek 486,186 [10], Walvis Bay 102,704 [12], Swakopmund about 75,921 [11].
- **Languages in daily life:** English is the only official language and the language of government. In Windhoek, Afrikaans and, to a lesser extent, German are widely used in public life [10]. Oshiwambo, Otjiherero and Khoekhoegowab are spoken at home and in the townships [10][4]. Swakopmund keeps a visible German heritage, with a German-language daily and German street names [11]. **French is close to absent.** The francophone anchor is the Franco-Namibian Cultural Centre (FNCC) on Robert Mugabe Avenue in Windhoek [9].
- **Currency:** Namibia dollar (N$), pegged to the rand. The rand also circulates. In October 2026 the Bank of Namibia raised its repo rate to 7.00% "to safeguard the peg" [7].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders:**
  - Since **1 April 2025**, Namibia requires a visa from 33 countries, France among them, by reciprocity [4]. The same rule applies to UK holders [2].
  - The visa is obtained on arrival or online as an e-visa. It costs **N$1,600** for non-African-Union nationals (N$1,200 for AU nationals, per Expat.com) and is a multiple-entry visa valid for the period requested [2][4].
  - It does not let you work. Working, volunteering or staying long requires a separate visa through a Namibian mission [2].
  - Passports must be valid at least 6 months after arrival and have 3 blank pages. Entry stamps are checked on departure, and an overstay can mean arrest or a fine [2].
  - Long-stay routes found:
    - Investor / long-term work permit: initially 2 years, minimum investment around **N$2 million** (lower in priority sectors) [4].
    - Permanent residence after 5 years of legal residence, or immediately with an investment above **N$10 million** [4].
    - A 5-year visa for "serious investors" was announced in October 2025. Criteria were not found [4].
    - Processing takes 3–4 months, according to Expat.com [4].
  - Expat.com notes contradictions in its own page. Verify all permit rules with the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHAISS). Its e-visa portal URL could not be reached on 2026-11-14.
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:**
  - Citizenship by descent: a person born anywhere with at least one Namibian parent is Namibian. Children born abroad are registered administratively [13].
  - Dual nationality is tolerated for Namibians by birth, after the *Tlhoro* (2008) and *Le Roux* (2011) High Court rulings. **Naturalised citizens must renounce** their other nationality [13].
  - Naturalisation requires **10 years** of residence, including for spouses of Namibians, since a 2010 constitutional amendment [13].
  - **No diaspora visa, right-of-return or Afro-descendant scheme was found** (not found as of 2026-11-14). The Ghana, Sierra Leone and Benin schemes have no Namibian equivalent.
- **Economy and society:**
  - Namibia has one of the highest income inequalities in the world: Gini 59.1 in 2015 [14].
  - Christianity accounts for 87.9% (2023), mostly Lutheran [14].
  - In 2021 Germany acknowledged the 1904–1907 Herero and Nama genocide and pledged €1.1 billion over 30 years [14]. The subject is still politically live.

## Cities

### Windhoek

- **Cost of living:**
  - The Brief rental survey (published 2024-01-02, based on adverts) gives these 1-bed averages [1]:
    - CBD: N$6,800 (~€340)
    - Pionierspark: N$6,800
    - Dorado / Elisenheim: N$5,500
    - Khomasdal / Otjomuise: N$4,500
    - Katutura: N$4,000, often utilities included
  - 2-bed averages: Klein Windhoek N$9,600; Eros N$8,000 [1].
  - On 2026-11-14 the MyProperty Eros page showed two 1-bed listings at **N$13,000 and N$16,500** (~€650–825). These are asking prices from a tiny sample, with no listing date [15].
  - livingcost.org (data 2026-06-21) [16]:
    - 1-bed in the centre about **US$501**; a cheaper 1-bed US$352.
    - Single person with rent: **US$1,040/month** (~€900).
    - Lunch menu: US$11.70. Fast-food meal: US$5.80.
  - Expat.com (October 2025) estimates **N$24,300/month (~€1,215)** for a single person (its own summary box says €1,100, so treat as approximate) and N$75,300 for a family of four, including N$20,800 of school fees [4].
  - Typical expat / middle-class areas: Klein Windhoek, Eros, Ludwigsdorf, Olympia, Pionierspark, Kleine Kuppe, Windhoek West [1][4].
- **Work & money:**
  - Windhoek is the administrative, commercial and financial centre. It hosts ministry head offices, banks and the national media [10].
  - Employers: government, banks (e.g. Bank Windhoek, FNB Namibia), mining head offices and NGOs.
  - Local salaries are low. livingcost.org gives a median net salary of about US$289/month (2026) [16]. The legal minimum wage has been N$18/hour since 2025-01-01 [4].
  - Afrobarometer (Round 10, 2024; paper published 2026-10-08) finds Namibians economically restrictive towards foreign job seekers. Only 23% back maintaining or increasing inflows [20]. Expect work permits to favour scarce skills.
  - Remote work for a euro income is feasible thanks to fibre in the suburbs. No digital-nomad visa was found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - StartupBlink 2026 ranks **Windhoek 525th globally**, the only Namibian city in the top 1,000. The country is 94th globally and 10th in Africa, down from 85th in 2025 [17].
  - Hubs and programmes:
    - **CcHUB Namibia**: about N$1m invested since 2021, 200+ startups supported, 100+ co-working members, 5,000-member community [18].
    - **ScaleUp Namibia**: run by NCRST and NIPDB, launched March 2025 [19].
    - Basecamp; Moodbod Digital Agency [19b].
    - NIPDB **SME Fund**: first call June–July 2026; 14 MSMEs shared N$1.1m [6].
    - **Startup Namibia** information hub [6b].
    - **Bellatrix** regional seed fund, launched May 2026 [19c].
    - **Accelerate36**: aims to list startups on the NSX Development Board (2026) [19c].
  - A Brief op-ed (April 2026) says the path to scale is "unclear" [19b].
  - Company registration: BIPA opened a one-stop shop in November 2025. The steps are BIPA registration, an investor permit, NamRA tax, Social Security and VAT above N$1m turnover [4].
  - Crypto: the **Virtual Assets Act 2023** is listed by the Bank of Namibia [7]. Retail adoption data was not found. Crypto here is a regulated niche, not a daily-life feature.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - **Power:** NamPower said in October 2022 that national load-shedding "has never happened". In May 2023 it admitted Eskom had been load-shedding Namibia "proportionately"; Namibia imports about 60% of its electricity, covering shortfalls through the Southern African Power Pool [21]. Reliability in 2025–26: not found (unverified).
  - **Water is the real constraint.** Windhoek was under **Category D water-scarcity restrictions (20% cut)** in August 2024. Dams were at 22.2% and no end date was set [22]. The City warned in February 2024 that consumption exceeded supply by 15% [22b].
  - **Internet:** Telecom Namibia, MTC and Paratus provide fibre or ADSL. Mobile is MTC or TN Mobile. Expect about N$800/month for home internet [4]. livingcost.org gives US$48/month for 50 Mbps+ [16].
  - **Mobile money:** M-Pesa-style mobile money is not widespread (not found). Bank e-wallets dominate.
  - **Ride-hailing:**
    - **Yango** operates and won a 2026 reliability award. It raised fares 5% in May 2026 [23].
    - Bolt piloted in February 2024 [23b].
    - Local LEFA said in April 2024 that it was threatened with closure [23b].
  - The city is car-dependent. The FCDO says do not hail taxis in the street [3].
- **Health & schools:**
  - Private hospitals: **Rhino Park Private Hospital** and the **Windhoek Central Hospital private wing** [4]. Serious cases are often evacuated to South Africa [4].
  - Local medical aid costs about N$2,000–5,000/month for a family [4].
  - Schools:
    - **Windhoek International School** (IB): N$84,000–166,000 per year in 2024-25 [4].
    - Delta School Windhoek [4].
    - Concordia College, St Paul's College [10].
    - **No AEFE-accredited French school was found.** Expat.com lists a French section at the Deutsche Höhere Privatschule (DHPS) Windhoek, but that page dates from 2009 and the school site could not be read (unverified for 2026) [4b]. Families otherwise use CNED [4]. The FNCC offers French-language culture [9].
- **Safety:**
  - FCDO (read 2026-11-14; update date not confirmed) [3]:
    - "Muggers often target tourists in Windhoek"; attacks "can take place at any time of day".
    - "Gangs sometimes try to enter vehicles at busy intersections in Windhoek."
    - Do not enter townships at night "unless you are with someone with local knowledge".
  - France Diplomatie keeps a vigilance map, page updated 2026-09-10 [5]. The detailed text could not be retrieved.
  - Expat.com describes crime as "controlled" but advises securing your home. Expats favour gated complexes [4].
  - Nuance: the leafy east and south (Klein Windhoek, Ludwigsdorf, Eros) are walled-house suburbs. The FCDO warns about townships at night without naming them [3]; Katutura and the informal north-west settlements are the townships in question (editorial reading, unverified by a dated crime source).
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - A small capital: 486k people [10], clean and orderly, more than 300 sunny days a year [10].
  - Social life runs through restaurants, braai culture, church and sports clubs.
  - The MOVEMENT international dance festival launched in June 2026 with French embassy support [9].
  - Christianity is strong [14]. The pace is slow and family-friendly.
  - Nightlife is modest (low confidence; no dated source found).
- **Diaspora & expat community:**
  - **Expat.com Namibia forum**: about 4,764 members (2025) [4].
  - French embassy events (14 July) and consular sessions at the FNCC; Namibia depends on the Johannesburg consulate [9].
  - Facebook / WhatsApp groups, returnee networks and Namibian-diaspora associations in France: **not found / not verified** (search unavailable).
- **Climate in practice:**
  - Hot semi-arid climate. Rain falls in summer (November–April), with February the wettest month [10].
  - July is cold: 13.7 °C on average, with near-freezing nights. Houses rarely have heating.
  - December averages 24.1 °C [10]. Droughts drive water restrictions [22].
- **Watch out:**
  - Extreme inequality [14].
  - Petty and vehicle crime [3].
  - Water scarcity [22].
  - Small job market, and restrictive attitudes towards foreign job seekers [20].
  - Nearly zero French speakers; no French school.
  - Visa required for French citizens since April 2025 [4].

### Swakopmund

- **Cost of living:**
  - livingcost.org (2026-06-21) [24]:
    - 1-bed in the centre about **US$469**; a cheaper 1-bed US$286.
    - Single person with rent: **US$834/month** (~€720).
    - Lunch menu: US$6.17.
  - Expat.com (2025) puts a sea-view apartment at **N$10,000–20,000** (~€500–1,000) [4].
  - Areas: Vineta, Vogelstrand, Ocean View, Kramersdorf, Waterfront and the Town Centre. Tamariskia and Mondesa are working-class [11].
  - Building approvals rose 119% in 2025 because of strong demand [25]. Expect rent pressure linked to the oil and gas activity [26].
- **Work & money:**
  - The economy rests on tourism, uranium (Rössing mine, 60–70 km away) and salt [11].
  - Since 2025–26 it is also a base for the offshore oil and gas boom [26].
  - In July 2026 the town CEO said "Swakopmund will not survive on tourism alone" [26]. The town is pushing education, light manufacturing and a smart city, with a one-month decision on complete investment applications [26].
  - Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - The town plans "technology hubs" [26]. No named incubator was found.
  - The startup scene is essentially Windhoek's.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - Demshi open-access fibre: 6,800 homes passed (Matutura, Tamariskia, Mondesa), with ISPs Paratus, Echo and IT Guru (September 2024) [27].
  - livingcost.org gives US$73/month for internet [24].
  - The town is walkable and small. Water on the coast comes partly from desalination (detail not found).
- **Health & schools:**
  - Private: **Cottage Medi-Clinic**, 70 beds [11]. Public: Swakopmund State Hospital [11]. The town plans a specialised hospital [26].
  - Schools: Private School Swakopmund, Namib High School and others [11][4]. No French school.
- **Safety:**
  - The FCDO says nothing specific about Swakopmund [3].
  - Rössing funded a new police station in the DRC informal area in March 2025 [28].
  - Generally perceived as calmer than Windhoek (low confidence; no dated city-level crime data found).
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - A German-flavoured seaside town with a promenade, cafés, adventure sports and dunes.
  - Quiet, retiree- and family-oriented. Little nightlife (editorial; low confidence).
  - Swakopmund has held a Pride parade since June 2016 (Windhoek since December 2013) [8].
- **Diaspora & expat community:** German-speaking community [11]. Diaspora or French groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice:**
  - Mild all year: mean daily highs about 18–23 °C, lows about 10–16 °C, under 20 mm of rain a year, and cold Atlantic fog [11].
  - Grey mornings are common, so damp and salt affect houses and cars.
  - Occasional hot east-wind days (not sourced).
- **Watch out:**
  - Fog and a grey climate.
  - Housing shortage and rising rents [25][29].
  - Small-town job market dependent on mining and oil.
  - Remote from Windhoek: 352 km by the B2 road [11].

### Walvis Bay (added: the country's main port and second economic pole)

- 102,704 inhabitants (2023) [12], about 30–35 km south of Swakopmund (distance not sourced).
- Port, fishing and salt: about 1 million t/year [12].
- A **green-energy export hub** is planned by 2027 [30]. The EU mobilised N$25bn for green hydrogen in September 2025 [30].
- Expat.com says rents start at **N$8,000/month** (~€400) [4]. Demshi fibre covers 4,000+ homes [27].
- New Era (April 2026) reports a housing crisis [29].
- Grittier and cheaper than Swakopmund. This is where logistics, port and energy jobs are.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of facts, not measurements. No city and no quiz question measures a trait. Neither Hofstede nor GLOBE country scores for Namibia were found (not found). Afrobarometer is the only survey source used.

**Windhoek**
- **Openness:** Suits people at ease in a multilingual (English / Afrikaans / German / Oshiwambo [10]), very unequal [14], post-colonial setting with a still-live genocide memory [14]. The cultural offer is modest but growing (MOVEMENT festival 2026 [9]).
- **Conscientiousness:** Rewards planners. Permits take 3–4 months [4], water rules are strict [22], and a car-based life needs security routines [3].
- **Extraversion:** Better for people who like small circles (braai, church, sport) than for big-city nightlife. The city has 486k people [10].
- **Agreeableness:** Socially, 80% would not mind foreign-worker neighbours [20]. Polite, reserved social codes reward patience.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** You need to tolerate crime precautions [3] and drought anxiety [22]. In exchange you get a calm daily pace.

**Swakopmund**
- **Openness:** A low-stimulus, nature-heavy place (desert, ocean) [11]. It suits people who find novelty outdoors rather than in culture.
- **Conscientiousness:** Life is quiet and orderly.
- **Extraversion:** Suits introverts and families.
- **Agreeableness:** You need to fit into a small community with a strong German-heritage identity [11].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** Low-stress overall. It still requires accepting fog [11], isolation and a narrow job market.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - On **21 June 2024** the High Court (*Dausab v Minister of Justice*) struck down the common-law sodomy offences. The government appealed in July 2024, and the outcome was not found [8].
  - On 16 May 2023 the Supreme Court ordered recognition of same-sex marriages concluded abroad for residence purposes [8].
  - A law signed **December 2024** bans same-sex marriage and the recognition of foreign ones, according to Wikipedia citing Mamba Online [8]. The primary Government Gazette text was not checked (secondary source only). **Same-sex couples cannot rely on a spouse permit.**
  - The FCDO says there is no anti-discrimination law and conservative attitudes, especially in rural areas [3].
  - Afrobarometer Round 10 (2024, published 2025-09-15) [31]:
    - 51% would accept a homosexual neighbour.
    - 68% oppose same-sex marriage.
    - 64% oppose equal rights.
  - Pride has taken place in Windhoek since December 2013 and in Swakopmund since June 2016 [8].
- **Foreigners / diaspora:**
  - Afrobarometer (published 2026-10-08) [20]:
    - Socially tolerant: 80% accept foreign-worker neighbours.
    - Economically restrictive: only 23% want inflows of foreign job seekers kept steady or increased; about three-quarters want fewer or none.
    - Contrasts with South Africa, which is hostile on both counts.
  - No specific reports on the treatment of Black diaspora or of colourism were found (not found).
  - Racial inequality is a structural legacy of apartheid-era rule [14]. Land data was not retrieved.
- **Conflict:** No armed conflict. The FCDO lists no terrorism history [3].
- **Women:**
  - The Brief (2024-12-09) describes a "persistent sexual and gender-based violence crisis" and no functional data system [32]. Quantified data was not found.
  - The FCDO page has no women-specific section [3].
  - Afrobarometer (2025-04-02): 72% support women's equal chance to be elected [33].
