## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** English and Swahili are both official; Swahili is the national language and is spoken by more people than English once second-language speakers are counted. English dominates business, schooling and government. Rural residents often speak only their community language. Sheng, an urban mix of Swahili and English, is the street language in Nairobi [22]. French is barely used outside the French embassy and school network and the Alliance Française. It is a real barrier for monolingual French speakers [22, editorial].
- **Currency:** Kenyan shilling (KES). Numbeo's November 2026 conversion implies about **1 EUR ≈ 143 KES** [5]. Daily life runs on **M-Pesa** mobile money (paying bills, buying goods, airtime, savings, and the Fuliza overdraft launched in January 2019). Cash withdrawals at agents can cost about 10% on small amounts [14].
- **(a) Entry and residence for French/EU passport holders:**
  - Before travelling, apply online for an **electronic travel authorisation (eTA)**, ideally at least 2 weeks ahead. Passports need 6 months' validity and 2 blank pages [2, GOV.UK, undated page, accessed 2026-11-14; written for British nationals, but the eTA applies to most nationalities].
  - **African passport holders:** since **30 May 2025** most African nationals (all except Libya and Somalia) are exempt from the eTA, with visa-free stays of 60 to 180 days depending on nationality; East African Community neighbours get the longest stays [23, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14; confirm on etakenya.go.ke]. For the diaspora this matters: entering on an African passport avoids the eTA, while a French passport still needs one. The eTA fee is USD 32.50 [23].
  - **New from 5 October 2026:** the eTA application includes compulsory **Kenyan government travel health insurance**. It applies to most travellers staying under 12 months, and foreign travel insurance is not accepted in its place [2, re-checked 2026-11-14]. The "90 days, multiple entries" detail could not be re-confirmed on the page (unverified).
  - **Residence permits** (Expat.com, updated 7 November 2025 [15]):
    - **Class D:** employment, 2 years, renewable, needs a Kenyan employer's offer.
    - **Class G:** investors and entrepreneurs, 2 years.
    - **Class K:** retirees and the self-funded over 35, with at least USD 24,000 a year in foreign income.
    - **Class N (digital nomad), created in 2024:** same USD 24,000 income floor; no work for Kenyan employers.
    - **Class C:** regulated professions.
    - Applications go through the online eFNS portal and take 2 to 6 months. An Alien Card must be obtained within 3 months of arrival.
    - Primary source immigration.go.ke could not be opened, so verify.
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:**
  - **Dual citizenship** has been allowed for Kenyans by origin since the 2010 Constitution. The President, Deputy President and some high offices are excluded. The 2011 Act required a dual national to declare the other nationality within 3 months of acquiring it; the source does not say whether this still applies [8].
  - Former Kenyans who lost citizenship by taking another nationality must apply for reinstatement. The procedure is not described in the source [8].
  - **Citizenship by descent:** a child born anywhere to one Kenyan parent is Kenyan at birth. Mothers pass citizenship as fathers do, retroactively for children born abroad [8].
  - **Spouses of Kenyans** can register after 7 years of marriage [8].
  - **No right-of-return or Afro-descendant scheme** comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found. A Kenyan diaspora policy or diaspora affairs department exists according to general knowledge, but no source was opened to confirm it: **not found**.
- **Current context (October–November 2026):**
  - France Diplomatie (updated 29 October 2026) flags a **pre-election period ahead of the general election on 10 August 2027**, with risk of deadly political violence [4].
  - It also flags October–December rain and El Niño flooding with a possible cholera, typhoid and malaria resurgence [4].
  - It reports a **first confirmed Ebola case on 6 October 2026** (imported via the DRC and Uganda), with related cases but no widespread community transmission reported [4].

## Cities

### Nairobi

- **Cost of living:**
  - **1-bed rent:** about KES 58,500/month (≈ €408) in the centre, range KES 30,000–90,000. About KES 30,600 (≈ €213) outside the centre, range KES 15,000–59,700 [5, Numbeo, updated 2026-11-06].
  - **2-bed rent by neighbourhood** (Expat.com, Aug 2025 data [15]):
    - **Kilimani:** KES 60,000–120,000.
    - **Westlands:** KES 80,000–150,000. Lively and popular with young professionals.
    - **Lavington** (3-bed house): KES 100,000–200,000.
    - **Karen** (3-bed house): KES 150,000–300,000. Leafy and close to international schools.
    - **Runda / Kitisuru:** above KES 300,000.
  - Landlords usually ask for 3 months' deposit plus 1–2 months in advance [15].
  - Lower- and upper-middle-income areas include Parklands, South B, South C, Madaraka and Highridge [9].
  - **Meal:** about KES 850 (≈ €6) at a cheap restaurant [5].
  - **Monthly budget:**
    - Numbeo: about €530 for a single person **excluding** rent [5].
    - Expat.com: about KES 152,500 (≈ €1,176) for a single person, and about KES 1,060,000 (≈ €8,175) for a family of four, mostly international school fees [15].
- **Work and money:**
  - Nairobi is East Africa's financial hub and produces about 27.5% of Kenya's GDP (2022) [9].
  - It hosts the **UN Office at Nairobi and UNEP**. The Nairobi Securities Exchange is one of Africa's largest [9].
  - NGO, UN and regional-HQ jobs are a major expat employer (editorial, consistent with [9]).
  - **Remote work:** the Class N digital-nomad permit exists [15]. Fibre is available at about KES 4,500/month for 60 Mbps or more [5].
  - **Salaries:** not found.
- **Entrepreneurship and tech:**
  - Nairobi is the "Silicon Savannah." **iHub** was founded in March 2010 by Erik Hersman, Juliana Rotich, David Kobia and Ory Okolloh, and acquired by Lagos-based **CcHUB** in September 2019 [18]. **NaiLab** shared iHub's building until 2017, and there were about a dozen hubs in 2015 [18].
  - **Konza Technopolis** is a government smart city and special economic zone 64 km south of Nairobi, with a national data centre. Phase One was due in 2020 but was delayed by funding; Wikipedia's infobox now gives 2030. The **KAIST Kenya** postgraduate institute was approved in March 2025 and is due to open academically in 2026 [11].
  - **Fintech:** M-Pesa underpins everything [14].
  - **Crypto and biometrics:** in May 2025 Kenya ordered Worldcoin ("World") to delete all biometric data collected in the country, after suspending it in August 2023 [20]. This shows a regulator that is alert on data and crypto-adjacent ventures. A Virtual Asset Service Providers Act (2025) is believed to exist **(unverified)**: no page confirming it could be opened in either pass, so do not cite it until checked on kenyalaw.org.
  - **Investor presence and 2025–26 funding figures:** not found (TechCabal blocked).
  - **Company registration ease:** not found.
  - Rest of World covered Nairobi's grassroots AI meet-ups (4 Aug 2026) and how cheap Chinese phones pushed Kenya into the digital economy (12 Feb 2026) [17].
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - **Power:** Kenya Power has a history of debt and billing complaints, and imports power from Ethiopia under a 2022 agreement [21]. Outage frequency: **not found**.
  - **Internet:** fibre at about KES 4,500/month [5].
  - **Transport:** the Nairobi Expressway (2022) eases Mombasa Road traffic. The SGR train runs to Mombasa (since 2017) [9]. Traffic congestion is notorious (editorial).
  - **Ride-hailing:** apps are widely used, but no source was opened to confirm names: **not found**.
- **Health and schools:**
  - **Hospitals:** Nairobi Hospital, Aga Khan University Hospital and Karen Hospital [15].
  - **Health insurance:** SHA replaced NHIF in 2024, with a contribution of 2.75% of gross salary. Private family cover costs KES 50,000–200,000 a year [15].
  - **Schools:** **Lycée Denis Diderot** is the approved French school, at KES 800,000–1.5 M a year. The International School of Kenya (IB/US) costs KES 3.18–4.89 M, Nairobi International School KES 2.8–4.2 M, and Braeburn (British) KES 1.5–3.2 M [15]. Enrol about 6 months ahead [15].
- **Safety** (UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14 [1]; France Diplomatie updated 2026-10-29 [4]):
  - Violent crime (mugging, armed robbery, carjacking) is frequent.
  - Higher-risk areas are **Eastleigh, the CBD, Mathare, Kibera and other informal settlements**.
  - Bag-snatching is common at bus and rail stations. Crime peaks around Christmas and New Year.
  - The terrorism threat is high. Al-Shabaab could target hotels, malls, nightclubs and places of worship.
  - **Protests** are frequent in Nairobi:
    - Storming of Parliament on 25 June 2024; 80+ deaths over the 2024 movement by a 2026 BBC count [13].
    - 16 killed and at least 400 injured in the June 2025 anniversary protests [13; the Wikipedia page gives no source for this figure, so treat it as unconfirmed].
    - 355 arrests around the 25 June 2026 commemorations (Interior CS Murkomen, via BBC, 25 June 2026). During the 30 June 2026 demonstrations police shot three people, one of whom died [13].
    - Human Rights Watch's 2026 World Report lists 26 people abducted in 2024 and 16 in 2025 as still missing [13].
- **Social life and vibe:**
  - Nightlife, music and food scenes exist but were not sourced in this pass: **not found**.
  - Hustle-driven, fast pace (editorial).
  - The leafy suburbs (Karen, Lavington) are family-oriented [15].
- **Diaspora and expat community:**
  - Expat.com's Kenya forum has about 25,000 members and a "Francophones au Kenya" thread [15].
  - Facebook and WhatsApp groups, returnee networks and Nairobi Accueil-type French associations: **not found** (not searched).
- **Climate in practice:**
  - Highland climate at 1,795 m. Cool evenings around 9 °C in June–July; warmest December–March, near 28 °C [9].
  - Long rains peak around March–May and short rains around October–December; October–December floods can cut roads [4].
  - No air conditioning needed. A jumper is needed in July (editorial).
- **Watch out:**
  - High crime with strict security habits (gated compounds).
  - Political protests and pre-2027 election tension [4][13].
  - Expensive international schools [15].
  - Heavy traffic.
  - Little French spoken.

### Mombasa

- **Cost of living:**
  - **1-bed rent:** about KES 30,000 (≈ €209) in the centre, range 20,000–50,000. About KES 20,700 (≈ €144) outside the centre, range 13,000–25,000 [6, Numbeo, updated 2026-10-03, only 13 contributors so low confidence].
  - **Meal:** about KES 350 (≈ €2.4) [6].
  - **Monthly budget:** not provided by Numbeo [6].
  - **Neighbourhoods:** upscale **Nyali** on the north mainland. **Bamburi** and **Shanzu** further north along the beaches [10].
- **Work and money:**
  - Main sectors: Kilindini port (serving Kenya and the interior), oil refinery, cement, food and chemical industries, and coastal tourism [10].
  - Youth unemployment was 44% in 2016 (old figure) [10].
  - **Remote work:** fibre at about KES 4,500/month is available [6].
- **Entrepreneurship and tech:**
  - Special Economic Zones are planned [10].
  - Local hubs and incubators: **not found**.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - The SGR train links Mombasa to Nairobi [10].
  - The **Likoni ferry** is the bottleneck to the south coast. The Dongo Kundu southern bypass, completed in 2024, offers a road alternative [19].
  - Recurring flooding [10].
  - Power and water reliability: **not found**.
- **Health and schools:** reference hospitals and international or French schools: **not found**. Families typically rely on Nairobi for specialist care (editorial, unverified).
- **Safety:**
  - FCDO [1] flags **Old Town and the Likoni Ferry area** as higher-risk for crime.
  - The high terrorism threat and kidnap risk extend to **coastal counties**.
  - **Lamu and Manda islands** should be reached by air only, not by road [1].
- **Social life and vibe:**
  - Swahili-Mijikenda coastal culture, Old Town Swahili architecture, Fort Jesus, and mosques dating back to about 1300 [10].
  - Music: taarab, chakacha, bango [10].
  - **Religion:** Christian 56.4%, Muslim 37.3% in Mombasa County (2019 census) [10]. Islam shapes dress, food and Ramadan rhythms more than in Nairobi (editorial).
  - Slower pace than Nairobi (editorial).
- **Nearby alternative, Diani Beach:** about 30 km south in Kwale County, with a 17 km beach, the Ukunda airstrip and a tourism-based economy. The 2024 bypass makes the drive from Mombasa easier [19]. Its reputation as a remote-worker and kitesurf base is commonly reported but **not sourced** here.
- **Diaspora and expat community:** not found.
- **Climate in practice:** hot and humid all year, with highs of 28.8–33.7 °C. Heaviest rain falls in April–May; January–February is the driest period [10]. Expect air conditioning costs (editorial).
- **Watch out:**
  - Terrorism and kidnap advisories for coastal counties [1].
  - Petty crime in Old Town and at Likoni [1].
  - Heat and humidity.
  - A thinner job market outside port, logistics and tourism [10].

### Kisumu (added: the cheaper, lakeside third pole of western Kenya)

- **Why it matters:** Kenya's main city on Lake Victoria, a port and trade hub towards Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi, Tanzania and the DRC. It has an international airport with daily flights to Nairobi and Mombasa [12].
- **Cost of living:** not found. It is presumed cheaper than Nairobi (editorial, unverified).
- **Work and money:** sugar, rice irrigation and lake trade. County GDP (PPP) was about USD 7.3 bn in 2022 [12].
- **Health and schools:** universities nearby (Maseno, JOOUST) [12]. Hospitals and international schools: not found.
- **Safety:** no crime data was found. Road accident black spots include Ahero and Sondu [12].
- **Climate in practice:** about 22.9 °C average with no true dry season. April is wettest, January driest [12]. Malaria risk is higher near the lake (editorial, unverified).
- **Watch out:** thin data. Election-period unrest in Kisumu is often mentioned, but **no dated source was found**, so this must not be used on the site until sourced.

### Nakuru (added: Kenya's newest city, a quieter highland base 160 km from Nairobi)

- **Why it matters:** became a city on 1 December 2021. It had Kenya's third-largest urban population in 2019 (570,674) and sits about 160 km from Nairobi by highway [16].
- **Work and money:** agriculture (coffee, wheat, barley), light manufacturing and retail-chain logistics. Metro GDP (PPP) was about USD 14 bn in 2022 [16].
- **Vibe:** described as cosmopolitan, entrepreneurial and hospitable. Lake Nakuru National Park and its flamingos are next door [16].
- **Climate in practice:** temperate highland climate, cold nights in June–August, about 963 mm of rain a year [16].
- **Cost, safety, health and schools:** not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This section is interpretive. No city or quiz question measures personality. Academic regional data (Hofstede Insights for Kenya, GLOBE, Afrobarometer) **could not be retrieved**, so no academic source is cited.

**Nairobi**
- **Openness:** suits people drawn to a cosmopolitan, multilingual (English, Swahili, Sheng) setting. Nairobi hosts UN agencies, a stock exchange, and a tech-hub culture since iHub in 2010 [9][18][22].
- **Conscientiousness:** suits people who handle paperwork well. Work permits take 2–6 months through eFNS, and an Alien Card is required within 3 months [15]. Daily security routines also matter [1].
- **Extraversion:** networking-heavy professional life (NGOs, startups, meet-ups such as the AI gatherings Rest of World covered) [17]. Nightlife was not sourced.
- **Agreeableness:** no sourced fact to anchor this one. Not assessed.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** important. Frequent violent crime, recurring protests met with lethal force (2024–2026), a high terrorism threat and a tense pre-2027 election period all weigh on daily life [1][4][13].

**Mombasa**
- **Openness:** suits people curious about an old Swahili-Islamic port culture (Old Town, taarab music) [10].
- **Conscientiousness:** fewer formal job tracks, more trade and tourism self-employment [10].
- **Extraversion:** tourist-strip beach life in Nyali and Bamburi versus quieter Old Town life (editorial).
- **Agreeableness:** a mixed Christian–Muslim population (56%/37%) calls for comfort with religious codes [10].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** suits people who cope with heat and humidity (highs of 28–34 °C) and with coastal terror advisories [1][10].

## Legal and sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - Sex "against the order of nature" is criminalised under Penal Code s.162, with **up to 14 years** (up to 21 if aggravated; the Wikipedia page is internally inconsistent and also quotes 21 as the general penalty). "Gross indecency" between men carries up to 5 years under s.165 [7].
  - The **High Court refused to strike these sections down on 24 May 2019** [7].
  - In 2023 the **Supreme Court (3–2)** upheld the right of the NGLHRC to register [7].
  - The **Family Protection Bill** (2023), modelled on Uganda's law and including the death penalty for "aggravated homosexuality," has stalled in committee [7].
  - In July 2025 Kenya voted to renew the UN SOGI expert mandate [7].
  - On **20 May 2026** the High Court ordered that gender markers on ID be changeable [7].
  - A Court of Appeal hearing on s.162/165 was set for May 2026, with the **decision pending** [7].
  - All of the above come from Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14.
  - **Daily practice** (UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14 [1]): same-sex relationships are described as "not tolerated" in a conservative society; public displays of affection can lead to arrest; criminals increasingly target LGBT+ people through dating apps (set-ups, robbery).
- **Racism, colourism and treatment of diaspora or foreigners:** no reliable source was found in this pass: **not found**.
- **Conflict and terrorism:**
  - The terrorism threat is high nationwide (Al-Shabaab, growing Daesh support), with a **high kidnap risk in northern counties bordering Somalia and in coastal counties** (FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14 [1]).
  - Lamu is accessible by air only [1].
- **Political violence:** the 2024 Finance Bill protests caused 80+ deaths (2026 BBC count). The June 2025 protests left 16 dead. The June 2026 commemorations saw 355 arrests and one death. Unresolved abductions remain (HRW World Report 2026) [13].
- **Pre-election period:** France Diplomatie (29 Oct 2026) warns of potentially deadly violence ahead of the 10 August 2027 vote [4].
- **Women's daily experience:**
  - The FCDO notes mugging and bag-snatching. Sexual assaults are rare but happen; it advises dressing conservatively away from tourist resorts, especially in Mombasa [1, accessed 2026-11-14].
  - Femicide is a widely reported concern in Kenyan public debate, but **no source could be opened** (the Wikipedia page returned 404): **not found**.
- **Health alerts:** Ebola case confirmed 6 Oct 2026; seasonal cholera, typhoid and malaria with the floods [4].
- **Privacy and biometrics:** in May 2025 the government ordered World/Worldcoin to delete Kenyan biometric data [20].
