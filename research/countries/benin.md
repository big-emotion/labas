# Bénin

> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

Method note: about 30 web searches and 6 page reads were run on 2026-11-14. Several source pages could not be opened (403 or dead links), so some facts rest on search-result excerpts. Those are marked "(excerpt)" in the source list. Third-party cost-of-living aggregators disagree a lot. Treat every price below as an order of magnitude.

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day**: French is the official language and the language of administration, business and schooling. On the street in the south (Cotonou, Ouidah, Porto-Novo), Fon dominates, with Goun around Porto-Novo and Mina/Ewe towards Togo. Yoruba/Nago is spoken towards Nigeria, and Bariba and Dendi in the north. Nigerian English and Yoruba are common in trade around Dantokpa market. This is general knowledge and no 2023–2026 source was opened for it.
- **Currency**: West African CFA franc (XOF), pegged at 655.957 XOF = 1 EUR. Conversions below use roughly 656 XOF/EUR.
- **Politics (context for a move)**:
  - An attempted military coup on 7 December 2025 was put down within a day. Nigeria carried out air strikes in Cotonou at Benin's request, and ECOWAS deployed troops [5][6].
  - Romuald Wadagni, the former finance minister, won the presidential election of 12 April 2026 with 94.05% of the vote against a single rival, after the main opposition was excluded [7][8]. He was sworn in on 24 May 2026 [9].
  - Afrobarometer (2024) reports that 60% of Beninese are dissatisfied with how democracy works, and that perceived freedom of expression fell by 28 points over ten years [30].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**:
  - A visa is required, as an e-visa obtained before travel. The US advisory notes that border officials refuse entry without one [12].
  - Law no. 2025-15 of 2 July 2025 and decree no. 2026-049 (reported 3 April 2026) reorganise entry into a transit visa (up to 8 days) and a "visa de séjour". The tourist/visit visa lasts up to 90 days, renewable once. The business visa allows 90 days per entry over 2 years. The professional and study visas allow up to 12 months, renewable once [13].
  - Residence: a "résident ordinaire" card is valid 3 years. A "résident privilégié" card is valid 10 years, after two renewals plus proven ties such as property, a viable business or marriage [13].
  - A work-based residence card needs a job offer validated by the Ministry of Labour. It is filed with the Direction de l'Immigration, reported to be in Porto-Novo. Fee estimates run from about 120,000 XOF to 150–450 EUR. These come from unverified third-party guides, so check the official portal [14].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**:
  - *Beninese parentage*: under the Nationality Code (law no. 2022-32 of 20 December 2022), a child of a Beninese father or mother is Beninese. Dual nationality is allowed: the 1965 code allowed it and the 2022 revision kept it. A foreign spouse can acquire nationality after a 5-year probation from the date of marriage [15].
  - *Afro-descendants (no Beninese parent)*: law no. 2024-31 of 2 September 2024 recognises Beninese nationality for adult descendants of Africans deported in the slave trade. Applicants must not hold another African nationality. Proof of ancestry can include a DNA test showing sub-Saharan descent [1][2][16].
    - The implementing decree was signed 20 November 2024. Amendments in 2025 extended access to applicants living abroad (law 2025-11) and created a personal ID number for recognised Afro-descendants (law 2025-12) [1].
    - Applications go through the **My Afro Origins** platform (myafroorigins.bj), launched 4 July 2025 [1][3].
    - The first three certificates were issued on 26 July 2025, including to the singer Ciara. A second ceremony on 27 December 2025 in Cotonou naturalised ten people [1][16].
    - To keep the status, holders must stay in Benin at least once within three years of receiving the certificate [1].
    - Around 50 people of African ancestry had moved to Benin under the programme by January 2026, per Semafor [16]. Spike Lee and Tonya Lewis Lee are Benin's ambassadors for African Americans [16].
    - Critics warn that wealthier returnees could buy prime land or build gated enclaves [17].
  - *Other routes*: ECOWAS citizens have free movement (unverified: not specifically sourced here).

## Cities

### Cotonou

The economic capital and seat of government: port, embassies, NGOs and almost all private-sector jobs.

- **Cost of living**:
  - *Neighbourhoods*: expats and the upper middle class concentrate in Haie Vive, Cocotiers, Patte d'Oie, Quartier Jak and Fidjrossè, all in the 12th arrondissement. The 7th–9th arrondissements are cited as good residential areas [10].
  - *Rent*: a one-bedroom costs about 150,000–300,000 XOF/month (230–460 EUR) in central districts and 100,000–200,000 XOF (150–300 EUR) further out, per Le Petit Journal (June 2024) [10]. Local unfurnished 2–3-room flats run 60,000–150,000 XOF/month [18].
  - *Furnished expat listings*: Fidjrossè-plage furnished flats are listed at about 500,000–800,000 XOF (760–1,220 EUR). Haie Vive furnished 3–4-bedroom flats run 1.5–2 million XOF (2,300–3,000 EUR) [18].
  - *Deposit*: by law the advance is 3 months of rent. Landlords often ask for more [10].
  - *Meals*: a basic meal with a drink is about 2,000 XOF (around 3 EUR) in a cheap restaurant (third-party aggregator, 2026) [19].
  - *Monthly budget*: estimates range from 420–520 USD to about 1,150 USD for a single person including rent. The aggregators disagree [19]. Overall costs are cited as about 33% below France [10].
  - *Imports*: imported goods such as cheese and charcuterie are expensive (2016 interview, OLD) [20].
- **Work & money**:
  - *Employers*: the port of Cotonou, trade and re-export to Nigeria, government, banks, telecoms (MTN, Moov, the state-owned Celtiis), NGOs and the UN, and the AEFE school network.
  - *Wages*: the minimum wage (SMIG) is 52,000 XOF/month (79 EUR) since 1 January 2023. The government admitted in July 2026 that some employers still pay 30,000–40,000 XOF [21]. A middle-manager salary of 400,000–800,000 XOF/month comes from a commercial blog (unverified) [21].
  - *Hiring culture*: hiring relies heavily on personal networks, and CVs carry less weight than in France [20].
  - *Remote work*: feasible on mobile 4G/5G plus a backup connection. Fixed internet is marginal (see infrastructure) and power cuts are frequent in 2026.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - *Company registration*: one of the easiest in the region. It is done online through the APIEx one-stop shop at monentreprise.bj and paid by mobile money or card. The APIEx practical guide lists 10,000 XOF for a sole proprietorship, 22,000 XOF for an SARL (private or notarised statutes) and 37,000 XOF for an SAS or SA (notarised statutes, so notary fees come on top), each with a stated 3 working hours. The 2026 ADPME checklist says a few hours to a few working days. You receive the RCCM, IFU (tax ID) and legal notice [22].
  - *Hubs*: **Sèmè City** is the state innovation and knowledge city. Its Sèmè One campus covers 4,500 m² in central Cotonou with coworking and a data centre, and it hosts **Epitech Bénin** [23]. Other hubs:
    - **EtriLabs**, with 2 sites in Cotonou, plus Parakou and Dakar [24]
    - **Blolab**, described as the first coworking space in Cotonou [24]
    - **Future Studio**
    - **Women EdTech**
    - **Startup Valley** at the Fondation UAC
    - The last three were named in a February 2026 government call with the ADSC and ADPME [24].
  - *Fintech*: **FedaPay** (funded by the Benin Business Angel Network, BBAN), **KKiaPay** and **FeexPay**. FeexPay announced its BCEAO payment-institution licence on 2 September 2025 and Beninese media called it the first Beninese fintech licensed; the BCEAO register (31 January 2026) also lists JULAYA Bénin and OPEN SI with Benin licences dated 4–8 September 2025, so treat "first" with caution [25].
  - *Mobile money*: 85.8% penetration in Q1 2025. Account market shares were 61.3% MTN MoMo, 22.7% Moov Money and 16.0% Celtiis Cash, with Celtiis up from 6.7% a year earlier (ARCEP Bénin, SFM dashboard at 31 March 2025, opened) [26].
  - *Crypto*: no Benin-specific law (unverified beyond the regional sources below). Crypto falls under BCEAO/UEMOA caution, and the BCEAO set up a C-CRYPTO review committee in 2025–2026 [27]. No reliable adoption data was found.
  - *Ecosystem size*: small. Cotonou ranked #945 in the StartupBlink 2025 city index, via an aggregator (unverified against StartupBlink itself) [24]. No 2025–2026 funding totals were found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - *Power*: in October–November 2026 the SBEE and SBPE reported voltage drops and load-shedding. They blame cuts in supply from Ghana and Nigeria, since about 60% of electricity is imported. Repairs were expected to take "a few months", with no end date [28]. Plan for a generator or inverter.
  - *Fixed internet*: fixed broadband penetration is about 0.42% (ARCEP, June 2025). Daily life runs on mobile data, with about 10.6 million active mobile internet subscriptions [26]. Moov has launched 5G [26].
  - *Ride-hailing*: Yango was suspended in December 2024 for operating without authorisation. Gozem was given two months to regularise [29]. Current 2026 status: not found.
  - *Other transport*: zémidjans (moto-taxis), regular taxis and the state-owned "Bénin Taxi" fleet [29].
  - *Traffic*: the Cotonou–Abomey-Calavi axis is among the most congested in the country [31].
- **Health & schools**:
  - *Hospitals*: CNHU Hubert Koutoukou Maga is the historic national referral hospital (not re-verified in 2026 sources). The new **CHIC – Centre Hospitalier International de Calavi**, about 18 km north of Cotonou, has 434 beds and places (earlier government and press figures said 436), was financed at about 175 million EUR (115–116 billion XOF) and is run with support from AP-HP International. The 16-ha site area was not re-verified. It has been opening in stages since 2025, with radiotherapy running since 1 April 2026 [32].
  - *Schools*: **EFE Montaigne** is an AEFE French school from primary to lycée. It is expensive with limited places, and forum feedback on quality is mixed but dated 2014–2015 [33]. Alternatives are the Catholic **Lycée Notre-Dame** and **Ufuk Bénin**, which teaches a French programme from kindergarten to middle school [33].
  - *Vaccines and malaria*: France Diplomatie's health page still lists yellow-fever vaccination as mandatory for entry. VisasNews reported in 2026 that Benin's health ministry no longer checks the certificate at entry points, but vaccination remains recommended, so carry the certificate. Malaria risk is high countrywide; ask a travel clinic about prevention [48].
- **Safety**:
  - *Official advice*: France Diplomatie (updated 15 Sept 2026) puts Cotonou in "vigilance renforcée", its lowest tier. At night, avoid the port areas and neighbourhoods along the railway lines in the city centre. Beaches are considered unsafe day and night. Thefts are frequent at Dantokpa market [11].
  - UK FCDO (2025–2026): street crime and mugging, carjacking risk, and a general terrorism threat that includes hotels, bars and beach resorts. Do not walk alone on the beach [4].
  - US State Dept: Level 2, "Exercise increased caution" (issued 4 July 2026) [12].
  - *Neighbourhoods*: Le Petit Journal also advises against Jonquet at night [10]. Haie Vive and Cocotiers are the expat comfort zone.
  - *Police*: two-thirds of Beninese trust the police (Afrobarometer Round 9) [30].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - *Nightlife*: Haie Vive is the bar and restaurant hub, with The Times/Les Moments, La Plancha and Le Livingstone named in travel blogs. Maquis with live bands play on weekends. Fidjrossè has beach bars, and Jonquet is the rougher late-night district (undated guides) [34].
  - *Pace and social codes*: faster and noisier than the rest of the country, but described as less stressful than France by a long-term French resident [20]. Strangers will not always greet you [20].
  - *Religion*: Christianity, Islam and Vodun coexist. Vodun is a living religion, with a national holiday on 10 January (general knowledge).
- **Diaspora & expat community**:
  - **Cotonou Accueil** is a French-speaking welcome association, FIAFE-affiliated, recommended on the Expat.com forum; current activity is unverified [35].
  - **InterNations Cotonou** has about 1,464 members and meets at the Institut Français [35].
  - The Expat.com Cotonou forum is very active.
  - **My Afro Origins** organises naturalisation ceremonies and diaspora events [1][3].
  - A large Nigerian community also lives here [36].
- **Climate in practice**: the south has four seasons. The long rains run April to mid-July, the short dry season mid-July to mid-September, the short rains to mid-November, and the dry season with harmattan from December to March [37].
  - Flooding hits low-lying areas such as Akpakpa, Ladji, Agla and Avotrou. On the night of 29–30 June 2026, heavy rain flooded several neighbourhoods and submerged the Houédonou bridge [37].
  - Since 2025, drainage works funded by the World Bank and the government (Sètovi, Vêdoko, Zogbo and others) have reduced how long water stays [37].
- **Watch out**:
  - power cuts in 2026
  - small and costly furnished housing for expats, with large deposits
  - flooding
  - uncertain ride-hailing
  - narrowing political space: the CPJ wrote to the president in September 2026 about press freedom [38]
  - the beach is unsafe to walk alone
  - the Cotonou–Calavi commute

### Ouidah

A historic town 40 km west of Cotonou: Route des Esclaves, Porte du Non-Retour, and the centre of Vodun culture.

- **Cost of living**: one blog (undated, OpenShores) cites rents of about 130 USD (around 85,000 XOF / 130 EUR) for a central one-bedroom, 150 USD near the beach and 80 USD on the outskirts. A whole house costs about 250 USD in the centre and 350 USD on the beach. Electricity runs 18–40 USD and mobile internet 12–20 USD. The blog estimates a 350–500 USD monthly budget (low confidence) [39]. Little long-term rental supply is visible online; the market is mostly Airbnb.
- **Work & money**: tourism and heritage, plus fishing and small trade. There are very few salaried jobs, so most residents are remote workers, retirees or entrepreneurs. Large projects include:
  - **Marina** hotel complex at Djègbadji near the Porte du Non-Retour [40]
  - **Route des Pêches** coastal road [40]
  - a golf course at Avlékété [40]
  - the main 330-ha campus of Sèmè City, under construction; opening date not confirmed [23]
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: no hub found in Ouidah itself; founders use Cotonou's hubs. Opportunities lie in tourism and hospitality around Vodun Days. The festival drew over 435,000 participants in 2025 and was held again on 8–10 January 2026 [41].
- **Daily infrastructure**: same national grid and power cuts as Cotonou [28]. Mobile money is everywhere. After the July 2026 rains, pumps were deployed in Ouidah as well as Cotonou [37]. Reaching Cotonou means the RNIE1 or the Route des Pêches; commute times were not found.
- **Health & schools**: no reference hospital or French/international school found in Ouidah. Families rely on Cotonou and the CHIC at Calavi, about 45–60 min away (estimate).
- **Safety**: France Diplomatie places Ouidah in the general "vigilance renforcée" tier by inference, since it is not named [11]. Beach warnings apply to the whole coast [4][11]. No Ouidah-specific crime reporting was found.
- **Social life & vibe**: small and calm. Vodun sites (Python Temple, Sacred Forest) and the January festival structure local life. Nightlife is limited. Religion and Vodun shape daily life more visibly than in Cotonou.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Ouidah is the symbolic heart of the Afro-descendant return policy. Vodun Days explicitly draws Afro-descendants [41][42], and My Afro Origins content features Ouidah [3]. No resident diaspora association was found.
- **Climate in practice**: same coastal regime as Cotonou. Waterlogging after heavy rain in July 2026 [37].
- **Watch out**:
  - evictions and land conflicts linked to the tourism mega-projects. Amnesty International reported in 2023 that Djègbadji communities were displaced [40]
  - few services
  - car dependence for anything serious, from hospitals to schools to admin
  - prices may rise as the projects open

### Abomey-Calavi (extra: where many Cotonou workers actually live)

- **Why it matters**: it is Cotonou's northern suburb and university town (UAC). It hosts the new **CHIC**, the country's largest hospital [32], and Startup Valley at the UAC [24]. Rents are lower, but the daily commute to Cotonou is notoriously congested [31].
- Cost: one furnished 4-room flat at Atrokpocodji is listed at about 650,000 XOF (unverified, no citation). Otherwise, specific long-term rent data was not found.
- Floods: 141 mm of rain fell near the university during the June 2026 event [37].
- Safety: "vigilance renforcée" tier [11].

### Porto-Novo (extra: the official capital, cheaper and calmer)

- **Why it matters**: the political capital, about 30 km east of Cotonou, close to Nigeria. It has Yoruba/Goun heritage and Afro-Brazilian architecture. Immigration paperwork is reportedly filed here [14].
- The AFD "Porto-Novo ville verte" programme (9.2 million EUR) is landscaping the lagoon banks: a green promenade, drainage and flood resilience. A Brut report from September 2025 shows the results [43].
- Rents are cited as lower than Cotonou (unverified blog) [18]. Little nightlife and few international schools.
- Safety: "vigilance renforcée" tier [11].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This is not measured data. No city or quiz question measures a personality trait; this section describes who tends to feel at ease in each environment, based on the cited facts above.

Academic context: no Benin-specific Hofstede scores exist. Hofstede's "West Africa" estimate (Ghana, Nigeria, Sierra Leone) gives high power distance (77) and low individualism (20) [44]. Afrobarometer Round 9 finds Central and West Africa at the top of the continent for tolerance of social differences, with higher interpersonal trust in West Africa [44].

**Cotonou**
- Openness: suits people curious about mixing worlds: Vodun, churches and mosques side by side, Nigerian trade culture, and a young tech scene at Sèmè City and EtriLabs [23][24].
- Conscientiousness: rewards planners. You need generator or inverter backups through the 2026 load-shedding [28], and housing deposits often above 3 months [10]. In return, business registration is fast and online [22].
- Extraversion: networking is how jobs are found [20]. Haie Vive bars and weekend maquis concerts reward sociable people [34].
- Agreeableness: greetings, family ties and respect for hierarchy matter, consistent with high power distance [44]. A newcomer who takes time for relationships does better.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): traffic on the Calavi axis [31], floods in the rainy season [37], power cuts [28] and a tense political climate after the December 2025 coup attempt [5] all call for a calm temperament.

**Ouidah**
- Openness: may suit people drawn to spirituality, history and memory: Vodun Days, the Route des Esclaves [41].
- Conscientiousness: self-starters who can build their own routine without a job market.
- Extraversion: lower need. A small town with a seasonal festival peak in January [41].
- Agreeableness: a close-knit community with strong religious and traditional life; respect for local codes is essential. Land is sensitive, given displacements around the Marina project [40].
- Emotional stability: a slower pace, but it demands comfort with isolation from services, which are concentrated in Cotonou and Calavi.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex sexual activity is legal (UK FCDO, 2025–2026 page) [4]. No anti-discrimination protection exists [45]. Same-sex relationships are "not widely accepted" and public displays of affection draw negative attention [4]. In the region, Burkina Faso (Sept 2025), Senegal (March 2026) and Niger (June 2026) have criminalised or tightened penalties [45]. No change in Benin's law was found as of November 2026.
- **Treatment of diaspora / foreigners**: the government actively courts Afro-descendants through the 2024 law, My Afro Origins and its celebrity ambassadors [1][16]. Critical voices (AllAfrica, Nov 2025) call the policy partly marketing [17]. No 2023–2026 source on racism or colourism toward diaspora returnees in Benin was found. Anecdotally, foreigners may be overcharged at markets (2016 interview, OLD) [20].
- **Conflict zones**:
  - The jihadist threat is concentrated in the north. France Diplomatie (zones updated 21 April 2026) formally advises against travel to the northern border areas, including the W and Pendjari parks [11]. A "discouraged unless imperative" band runs from Kandi to Tchaourou, including Nikki [11].
  - UK FCDO extended "all but essential travel" advice to all of Alibori and Atacora in December 2025 [4][46].
  - The US says Do Not Travel to areas bordering Burkina Faso, Niger and northern Nigerian states, and reported more kidnappings near Kalalé and Nikki in late 2025 [12].
  - An "Urgence Attentat" notice appeared on France Diplomatie's Benin page (18 March 2026, updated 7 April 2026), but it reflected France raising its own domestic Vigipirate posture, not a Benin-specific threat level. It no longer appears in the page version updated 15 September 2026 [11].
  - Coup attempt on 7 December 2025 in Cotonou [5][6].
  - None of the four cities above is in a red or advised-against zone.
- **Women's daily experience**:
  - A national study found that gender-based violence affected 45.9% of women in 2022, cited at the October 2024 CEDAW review [47].
  - 28.4% of women were in a union before 18 (EUAA/Belgian COI, May 2025) [47].
  - The government is campaigning against digital gender-based violence (16 Days of Activism, 25 November 2025) and is building victim-support centres (January 2026) [47].
  - No Cotonou-specific street-harassment survey was found.
  - Official travel advice has no women-specific section [4][11].
- **Press freedom**: the CPJ wrote to the president in September 2026, and journalist Comlan Sossoukpè was detained in July 2025 [38].

## Questions that would discriminate between this country's cities

1. « Tu as besoin d'un salaire local ou tu travailles à distance / tu as déjà tes revenus ? » (local salary → Cotonou ; remote income or retirement → Ouidah or Porto-Novo)
2. « Tu préfères une grande ville animée ou une petite ville calme au bord de la mer ? » (Cotonou vs Ouidah)
3. « Tu as des enfants qui doivent aller dans une école française ? » (yes → Cotonou)
4. « Ce qui t'attire au Bénin, c'est plutôt les affaires et la tech, ou l'histoire, la mémoire et le vodun ? » (Cotonou vs Ouidah)
5. « Tu veux pouvoir sortir le soir dans des bars et des concerts plusieurs fois par semaine ? » (yes → Cotonou)
6. « Être à 10 minutes d'un grand hôpital, c'est indispensable pour toi ? » (yes → Cotonou or Abomey-Calavi)
