## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day**: Amharic is the working language of Addis-Abeba and of Bahir Dar
  (Amhara region); Afaan Oromo dominates Oromia; English is used in business, NGOs, the AU/UN
  bubble and higher education. French is marginal outside the French school and embassy circle
  (Lycée Guebre-Mariam teaches in French, English and Amharic [10]). Language picture is general
  knowledge, not sourced in this round (?).
- **Currency**: Ethiopian birr (ETB). The birr was floated in 2024 and fell from about 57 to about
  106 per USD within about a week [11] (that figure is tagged "citation needed" on Wikipedia, checked
  2026-11-14; treat as approximate). On 6 Nov 2026 it traded around **161 ETB per USD** [1]. EUR figures
  below use an *assumed* ~185 ETB per EUR (EUR/USD ≈ 1.15, not verified in this round).
- **Inflation**: 14.9 % year-on-year in September 2026; central bank policy rate 16 % [1].
- **(a) French / EU passport holders**: visa and residence-permit rules were **not found** in this
  round (FCDO and France Diplomatie pages fetched did not include their entry sections [2][3]).
  A "golden visa" fee article is referenced by The Rio Times but was not opened [1]. The official
  e-visa portal is evisa.gov.et [17]; aggregator sites quote USD 52–82 for a 30-day tourist e-visa,
  but no official fee table or French-specific rule could be read (unverified). A tourist e-visa is
  not a residence or work permit.
- **(b) Diaspora with Ethiopian roots**: Ethiopia does **not** allow dual nationality; a dual
  national is treated solely as Ethiopian until loss of Ethiopian nationality [5]. Foreign nationals
  of Ethiopian origin can obtain a special identity card (commonly called the "Yellow Card") giving
  visa-free entry, residence and work rights, access to public services and the right to own real
  property; people who took Eritrean nationality are excluded [5]. Proclamation number and any
  2024–2026 changes: not found. Ethiopia has **no right-of-return scheme for the wider African
  diaspora** comparable to Ghana or Benin that we could confirm (not found).

## Cities

### Addis-Abeba

Capital, ~6.3 million people (2025 estimate), 2,355 m altitude — Africa's highest capital; seat of
the African Union and the UN Economic Commission for Africa [13].

- **Cost of living**: asking rents read on 9 Nov 2026: furnished 1-bed in **Bole** US$700
  (≈ Br112,600 ≈ €610); furnished 1-bed in an unnamed "highly secured" area US$1,200 (≈ €1,040);
  furnished 2-bed in Bole Br170,000–250,000 (≈ €920–1,350) [1]. These are asking prices, not
  verified rents; the article explicitly could not confirm a citywide average, a meal price or a
  monthly total [1]. An Expatistan result seen in search gave Br78,247 (≈ €420) for an 85 m²
  furnished flat in a "normal" area (unverified: page returned HTTP 403 on 2026-11-14) [16]. Other aggregator
  snippets cite Bole/Kazanchis 1-bed at US$400–1,200 and outer districts (Nifas Silk, Kolfe) at
  US$150–400 — low confidence, methodology unclear [16]. Meal price and monthly budget: **not
  found** from a reliable source.
- **Transport costs**: government tariff of Oct 2024, quoted by The Rio Times on 9 Nov 2026 — minibus Br4.5–65, city bus Br7–35, light rail
  Br2–6 (current fares unconfirmed) [1].
- **Work & money**: biggest employment sectors are trade, manufacturing, civil administration,
  transport and education/health [13]. The AU, UNECA and the NGO/diplomatic world are major
  employers of internationals [13]. Remote-work feasibility and salary levels: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: **iceaddis** (since 2011, Bole Medhanialem) offers incubation,
  acceleration, co-working and early-stage investment [12]. Other hubs, fintech adoption and
  company-registration ease: not found in this round. Mobile money: **telebirr** (Ethio telecom,
  launched 11 May 2021) [7]; Safaricom data prices rose ~44 % in Dec 2025 [1]. The crypto /
  bitcoin-mining angle could not be verified this round (not found).
- **Daily infrastructure**: mobile internet users reached 46.6 million nationally by FY 2024/25
  [14]. Social media was blocked from 9 Feb 2023 per Wikipedia [14] — current status not verified.
  Individuals cannot hold private satellite connections according to the same page [14]. Power-cut
  frequency, water and ride-hailing apps: not found this round.
- **Health & schools**: **Lycée Guebre-Mariam** (MLF school under agreement with AEFE, Churchill Road,
  ages 3–18, French bac, 1,759 pupils in 2017/18 — old figure) [10]. British International School parents say fees rose by up to 36 % for
  2026/27; the dispute is before the Federal First Instance Court and the school says the rise
  covers its costs [1]. Reference hospitals: not found this round.
- **Safety**: FCDO (updated 30 Oct 2026) does **not** advise against travel to Addis-Abeba itself,
  but advises against all-but-essential travel in much of East Shewa and the rest of North Shewa
  (Oromia) and on the A4 road from Addis-Abeba to Cheliya, except the Finfine special-zone section
  [2]. France Diplomatie (30 Sep 2026) focuses on
  Tigray, Afar and the Amhara–Tigray border and does not mention Addis-Abeba [3]. Neighbourhood-level crime data: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**: not researched this round (no sourced nightlife, music or food facts).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found this round (no associations or groups verified).
- **Climate in practice**: mild all year thanks to altitude; short rains Feb–May, long rains
  June to mid-September (coldest, wettest period), dry season mid-Nov to January [13].
- **Watch out**: steep currency depreciation since 2024 and double-digit inflation make local
  salaries and rents volatile [1][11]; rising school and data costs [1]; regular national security
  shocks and past internet shutdowns [14]; capital surrounded by roads the FCDO flags [2].

### Bahir Dar

Capital of the Amhara region, on Lake Tana near the Blue Nile Falls; population figures vary widely
(640,000 for 2024 per one figure) [6]. Bahir Dar University has ~45,000 students [6].

- **Cost of living**: not found.
- **Work & money**: commerce, tourism, health, education, the university and horticultural exports
  by air [6]. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: internet in Amhara was cut in 2023 and restored on 15 July 2024; mobile
  communications were blocked again at times in 2024 [8]. Road transport between Addis-Abeba and
  Bahir Dar was halted in August 2024 because of fighting [8].
- **Health & schools**: not found.
- **Safety — serious**: **FCDO advises against ALL travel to the Amhara region** (page updated
  30 Oct 2026); Bahir Dar is not exempted [2]. The Fano insurgency began 9 April 2023; a state of
  emergency with curfew and warrantless arrests was declared 4 Aug 2023; Fano launched operations
  in Bahir Dar on 29 Feb 2024; the conflict was still listed as ongoing in 2026, with a new Fano
  offensive across central Amhara in Feb 2026 [8]. The UN reported more than 2,000 civilians killed, in a
  statement Wikipedia dates to July 2024 [8]. **Editorial recommendation: flag Bahir Dar as `unsafe` and do not propose it.**
- **Social life & vibe**: palm-lined avenues, lake life, UNESCO biosphere reserve since 2015,
  60,000-seat stadium [6] — but none of this is accessible under the current advice.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: very wet July–August (≈ 400 mm per month), wet June and September;
  dry season is sunny with cool nights [6].
- **Watch out**: active armed conflict, curfews and communication blackouts [2][8].

### Hawassa (suggested addition)

Why: regional capital (Sidama), ~577,000 people (2023 estimate), lakeside in the Rift Valley, a
major university, an airport and the Hawassa Industrial Park (apparel, >35,000 workers at peak,
hit by Ethiopia's AGOA exclusion in 2022) [9]. Highland-moderate climate, wet March–October, dry
Nov–Feb [9]. FCDO (30 Oct 2026) restricts Sidama only within 10 km of the Kenya and South Sudan borders;
Hawassa itself is not named as an advised-against area, and the Hawassa–Mega road is exempted from
the Guji-zone advice [2]. No city-level crime source was found. Costs: not found.

### Dire Dawa (suggested addition, low priority)

Why: one of two chartered cities, ~600,000 people (2025), industrial and rail hub on the
Addis-Abeba–Djibouti railway [15]. Hot (average max ~33 °C, record 42 °C), majority Muslim
(70.9 % in 2007 census) [15] — a very different daily life from Addis. Security and costs: not
found this round.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of sourced facts. No city or question measures a personality trait.
Hofstede, GLOBE or Afrobarometer data for Ethiopia were **not consulted** this round.

**Addis-Abeba**
- Openness: suits people curious about a city that is neither francophone nor anglophone by
  default and that lives on its own calendar and alphabet; the AU/UN presence brings a very
  international bubble [13].
- Conscientiousness: helps — a floated currency, ~15 % inflation and school fees that parents say
  rose by up to 36 % in a year [1] reward people who plan budgets and paperwork carefully.
- Extraversion: not assessed (nightlife not researched this round).
- Agreeableness: not assessed (social codes not researched this round).
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): matters — regional conflicts, flagged roads around the
  capital [2] and past internet shutdowns [14] mean living with uncertainty.

**Bahir Dar**: not assessed — an active conflict zone [2][8] is not a lifestyle choice.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT** (Wikipedia, read 2026-11-14 [4]): same-sex acts are criminal for men and women (Criminal
  Code 2004, art. 629, minimum one year under art. 630; up to 10 years under art. 630(1)(b) when
  "made a profession"; Wikipedia's summary lists up to 15 years); statutory ban on same-sex
  marriage since 2009; on 10 Aug 2023 authorities raided hotels, guest houses and entertainment
  venues in Addis-Abeba over alleged homosexual activity and arrested some managers; mob violence
  reported by ILGA World (2021); 97 % said society should not accept homosexuality (Pew, 2007 —
  old) [4]. FCDO and France Diplomatie pages give no country-specific LGBT detail [2][3].
- **Conflict zones**: FCDO (30 Oct 2026) advises against all travel to Amhara, Tigray and Gambela
  and to large parts of Afar, Oromia, Somali and Benishangul-Gumuz regions and border areas [2].
  France Diplomatie (30 Sep 2026) reports that Tigray Defence Forces took Mekele airport on
  23 Sep 2026 (flights to Tigray suspended), drone strikes in Tigray, and an Alliance For Survival
  offensive in Afar that reached Erebti on 27 Sep 2026 [3]. The Amhara war has been ongoing since April 2023 [8].
- **Racism/colourism, treatment of diaspora/foreigners**: not found this round.
- **Women's daily experience**: not found this round (FCDO page only links generic advice [2]).
