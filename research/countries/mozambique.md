## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day:** Portuguese is the official and working language; Makhuwa, Tsonga (Changana in Maputo), Sena are widely spoken; English is used in business with South Africa and in tourism [7]. French is **not** a working language: a francophone arriving without Portuguese will struggle in administration and daily life (editorial inference from [7], no French-specific source found).
- **Currency:** Mozambican metical (MZN / MT) [4]. Rate used in this dossier: France Diplomatie prices 3,050 MZN at "about 40 €" (Nov 2026), i.e. roughly **76 MZN = 1 €** [3]. Treat EUR figures as approximations.
- **(a) French / EU passport holders:** no general visa exemption for ordinary French passports. Short stays (tourism/business, max 30 days) go through the online authorisation at evisa.gov.mz, started 5–7 days before departure; cost about 40 € (650 MZN visa fee + 2,400 MZN service fee) per France Diplomatie [3] (updated 9 Nov 2026). FCDO describes the same mechanism as an "Electronic Travel Authorisation", 30 days, about USD 47 for British nationals [2b] (page updated 20 Aug 2026). Visiting family or friends needs a visa obtained **before** departure; long stays (work, residence) require a visa from the Mozambican embassy in Paris [3]. Passport valid 6 months beyond the stay, 2 blank pages, strictly enforced [3]. A host receiving family or friends must declare them to SENAMI (migration service) within 5 days, fine 1,000 MZN per day late; overstaying costs USD 50 per day [3] (page updated 9 Nov 2026). FCDO: ETA fee is USD 37 + 650 MZN processing, apply at least 5 working days ahead [2b]. Residence/work permit procedures: not found in this run.
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:** dual nationality is allowed in most cases since the 2004 Constitution (in force January 2005) [9]. A person born abroad is Mozambican at birth if at least one parent was born in Mozambique, but must be registered and must declare within one year of reaching majority that they wish to keep the nationality [9]. Grandchildren are not covered separately (only via a Mozambique-born parent) [9]. Naturalisation: 5 years of residence [9]. No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found [9].
- **Diaspora relevance:** Mozambique's diaspora is mostly in South Africa and Portugal, not France (editorial; no source found in this run). Expat.com estimates 15,000–20,000 expatriates, mostly Portuguese, South African, Chinese, Brazilian and Indian (undated page) [7].

## Cities

### Maputo

Capital, about 1.09 million residents (2017 census), 2.7 million in the metro area with Matola [10].

- **Cost of living:** 1-bed in the city centre 34,750 MZN/month (range 20,000–51,120), ≈ 457 € (range ≈ 260–670 €); outside the centre 24,157 MZN (≈ 318 €) — Numbeo, updated 6 Nov 2026, only 17 contributors, so low confidence [4]. Inexpensive meal 650 MZN (≈ 8.5 €); mid-range dinner for two 4,000 MZN (≈ 53 €); home broadband 60 Mbps+ 5,600 MZN (≈ 74 €) [4]. Expat.com estimates about USD 1,200/month for one person and USD 3,000 for a family of four (undated) [7]. Expat/middle-class neighbourhoods named in sources: Polana Cimento, Sommerschield, Costa do Sol; Polana Caniço is a more popular (informal) bairro; Baixa is the historic downtown [10]. Rent levels per neighbourhood: not found.
- **Work & money:** port, government, NGOs/international agencies and the gas economy dominate (editorial). TotalEnergies' delayed LNG project is often described as a drag on the economy (unverified: not stated in [11], and no source re-checked in this run). Salary data: not found. Remote work is feasible only with a backup connection (see infrastructure).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** not verified in this run. Known names to check with search: Ideialab (Maputo innovation hub), Standard Bank incubator, Bank of Mozambique fintech sandbox — **unverified, do not publish without a source**. Crypto adoption: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:** 19.8% internet penetration nationally (Jan 2025); median mobile download 12.6 Mbps (down 36% in a year), fixed 16 Mbps (up 22.5%) [12]. Mobile operators: Vodacom, Movitel, mCel/Tmcel [13] (outdated page, 2012–2014 data). Mobile money (M-Pesa, e-Mola, mKesh): not verified in this run. During the post-election protests, authorities cut mobile internet countrywide on 25 October 2024 (Amnesty, 31 Oct 2024) [19], and NetBlocks confirmed Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp restrictions on 31 October 2024 [20]; Civicus reported curfew-style mobile shutdowns until 10 Nov 2024 (cited in search, not opened). Flooding in the rainy season (Nov–Apr) can make roads impassable, including in Maputo; fuel supply can be unpredictable [2]. FCDO: "Police officers frequently stop drivers to check paperwork and sometimes try to extract bribes" [2]; the advice to ask for a written fine was not found on the page re-read in this run (unverified). Ride-hailing apps: not found. Power cuts / water: not found.
- **Health & schools:** Eduardo Mondlane University, Pedagogical University, São Tomás and Catholic University are in or around Maputo [10]. French school: the Lycée Gustave Eiffel in Maputo has 425 pupils, 67 of them French (France Diplomatie, page updated 13 Nov 2026) [21]; the same page names the France-Mozambique Cultural Centre (CCFM) in Maputo and counts 570 registered French residents (figure dated 4 Feb 2020) [21]. Reference private hospitals: not verified in this run. France Diplomatie flagged a measles increase (alert 13 Mar 2026) [1].
- **Safety:** France Diplomatie (9 Nov 2026): armed robberies, assaults and burglaries have risen since 2025, especially in the centre and areas frequented by the international community; pedestrians should be particularly careful on Avenida Marginal, Avenida Friedrich Engels and around the Baixa port area; kidnapping for ransom happens regularly in Maputo and Beira, mostly targeting local business owners; foreign residents and tourists "ne sont pas visés" for now but the risk is not excluded [5]. FCDO (advice updated 20 Aug 2026): street crime common in Maputo "and is increasing in other cities and tourist destinations", knives and firearms used, carjacking risk ("avoid driving alone at night"); "do not travel after dark" is given as advice during political unrest [2]. Post-election unrest Oct 2024–Mar 2025: barricades in Maputo, looting, a reported mass prison escape, at least 277 deaths nationally by end of Jan 2025 (Amnesty) [11][14]; ended with a Chapo–Mondlane agreement on 23–24 Mar 2025 [14]. Protests can still erupt at short notice, especially at toll gates (FCDO) [2].
- **Social life & vibe:** cultural melting pot of Bantu and Portuguese influences with Arab, Indian and Chinese layers; music venues, a local film industry, the Núcleo de Arte artists' collective and the Dockanema documentary festival [10]. Nightlife names, marrabenta scene, sport: not verified in this run.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** Expat.com Mozambique forum [7]. Facebook/WhatsApp groups and returnee networks: not found in this run.
- **Climate in practice:** tropical savanna, mean 22.8 °C; January hottest (26.8 °C) and wettest, July coolest (18.8 °C) [10]. Rainy/cyclone season roughly Dec–Mar (France Diplomatie) or Nov–Apr (FCDO) [5][2]. France Diplomatie posted an El Niño alert on 2 Nov 2026 [1].
- **Watch out:** Portuguese is non-negotiable; rising violent street crime since 2025; kidnapping economy aimed at business owners; political tension after the 2024 election (a figure of 2,790+ people still detained a year later, attributed to Plataforma Decide, Oct 2025, via [14] — unverified, primary source not opened); flooding.

### Vilankulo

Coastal town in Inhambane province, ~24,000 residents (2010 estimate, old) [15].

- **Cost of living:** not found (no Numbeo page reached). Tourism pricing likely pushes rents up near the beach (editorial, unverified).
- **Work & money:** economy driven by tourism investment; gateway to the Bazaruto Archipelago (diving, snorkelling, dhow and boat trips) [15]. Realistic paths are hospitality, diving, conservation or remote work (editorial). Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** not found; small tourism businesses dominate (editorial).
- **Daily infrastructure:** airport with flights to Johannesburg and Maputo [15]. Internet quality, power, water: not found. National mobile median 12.6 Mbps applies at best [12].
- **Health & schools:** not found. Assume serious cases go to Maputo or South Africa (editorial, unverified).
- **Safety:** not named in France Diplomatie's security page; Inhambane province falls under "heightened vigilance" nationwide, far from the advised-against northern provinces [5]. Beaches are generally unsupervised, with strong currents (France Diplomatie, 9 Nov 2026) [5]. FCDO (20 Aug 2026) says street crime "is increasing in other cities and tourist destinations" beyond Maputo, which applies to tourist towns like this one without naming it [2].
- **Social life & vibe:** small, beach- and ocean-centred, tourism rhythm (editorial from [15]). Local football club Vilankulo F.C. [15].
- **Diaspora & expat community:** not found.
- **Climate in practice:** 14–31 °C; ~780 mm rain a year, mostly Nov–Mar [15] (1961–1990 data). Cyclone exposure on this coast: not verified in this run.
- **Watch out:** isolation from serious healthcare and schools; dependence on tourism seasons; very little verified data.

### Inhambane / Tofo (suggested addition)

Why: the province's capital (~80,000 residents, 2017) with the beach village of Tofo 22 km away, known for whale-shark and manta diving [16]; it is the other realistic "small coastal life" option in the south besides Vilankulo, with daily flights from Maputo [16]. Cost, community, health: not found in this run. Climate: highs 25.6–31.4 °C, ~950 mm rain mostly Dec–Feb [16].

### Beira (suggested addition)

Why: fourth-largest city (~590,000 in 2017) and the country's second port, outlet for Zimbabwe, Zambia and Malawi — the main non-capital career option [17]. Watch out: Cyclone Idai (March 2019) reportedly destroyed up to 90% of the city [17]; 1,594 mm rain a year, Nov–Apr [17]; France Diplomatie names Beira among cities with regular kidnappings for ransom (Nov 2026) [5].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This is an editorial reading of cited facts; it does not mean any city or quiz answer measures a personality trait. No Hofstede or GLOBE scores for Mozambique could be retrieved in this run.

**Maputo**
- Openness: suits people curious about a lusophone, mixed Bantu-Portuguese-Indian city with an art collective and a documentary festival [10] — and willing to learn Portuguese [7].
- Conscientiousness: rewards people who plan around paperwork (pre-departure visas, strict passport rules [3]) and around security routines (varying routes, not walking at night [5]).
- Extraversion: a capital-sized cultural scene [10]; but evening outings are constrained by security advice (avoid driving alone at night; no travel after dark during unrest) [2].
- Agreeableness: social codes not documented in this run; nothing to cite.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): helpful given rising crime since 2025 [5], kidnapping risk for business owners [5] and the 2024–25 protest wave [14].

**Vilankulo**
- Openness: fits people drawn to ocean life (diving, dhows, Bazaruto) over urban culture [15].
- Conscientiousness: self-reliance matters where services are thin (editorial; services data not found).
- Extraversion: small-town tourism social life; more suited to people happy with a small circle (editorial).
- Agreeableness: not documented.
- Emotional stability: lower urban crime pressure than Maputo is plausible but **not sourced**; isolation and cyclone season are the main stressors (editorial).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:** same-sex activity legal since the Penal Code took effect on 30 June 2015; no same-sex marriage or recognition; the 2007 labour-law protection against sexual-orientation discrimination lapsed when Labour Law 13/2023 took effect in Feb 2024; the 2020 Penal Code (Law 24/2019) treats sexual orientation and gender identity as aggravating grounds for homicide (Art. 160) and bans incitement based on gender identity (Art. 191); in 2017 the Constitutional Council struck down the "moral order" ground used to block the LAMBDA association [8] (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14). FCDO (advice updated 20 Aug 2026): "There are no laws against same-sex sexual activity"; attitudes in Maputo "tend to be more liberal than in more remote rural areas" [2]. Polls cited in [8] show support for same-sex marriage in Maputo rising from 37% (2013) to 47% (2017) — old data, Maputo only.
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners:** no reliable source found in this run.
- **Conflict zones:** insurgency by IS-Mozambique / "Al-Shabaab" in Cabo Delgado, spilling into Niassa and Nampula [2][11]. France Diplomatie (page updated 9 Nov 2026; risk map last changed 11 Mar 2026) formally advises against all of Cabo Delgado including Pemba, the eastern third of Niassa, the Tanzania border strip, the Ibo and Quirimbas islands and several Nampula districts, and advises against non-essential travel to western Niassa and parts of Nampula province including the cities of Nampula and Nacala [5]; FCDO (20 Aug 2026) advises against all but essential travel in Cabo Delgado except Palma, Pemba and Afungi, and against all travel in Memba and Eráti (Nampula) and Mecula and Marrupa (Niassa) [2a]. Amnesty (2025): more than 330,000 displaced in Cabo Delgado [11]. Neither Maputo nor Vilankulo is in these zones.
- **Political repression:** Amnesty 2025/26 reports protest killings (at least 277 by end Jan 2025), radio suspensions, detention of ~16 journalists in Muidumbe district (Cabo Delgado) in June 2025 and the enforced disappearance of journalist Arlindo Chissale (7 Jan 2025) [11].
- **Women's daily experience:** Afrobarometer Round 9: 68% say domestic violence should be treated as a criminal matter [18]; Afrobarometer dispatch AD1208 (July 2026) reports obstacles for women in employment, education and public leadership (findings not read) [18]. FCDO has no women-specific guidance [2]. Street harassment data: not found.
