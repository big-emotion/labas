## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Landlocked enclave** entirely surrounded by South Africa; every arrival goes through South
  Africa, so you also need to meet South Africa's entry rules [5]. Bloemfontein (SA) is the de
  facto service city for private healthcare (90 min / ~140 km from Maseru) [6][7].
- **Languages used day to day:** Sesotho everywhere; English is the co-official language of
  administration, business and education [12]. **French is essentially absent** — no francophone
  institution found in the sources consulted. Francophone-friendliness is low.
- **Currency:** loti (LSL, plural maloti), pegged 1:1 to the South African rand, and the rand
  circulates freely (Common Monetary Area) [13]. EUR conversions below use ~20 LSL = 1 EUR and
  USD→EUR ~0.9 — an approximate working rate, **not sourced, verify on the day**.
- **Entry / residence, (a) French/EU passport holders:** the France Diplomatie page fetched on
  2026-11-14 only showed alerts (El Niño vigilance, 4 Nov 2026), not the entry rules [4]; visa
  status for French nationals is **not confirmed by an official source**. Secondary sources
  disagree: World Travel Guide lists France among nationalities allowed 14 days visa-free, VisaGo
  (Mar 2026) says 90 days (both unverified against Lesotho immigration) [23]. Lesotho's Cabinet
  approved visa-free entry for all EU citizens, announced by PM Matekane on 14 May 2025, with no
  entry-into-force date given (VisaNews, 22 May 2025) [24]; whether it is now in force was not
  confirmed. For comparison, UK nationals may stay
  90 days visa-free, extendable by 90 days at the Visa Application Centre, Manthabiseng Convention
  Centre, Maseru (FCDO, undated page, read 2026-11-14) [5]. Work/residence permit procedures: **not
  found**. The `evisalesotho.gov.ls` domain did not resolve.
- **Entry / residence, (b) diaspora with Basotho roots:** the **Eighth Amendment to the
  Constitution (2018)** allows dual nationality and lets women pass nationality to a spouse;
  Basotho who previously lost citizenship by taking another one must apply for reinstatement [11].
  Children born abroad acquire nationality if a parent is a citizen other than by descent [11]. No
  diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme for the wider Afro-descendant diaspora was found.
- **Economic context (matters for any job plan):** the US "Liberation Day" tariff list of 2 April
  2025 gave Lesotho the highest rate, 50%; it was cut to 15% as of 1 August 2025. Factories closed
  and the government declared a state of economic emergency; women, the majority of garment
  workers, were hit hardest [14][12]. Unemployment has hovered around 23–28% (2008–2017 data) [13].
  The economy leans on remittances, textiles, diamonds (Letšeng is currently the only operating
  mine, Lesotho Times, 12 Nov 2026) and water/power sales to South Africa via the Lesotho Highlands
  Water Project [13][15].
- **Politics:** Sam Matekane's Revolution for Prosperity has governed since October 2022 [12]. A
  constitutional reform (Tenth Amendment) is being challenged in court (Lesotho Times, 12 Nov
  2026) [15].

## Cities

### Maseru (capital, ~331,000 in 2016)

- **Cost of living:** aggregators disagree sharply, so treat as ranges.
  - 1-bed rent: USD 240 centre / 190 outside (globalcostdata, 2026; seen only in search results,
    unverified) [1]; average rent for a single person USD 350, range 150–650, bedroom count not
    stated (livingcostindex, 2026) [2]; USD 658 for a central studio (nomads.com, updated 2026-11-14) [3].
    Working range: **~4,500–12,000 LSL (~230–600 EUR)**; the top end is a furnished expat flat.
    Neighbourhoods: the sources consulted only name the Kingsway CBD (west: offices and banks; east:
    markets) and Thetsane (southern industrial district) [8]. Residential expat neighbourhoods
    (often cited informally as Maseru West, Hillsview) were **not confirmed** by a source in this
    pass.
  - Meal: dinner ~USD 9 (~160–180 LSL / ~8 EUR); coffee ~USD 2 [3].
  - Monthly budget: single person USD 1,079 incl. rent (budget USD 474, comfortable USD 2,160) [2];
    expat USD 1,137, local USD 345 [3]; expat.com: ~USD 800 single, ~USD 2,200 family of four [16].
    Average monthly salary ~USD 400 [2].
- **Work & money:** government, banks and offices along Kingsway; flour mills on Moshoeshoe Road;
  textile and footwear factories in Thetsane, shrinking since the Multi Fibre Arrangement ended and
  hit again by the 2025 US tariffs [8][14]. NGOs and development agencies are a classic expat
  employer (not quantified). Remote work: possible but limited by slow internet (5 Mbps average per
  nomads.com) [3]; coworking hot desk ~USD 130/month [3].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** small and young, but not empty. Vodacom Lesotho runs a six-month
  "Software Factory Hub" acceleration programme for local tech start-ups; its first cohort
  graduated in Maseru in late October 2025 (The Reporter, 3 Nov 2025) [25]. The government's
  Lesotho Entrepreneurship Hub and Seed Financing Facility, aimed at youth- and women-owned
  businesses, was launched by the Prime Minister in 2023 (African Business / APO, June 2023) [26].
  No investor, crypto or TechCabal/Disrupt Africa coverage was reached. Still a thin ecosystem.
- **Daily infrastructure:** two mobile operators, Vodacom Lesotho and Econet Telecom Lesotho [17].
  Mobile money (M-Pesa / EcoCash) is widely reported but **not confirmed** in this pass (Vodacom's
  site did not render). Internet: rated "slow", ~5 Mbps [3]. Power cuts and water supply: **not
  found** in the sources read. Transport: minibus taxis and "4+1" shared taxis, often poorly
  maintained and uninsured (FCDO) [5]; roads in Maseru and to the airport/border are good, but
  driving is aggressive (Canada, 15 Oct 2026) [7]. Ride-hailing apps: **not found**. Moshoeshoe I
  International Airport is at Mazenod [8].
- **Health & schools:** Queen Mamohato Memorial Hospital (public) and Maseru Private Hospital;
  serious cases go to Mediclinic Bloemfontein (SA) — insurance with evacuation is strongly advised
  [6][7]. Emergency number 112 [6]. HIV prevalence 22.8% (ages 15–49, 2021) and the world's highest
  TB incidence [12]. Schools: Machabeng College (international school, widely known) could not be
  verified (page 404); **no French school found**.
- **Safety:** US State Dept Level 2 "exercise increased caution" (crime, health), dated 17 June
  2026 (seen in search results; page returned 403) [18]. Canada: "high degree of caution"; crime
  most prevalent in Maseru, Leribe and Maputsoe; violent crime "often targets foreigners" near
  restaurants, hotels, the business district and poorly lit roads; no visible police at night
  (15 Oct 2026) [7]. FCDO: armed carjacking risk in urban areas; burglaries peak before Christmas
  and Easter; occasional political demonstrations in Maseru [5]. Famo-music gang feuds turned
  political; some famo groups were banned as "terrorists" in 2024 [19]. nomads.com rates lack of
  crime "very bad" (crowd-sourced, low reliability) [3].
- **Social life & vibe:** small capital; Pioneer Mall (2009) is the main mall/restaurant cluster
  [8]. Music: famo is the dominant local genre; radio plays Afropop, Sotho hip-hop, deep and soulful
  house, kwaito, jazz [19]. Food: pap, motoho (fermented sorghum porridge), chesanyama (grilled
  meat), ginger beer [12]. Football is the main sport [12]. 92% Christian (2020 est.) — church
  weighs in daily life [12]. Nightlife details: **not found**.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** expat.com Lesotho forum: 591 members, 16 topics — very small
  [16]. Named returnee associations, Facebook/WhatsApp groups: **not found** in this pass.
- **Climate in practice:** highland subtropical; rains October–April as summer thunderstorms, peak
  in January; up to 30 °C in summer; July lows around −3 °C and lowland frosts to −7 °C [8][12].
  Houses are rarely built for cold (unverified) — plan heating. France Diplomatie flags El Niño
  vigilance (Nov 2026) [4].
- **Watch out:** very small job market outside government/NGOs; textile crisis since 2025; slow
  internet; private healthcare effectively means Bloemfontein; carjacking and night-time crime;
  English + Sesotho only.

### Teyateyaneng ("TY", Berea district capital)

- About 40 km north-east of Maseru on the Main North 1 road; ~75,000 (2005 figure, old) [9].
- **Cost of living:** **not found** (no aggregator covers it); expect lower than Maseru.
- **Work & money:** retail, carpet and tapestry factories; little industry because Maseru is so
  close [9]. Realistically a commuter or crafts town.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** **not found.**
- **Daily infrastructure:** **not found** beyond the Main North 1 highway link [9].
- **Health & schools:** district referral hospital and several schools [9]; nothing international.
- **Safety:** no town-level advisory; Canada names Maseru, Leribe and Maputsoe as hotspots, not TY
  [7]. Low confidence.
- **Social life & vibe:** described as the country's "athletic, art and entertainment capital";
  home of Lioli FC (founded 1934); Khoisan rock art and Kome cave dwellings nearby [9]. Altitude
  1,693 m.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** **not found.**
- **Climate in practice:** same highland pattern as Maseru [12].
- **Watch out:** very thin services and data; most jobs and paperwork are in Maseru.

### Roma (extra: university town)

- Why it matters: home of the National University of Lesotho and the NUL International School,
  34 km south-east of Maseru, in a cliff-ringed valley; a sizeable residential area for academics
  and students [10]. The calmest option found for someone tied to a university job or studies.
- Cost, infrastructure, safety: **not found** in this pass.

*(Maputsoe/Leribe was considered: the northern textile and border town facing Ficksburg, ~55,500
people [20], but Canada lists it among the highest-crime areas [7] and it offers little for a
newcomer, so it is not proposed.)*

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No academic regional-personality data exists for Lesotho in the sources consulted (no Hofstede or
GLOBE country score found). Afrobarometer surveys Lesotho regularly (rounds through 2024; e.g. 71%
support free movement in Southern Africa) [21]. The notes below describe who tends to be
comfortable, not what anyone "is".

**Maseru**
- Openness: suits people curious about Sesotho culture and mountain life rather than seekers of a
  cosmopolitan scene; the expat forum is tiny [16] and French is absent.
- Conscientiousness: rewards planners — health evacuation insurance, night-time precautions and
  cross-border logistics via South Africa are routine needs [5][7].
- Extraversion: limited nightlife data; social life seems to run through church, football and
  private circles [12] — comfortable for moderate rather than high-stimulus seekers.
- Agreeableness: a small, church-centred society (92% Christian) [12] where community ties matter.
- Emotional stability: useful given crime warnings, an economic shock since 2025 and periodic
  political tension [5][7][14].

**Teyateyaneng / Roma**
- Suit people who want calm, crafts or academia and can tolerate isolation and thin services
  [9][10]. Low confidence.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:** sources conflict. Wikipedia says male same-sex activity was decriminalised in 2012
  (Penal Code) and that the Labour Act 2024 bans employment discrimination on sexual orientation and
  gender identity; no same-sex marriage or partnership recognition [22]. Canada (15 Oct 2026):
  same-sex acts "not criminalized" [7]. The FCDO page (undated, read 2026-11-14) still says sex
  between men is illegal but not enforced [5]. Pride marches have been held in Maseru since 18 May
  2013 [22]. Social taboo remains.
- **Racism / treatment of foreigners or diaspora:** no reliable source found. Canada notes violent
  crime "often targets foreigners" [7] — this is about perceived wealth, not documented racism.
- **Conflict:** no armed conflict. Famo-linked gang violence led to bans on some groups in 2024
  [19]; occasional demonstrations in Maseru [5]; during a protest by some 40,000 garment workers,
  security forces killed two protesters under the Matekane government (in office since Oct 2022;
  exact date not given by the source) [12].
- **Women's daily experience:** gender-based violence is "very high" (FCDO) [5]; the UN recorded
  91.6 reported rapes per 100,000 in 2008, described as the world's highest [12]; women travelling
  alone may face harassment (Canada, 2026) [7]; nomads.com rates "safe for women" as bad
  (crowd-sourced) [3]. Women make up most of the garment workforce hit by the 2025 tariffs [12].
