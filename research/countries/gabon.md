## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: French is the official language and the everyday language of Libreville. Regional languages include Fang, Mbete, Myene, Nzebi, Punu, Teke and Vili [6]. Fang dominates in the north, for example in Oyem [13].
- **Currency**: the CFA franc (XAF), used across CEMAC [10]. Cards work only in some hotels and large shops in the capital. Cash withdrawals are mainly at big hotels and bank head offices [9].
- **Political context**: the military took power on 30 August 2023. A new constitution was approved by referendum in November 2024. Brice Oligui Nguema won the presidential election of 12 April 2025 with more than 90 % of the vote [6]. France Diplomatie describes the transition as ending (2026) [8].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**: a visa is required and is requested online through the Direction générale de la documentation et de l'immigration (DGDI). The passport must be valid for more than 6 months, and the yellow-fever certificate is checked systematically on arrival. A stay of more than 3 months needs an *autorisation d'entrée de durée indéterminée* (France Diplomatie, updated 15 Sept 2026) [2]. A French driving licence must be registered within 3 months [9]. Work permits take about 6 to 8 weeks and need an employer sponsor (Expat.com, Dec 2025) [12].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**: dual nationality has been allowed since 1962. A child with at least one parent who is Gabonese by origin is Gabonese at birth, wherever they are born. Under the 1998 code, a child born abroad out of wedlock to a Gabonese mother must complete a registration step [7]. Naturalisation normally needs 5 years of residence, or 3 years of marriage to a Gabonese citizen [7]. **No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found**, unlike the schemes in Ghana or Benin.

## Cities

### Libreville

About 899,000 inhabitants (2025 estimate), roughly half the population [14]. It is the capital, the main port (Owendo) and home to the largest market, Mont-Bouët [14]. Akanda, to the north, has been the capital of Estuaire province since 2022 [14].

- **Cost of living**:
  - Gabon sits at the high end for French-speaking Africa [11].
  - Rent: about 543,000 XAF (~€828) a month for a one-bedroom in the centre, and 1,250,000 XAF (~€1,906) for three bedrooms. Expat.com uses Numbeo data from August 2025 [11].
  - Monthly budget: about 1,053,000 XAF (~€1,605) for a single person, and about 3,940,000 XAF (~€6,000) for a family of four in a villa with school fees [11].
  - Landlords ask for a deposit of 2 to 3 months, often plus 3 months in advance, and want guarantees such as an employment contract [11].
  - Neighbourhoods: Batterie IV (villas, security), La Sablière (secure compounds), Bord de Mer (sea view, nightlife), Nzeng-Ayong (newer builds), Nomba and Akébé (good value) [11]. Nzeng-Ayong appears in both lists: the part "behind the prison" is on Belgium's avoid list [3].
  - Meal prices: not found (Numbeo returned HTTP 429).
- **Work & money**:
  - Oil, mining and services hire skilled people [11].
  - Average net salary is about 250,000 XAF (~€381) a month (Expat.com, 2025). National unemployment was 20.4 % in 2023 [11].
  - About 85 French companies employ around 12,500 people (France Diplomatie, 2025) [8]. Oil makes up about 43 % of GDP and 81 % of exports [6].
  - A business visa needs an invitation letter and an employer letter [11].
  - Remote work: fibre in Libreville gives speeds comparable to France, according to France Diplomatie (Sept 2026) [10].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech** (added in the fact-check pass; thin, press-only):
  - The HCO incubator for management trades sits at the INSG (Institut National des Sciences de Gestion) in Libreville. Moov Africa-Gabon Telecom signed a funding agreement for it on 29 April 2025, aimed at fintech, e-health and digital education [26].
  - Les Afriques (Oct 2025) names the Centre d'Innovation Numérique de Libreville and Ogooué Labs as incubators, the government's "Gabon Numérique" programme, and the networks "Les Femmes Numériques du Gabon" and She Leads Africa [25]. The article does not say where Ogooué Labs is based.
  - An incubator in Libreville under the World Bank-funded e-Gabon plan was announced as early as January 2017; whether it opened was not confirmed [27].
  - Gabon is in CEMAC, so the regional banking rules apply [10]. Investment agency, company-registration steps and crypto status: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Internet: fibre in Libreville is comparable to France. 3G/4G works with an official local SIM; buy SIMs only in operator shops [10].
  - Operators: Gabon Telecom is 51 % owned by Maroc Telecom [15]. No sourced page on Airtel or Moov was found.
  - Power cuts and water shortages: **not found** in a source fetched this session (SEEG pages unavailable). This is a well-known complaint, but it is not sourced here, so verify it in a second pass.
  - Transport: taxis must be negotiated before boarding, and most double their fare after 21:00 [4]. Violent robberies of taxi passengers have been reported, so ask for an exclusive (*course*) booking [5]. Ride-hailing apps: not found.
  - Roads in central Libreville are generally good [5].
- **Health & schools**:
  - Health insurance: the CNAMGS gives basic cover. An international policy with medical evacuation is strongly advised [11][16].
  - Clinics named by Expat.com: Polyclinique El-Rapha and Clinique Mandji [11]. Public reference hospitals (CHU): not verified.
  - Malaria is the leading risk, and French nationals die of it in Gabon regularly. The highest-risk months are September to December and February to June [16].
  - Schools: the French-approved (*homologué*) secondary school in Libreville is the **Lycée Blaise Pascal**, one of only 11 French-approved schools in Gabon for 2025-2026 (Gabonreview, 29 July 2025) [28]. Expat.com's main guide wrongly places a "Lycée Français Victor Hugo" in Libreville (€4,000 to €8,000 a year) [11]. Victor Hugo is in Port-Gentil [12][28]. So treat that fee range as unverified for Blaise Pascal. Expat.com also lists the International School of Gabon (IB, €7,000 to €12,000) [11] and the American International School of Libreville ($7,500 to $16,000) [12].
  - Beware of private schools that call themselves "French" without approval; the Gabonese ministry has warned about them [28].
- **Safety**:
  - France Diplomatie (zones updated 5 Mar 2026, page updated 15 Sept 2026) rates crime as "modéré jusqu'à présent" (moderate so far) [1].
  - Armed gangs carry out burglaries at night; do not resist [1].
  - France and Belgium (25 June 2026) flag the same Libreville districts: Petit Louis, Mont-Bouët, the bus station (Gare Routière), Plaine de Rio, London, Lalala, Oloumi, Nkembo, Awendje, Nzeng-Ayong behind the prison, PK5, PK9, PK12 and Bas de Gué-Gué. Both also flag beaches away from the busiest spots, Cap Estérias and Pointe Denis included, especially at night [1][3].
  - Belgium says urban crime has been rising in recent years, especially in Libreville and Port-Gentil [3].
  - Canada (15 Oct 2026): "calme, mais tendue" (calm but tense) [4]. FCDO: carjackings in Libreville [5].
  - Since the 2023 coup, some tourists have been detained or questioned [5]. Bank fraud using fake transfer orders is reported [1][3].
  - Older press confirms the same districts have been flagged since at least 2017 [18][19].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - The Bord de Mer seafront has nightlife [11].
  - About 80 % of people are Christian and 11 % Muslim (2021) [6], so church life is visible.
  - Music, food and sport scenes: not sourced in this session.
- **Diaspora & expat community**:
  - Roughly 30,000 expatriates live in Gabon [11]. Locals are described as welcoming, especially to French speakers [11].
  - The Expat.com Gabon forum has about 13,100 members [11]. Its threads are active in 2025 and 2026 on housing scams, salaries and jobs in "LBV" (Libreville) [20]. Treat them as a sign of community, not as facts.
  - Returnee associations and Facebook/WhatsApp groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice**:
  - The climate is a tropical monsoon type, with a dry season from June to August [14].
  - Rains run from October to mid-December and from mid-February to May. Roads outside towns can become impassable [4].
  - El Niño is expected to strengthen in late 2026 and 2027, which could bring floods and storms [4].
- **Watch out**:
  - The cost of living is high for local salaries [11].
  - Landlords demand 2 to 3 months' deposit plus advance rent [11].
  - Cars are broken into and phones snatched around markets [4].
  - Health care is limited and insurance is essential [16].
  - Malaria is a serious risk [16].
  - Police are widely seen as corrupt and untrustworthy (Afrobarometer, Aug 2025) [17].
  - Checkpoints are frequent outside the city [4][5].

### Port-Gentil

Population: 136,462 (2013 census) [21]. It sits on Mandji Island, about 144 km from Libreville, and **there is no road link to the capital**: you travel by air or sea [22]. Called the "economic capital", it generates about three-quarters of the country's wealth and has the only refinery (SOGARA) [22].

- **Cost of living**:
  - Rent: about 600,000 XAF (~€915) for a one-bedroom in the centre, and about 250,000 XAF (~€381) outside the centre [12].
  - Monthly budget, rent included: about 975,000 XAF (~€1,486) for a single person with a central one-bedroom, and about 2,538,000 XAF (~€3,870) for a family of four with a central three-bedroom (Expat.com, updated 4 Dec 2025) [12]. The same page's summary box says €1,400 and €3,500 without saying whether rent is included [12].
  - Neighbourhoods: Sogara is the preferred expat area, with secure residences. The centre has the shops. N'Tchengue is newer but partly unpaved [12].
  - Many expats get housing through their employer [12].
  - Before signing, check the property for water leaks and drainage in the rainy season [12].
- **Work & money**:
  - Oil and gas employ about 7,000 people directly. Employers include Total, Perenco, BW Energy, Maurel & Prom and Petronas. Construction was reported up 48 % in 2024 [12].
  - Jobs depend heavily on oil-price cycles. Timber is the second industry [21].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found. There is an IUT locally; universities are in Libreville [12].
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - City roads are poorly maintained and taxis are common [22].
  - Fixed internet: there are WiMax providers (Solsi, TLDC) [15]. Fibre quality: not found.
  - Power and water: not found.
- **Health & schools**:
  - School: Collège/Lycée Victor Hugo, French-approved, French curriculum to the baccalauréat [12][28]. Expat.com's Port-Gentil page gives €3,420 to €4,395 a year [12], but its main Gabon guide gives that same range for Collège Henri-Sylvoz in Moanda [11]. Ask the school directly for current fees.
  - Hospitals: not found.
  - Medical evacuation insurance is strongly advised [16].
- **Safety**:
  - Belgium (June 2026) and Canada (Oct 2026) say violent crime is more common in Libreville and Port-Gentil than in rural areas [3][4].
  - France flags the beaches between Pointe Chapuy and Cap Lopez and the banks of the three rivers [1].
  - Piracy risk is high in the Gulf of Guinea, and pleasure boating is discouraged [1].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - A smaller, tight-knit expat circle of about 5,000 to 7,000 people: French, Lebanese, Chinese, Senegalese and Beninese [12].
  - Port-Gentil is a historical opposition stronghold. It saw unrest in 1990 and after the 2009 election (3 dead officially) [22].
  - It has beaches, a golf course and a casino [21].
- **Diaspora & expat community**:
  - The Expat.com Port-Gentil forum has about 554 members [12].
  - A November 2026 thread, "Vie Port Gentil au Gabon", had no replies [20]. The community is small.
- **Climate in practice**:
  - The wet season runs from October to May, with about 2,000 mm of rain a year [21].
  - Temperatures average 23.5 °C in July and about 27 °C in February and March [21].
- **Watch out**:
  - The city feels isolated: no road to Libreville [22].
  - The economy is a one-industry town tied to oil [21][12].
  - Rents are as high as in Libreville [12].
  - There are few English or IB schools [12].

### Franceville (additional: third city, inland, mining and research)

Population: 110,568 (2013 census) [23]. It serves the Moanda manganese mines (COMILOG/Eramet) and has the Grand Poubara dam [23]. It is home to the USTM science university and the CIRMF biomedical lab, which has a BSL-4 laboratory [23]. Franceville is the terminus of the Transgabonais railway and is linked to Libreville by an 828 km road and by flights [23].

It is relevant for engineers, researchers and mining profiles. The rest is not found: rents, schools, social life. France flags the Poto-Poto market [1].

### Oyem (additional: northern Fang hub, cross-border trade)

Population: 60,685 (2013 census) [13]. Oyem is a cross-border trading hub near Cameroon and Equatorial Guinea, a centre of Fang culture, and has a Sobraga brewery [13]. It is relevant mainly for people with family roots in Woleu-Ntem. France urges vigilance near the Equatorial Guinea border [1].

There is very little sourced data on daily life.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are interpretive readings built on the facts above. No city or quiz answer measures a trait. No Hofstede or GLOBE scores for Gabon were found. Afrobarometer has surveyed Gabon in 2015, 2017, 2021, 2023 and 2024 [17].

**Libreville**
- **Openness**: suits people who like a mixed capital. It has around 30,000 expatriates and a cosmopolitan seafront [11]. It is socially conservative on LGBT issues [5].
- **Conscientiousness**: helps a lot. Rentals need guarantees and months paid in advance [11], visas and permits go through DGDI [2], and licences must be registered within 3 months [9].
- **Extraversion**: rewards people who build networks. Jobs and housing travel through contacts (Expat.com forum threads [20]), and Bord de Mer has nightlife [11].
- **Agreeableness**: helps with daily friction such as police checkpoints [5], negotiated taxi fares [4] and landlord demands.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: needed. The situation is "calme, mais tendue" (calm but tense) [4], there are night-time burglaries [1], and medical evacuation may be required [16].

**Port-Gentil**
- **Openness**: a smaller, more closed world built around oil [12].
- **Conscientiousness**: suits people with a defined employer-sponsored job [12].
- **Extraversion**: social life runs through the small expat circle [12]. Less nightlife choice (inferred).
- **Agreeableness**: a close-knit community rewards people who fit in [12].
- **Emotional stability**: physical isolation (no road out [22]) and dependence on the oil cycle call for tolerance.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex acts were criminalised on 5 July 2019 (up to 6 months in prison). They were decriminalised in 2020: the National Assembly voted on 23 June, the Senate on 29 June, and the law was signed on 7 July [24]. They are legal today (FCDO 2025 [5]; Canada, Oct 2026 [4]).
  - There is no anti-discrimination law. The constitution adopted by referendum in November 2024 defines marriage as a union of two people of opposite sex. The article cannot be amended, and the ban took effect on 12 April 2025 [24].
  - Canada (2026): same-sex relations are "pas tolérées socialement" (not socially tolerated) [4]. FCDO: "conservative society, discrimination can be a problem" [5].
- **Racism, colourism and treatment of the diaspora or foreigners**:
  - No reliable source found on colourism or how diaspora returnees are treated.
  - Expat.com (Nov 2025) describes locals as welcoming to French speakers [11]. This is a commercial guide, so the claim is weak.
  - Since the 2023 coup, foreign tourists have sometimes faced "heightened scrutiny", detention or confiscated passports (FCDO) [5].
- **Conflict**:
  - No armed conflict.
  - Military takeover on 30 August 2023; election on 12 April 2025 [6].
  - Gulf of Guinea piracy: hostage-taking attacks in Gabonese waters since December 2019 [1].
  - Be vigilant at the northern border with Equatorial Guinea [1].
- **Women's daily experience**:
  - The FCDO notes that rape has occurred, within its general crime warning [5].
  - Afrobarometer (6 Nov 2026): close to half of Gabonese think equal voice in the household weakens men's authority [17].
  - 72 % think a woman's complaint of harassment would be believed in their community [17].
- **Drugs and iboga**:
  - Penalties go up to 10 years in prison [9].
  - Iboga initiation rituals have caused deaths [3][18].
- **Police**: widely perceived as corrupt, untrustworthy and abusive (Afrobarometer, 28 Aug 2025) [17].
