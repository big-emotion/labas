## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: Comorian (shikomori, which varies by island: shingazidja, shindzuani, shimwali) is spoken by about 96.9% of people. French and Arabic are co-official with it. French is the language of administration, school from the upper grades and business, and it is an asset for expat jobs. Sources: [6], [3].
- **Religion**: Sunni Islam has been the state religion since 2009 [6]. It shapes daily life through modest dress, no alcohol in public and the rhythm of Ramadan [5], [1].
- **Currency**: Comorian franc (KMF), pegged to the euro [6]. The fixed rate is about 491.97 KMF = 1 EUR. That figure is a standard reference value, not confirmed on an opened page; this dossier uses it for conversions. Cash is essential because banks and ATMs are scarce [5].
- **Entry for French/EU passport holders**: everyone needs a visa. A visa on arrival costs EUR 30 at Hahaya airport or other entry points [10] and allows a single-entry stay of up to 45 days [3]. Passports need 6 months of validity after arrival [10]. Paying in cash is the usual advice but was not confirmed on an opened official page.
  - To stay longer, you can switch to a one-year visa for about EUR 240. This needs grounds such as a work contract or an investment project [3].
  - A work permit requires sponsorship by a local employer and is a temporary, renewable one-year permit [1].
  - Overstaying can bring an entry ban: 2 years for 1–30 days over, 3 years for 30–90 days over, plus fines [1]. This comes from a single blog and is unconfirmed officially.
- **Diaspora with family roots**:
  - Comorian nationality passes by descent to a child born abroad with at least one native-born Comorian parent [7].
  - Dual nationality has been tolerated since the 2001 constitution. The 1979 Nationality Code still lists it as grounds for losing nationality, so there is a legal conflict [7].
  - No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found.
  - The 2008 "citoyenneté économique" programme sold passports to foreigners. It was suspended in January 2018 after a scandal, and former president Sambi received a life sentence in November 2022 [7]. It is irrelevant to the diaspora and should not be presented as a route.
- **Diaspora weight**: remittances are about 25% of GDP. There are more Comorians in France (Marseille, Mayotte, Réunion) than in the islands, according to Wikipedia [6]. The older French Foreign Ministry estimate from 2006 is 150,000–200,000 Comorians in mainland France [8]. In practice, many readers of Là-bas who look at the Comoros will be returnees with family on one specific island, and that island matters more than any score.

## Cities

### Moroni (Grande Comore / Ngazidja)

Capital and largest city, with about 111,000 inhabitants [9]. The article dates that figure to 2024 in one place and to 2019 in another, so treat the year as uncertain.

- **Cost of living**
  - Rent from a January 2026 blog [1]:
    - furnished 45 m² studio: 75,000 KMF (≈ EUR 152) in a standard area; 112,450 KMF (≈ EUR 229) in an upmarket area
    - furnished 85 m² flat: 158,325 KMF (≈ EUR 322) in a standard area; 227,554 KMF (≈ EUR 463) in an upmarket area
  - Africarrières, February 2026 [4]: studio or 1-bed in central Moroni 150,000–300,000 KMF (≈ EUR 305–610); 2–3 rooms on the outskirts 80,000–200,000 KMF (≈ EUR 163–407).
  - Expat.com, 14 October 2025 [3]: modest flat EUR 300–600 (utilities excluded); villa with generator and air conditioning EUR 800–1,500. Landlords ask for several months' deposit plus an advance. There is no reliable online rental platform, so housing is found by word of mouth, through employers or through agents.
  - Neighbourhoods [9], [1]:
    - Médina (Badjanani, Mtsangani): historic centre
    - Coulée, Hankounou, Caltex, Iroungoudjani, Magoudjou, Zilmadjou: residential districts
    - Maluzini: about 5 minutes from the centre, sea-view residences
    - Typical expat or middle-class streets were not reliably identified.
  - Meals: local restaurant EUR 5–10; expat restaurant EUR 20–30 per person [3]. Lunch menu ≈ USD 6.4 [1].
  - Monthly budget for one person:
    - about USD 1,207 including rent [1]
    - EUR 780–1,480 [3]. The same page's key-facts box gives USD 700, so Expat.com contradicts itself.
    - a local household spends 250,000–400,000 KMF (≈ EUR 510–815) [4]
  - Imported goods are expensive, and shortages of some products and fuel are common [3].
- **Work & money**
  - Expat roles are mostly in international cooperation (UN, NGOs), management, consultancy and technical posts [3].
  - Growth sectors: tourism, export crops (vanilla, ylang-ylang, clove), fishing and services [3].
  - Average salary ≈ USD 133/month [3]. Official minimum wage (SMIG) ≈ 40,000 KMF/month (≈ EUR 81) [4].
  - Remote work is possible but costly: internet is expensive and of variable quality (see below).
  - The UN classes Moroni as a hardship duty station, category D (severe), where families are allowed [11].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**
  - Companies are registered through ANPI (the national investment promotion agency) [3]. Its site was unreachable during this research.
  - The World Bank ranked the country 160/190 in Doing Business 2020 [3]. That index has been discontinued and the figure is dated.
  - The scene is small but it exists. Africarrières (Feb 2026) says the Comoros lack a formal tech ecosystem compared with Tanzania, Kenya or Madagascar [4].
    - Com'Work: coworking space and incubator in central Moroni, founded in 2018. It runs pre-incubation (3 months in person or 6 online), incubation and à-la-carte support, and has a 10-desk open space [21]. Listed as an AfriLabs member hub.
    - Innov'Lab: incubator set up in February 2017 by the chambers of commerce (UCCIA), with coworking, a 2-year incubation programme and partners such as Meck Moroni for credit [22].
    - A BIC Africa incubation bootcamp in Moroni on 26–27 November 2025 brought together 18 people from 12 incubators across the islands [23].
    - Both incubator profiles date from 2023; their current activity was not re-confirmed.
  - Fintech and crypto activity, and investor presence: **not found**.
  - The national daily Al-Watwan headlines economic-integration programmes (PAIE, October 2026). It carried no startup coverage on its homepage [12].
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - Power cuts are frequent; a generator or solar panels are advised [1], [3]. Grande Comore has no permanent rivers, so electricity depends on fuel-fired generation [8].
  - Water supply is irregular in some districts, so a storage tank is useful [1].
  - Telecoms are a duopoly: Comores Télécom (Huri) and Telma / Yas (Axian group) [1].
  - Internet: 4G covers ≈ 84%, with an average of ≈ 9 Mbps; fixed lines are rare and 4G routers are common [1]. Comores Télécom advertises a fibre offer [12].
  - Internet and phone cost about EUR 50–80 per month [3].
  - Internet was severely disrupted during the January 2024 post-election unrest [13].
  - Mobile money: details **not found**.
  - Transport: shared taxis cost 500–1,500 KMF per trip and petrol ≈ 700–800 KMF/L [4]. Ride-hailing apps: **not found**.
  - Roads off the ring road are poor, and night driving outside towns is discouraged [5], [1].
- **Health & schools**
  - Health capacity is limited; serious cases are evacuated to Réunion, Mayotte or mainland France [3].
  - There are 0.42 doctors per 1,000 people, a national figure [11].
  - GP visit: 5,000 KMF [1]; private clinic visit: 15,000–30,000 KMF [4]. Reference hospital names were not confirmed on opened pages.
  - Malaria and dengue are present. International insurance that covers evacuation is strongly advised [3].
  - Schools:
    - Lycée français international Henri-Matisse is in Moroni [9]. It has signed an agreement with AEFE (an "établissement conventionné") and had nearly 400 pupils in 2025–26, according to Mayotte Hebdo (27 March 2026) [20]. That contradicts Expat.com, which says no AEFE school exists [3]. Planned fee rises are EUR 155 for 2026–27 and EUR 444 for 2027–28, and non-scholarship parents are contesting them [20]. The base fee of "from EUR 1,464 a year" comes from one blog only [1].
    - Université des Comores was founded in 2003 [9].
- **Safety** (UK FCDO, updated 10 Dec 2025 per the original pass, re-read 2026-11-14 [5]; France Diplomatie page updated 4 Nov 2026 [2])
  - Crime levels are low, but the FCDO advises precautions against pickpocketing [5] (re-read 2026-11-14). The idea that street robbery is rare but more likely alone at night was not found on the FCDO page.
  - Protests can block the road between Moroni and Hahaya airport.
  - January 2024 election unrest: the FCDO reports that "a protestor was killed and several injured" in clashes with security forces [5]. Wikipedia adds at least 25 injured and a curfew in Moroni, Bambao and Itsandra [13]; that detail was not re-verified.
  - Anti-French demonstrations linked to the Mayotte dispute happen from time to time [5].
  - Karthala volcano is near the city; its last eruption was in 2007 [5].
  - Cyclone season runs January–May [5].
  - France Diplomatie flagged an El Niño weather alert for Comoros on 4 Nov 2026 [2].
  - Neighbourhood-level nuance: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**
  - Conservative Muslim society; social life revolves around extended family, neighbours and hospitality [1]. Displays of affection in public are frowned upon [5].
  - Nightlife is minimal. This is an inference from the alcohol and dress norms in [1] and [5], not directly sourced.
  - In July 2026 the Médina was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage list together with five other Comorian medinas (Le Monde, via [9]).
  - The diaspora's summer return (grand mariage season) is a known feature but was not confirmed on an opened page.
- **Diaspora & expat community**
  - About 5,000 expats live in the country [3].
  - The Expat.com Comoros group has 1,344 members [3].
  - No named returnee association, Facebook or WhatsApp groups were opened. **Not found.**
- **Climate in practice**
  - About 2,700 mm of rain a year. Rain is heavy December–July, with January the wettest month (364 mm); September–November is the driest period [9].
  - High humidity, and the cyclone risk in January–May overlaps with the wet season [5].
- **Watch out**
  - Power and water cuts.
  - Expensive, patchy internet.
  - Shortages of imported goods.
  - Limited healthcare: plan for evacuation insurance.
  - Housing found by word of mouth, with large deposits.
  - Political flare-ups around elections.
  - Conservative social codes.

### Mutsamudu (Anjouan / Ndzuani)

Capital of Anjouan and second city, ≈ 30,000 inhabitants (2013 figure, dated) [14].

- **Cost of living**: no city-specific rent or price data found. Africarrières does not cover Anjouan [4]. Expect lower rents than Moroni (inference, low confidence).
- **Work & money**
  - The only deep-water port in the country, with freight lines to Europe, China and mainland Africa; goods bound for Moroni are trans-shipped here [14], [9].
  - The island economy is agricultural (vanilla, ylang-ylang, clove) and also ships produce to Mayotte [15].
  - Remote work: not documented.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - Ouani airport, north of town, has flights to Moroni, Mohéli, Mayotte and Antananarivo [14].
  - Anjouan and Mohéli have small hydro plants, unlike Grande Comore [8].
  - Ferries between the islands cost 5,000–15,000 KMF [4]. The FCDO warns that inter-island boats can be overloaded and have capsized [5].
- **Health & schools**
  - Hombo regional hospital [15].
  - Expat.com describes healthcare on Anjouan and Mohéli as essentially absent [3].
  - University campus in Patsy; an Alliance française (1969) with a library and a 250-seat theatre [14].
- **Safety**
  - The FCDO says protests can occur on Anjouan but gives no island-specific detail [5]. Al-Watwan reports opposition meetings multiplying on Ndzuani (2 Nov 2026) [12].
  - Historical context: secession in 1997, then a military intervention in March 2008 [15].
- **Social life & vibe**
  - Medina of narrow, sometimes covered alleys around 17 mosques; restored Ujumbé palace; citadel [14]. Inscribed with the UNESCO-listed Comorian medinas in July 2026 (via [9]).
  - Densely populated, and the most crowded island [15].
- **Diaspora & expat community**
  - Anjouan is the main source of migration to Mayotte by kwassa boat. About 12,000 deaths are estimated, a historical cumulative figure [15]. That makes family links to Mayotte very strong.
  - No named groups were found.
- **Climate in practice**: 27–32 °C all year; hottest December–April; humidity often above 90% [14].
- **Watch out**
  - Even thinner healthcare than Moroni.
  - Few services for foreigners.
  - Hard political history.
  - Risky sea crossings between islands.
  - Very little reliable information online.

### Fomboni (Mohéli / Mwali): extra city

Included because it is the third city (21,987 inhabitants in 2019) and has a reputation as a calm town [16].

- Economy: subsistence farming and fishing, and more than half of residents are below the poverty line [16].
- Services: hospital centre, about 10 pharmacies, Alliance française, covered market (2015), bank branches, Bandar Es Salam airport 2 km away [16].
- Climate: about 2,000 mm of rain a year; average 25.2 °C [16].
- Fit: suited to people who want a slow pace or nature, not to careers. Expat.com calls healthcare on Mohéli "non-existent" [3].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This is an interpretation built on the facts above. No question or city measures a personality trait. No Hofstede, GLOBE or regional personality study specific to the Comoros was found. Afrobarometer ran its first Comoros survey in 2025 (Round 10) [17]. It found that 58% prefer democracy, 64% rate the economy as bad, and more than half reject gender equality in employment.

**Moroni**
- Openness: rewards people curious about Swahili-Arab-Comorian culture and an Islamic heritage city (UNESCO medina). It is less rewarding for anyone who needs a cosmopolitan, varied scene [9], [1].
- Conscientiousness: helps a lot. Planning for generators, water tanks, cash and evacuation insurance makes daily life workable [1], [3].
- Extraversion: social life is family- and neighbourhood-based rather than nightlife-based [1]. Sociable people do well if they invest in community networks.
- Agreeableness: respect for modest dress, Ramadan and public reserve is expected [5].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for power cuts, shortages and occasional political unrest [1], [3], [13].

**Mutsamudu**
- Openness: an old medina and a port town [14], with very few foreigners.
- Conscientiousness: even more self-reliance is needed, because healthcare is thin [3].
- Extraversion: dense, close-knit town [15].
- Agreeableness: conservative codes apply, as nationally [5].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): political tension and isolation [12], [15].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**
  - Same-sex acts are illegal for men and women. Under the 2020 Penal Code (art. 300) the penalty is 6 months to 2 years plus a fine of 100,000–300,000 KMF; the 1982 code allowed up to 5 years [18].
  - The UK FCDO page (re-read 2026-11-14) still states up to 5 years [5], which reflects the 1982 code.
  - In 2022 a man was prosecuted after a leaked video, the first known use of the 2020 code. In December 2024 two women got 5- and 6-month sentences after detention since June [18] (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14).
  - The government rejects UN recommendations to decriminalise [18].
- **Treatment of foreigners and diaspora**
  - Anti-French demonstrations linked to Mayotte happen [5].
  - No reliable reports found on racism or colourism towards the diaspora.
  - In Mayotte, Anjouanais migrants face hostility, including the "décasages" evictions [15]. This concerns French territory, not the Comoros themselves.
- **Conflict and politics**
  - No armed conflict. Post-election violence in January 2024 [13].
  - The terrorism threat cannot be ruled out but there is no recent history of it [5].
  - Anjouan has a history of secession and the 2008 intervention [15].
- **Women's daily life**
  - Dress codes apply to everyone [5].
  - Some customary land (magnahouli) passes through the female line, and a divorced wife keeps the home by custom [19]. The source is old and flagged as outdated.
  - Afrobarometer 2025: more than half of respondents reject gender equality in employment [17].
  - Global Gender Gap 2022: rank 134 of 146 [19].
  - No dated data was found on gender-based violence.
