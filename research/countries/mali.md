## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages:** Bambara is the everyday lingua franca. Soninké, Peul (Fulfulde) and others are spoken regionally [6]. The constitution adopted by referendum on 18 June 2023 and promulgated on 22 July 2023 demoted French to a "working language" [16], and in March 2025 the AES countries announced they would leave the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF) [7]. In practice French remains the language of administration and business in Bamako [6].
- **Currency:** West African CFA franc (XOF), pegged at 655.957 XOF = 1 EUR. Mali left ECOWAS but stays in UEMOA.
- **(a) French/EU passport holders:**
  - **France:** a visa is required. It is issued only by the Malian consulates general in Paris or Lyon, by appointment, valid 30 days and renewable on site. There is no visa on arrival, and travellers without a visa are sent back. A yellow-fever certificate is also required (France Diplomatie, updated 2026-09-15) [2].
  - **UK:** an e-visa is issued online by the Malian Ministry of Foreign Affairs (FCDO, undated) [4].
  - **Residence permit:** not found.
  - **Flights:** Air France is barred from flying to AES countries [7].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:**
  - **Dual nationality:** allowed since 1995 (Loi n° 95-70) [8].
  - **By descent:** a child born abroad to a Malian parent is Malian at birth. Under the 2011 code, nationality passes equally through either parent [8]. A diaspora member can therefore usually claim Malian documents (passport or NINA card) through a Malian consulate.
  - **By marriage:** a foreign spouse of a Malian national may naturalise [8].
  - **General naturalisation:** about 10 years of residence [8].
  - **Afro-descendant or right-of-return scheme:** none found (not the Benin or Ghana model).
  - **AES passport:** a common AES biometric passport has existed since January 2025 [7].
  - **ECOWAS free movement:** after the ECOWAS exit of 29 January 2025, AES citizens still have visa-free movement, residence and establishment under ECOWAS protocols; ECOWAS recognises AES-issued ID documents "until further notice" [7].
- **Diplomatic climate with France:**
  - French forces were expelled in 2022 [7].
  - A French DGSE agent was detained in August 2025 and sentenced in June 2026, then pardoned in August 2026 [7].
  - Expect a cool official climate towards French nationals. Dual nationals entering on a Malian passport avoid the visa issue (inference, not sourced).

## Cities

### Bamako

- **Cost of living:**
  - Expat.com puts the monthly budget at about €750 for one person and €2,050 for a family of four (page © 2026, figures undated). These are country-wide figures, not Bamako-specific [6].
  - 1-bed rent: not found, because Numbeo was unavailable (HTTP 429).
  - Meal price: not found.
  - Neighbourhoods often cited for middle-class or expat housing are ACI-2000 (the new business district west of downtown), Hippodrome (Commune II), and Badalabougou and Baco-Djicoroni (Commune V) [9]. No rent figures were found for them.
  - Prices are distorted by the blockade (see Watch out).
- **Work & money:**
  - The Bamako district holds about 70% of Mali's industrial activity. Services, crafts and trade dominate [9].
  - Expat jobs are mainly in construction, public works and electricity [6].
  - Saudi Arabia has invested in Bamako for decades and China has become an important investor in recent years [9].
  - TotalEnergies has sold its Malian assets (sale date not given in the source) [7].
  - Remote work: limited by the power cuts (see infrastructure).
  - Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - Donilab, a Bamako incubator, is often cited, but its site did not resolve when fetched (ENOTFOUND). Its status is unverified.
  - Orange Money and Moov Money are widely known as the mobile-money rails, but no source for them was read in this pass.
  - Investor presence, hubs and company registration: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - **Power:** during the 2025 blockade, fuel was prioritised for Energie du Mali (EDM). Many towns had rolling blackouts or weeks without power, and satellite imagery shows less night lighting in Bamako [5].
  - **Fuel:** queues lasting hours; fuel went to military and government users first [5]. The FCDO (updated 2026-07-10) still reports an ongoing shortage and higher prices [3].
  - **Transport:** Sotrama minibuses and bush taxis [9].
  - **Ride-hailing apps:** not verified.
  - **Internet, fibre, water:** not found. The Wikipedia telecom article is outdated (2011–2012 data) [10].
- **Health & schools:**
  - **Hospitals:** the reference public hospitals are Point G (on the hill above the city) and Gabriel Touré [9]. Private clinics: not found.
  - **French and international schools:** status not verified. The fetched AEFE directory came back empty.
  - **Schools during the blockade:** Malian schools closed nationwide from 26 October to 9 November 2025 [5].
- **Safety:**
  - **France Diplomatie (updated 2026-09-10):** all travel formally advised against; French nationals advised to leave temporarily while commercial flights still run [1].
  - **FCDO (updated 2026-07-10):** advises against all travel to the whole of Mali. JNIM has "the capability and intent" to target Bamako. The kidnapping threat is "very high". Crime is high (street robbery, armed robbery, fake police at night, taxi scams at the airport) [3].
  - **25 April 2026:** coordinated JNIM/FLA attacks hit Kati (a car bomb killed defence minister Sadio Camara) and the area near Bamako's Modibo Keïta airport, where fighting and explosions were reported. A three-day curfew (21:00 to 06:00) followed [11].
  - **28 April 2026:** JNIM declared a blockade of Bamako [5][11].
  - **1 June 2026:** a mine hit a bus on the Bamako–Kayes road, killing 8 [11].
  - **5 September 2026:** JNIM claimed a military post near the capital (claim only, not independently confirmed) [11].
  - **10 September 2026:** a JNIM attack on the Dioura military camp reportedly killed more than 100 soldiers [11].
  - No reliable source was found for neighbourhood-level nuance.
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - Bamako is a historic music capital (Salif Keita, Ali Farka Touré), with street percussion and djembe [9].
  - Nightlife is constrained by curfews and the FCDO's advice against moving at night [3][11].
  - Religion: a Muslim majority shapes daily rhythm (unverified: no source read in this pass).
- **Diaspora & expat community:**
  - The Expat.com Mali forum has about 8,370 members and a "présentez-vous ici – 2026" thread [6].
  - No returnee associations or Facebook/WhatsApp groups could be verified.
- **Climate in practice:**
  - **Hot season:** March to May.
  - **Mildest months:** November to February.
  - **Rain:** almost none from November to April; the rainy season peaks in July and August [9].
- **Watch out:** active armed conflict around the capital, fuel and power shortages, kidnapping risk for Westerners, no Air France flights, and visas only through Paris or Lyon for French passports [1][2][3][5][7].

### Ségou

- **Cost of living:** not found. Very likely cheaper than Bamako, but this is unsourced.
- **Work & money:**
  - Ségou hosts the headquarters of the Office du Niger, the state irrigation scheme [12].
  - Other activity: fishing, livestock, millet, two cotton-processing plants, pottery and crafts [12].
  - Remote work: not realistic given the power situation (inference).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:** tanker attacks were reported on the Niono–Ségou road in September 2025 (the Wikipedia digest's only source for this is a social-media post, so treat as unverified). Some fuel reached Ségou in November and December 2025 [5].
- **Health & schools:**
  - Université de Ségou, founded in 2009 [12].
  - Hospitals: not found.
- **Safety:**
  - Covered by the national advice against all travel [1][3].
  - The Ségou region saw fighting near Niono on 26 and 30 July 2026 (casualty claims from the army, Africa Corps and JNIM, none independently verified), and JNIM claimed control of an army barracks in San (Ségou region) on 9 August 2026 [11].
  - No attack on Ségou city itself was found in the sources read.
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - Ségou is the cultural capital of the Bambara and hosts the annual Festival sur le Niger, paired since 2019 with the Ségou'Art contemporary art fair (early February) [12]. Whether the 2026 edition took place: not verified.
  - River life on the Niger.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** not found.
- **Climate in practice:** hot semi-arid, about 640–660 mm of rain from June to September [12].
- **Watch out:** isolation if the roads are cut, the security situation in the region, and thin healthcare (inference).

### Sikasso (additional: second city, southern trade hub)

- **Why it matters for living:**
  - Mali's second-largest city: 225,753 people at the 2009 census.
  - A crossroads towards Côte d'Ivoire, Burkina Faso and Ghana.
  - Self-sufficient in fruit and vegetables.
  - Wetter climate: about 1,200 mm of rain, from May to October [13].
  - Hosts the Triangle du balafon festival [13].
- **Safety:** the Sikasso–Zégoua road (the Ivorian corridor) was a hotspot of the fuel blockade. More than 40 tankers were destroyed near Sikasso in October 2025, and there was another attack on 6 November 2025 [5].
- Everything else: not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

Context first: in 2025–2026 daily life in Mali's cities is shaped mainly by conflict, curfews and shortages. Read every line below as "who would cope", not "who would flourish". No academic regional-personality study on Mali was found in this pass. Afrobarometer Round 10 (2024) shows strong support for the AES (87% feel Mali's interests are recognised in AES decisions, versus 41% for ECOWAS) [14]. That is an indicator of political climate, not of personality.

**Bamako**

- **Openness:** suits someone drawn to a deep music heritage and a Bambara-speaking environment, and willing to live with French losing official status [7][9].
- **Conscientiousness:** suits planners. Fuel queues, curfews and fixed daylight-only travel routines reward people who organise ahead, as the FCDO itself advises [3][5].
- **Extraversion:** street-level sociability is strong, but night outings are restricted by curfews and security advice [3][11].
- **Agreeableness:** suits someone who can handle frequent police checkpoints and a tense diplomatic climate towards France calmly [3][7].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** a very high tolerance is needed, given attacks near the airport, a blockade and the kidnapping threat [3][5][11].

**Ségou**

- **Openness:** suits someone drawn to Bambara arts, the festival and river culture [12].
- **Conscientiousness:** suits people who stock up ahead, because road supply can be cut [5].
- **Extraversion:** a smaller, slower town (inference from size [12]).
- **Agreeableness:** not assessed; no source.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** high, because the region is near active fighting (Niono, San) [11].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - The new penal code, passed on 31 October 2024 and in force since 13 December 2024, criminalises homosexuality and its "promotion". Penalties are 2 to 7 years in prison plus a fine of 200,000 to 500,000 XOF [15].
  - The FCDO (updated 2026-07-10) reports arrests for "immoral behaviour" and a risk of violence and blackmail [3].
- **Treatment of foreigners and the French:**
  - The FCDO says British nationals are seen as "legitimate targets" for kidnappers [3].
  - Franco-Malian relations are broken at state level (expulsions in 2022, the detention of a DGSE agent in 2025–2026) [7].
  - No reliable source was found on everyday racism or colourism towards the diaspora.
- **Conflict zones:**
  - The whole country is formally advised against [1][3].
  - The JNIM blockade has been active since 3 September 2025 and was renewed on Bamako on 28 April 2026 [5].
  - The north and the centre are active fronts in 2026: the FLA took Kidal in late April and the army retook it in early October 2026; Tessit is listed under JNIM control; government forces held Ménaka as of 29 April 2026 (Reuters); Dioura (10 September 2026) and San (August 2026) were attacked [11].
- **Women's daily experience:** no specific section exists in the FCDO pages read. Afrobarometer 2024 reports that 77% of adults support girls continuing school after pregnancy [14]. No other reliable source was found in this pass.
