## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: Algerian Arabic (Darja) in the street; Tamazight (Kabyle in particular)
  in Kabylie and among many Algiers families; French widely understood in business, medicine,
  higher education and in Algiers/Oran middle classes. Public schools teach mainly in Arabic with
  French as a second language [4, 2025-12]. Fine-grained figures on French use: not found.
- **Currency**: Algerian dinar (DZD). Expat.com gives a city-centre 1-bed rent of DZD 26,000 ≈ USD 178
  (Dec 2025) [4], implying roughly DZD 146 per USD at that time. Numbeo reports in EUR only [5][6].
  Strict currency controls; exchange goes through banks or official offices [4]; France Diplomatie
  notes cash withdrawal is hard and exchange is preferable [2, 2026-09-15]. The official/parallel
  ("square") rate gap is well known but was not verified with a dated source in this run.
- **(a) French / EU passport holders**: a visa obtained beforehand from an Algerian consulate, paper
  forms, no central e-visa; processing from fast to several months; work visas need prior work
  authorisation from Algerian labour authorities [4, 2025-12]. Online content production can be
  treated as journalism and must be declared in the visa application [2, 2026-09-15]. Context:
  France–Algeria relations are at a very low point (ambassador recalls July 2024 and April 2025,
  tit-for-tat expulsion of 12 officials each on 15 Apr 2025, cancellation of the diplomatic-passport
  visa waiver 7 Aug 2025, law passed by the National People's Assembly on 24 Dec 2025 declaring
  French colonisation a crime) [9]. France Diplomatie's Algeria page carries a "vigilance renforcée"
  alert dated 10 Sep 2026, but it is a **multi-region alert for French nationals abroad**, not an
  Algeria-specific one [1].
- **(b) Diaspora with Algerian roots**: nationality passes by descent from **either parent** since
  Ordinance 05-01 (2005) [8]. The Nationality Code makes no explicit provision for dual nationality,
  but it is tolerated in practice (the renunciation requirement was removed in 2005); some senior
  public posts require sole nationality (unverified in this run) [8]. **Algerian citizens residing
  abroad who hold a valid foreign passport can enter visa-free if they prove Algerian citizenship**
  (Wikipedia dates this to 2024; the implementing text was not read) [8]. Practical traps (FCDO,
  Sep 2026): a dual national who enters on an Algerian passport must leave on one; mothers cannot
  renew their children's passports without the father's written consent; British males of Algerian
  origin may be liable for national service even if they live abroad [3].
- **Diaspora with roots elsewhere in Africa**: no Afro-descendant or right-of-return scheme found.
  Note the treatment of sub-Saharan migrants (see Legal & sensitive context) [11][12].

## Cities

### Alger

- **Cost of living**
  - 1-bed rent, city centre: **≈ €146/month (range €100–400)**; outside centre ≈ €116 (range €80–200)
    — Numbeo, updated 8 Nov 2026 [5]. Expat.com (national figures, not Algiers-specific): DZD 26,000
    (≈ USD 178) city centre, DZD 17,600 (≈ USD 120) suburbs, Dec 2025 [4]. Algiers is the
    tightest and dearest market in the country [4].
  - Typical expat/middle-class areas: Hydra, El Biar, Ben Aknoun, Bouzaréah — the "Heights of
    Algiers" where most embassies sit [7]. Neighbourhood-specific rents for these: not found (expect
    them toward the top of Numbeo's range or above — unverified).
  - Cheap restaurant meal ≈ €3 (range €1.50–7) [5]. Single person, excluding rent ≈ €403/month [5].
    Utilities incl. internet ≈ DZD 11,000/month for a single expat [4].
  - Average net salary ≈ €294/month [5] — local salaries are very low relative to diaspora incomes.
- **Work & money**: administrative and financial capital, ≈ 20% of national GDP; seat of Sonatrach,
  Air Algérie, Bank of Algeria and the ministries [7]. Remote work for a foreign employer is
  technically possible (fibre exists, see below) but cash economy, currency controls and banking
  that needs residency + in-branch visits complicate being paid from abroad [4]. Salaries by sector:
  not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: a state-led startup framework run by the Ministry of the Knowledge
  Economy, Startups and Micro-Enterprises — the "Startup" label (company ≤ 8 years, ≤ 250 staff,
  ≥ 50% held by individuals/approved funds), "Projet innovant", "Incubateur" and "Scale up" labels,
  tax exemptions, and the **Algerian Startup Fund** (public VC created with 6 public banks) [13].
  Algeria Venture is described as the public accelerator network [13] (its own site did not resolve).
  Best-known local scale-up: **Yassir** (ride-hailing, food, grocery, "Yassir Cash" payments, travel,
  events; also present in Morocco, Tunisia, Senegal, South Africa) [14]. **Crypto: buying, selling,
  using and holding "virtual currency" is prohibited** (Finance Law, Art. 117, OJ 28 Dec 2017) [15] —
  a hard no for a crypto-oriented profile. Foreign investor presence: not found. Company-registration
  ease: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: Algiers has a metro (extension Place des Martyrs–Bab El Oued under
  construction, photographed Sep 2026), tramway, commuter rail, buses and cable cars [7][16].
  Ride-hailing: Yassir [14]. Internet: Algérie Télécom (state) is the main fixed/fibre operator;
  ~36.2 M users, 76.9% penetration (2024) [10]. Broadband 60 Mbps+ ≈ €11/month [5]. Nationwide
  internet restrictions during the baccalauréat exams are widely reported in past years but were
  **not verified with a dated source in this run**. Online payment: banks are rolling out services —
  e.g. Crédit Populaire d'Algérie launched online payment from abroad (TSA, 14 Nov 2026) [16]. Power
  and water reliability data: not found. Earthquake risk is real (May 2003 quake, 2,200+ dead) [3].
- **Health & schools**: public care cheap but crowded; expats mostly use private clinics in Algiers and
  Oran; emergency number 14 [4]. Named reference hospitals: not found in this run. **Lycée
  International Alexandre Dumas (LIAD)**, Chemin Arezki Mouri, Ben Aknoun — French curriculum
  (AEFE network), maternelle to bac incl. BFI section, 2,500+ pupils, plus EPIAD annexe in Algiers [17].
- **Safety**: green zone "vigilance normale" for Algiers and Oran, heavy security presence, level
  judged satisfactory with basic precautions (France Diplomatie, updated 15 Sep 2026) [2]. FCDO
  (updated 25 Sep 2026): high terrorism threat nationally, concentrated at borders/Sahara/rural
  mountains; pickpocketing, robbery and petty theft in big cities; use a good local guide for the
  Casbah and tell your hosts your plans [3][1]. Avoid demonstrations; do not photograph official buildings
  [2]. Neighbourhood-level crime data: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**: dense capital (≈ 4.3 M urban area, 2025) [7]; museums, theatres, Jardin
  d'essai du Hamma, beaches nearby, major football clubs and stadiums [7]. Religion is central —
  78% of Algerians say religion is "very important" (Afrobarometer R6, 2015) [18]. Nightlife exists
  but is discreet and mostly hotel-based (unverified, no dated source found).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: named associations, Facebook/WhatsApp groups, returnee networks:
  **not found** (search unavailable). TSA runs a dedicated "Diasporas" news section [16].
- **Climate in practice**: Mediterranean; rain mostly Oct–Apr, wettest Nov–Dec (≈ 685 mm/yr); July–Aug
  highs ≈ 32 °C, record 48.7 °C in July 2023 [7].
- **Watch out**: visa and work authorisation slow and paper-based [4]; currency controls and cash
  economy [2][4]; France–Algeria diplomatic tension directly affects French nationals [1][9]; vague
  May 2024 penal-code amendments criminalise criticism online (Freedom House 2025, HRW 2025) [11][12]; no crypto [15]; dual-national
  traps (exit passport, children, military service) [3].

### Oran

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed city centre **≈ €180/month (range €110–432)**; outside centre ≈ €105
  (range €70–259); cheap meal ≈ €3; broadband ≈ €14/month; average net salary ≈ €263 — Numbeo,
  updated 6 Nov 2026 [6]. Expat.com calls Oran "moderately priced" vs Algiers [4]. Single-person
  monthly budget: not given by Numbeo [6]. Neighbourhood rents: not found.
- **Work & money**: Algeria's second city (≈ 1.5 M metro) and a major port serving Arzew (oil & gas
  port) and Sonatrach; LNG/energy events hosted; ferries to Marseille, Sète, Alicante, Almería [19].
  Salaries by sector: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: national startup label framework applies [13]; three universities incl.
  École Nationale Polytechnique d'Oran and USTO (Bir El Djir) [19]. Oran-specific incubators or hubs:
  not found. Crypto prohibited nationally [15].
- **Daily infrastructure**: tramway since 1 May 2013 (centre–Es-Sénia); metro planned [19]. Water: 51%
  surface, 38% desalination, 11% groundwater; five desalination plants [19]. Yassir available
  nationally [14]. Power cuts: not found.
- **Health & schools**: private clinics used by expats [4]; LIAD has an Oran site [17]. Named
  hospitals: not found.
- **Safety**: green zone, "vigilance normale" (France Diplomatie, 15 Sep 2026) [2]; general big-city
  petty-crime advice (FCDO, Sep 2026) [3].
- **Social life & vibe**: birthplace of **raï** (Cheb Hasni, Cheb Khaled, Rachid Taha) [19];
  hosted the 2022 Mediterranean Games, 50,000-seat stadium in Bir El Djir [19]; local specialities
  (créponné sorbet, oranais pastry) [19]. Reputation as Algeria's most relaxed, festive city is common
  but no dated source was retrieved.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: hot semi-arid; rain Nov–May (≈ 330–355 mm/yr, much drier than Algiers);
  August highs ≈ 32 °C, January ≈ 17 °C [19].
- **Watch out**: drier climate and reliance on desalination [19]; same national constraints as Algiers
  (visa, currency, expression laws, crypto ban).

### Constantine (additional city — why it matters)

Third city (≈ 450,000; ≈ 940,000 agglomeration), capital of eastern Algeria, major university pole
(Université Constantine 3 in Ali Mendjeli, among Africa's largest), 14.7 km tram, international
airport; Arab Capital of Culture 2015 [20]. Inland, so cooler winters (January mean low ≈ 2 °C) [20].
Relevant for students, academics and families from the East. Rents and daily-life data: not found.
(Annaba is also mentioned by Expat.com as moderately priced [4] and has a LIAD site [17]; not
researched further.)

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No academic regional-personality study for Algerian cities was retrieved; Hofstede's comparison
page returned no data in this run. The lines below are editorial readings of cited facts, not
measurements, and no quiz question measures a trait.

**Alger**
- Openness: suits people curious about layered history (Casbah, colonial and post-independence
  architecture, museums) [7] who accept that public expression is constrained by law [11].
- Conscientiousness: helps a lot — paper visa files, in-branch banking, residency documents [4].
- Extraversion: family- and neighbourhood-centred sociability; public nightlife limited (unsourced).
- Agreeableness: religion matters to most people (78% "very important", 2015) [18]; respecting
  social codes eases daily life.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for a dense capital (≈ 4.3 M), traffic, bureaucracy
  and the diplomatic climate for French nationals [1][7][9].

**Oran**
- Openness: Mediterranean port city with raï heritage and ferry links to Spain and France [19].
- Conscientiousness: same national paperwork as Algiers [4].
- Extraversion: music culture and big sporting venues [19] suit sociable people (interpretive).
- Agreeableness / Emotional stability: smaller and cheaper on the periphery than Algiers [6]; likely a
  gentler daily pace (interpretive, unsourced).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex sexual acts are illegal (FCDO, page updated Sep 2026) [3]. Penal Code Art. 338
  (2 months–2 years) and Art. 333 (6 months–3 years) [21]. Freedom House 2025: discrimination,
  violence, police mistreatment, prison of up to two years; many activists have left [11]. HRW reported mass convictions for
  homosexuality (15 Oct 2020, cited in [21]).
- **Treatment of sub-Saharan Africans / foreigners**: nearly 20,000 migrants expelled to Niger Jan–Aug 2024,
  often without screening, at least eight reported deaths (HRW World Report 2025) [12]; Freedom House
  2025 describes racial discrimination against sub-Saharan migrants, arbitrary arrests and
  deportations (≈ 20,000 Jan–Sep 2024 per Alarme Phone Sahara) [11]. Black diaspora members with regular status
  were not specifically documented in the sources read; colourism/racism reports beyond migration
  enforcement: not found.
- **Diaspora / French nationals**: France is a declared target of AQIM/IS; reinforced vigilance alert
  for French nationals abroad (multi-region, 10 Sep 2026) [1][2]; deep diplomatic crisis 2024–2025 [9];
  French-Algerian writer Boualem Sansal arrested 16 Nov 2024, sentenced to 5 years (27 Mar 2025),
  pardoned by President Tebboune on 11–12 Nov 2025 at Germany's request and flown to Germany [22];
  French-Algerian singer Djamila Bentouis sentenced 4 Jul 2024 to two years over a pro-Hirak song,
  reduced to 18 months on appeal (2 Oct 2024) [11][12].
- **Conflict zones**: red zones along the Libya, Mali, Niger borders and Tunisian border south of
  Tébessa; orange zones in Aïn Defla, Batna, Sétif wilayas, Aurès and Chréa massifs, Moroccan border
  (closed since 1994), Tindouf and Hassi Messaoud regions (map 2 Mar 2026, page 15 Sep 2026) [2]. FCDO
  (25 Sep 2026) advises against all travel within 30 km of the Libya, Mali, Mauritania, Niger borders
  and of the Tunisian border in Illizi and Ouargla provinces and the Chaambi mountains, and against
  all but essential travel within 30 km of the rest of the Tunisian border [1].
- **Women's daily experience**: Family Code based on Islamic law; male guardian needed for marriage;
  fathers are legal guardians; women's groups report 100–200 women killed in domestic abuse per year,
  no law on spousal rape (Freedom House 2025) [11]. Mothers cannot renew children's passports without the father's written consent (FCDO)
  [3]. 78% of Algerians agreed women should have equal rights (Afrobarometer 2015) [18].
- **Expression & religion**: Freedom House 2025 "Not Free", 31/100; May 2024 penal-code amendments
  criminalise social posts criticising officials or security forces; non-Muslim proselytising illegal
  [11][12].
