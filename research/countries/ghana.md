# Ghana

> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

Conversion used throughout: about 12 GHS per USD (retail, Sept 2026 [3]) and about 14 GHS per EUR (implied by The Africanvestor's own conversions, June–Sept 2026 [8]). The cedi lost about 10% against the dollar in 2026 [27]; recheck before quoting EUR figures to readers.

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: English is the language of administration, business and school; Twi (Akan) dominates street life in Accra and especially Kumasi; Ga is the historic language of Accra. French is a minority skill: five Alliances Françaises (Accra, Kumasi, Cape Coast, Takoradi, Tema) and one AEFE-accredited French school, in Accra [20][21]. A French speaker without English will struggle at work and with paperwork (expat.com FR forum testimony on work-permit difficulties, undated [24]).
- **Currency**: Ghanaian cedi (GHS). Inflation 5.5% in October 2026, up from 5.2% in September, versus 8% in October 2025 (Ghana Statistical Service via GNA, Nov 2026) [27].
- **Mobile money**: the 1% E-Levy on electronic transfers was repealed from 2 April 2025 (Act 1128) [25]; mobile money (MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash) is the default way to pay, and a KPMG survey cited in Dec 2025 put weekly use at 73% of retail customers [25].
- **(a) French / EU passport holders**: a visa is required in advance; the e-visa portal run by the Ghana Immigration Service now accepts 48 entry points (VisasNews, Sept 2026) [22]. The US advisory also notes six-month passport validity and yellow-fever proof [17]. Living and working requires a work permit then a separate residence permit; a GIS fee list effective 2 March 2026 (YEN, 22 Feb 2026) cites USD 388 for a foreign national's work permit (USD 194 for foreigners with a Ghanaian parent or spouse) and USD 40 for the non-citizen ID card; the residence-permit amounts in that list are for students only, so the adult residence fee is not confirmed. This conflicts with older guides citing USD 1,000 for a one-year work+residence permit (Mondaq 2025) [23]. Low confidence: confirm with GIS.
- **Business route**: the new Ghana Investment Promotion Authority Act (passed 4 April 2026, assented 15 July 2026 per KPMG/Clyde & Co) abolishes the old minimum foreign capital (USD 200,000 JV / 500,000 wholly foreign) for most sectors; trading companies still need USD 500,000 (down from USD 1,000,000) and at least 75% skilled Ghanaian staff (Clyde & Co, Aug 2026) [26]. Big change for a diaspora founder with a foreign passport.
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**:
  - *Ghanaians who took another nationality*: dual citizenship has been allowed since the 1996 constitutional amendment; a 2025 bill to let dual citizens hold offices such as ambassador or service chief was still not law in Nov 2026 (Council of State advised against it in July 2026) [28]. Former Ghanaians are exempt from the foreign-capital rules [26].
  - *Historic diaspora (descendants of enslaved Africans)*: Right of Abode (law of 2000) lets holders live and work indefinitely on their own passport [29]. Citizenship by the Beyond the Return programme (2020–2030): 524 people granted citizenship in 2024 (Al Jazeera, Sept 2025) [12]. **Applications were suspended on 1 Feb 2026** (Ministry of the Interior and Diaspora Affairs Office; Citi Newsroom, 2 Feb 2026); "revised timelines and updated guidelines" promised "in due course", no resumption date found as of this research [30]. The suspension followed a 30 Jan 2026 notice reportedly giving an application window of 2–6 Feb, DNA proof of descent, a two-year residence permit and fees of GHS 1,500 plus GHS 25,000 if shortlisted (advocacy/commercial sites, Feb 2026; low confidence, not confirmed on an official page) [45].
  - African passport holders: President Mahama announced free visas for all Africans from 25 May 2026 (GNA, April 2026); reports differ on whether this means no visa or a free e-visa, and implementation was not confirmed on an official page [44]. (The earlier claim of visa-free entry "from January 2025" traces to an announcement under the previous government that we could not confirm was ever implemented; treat as unverified.) Relevant to African passports only, not to French passports.

## Cities

### Accra

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed per month: East Legon GHS 5,000–14,000 (≈ EUR 360–1,000); Cantonments GHS 8,000–20,000 (≈ EUR 570–1,430); Osu GHS 4,000–12,000; Spintex GHS 3,000–8,000 (≈ EUR 215–570); Madina GHS 600–3,000 (local, non-expat). The law caps advance rent at six months for tenancies over six months, but in practice one property portal estimates Accra tenants pay about 1.93 years upfront on average and another says 6–12 months is usual (Rio Times, 14 Sept 2026, trade estimates) [3]. Broadband 60 Mbps+ ≈ GHS 497/month, utilities ≈ GHS 851/month for 85 m² [3]. Accra ranked 5th most expensive city in Africa for rent (Pulse, Jan 2025) [4]. Expat guides estimate USD 800–1,500/month for an average expat lifestyle (blog, unsourced; low confidence) [24]. Meal prices: not found in a reliable dated source.
- **Work & money**: concentration of headquarters, banks, telecoms, NGOs, embassies and multinationals; Greater Accra unemployment 21.5% (2025, expat.com FR) [24]. Salaries for locally hired roles are low: one forum post cites GHS 1,400–2,400/month for a French teacher (undated, low confidence) [24]. Remote work is feasible in middle-class areas with fibre or Starlink plus backup power.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Greater Accra holds about 70% of Ghana's startups (2022 report) [6]. Named hubs: MEST Africa (17-year-old programme, fully funded training then seed), Impact Hub Accra, Ghana Hubs Network (annual gathering 2025) [5][6]. Paystack opened an Accra office in Dec 2025 [5]. Ghana startup funding 2025 ≈ USD 56–66 million depending on tracker, down ~48% year on year; fintech, agri-food and logistics took 84% [31]. Crypto: Parliament passed the Virtual Asset Service Providers Bill in Dec 2025 (Bank of Ghana, 22 Dec 2025) and the President assented around 29–30 Dec 2025; licensing is split between the Bank of Ghana and the SEC. As of Sept 2026 no full VASP licence had been issued: about 20 firms were in an SEC sandbox and the law is expected to take full effect in 2027 (Mariblock, 3 Sept 2026) [32]. Company registration for foreigners eased by the 2026 GIPA Act [26].
- **Daily infrastructure**: power cuts ("dumsor") recurred through 2025 and 2026 (scheduled ECG maintenance cuts; May 2025 Achimota substation fire; April 2026 Akosombo fire) [7]. Water: Accra demand ~220 million gallons/day vs ~140 million produced (GWL MD, 2025) [33]. Floods every June: Kaneshie and Odawna submerged June 2025, 3,000+ affected at Odawna in May 2025 (NADMO) [34]. Internet: national median fixed download 52.8 Mbps in H1 2026 (Ookla via TelecomLead); Starlink licensed, GHS 770/month basic plan, at times "sold out" in Accra [35]. Ride-hailing: Uber, Bolt, Yango operate; the DVLA chief executive said on 11 Aug 2026 that 75% of the ride-hailing drivers' licences it checked were fake (a sample, not all drivers) [36]. Traffic is heavy; trotro (minibus) is the mass transport.
- **Health & schools**: private Nyaho Medical Centre, Lister Hospital (Airport Hills), Euracare (Labone/airport area); public 37 Military Hospital and Korle Bu Teaching Hospital (long waits); private hospitals mostly do not accept the NHIS (expat guides, 2025–26) [37]; UK FCDO publishes a list of medical facilities [37]. Schools: Lycée Français International Jacques Prévert in East Legon (AEFE, parent-run, 715 pupils in 2022, fees by nationality tier) [21]; English-language international schools include Lincoln Community School (non-profit, founded 1968, IB PYP/MYP/DP plus an American track) and Ghana International School (British model); one 2025–26 fee guide gives roughly GHS 70,000–120,000/year for primary and GHS 120,000–180,000 for secondary/IB (unsourced estimate, low confidence; check school fee pages) [46].
- **Safety**: US State Dept Level 2 nationwide (7 July 2026), citing crime and violence against women and against LGBT people [17]. France Diplomatie (updated 15 Sept 2026): Greater Accra "vigilance renforcée"; avoid walking or taxis at night in Nima and Jamestown; snatch thefts, sometimes violent, frequent in Osu, Cantonments, Ridge, Airport Residential, East Legon, where walking alone at night is discouraged; vehicle hijackings and armed attacks on cars and minibuses, usually after dark, including on major roads (not tied to named Accra neighbourhoods) [2]. UK FCDO: armed robbery more common since 2021, criminally motivated kidnappings in Accra, Takoradi and Kumasi, including of foreign nationals, attacks on vehicles on Graphic Road and George Walker Bush Highway (undated page, accessed 2026-11-14) [1].
- **Social life & vibe**: dense nightlife and restaurant scene in Osu, Labone, East Legon; December season ("December in Ghana", popularly "Detty December") with Detty Rave (7th edition, 27 Dec 2025, Spintex) and 125,000+ international visitors in Dec 2024, many diaspora — but also price spikes, crowding and traffic flagged by residents [38]. Chale Wote street-art festival in Jamestown [38]. Religion (Christian churches, Muslim zongos) is very visible and Sunday is a church day; Ghanaians are described as hospitable and tolerant across ethnicity and religion (Afrobarometer, 1 July 2021: 92% / 91% tolerance of other ethnicities / religions) [18].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: one of Africa's most visible returnee scenes (editorial judgement, no ranking source); Villa Diaspora co-living in Kwabenya helps with schools, land and clubs; Sanbra City gated development; Tabom (Afro-Brazilian) community in Jamestown as historical model (Al Jazeera, Sept 2025) [12]. InterNations Accra chapter, "Accra Expats International Friends" Meetup group, several expat Facebook groups (Talesmag post report, July 2025) [39]. Alliance Française d'Accra (since 1957) hosts the March Francophonie festival [20]. Community sources, not fact sources.
- **Climate in practice**: hot and humid all year; heavy rains peak May–June with flash floods, a shorter rainy season around Sept–Oct (general climatology; flood timing confirmed by 2025–2026 press) [34]. Plan your route and housing outside flood-prone zones (Kaneshie, Odawna, Weija lowlands).
- **Watch out**: often 6 months to 2 years' rent upfront despite the legal six-month cap [3]; dumsor and water rationing mean budgeting for an inverter/generator and water tanks (USD 50–100/month suggested by a 2026 guide) [40]; costs inflated in December and in returnee-heavy areas (studio rent 120 → 450 GHS 2019–2025 in one Al Jazeera example) [12]; very strong hostility to LGBT people (see Legal section).

### Kumasi

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed citywide average ≈ GHS 1,400 (≈ EUR 100), range GHS 800–2,500; better 1-beds in Bomso, Ayeduase (near KNUST), Daban, Ahodwo, Nhyiaeso; expat-standard furnished units GHS 4,000–10,000 (≈ EUR 285–715), mainly Ahodwo, Daban, Nhyiaeso (The Africanvestor, updated 21 Sept 2026, data as of June 2026) [8]. A 2026 guide says Kumasi is 30–40% cheaper than Accra for equivalent housing [40]. Meals: not found.
- **Work & money**: Ashanti commerce, Kejetia/central market trade, gold and cocoa economy, universities (KNUST, Kumasi Technical University). Salaries: not found. Remote work possible but fewer coworking options than Accra.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Kumasi Hive (since 2016, hardware/prototyping), Hapa Space (AfriLabs member, since 2015), KNUST's Kumasi Innovation Hub, KsTU's K-Innovation Hub (opened 14 April 2025); KNUST + Kumasi Airport City tech hub announced June 2025 (opening not confirmed) [9]. Ashanti stakeholders complained in Oct 2025 of unequal digital funding vs Accra [9]. Investor presence: thin (?).
- **Daily infrastructure**: dumsor notices regularly include Ashanti Region (2025) [7]. Fibre: CSquared metro fibre and the new GVA fibre service both cover Kumasi (Oct 2025) [35]. Water from Barekese and Owabi dams; no 2025 Kumasi plant shutdown found, but galamsey pollution has forced shutdowns elsewhere (Kyebi, Kwanyako, Bonsa) [33]. Uber and Yango available [36]. Traffic in the centre is notoriously dense (no dated source found).
- **Health & schools**: Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospital is the reference public hospital (no dated expat-level review found); a comparison notes fewer expat services and limited emergency care [40]. International schools: International Community School (ICS), Tumi International School; 5–8 options (2026 guide) [40]. No French school; Alliance Française Kumasi (since 1956, DELF/DALF centre) [20].
- **Safety**: UK FCDO mentions kidnappings reported in Kumasi as well as Accra (accessed 2026-11-14) [1]; France Diplomatie keeps southern and central Ghana outside red/orange zones but asks for "vigilance accrue" in the centre and north (15 Sept 2026) [2]. Neighbourhood-level data: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**: Asante royal capital (Manhyia Palace, festivals, kente crafts), calmer pace than Accra, smaller but welcoming expat community (expat guides 2026) [40]. Strongly Christian south of the city, Muslim zongo quarters; nightlife exists but much smaller than Accra (low confidence, no dated source).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: small; Alliance Française Kumasi events, KNUST academic community; expat forum threads such as "Kumasi Expats" on ExpatExchange [40]. No named returnee network found.
- **Climate in practice**: inland, slightly less humid, two rainy seasons (roughly Apr–Jul and Sep–Nov) — general climatology, no dated source.
- **Watch out**: fewer international flights and services; healthcare evacuation to Accra or abroad for serious cases; English and Twi essential; galamsey pollution in the wider Ashanti region.

### Takoradi (Sekondi-Takoradi) — added: oil & gas jobs and coastal living

- **Why**: Western Region port city and base of Ghana's offshore oil industry; cheaper coastal alternative to Accra.
- **Cost of living**: central 1-bed ≈ USD 210 (≈ EUR 180); frugal budget ≈ USD 500/month, comfortable ≈ USD 800 (GlobalCostData, aggregator, 2026, low confidence) [41]. 3–4-bed villas in European Town or New Takoradi USD 1,200–3,000/month (expat.com) [41].
- **Work**: oil & gas services, port, mining in the hinterland (Tarkwa). Details/salaries: not found.
- **Infrastructure**: Uber available [36]; water stress nearby — Bonsa headworks (75% of Tarkwa-Nsuaem supply) shut by galamsey pollution in Jan 2025 [33]. Alliance Française Takoradi exists [20].
- **Safety**: kidnappings reported in Takoradi, including foreigners (UK FCDO, accessed 2026-11-14) [1].
- **Diaspora**: not found.

### Cape Coast — added: diaspora heritage, university town, beach

- **Why**: Cape Coast and Elmina castles make it the emotional centre of the "return" movement; University of Cape Coast gives a student/academic base; cheaper than Accra.
- **Cost of living**: furnished 1-bed USD 250–450 (expatlife.ai, aggregator) vs a higher USD 704 central figure (CityCost); single-person budget ≈ USD 808/month incl. rent (CityCost) — conflicting aggregators, low confidence [42].
- **Diaspora**: Pan African Village near Asebu (Central Region) with ~20,000 plots set aside for diasporans; a family challenged the allocation in 2023 and a High Court injunction ordered construction halted, yet development continued, and about 150 farmers say they lost their livelihoods (Al Jazeera, 26 Sept 2025) [12]. Alliance Française Cape Coast [20].
- **Infrastructure**: Kwanyako headworks (Central Region) shut in 2025 by galamsey silt, ~10 districts short of water; restored Oct 2025 but rationing persisted (Citi/GhanaWeb 2025) [33].
- **Safety, health, schools**: not found in dated sources.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

Context: Ghana is generally described in academic work as high power distance and collectivist (extended-family obligations), but we could not open Hofstede Insights' official profile; one secondary table gives power distance 77 and individualism 20 (low confidence) [43]. A 2015 Yale Fox Fellowship policy brief on Ghanaian workers reported preliminary links between personality and wages (lower wages for emotionally unstable workers, higher for open ones, a negative link for agreeableness) — about individual workers, not about cities [43]. These are interpretations of city conditions, not measurements of anyone's personality.

**Accra**
- Openness: rewards people curious about a mixed scene — returnees, Nigerians, Lebanese, Europeans, a growing Afrobeats/art festival calendar [12][38].
- Conscientiousness: rewards planners — months to years of rent upfront, permit sequencing (work then residence), backup power and water [3][7][23].
- Extraversion: social life runs on events, churches, December parties and networking hubs (MEST, Impact Hub) [5][38].
- Agreeableness: hierarchy and greetings matter (high power distance) [43]; returnee clustering and perceived privilege create friction with locals [12].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for traffic, June floods, dumsor and December price spikes [7][34][38].

**Kumasi**
- Openness: rewards interest in Asante tradition and Twi rather than international nightlife [40].
- Conscientiousness: small market for services means organising things yourself; fewer expat providers [40].
- Extraversion: community life is more family, church and chieftaincy-centred; quieter for a newcomer without local family (low confidence).
- Agreeableness: deference to traditional authority (Manhyia) is part of daily codes (low confidence, no dated source).
- Emotional stability: lower cost and calmer pace reduce stress; limited emergency care increases it [40].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex relations already criminalised (Criminal Offences Act s.104). The Human Sexual Rights and Family Values Bill, 2025, which also targets advocacy, promotion and funding, was passed by Parliament on 29 May 2026; as of 3 Nov 2026 it was not yet law (no presidential assent; Mahama said on 1 June there was "quite a while to go") [10][11]. UK FCDO: prison sentences of 3–25 years possible, arrests at a 2021 gathering, pending bill may raise risk of violence and blackmail [1]. Afrobarometer (fieldwork 2019, published 2021): only 7% are tolerant toward people in same-sex relationships [18] (the ranking "among the least tolerant of 34 countries" is not on the cited page; unverified). Là-bas should flag Ghana as hostile for LGBT people.
- **Diaspora / foreigners**: Ghanaians are very tolerant of other ethnicities (92%), religions (91%) and nationalities (74%) (Afrobarometer 2021) [18]. Returnee clustering and rising rents create friction and resentment of perceived privilege (Al Jazeera, Sept 2025) [12]. Being labelled "obroni" (foreigner) as a diaspora member: no dated reliable source found.
- **Conflict zones**: France Diplomatie (15 Sept 2026): red zone along the Burkina Faso border including Tumu, Navrongo, Bawku (curfews in force; 20:00–04:00 in Bawku); orange zones Gbele park, north-west Côte d'Ivoire border, parts of the Togo border; night driving on isolated northern roads strongly discouraged [2]. UK FCDO advises against all but essential travel to Bawku Municipal (undated page, accessed 2026-11-14) [1b]. US State Dept: Level 3 for Upper East, North East, Upper West and Savannah west of N12 (July 2026) [17]. None of the dossier's cities are in these zones.
- **Women**: Afrobarometer Aug 2024 survey (2,400 adults): 16% say women frequently face sexual harassment in public; only a quarter think authorities do enough to protect women [19]. US advisory cites violence against women (July 2026) [17]; UK FCDO: isolated sexual assaults at tourist beaches, avoid walking or taxis alone after dark [1].
