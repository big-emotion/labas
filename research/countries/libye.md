> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

> **Research limits (read first).** The shared web-search budget for this workflow ran out after two searches for this country. Everything else comes from pages opened directly (official travel advice, Human Rights Watch, Amnesty, Central Bank of Libya, Libyan news homepages, DataReportal, Wikipedia). Numbeo returned HTTP 429 and OpenSooq (the main Libyan classifieds site) returned HTTP 403, so **no sourced rent figures were found**. Local lifestyle blogs, social media groups and startup-ecosystem sources were **not reached**. Treat the gaps below as real gaps, not as "nothing exists".

> **Editorial bottom line.** As of the latest advice we checked (FCDO, updated 21 July 2026; France Diplomatie, updated 29 October 2026), Tripoli is in France's **red zone ("formellement déconseillé")** and Benghazi in its **orange zone**. Under the product rule that `unsafe` cities are never proposed, **both Tripoli and Benghazi should currently be flagged `unsafe`** for a French-passport or diaspora audience [1][2][3].

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day**: Libyan Arabic is the everyday language. Amazigh (Tamazight) is spoken in the Nafusa mountains and Zuwara (general knowledge, not separately sourced here). English is used in the oil sector and at private universities. French is rare. Not sourced in this pass: a French-speaker should not expect to get by in French.
- **Currency**: Libyan dinar (LYD). Central Bank of Libya rates on 12 Nov 2026: USD buy 6.43 / sell 6.46; **EUR buy 7.13 / sell 7.16** [8]. This dossier uses **1 EUR ≈ 7.15 LYD**. Cash shortages recur: "Haftar meets Hammad to discuss cash liquidity and fuel crises" (Libya Observer, 17 Dec 2025) [9]. Libya Observer's black-market table was empty, last updated 9 Feb 2026 [9]. The CBL is pushing e-payments through LyPay (lypay.gov.ly), and its governor reviewed e-payment expansion on 12 Nov 2026 [8][10].
- **Entry and residence: (a) French/EU passport holders.** A visa must be obtained **before arrival** from the Libyan Embassy in Paris (6-8 rue Chasseloup-Laubat, 75015). Travellers whose passports carry Israeli visas or stamps are refused entry. Page updated 29 Oct 2026 [1]. A secondary source reports that visa-on-arrival for air travellers was suspended. It is undated and unofficial **(unverified)** [11]; the France Diplomatie page does not mention visa-on-arrival at all [1]. **No source found on residence/work permits** (Expat.com's Libya guide dates from April 2018 and says nothing on permits [12]).
- **Entry and residence: (b) diaspora with family roots.**
  - **Libyan father**: nationality passes automatically by descent (Law No. 24 of 2010). A birth abroad must be registered [13].
  - **Libyan mother and foreign father**: HRW's 2026 chapter states plainly that children of Libyan mothers and foreign fathers **cannot obtain Libyan nationality**, and that a 2010 law requires women to obtain permission to marry a non-Libyan [5]. Wikipedia describes a discretionary route on paper [13], but in practice do not count on it.
  - **Dual nationality**: only with state permission. Acquiring another nationality without authorisation can lead to loss of Libyan nationality [13] (Wikipedia, sources up to about 2021, so flag as possibly dated).
  - **Naturalisation**: 10 years' residence, must be under 50 [13].
  - **Diaspora or Afro-descendant schemes**: no diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme found. Nothing comparable to Ghana's or Benin's schemes.
- **Who controls what**: the GNU (Tripoli) governs the west. The LAAF (Haftar), allied with the eastern House of Representatives, controls the east, including Benghazi, and the south [5].
- **Consular cover**: France's embassy reopened in Tripoli in March 2022 (Janzour, +218 91 220 97 16), but consular services run from the **consulate general in Tunis** [2][14]. The UK provides **no in-person support in Libya** (British Embassy Tunis) [3].
- **Internet (national)**: 88.5% internet penetration; median mobile download 16.9 Mbps and median fixed download 10.9 Mbps (early 2025, DataReportal) [15].

## Cities

### Tripoli (طرابلس)

Capital. City about 1.18 M, metro about 3.25 M (2023 est.) [16].

- **Cost of living**: **Not found.** Numbeo was blocked (HTTP 429) and OpenSooq listings were blocked (HTTP 403). Expat.com "key facts" put a monthly budget at about **$800 for a single person** and **$2,400 for a family of four**. These figures date from **2018 and are stale**, and the dinar has since been devalued [12]. Older dated context: in Nov 2019, during the Haftar offensive on Tripoli, Reuters (via SABC) reported furnished two-bedroom flats at about $2,140–2,855 a month and landlords asking **six months' rent up front, in cash** [23]. That spike was war-driven and predates the 2021 devaluation, so treat it only as a sign that rents jump with displacement. Aggregator sites (Expatistan, Expat Arrivals) quote 1,000–2,100 LYD for a furnished one-bedroom, but their pages were blocked and are undated **(unverified)**. Neighbourhoods commonly named by expats (Gargaresh, Hay Andalus, Ben Ashour, Janzour) are **not sourced in this pass**. Janzour is confirmed only as the location of the French embassy [14].
- **Work & money**: the country's leading centre for banking, finance and communications. Industries include food processing, textiles, construction materials and tobacco. The Tripoli International Fair is held every year [16]. Expat career paths in Libya: oil, telecoms, NGOs, and teaching English at universities (Expat.com, 2018) [12]. Remote work is technically possible (16.9 Mbps median mobile) [15], but blackouts get in the way (see infrastructure). Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: the Ministry of Youth announced **Global Entrepreneurship Week 2026 (16–22 Nov)** (Libya Herald, Nov 2026) [10]. The CBL is pushing e-payments and LyPay [8]. The one named support programme found is **Tatweer Research's TEC / TEC+** (Benghazi-based, UNDP- and EU-backed): in Feb 2023 TEC+ set out to train 50 startups from Tripoli, Benghazi and Sebha over four months, with grants for the best 30 [24]. Whether it still runs in 2026: not found. Investors and company-registration steps: **not found**. Crypto: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - **Power**: blackouts during extreme heat (40–50 °C) triggered protests in Tripoli in summer 2026, dated June or 25 Jul–2 Oct 2026 depending on the section of the article. Protesters welded shut government buildings [17]. Businesses rely on diesel generators [18].
  - **Weather damage**: flooding hit Tripoli in Dec 2024 [4].
  - **Transport**: Mitiga airport closes during clashes (Aug 2023, May 2025) [4][19]. France Diplomatie calls city driving and night driving dangerous [14].
  - **Ride-hailing and mobile money**: apps not found.
- **Health & schools**: France Diplomatie says facilities are below French standards and **advises against any surgery** because blood-transfusion safety is not guaranteed. Ambulance: 1415. Medical air evacuation may not be authorised [20]. Reference hospitals: not found. French or international schools: **not found** (AEFE directory page did not load).
- **Safety**:
  - **Official advice**: France puts Tripoli in its **red zone**, travel "formellement déconseillé" (29 Oct 2026) [2]. FCDO advises against all but essential travel (21 Jul 2026) [3].
  - **May 2025 clashes**: on **12–19 May 2025**, heavy militia fighting followed the killing of SSA commander al-Kikli ("Ghneiwa"). Fighting was worst in Abu Salim and Salah Eddin. Ain Zara, the port and Mitiga were also hit [19]. Amnesty cites an NGO count of **53 civilian deaths** [7]. Afterwards, 10 charred bodies and 67 bodies in hospital refrigerators were found in Abu Salim and Al Khadra [5]. In Sept 2025, the GNU and the Radaa militia reached a de-escalation deal [7].
  - **Earlier fighting**: Tripoli fighting killed 10 in Feb 2024, and Tajoura clashes killed 9 in Aug 2024 [6]. Clashes in 2022 killed 32 [4].
  - **Crime**: the FCDO describes widespread crime, including armed robbery and carjacking [4].
  - **Neighbourhood nuance**: Souq al-Juma is the Radaa stronghold, and Abu Salim has been a repeated flashpoint [19]. Central-west districts (Janzour, Gargaresh) may be calmer, but this is **not sourced** (?).
- **Social life & vibe**: Ottoman Medina, Red Castle Museum, Martyrs' Square on the seafront, and strong football culture (Al Ahly Tripoli, Al-Ittihad) [16].
  - **Alcohol**: banned. A non-Muslim found drunk in public faces 2–6 months in prison and expulsion (Law 4/1994) [14].
  - **Dress**: modest dress expected [14].
  - **Morality police**: announced Nov 2024. It ordered mandatory hijab and banned mixing between unrelated men and women [6].
  - Nightlife in the Western sense: none found.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com Libya forum (low activity; threads on Benghazi housing, SIM cards, Western Union) [12]. Facebook/WhatsApp groups: **not found** (not searched).
- **Climate in practice**: hottest in Jul–Aug, with highs of about 33–34 °C [16]. Heatwaves reach 40–50 °C [17]. Rain falls mainly Nov–Jan (about 57–67 mm/month), with about 318 mm a year [16]. Summer heat plus blackouts is the hard combination.
- **Watch out**:
  - Militia clashes can start without warning [4].
  - Arbitrary arrests of activists and content creators [7].
  - Racist crackdowns on Black Africans (see Legal section).
  - Cash shortages [9].
  - No French school found.
  - Consular help only from Tunis [2].

### Benghazi (بنغازي)

Second city, capital of Cyrenaica. About 859 k residents, metro about 1.5 M (2023) [21].

- **Cost of living**: **Not found** (Numbeo and OpenSooq blocked). Expat.com shows a forum thread asking which Benghazi area is best to rent in [12]. Residential districts include Al-Hawari, Al-Quwarsha and Al-Fuwayhat. The coastal districts Al-Kwayfiya, Garyounis and Bu-Fakhra hold the beaches [21].
- **Work & money**:
  - **Main activity**: port imports, oil-driven commerce, banking and retail, plus cement, food processing and textiles [21].
  - **Construction**: post-war reconstruction (Hawari, Venicia) [21].
  - **Trade fair**: the Benghazi International Trade Fair has run since 2021 [21].
  - **Salaries**: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Tatweer Research, the organiser of the TEC / TEC+ startup programme, is Benghazi-based; TEC+ trained startups from Benghazi in 2023 [24]. Current (2026) activity not confirmed.
- **Daily infrastructure**: Benina International Airport, with frequent daily flights to Tripoli [21]. Power, internet and ride-hailing: city-level data not found. National figures apply [15].
- **Health & schools**: Libyan International Medical University (private) and the University of Benghazi [21]. The same national caution on surgery applies [20]. Hospitals and French schools: not found.
- **Safety**: France puts Benghazi in its **orange zone** ("déconseillé sauf raison impérative", 29 Oct 2026) [2]. FCDO: all but essential travel (21 Jul 2026) [3]. The city is run by the LAAF.
  - **Abductions**: HRW (2026) reports abductions of lawmakers in LAAF territory: Seham Sergewa, taken from her Benghazi home in July 2019, and Ibrahim al-Drissi, taken in May 2024; images of both apparently being abused circulated in 2025 [5].
  - **Expression**: political dissent is very risky [5].
  - **2025 clashes**: HRW's 2026 chapter does not mention any armed clashes in Benghazi in 2025 [5]. That is an absence of reporting, not proof of calm.
- **Social life & vibe**: medina ("al-Blaad") and the Italian quarter. Much of the historic centre was demolished in March 2023 [21]. The coast has beaches and resorts [21]. Football: Benina stadium hosted Libya's U-23 national team in Nov 2026 [9]. Population almost entirely Sunni Muslim [21].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found. The Egyptian and other African immigrant communities are noted [21].
- **Climate in practice**: hottest in Jul–Aug (highs about 31–32 °C, records about 45 °C). Wettest in Dec–Jan (about 62–69 mm/month), with about 265–270 mm a year [21]. Slightly milder than Tripoli.
- **Watch out**:
  - Life under a military authority with no tolerance for dissent [5].
  - Ethnic violence against the Tebu in the Kufra region, Aug–Oct 2025 (in the south-east, not in the city) [7].
  - Mass graves of migrants found in the east, Feb 2025 [5].
  - Little or no consular access.

### Misrata (extra city: why it matters)

Libya's self-described "economic and trade capital". The economy runs on the Libyan Iron and Steel Company, Al-Naseem Dairy (about 750 jobs), Misurata University and a large airport [22]. FCDO advises against all but essential travel there [3]. France puts it in its **orange zone** [2]. The summer 2026 blackout protests spread to Misrata [17]. It matters as the country's business-minded city, but nothing on cost of living, expats or tech was found. **Not recommended for scoring yet.**

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This is an interpretation of the cited facts. Neither the cities nor any quiz question measures personality. No Hofstede, GLOBE or Afrobarometer data on Libya could be retrieved in this pass (the Hofstede tool and Afrobarometer pages did not return Libya content).

**Tripoli**
- **Openness**: suits people curious about Ottoman and Roman heritage (Medina, Arch of Marcus Aurelius) [16]. It frustrates those who need artistic or nightlife freedom: alcohol ban, morality police, arrests of content creators [6][7][14].
- **Conscientiousness**: people who plan obsessively cope better. They vary routes, keep cash, keep generators and track security news, as the FCDO advises [4].
- **Extraversion**: social life is family- and male-network-centred. Football is a real public outlet [16]. Mixed public socialising is restricted [6].
- **Agreeableness**: rewards discretion and deference to social codes [14]. Public dissent is dangerous [7].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: a very high stress tolerance is needed. Sudden militia fighting (May 2025) [19], blackouts in 40–50 °C heat [17] and cash shortages [9] are the norm.

**Benghazi**
- **Openness**: fewer outlets for curiosity than Tripoli. There is a coastal lifestyle [21], but the heritage centre was partly demolished [21].
- **Conscientiousness**: may suit people comfortable with rules and paperwork, since daily life runs under a military authority [5].
- **Extraversion**: beach districts and football [9][21]. No public nightlife found.
- **Agreeableness**: strong conformity expected. Political speech carries severe risk [5].
- **Emotional stability**: no street clashes reported in HRW's 2026 chapter [5], but arbitrary detention and abductions are a background risk [5].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - **The law**: consensual same-sex relations are criminalised. Penalties include flogging and up to 5 years in prison (HRW World Report 2026) [5][4].
  - **Feb 2025**: a militia published forced "confession" videos [7].
  - **During 2025**: at least 9 arrests in Tripoli over actual or perceived sexual orientation or gender identity (Libya Crimes Watch, cited by Amnesty) [7].
- **Racism / treatment of Black Africans** (critical for this product's audience):
  - **March 2025**: racially motivated mass arrests of sub-Saharan Africans across western Libya, with incitement by some GNU officials. A foreign national was killed in racial violence in Sabratha on 13 Mar 2025 (Amnesty 2025) [7].
  - **2025, HRW**: HRW reports collective expulsions and "hate speech and incitement against black communities" [5].
  - **Migrant detention**: migrants in detention face torture, forced labour and sexual assault [5][6].
  - **What this means**: Black diaspora members, including French nationals, may face profiling at checkpoints. This is an **editorial inference** from those reports, with no specific source on diaspora nationals found.
- **Conflict**:
  - **Tripoli**: militia clashes in May 2025 [19][7].
  - **Kufra**: ethnic violence against the Tebu, Aug–Oct 2025 [7].
  - **Rest of the country**: France and the UK advise against all travel outside the listed cities [2][3].
  - **Terrorism**: Daesh is still present [4].
- **Women's daily experience**:
  - **Morality police (Nov 2024)**: mandatory hijab, a ban on mixing between unrelated men and women, and male guardian permission to travel [6].
  - **Law**: no domestic-violence law. Sentences are reduced for so-called "honour" crimes, and a rapist can escape prosecution by marrying the victim (HRW 2026) [5].
  - **Arrests**: a woman content creator was arrested in March 2025 [7].
  - **Nov 2026**: a Tripoli court sentenced seven former security personnel to death for murdering two women [9].
- **Freedom of expression**: 30 penal-code articles carry the death penalty, including for speech and association [5]. Activist Abdelmoneim al-Mureimi, abducted by Tripoli's Internal Security Agency on 30 June 2025, died in custody on 4 July 2025 [5].
