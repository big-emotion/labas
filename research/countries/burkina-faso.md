## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day.** Mooré is spoken by more than half the population. Dioula (Jula) is the trade language of the west, around Bobo-Dioulasso. Fulfulde is spoken in the north (general knowledge, not separately sourced). The country has over 60 indigenous languages. A constitutional amendment dated January 2024 made the national languages official and reduced French to a "working language" alongside English [13]. In 2025 Burkina Faso announced it was leaving La Francophonie [13][14]. French is still the language of the press (lefaso.net and burkina24.com both publish in French [21][22]), of schools and of business paperwork.
- **Currency.** West African CFA franc (XOF), pegged at 655.957 XOF = 1 EUR [13].
- **Religion.** 2019 census: Muslim 63.8%, Christian 26.3%, animist 9% [13].
- **Politics.** The military took power in two coups in 2022 (January and 30 September) [1b][13]. In May 2024 the transition was extended by five years. All political parties were dissolved in January 2026. In April 2026 the president said democracy is "not for us" [15]. Burkina Faso left ECOWAS on 29 January 2025 and is part of the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) with Mali and Niger [14].
- **(a) Entry with a French or EU passport.** A visa is required. Short stays under 90 days use the e-visa at visaburkina.bf [23]. The site also lists long-stay visas and residence permits, but gives no costs [23]. Expat.com says the e-visa launched on 17 August 2023 [6]. A yellow-fever certificate is required at the border [3]. In practice, France Diplomatie (10 Sept 2026) warns French nationals of possible arrest and detention, and France has no consular presence [5]. The UK also has no embassy; it covers Burkina Faso from Accra and cannot give in-person help [1]. Expat.com says the US Embassy suspended routine visa services on 10 October 2025 [6].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots.**
  - **Citizenship.** Anyone born to at least one Burkinabè parent is a citizen, wherever they were born. The nationality law does not mention dual nationality, so it is generally assumed to be allowed [8].
  - **Naturalisation.** Normally 10 years of residence. Foreign spouses can apply after 5 years of marriage [8].
  - **Diaspora schemes.** No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found [8].
  - **Visa fees.** On 11 September 2025 the Council of Ministers abolished visa fees for citizens of African countries [6][24]. This is a free visa, not a visa exemption: an application through visaburkina.bf is still required. Before the change a tourist visa cost the equivalent of about 83 EUR [24]. Expat.com noted no start date as of 12 September 2025 [6]; whether the portal now charges Africans nothing: **not verified**.
  - **AES citizens** (Mali, Niger) keep visa-free movement and residence rights [14].
  - **Consular services abroad.** Lefaso.net reports a new digital ID service, "YORI ID", launched for Burkinabè in the United States (headline seen 2026-11, undated) [21].

## Cities

### Ouagadougou

Capital. Population about 2.4 million in 2019; 2.875 million estimated in 2022 [9][10].

- **Cost of living.**
  - *Rent:* a 1-bed flat in the centre costs about 250,000 XOF/month (about 381 EUR). A 2-bed costs 250,000–500,000 XOF (380–760 EUR). A 3-bed villa costs 400,000–800,000 XOF (610–1,220 EUR) [6].
  - *Neighbourhoods:* Ouaga 2000 is calm and modern, with villas, private security and the international institutions. Zone du Bois and Koulouba are quiet and close to the centre [6].
  - *Meals:* a cheap restaurant meal is about 3,000 XOF (4.6 EUR); a mid-range dinner for two about 25,000 XOF (38 EUR) [6].
  - *Monthly budget:* about 650,000 XOF (about 990 EUR) for a single person, and 2.4–3 million XOF for a family of four [6]. Expat.com's own summary gives a wider range (650,000–800,000 XOF for one person, 2.4–3 million XOF for a family) than its detailed table (650,000 XOF and 2.38 million XOF), so treat these as rough estimates. The page is undated, but its newest dated item is from October 2025.
  - *Moving in:* expect a deposit of 1–2 months' rent and a one-year lease [6].
- **Work & money.**
  - The local economy is mostly administration and trade, with a little food processing and textile industry [10].
  - Gold dominates the national economy: production reached a record 90.8 t in 2025 (burkina24, 2026-11-11) [22].
  - The legal minimum wage is 45,000 XOF/month (since 1 July 2023) [6].
  - Banking is active: Coris Bank opened a digital "Cash Point" and Vista Bank has a new head (burkina24, 2026-11-12/13) [22].
  - Remote work for a foreign employer is technically possible but limited by power cuts and the security context. No data found on how feasible it is in practice.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech.**
  - There is an active state-led digital agenda. The 21st "Semaine du Numérique" in 2026 had technological sovereignty as its theme [21][22]. Lefaso.net also reports the EDTECH Faso Forum (2nd edition) and the "Le Faso Digital" awards (2nd edition) [21].
  - Large companies must use certified e-invoicing from 1 January 2027 (DGI, burkina24, 2026-11-06) [22].
  - Hubs and incubators: AfriLabs lists Jokkolabs Burkina Faso (Ouagadougou) and the Burkina Business Incubator among its member hubs (accessed 2026-11-14) [25]. Ouagalab could not be confirmed as still active.
  - Investor presence, crypto adoption and rules, company-registration time: **not found** (web search budget ran out during verification).
- **Daily infrastructure.**
  - *Power:* cuts are frequent, and a generator or solar kit is commonly needed [6]. The utility's website (sonabel.bf) refused the connection.
  - *Phone and internet:* the operators are Orange, Moov Africa (formerly Onatel) and Telecel [16]. Recent fibre and mobile-money figures: **not found**. The Wikipedia data are from 2012–2020.
  - *Transport:* SOTRACO buses, the green taxis, and mostly motorbikes [10]. Ride-hailing apps: **not found**.
  - *Water:* **not found**.
- **Health & schools.**
  - *Hospitals:* the Yalgado-Ouédraogo university hospital (CHU) and the Charles de Gaulle paediatric hospital, plus private clinics [9][10]. The FCDO says medical facilities are "very limited" and serious cases may need evacuation to Europe; the ambulance number is 18 [4]. Expat.com says about 31% of health facilities are affected by the security crisis [6].
  - *Schools:* the Lycée Saint-Exupéry (French curriculum) [9] and the International School of Ouagadougou (American curriculum, about 250 pupils) [6][9]. International-school fees run 3–8 million XOF/year (4,575–12,200 EUR) [6]. The Lycée Saint-Exupéry still presents itself as an AEFE-conventionné school and announces enrolment for 2026–2027 on its site (accessed 2026-11-14) [26]; how the break in diplomatic relations affects it is not stated.
- **Safety.**
  - *Official advice:* the FCDO advises against all travel and says the terrorism threat is "increasingly" present in the capital (2026-10-26) [1][2].
  - *Street crime:* thieves, often armed, are most active near the UN roundabout (Rond-point des Nations Unies) and the former central market [2].
  - *Roads:* attacks are possible on all roads, including Ouagadougou–Bobo [2].
  - *Past attack:* on 15 January 2016, gunmen attacked the Splendid Hotel and Cappuccino café, killing 28 people [9].
  - *Political tension:* protests are common [2]. Under the state of emergency some civil rights can be suspended, though Ouagadougou is not one of the eight regions it covers [2].
  - *Neighbourhoods:* Ouaga 2000 is described as calm and guarded [6]. No more detailed neighbourhood data found.
- **Social life & vibe.**
  - *Culture:* the city calls itself the film capital of Africa. It hosts FESPACO (Africa's largest film festival), the SIAO crafts fair, FESPAM and FITMO [9]. Coming up: Jazz à Ouaga, 27–29 Nov 2026; "Rue des étoiles" plaques on avenue Kwame N'Krumah; Baba Village 2026 (burkina24, 2026-11-13/14) [22].
  - *Green space:* Bangr Weogo urban park [10].
  - *Religion:* majority Muslim with a large Catholic minority [13].
  - *Nightlife, food, sport:* maquis (open-air bars and eateries), grilled food and nightlife detail: **not sourced in this pass**.
- **Diaspora & expat community.**
  - Diaspora ties exist: the Burkinabè diaspora in Côte d'Ivoire is donating to families of fallen soldiers (burkina24, 2026-11-13) [22]. The large wave of returnees from Guinea is a forced return, not a lifestyle move: more than 52,000 people (communiqué of 13 Nov 2026) [21].
  - Named Facebook/WhatsApp groups or returnee associations: **not found**.
- **Climate in practice.** Hot semi-arid climate. March–April is extreme, with highs up to 43 °C (record 46.1 °C). Rains fall May–September. October–February is cooler, with nights around 16 °C [9].
- **Watch out.**
  - Whole-country "do not travel" advice from the UK and France.
  - French nationals specifically risk arrest and detention, with no French consulate to help [5].
  - The death penalty was reinstated in October 2026 for terrorism, espionage and treason [2].
  - Independent media are being suppressed: TV5Monde banned (May 2026), a journalist forcibly disappeared, civic groups dissolved [18].
  - Frequent power cuts [6].

### Bobo-Dioulasso

Second city and historic "economic capital". About 1.13 million people at the 2023 census [11][12].

- **Cost of living.** Rents, meal prices and monthly budget: **not found** (Numbeo was blocked; Expat.com gives no Bobo figures). It is generally cheaper than the capital, but this is not sourced.
- **Work & money.**
  - Agricultural trade and textiles (cotton), plus food processing and mechanics [11][12].
  - Mango exports have grown since 2005; cashew and shea are other cash crops [11][12].
  - The city has lost economic weight since the Ivorian crises of 2002 and 2010–11 [12].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech.** **Not found.**
- **Daily infrastructure.**
  - Rail: Sitarail station on the Abidjan–Ouagadougou line (as of 2014: 3 passenger trains a week) [11][12].
  - Air: an airport (2015: 2 flights a week to Abidjan and to Ouaga) [11]. These data are old; current service is unknown.
  - Power cuts, internet, ride-hailing: **not found** for Bobo specifically.
- **Health & schools.**
  - The Sourou Sanou university hospital (CHU) has 593 beds. A 500-bed Chinese-funded hospital at Pala had its first stone laid on 20 Oct 2020 [12]; whether it is open now is unknown.
  - Universities: Nazi-Boni (public), plus UCAO and U-AUBEN (private) [12].
  - French or international schools: **not found**.
- **Safety.**
  - The FCDO advises against all travel (2026-10-26) [1].
  - Hauts-Bassins, Bobo's region, is one of the eight regions under the state of emergency [2]. This is a key difference from Ouagadougou.
  - The Ouaga–Bobo road is exposed to attacks [2].
  - Local press reports an anti-drug operation in which more than 4 tonnes of banned products were seized and 22 people arrested (lefaso.net, 2026-11) [21].
- **Social life & vibe.**
  - Widely seen as the country's cultural and music capital, home of the balafon (the West African wooden xylophone) [11][12].
  - It hosts the Semaine nationale de la culture (SNC), held every two years since 1990, plus the FITMO, Yeleen and Rencontres musicales africaines festivals [12].
  - Landmarks: the Sudano-Sahelian Grand Mosque (1880s–1890s), the old Dioulassoba quarter, and the sacred pond of Dafra [11][12].
  - Dioula is the street language (general knowledge).
- **Diaspora & expat community.** **Not found.**
- **Climate in practice.** Wetter than Ouaga, about 1,000 mm/year. The dry season runs October–April, with highs often above 38 °C [11].
- **Watch out.**
  - State of emergency in its region [2].
  - Isolated by insecure roads [2].
  - Very little current, verifiable daily-life data.

### Other city considered: Koudougou

Third city, 160,239 people in 2019, 75 km west of Ouagadougou on the railway [17]. It is a university town (University of Koudougou, since 2005) with agriculture and shea/textile trades [17]. It does not add a distinct living profile for the diaspora and falls under the same whole-country advice. **Not recommended for addition.**

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of cited facts. No city "measures" a trait. Hofstede/Culture Factor scores for Burkina Faso [20]: Power Distance 70, Individualism 15, Achievement 50, Uncertainty Avoidance 55, Long-Term Orientation 27, Indulgence 18. These may be regional estimates for West Africa rather than a dedicated national survey; treat them as indicative. Afrobarometer's latest Burkina round (Round 9, 2022) finds 85% of people feel strong ties to fellow citizens [19].

**Ouagadougou**
- **Openness:** suits people drawn to African cinema, crafts and live culture (FESPACO, SIAO, Jazz à Ouaga) [9][22]. The space for public debate has shrunk sharply (media bans, dissolved civic groups) [18].
- **Conscientiousness:** helps with visa formalities, generator and solar planning, and security discipline (no night travel, avoiding crowds) [2][5][6].
- **Extraversion:** festival life and community events continue [22]. Crowds are officially advised against [5].
- **Agreeableness:** a strongly collective, high-power-distance society, where respect for hierarchy and community counts (Individualism 15, Power Distance 70) [20].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** very high stress tolerance is needed: terrorism threat, possible coups, arbitrary-detention risk, power cuts [1][2][5][6].

**Bobo-Dioulasso**
- **Openness:** a music- and heritage-centred city (balafon, SNC) [11][12].
- **Conscientiousness:** expect thin services and insecure intercity roads [2].
- **Extraversion:** a street and festival culture around the SNC [12].
- **Agreeableness:** same collective norms as above [20].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** a state of emergency covers the region [2].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT.** Same-sex acts and their "promotion" have been criminalised since **1 September 2025**: 2–5 years in prison plus a fine, and **foreigners are deported** [7]. The FCDO confirms sentences of up to 5 years (page updated 2026-10-26) [2]. Before 2025 they were legal [7]. Press reports date the parliamentary vote to early September 2025; Wikipedia's dates are internally inconsistent, so rely on the FCDO wording [2][7].
- **Conflict.**
  - The Islamist insurgency continues: HRW reported an Islamist armed group attack on a mining site in the north on 2026-11-04 [18].
  - HRW found crimes against humanity "by all sides", including army-led ethnic cleansing of Fulani communities (2026-04-02), and an army massacre of more than 130 civilians (2025-05-12) [18].
  - The UN human rights office was forced to close in July 2026 [18].
  - A state of emergency covers 8 regions, including Hauts-Bassins (Bobo) [2].
- **Civil liberties.**
  - All political parties were dissolved (Jan 2026) [15].
  - Critical journalists were conscripted (2024–25) [15]. Journalist Atiana Serge Oulon has been forcibly disappeared for two years [18].
  - The largest student union was suspended (May 2026) [18].
  - The death penalty was reinstated (Oct 2026) [2].
- **Treatment of foreigners and the diaspora.** French nationals risk arrest and detention, and relations with France have been severed (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-10) [5]. No reliable source was found on racism or colourism toward diaspora returnees.
- **Women's daily experience.** Afrobarometer (dispatch AD1107, January 2026) reports that women still face barriers to equality in leadership and in owning assets and land [19]. The FCDO page has no country-specific advice for women [1]. Harassment data: **not found**.
