## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: Tunisian Arabic (Derja) on the street. French is widely used in business, administration, higher education and among private-sector professionals. Public schools teach in Arabic, with French as a second language [S6]. Expat.com describes French as "well established" in tourism and education [S7]. English is spreading in tech and startups **[unverified]**.
- **Currency**: Tunisian dinar (TND/DT). The dinar is not convertible outside Tunisia, and exchanging it is regulated **[unverified]**. Cash remains the norm in daily life (Expat.com Sousse guide, Dec 2025) [S7].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**: no visa for stays under 3 months. Since 1 January 2025, a passport valid for at least 3 months on entry is required and the national ID card is no longer accepted [S2][S6]. For longer stays, you apply to the Ministry of the Interior for a visa and a *carte de séjour*. Expat.com gives the deadline as within 3 months of arrival in its general guide, but as 30 days in its Sousse guide [S6][S7]: confirm locally. A 10-year card may be granted after 3 years of legal residence. There is no retiree visa as such: retirees need a minimum monthly transfer of €400 into a local account. Work-permit processing times: not stated by the source (an earlier draft's "4–12 weeks" was not found on the page and has been removed). Buying property requires authorisation from the Interior Ministry [S6][S7]. Overstays are fined on exit [S6].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**: Tunisia has allowed dual nationality since 1975. A child of a Tunisian father **or** mother acquires nationality, and mothers have had the same standing as fathers since a 2010 amendment. Children born abroad to a Tunisian mother before the reforms can claim nationality by declaration. Naturalisation takes 5 years of residence in general, or 2 years for the spouse of a Tunisian (Wikipedia, which may lag recent amendments) [S8]. Tunisia has no Afro-descendant "right of return" scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's: not found. **Important for the sub-Saharan diaspora**: Tunisia is a North African country with a documented 2023–2024 wave of hostility towards Black African migrants (see Legal & sensitive context) [S9][S5].

## Cities

### Tunis (Grand Tunis, incl. La Marsa, Carthage, Les Berges du Lac)

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed in the centre costs **1,285 TND/month (range 900–1,610)**, about €387 (€271–485). Outside the centre it costs **768 TND (500–1,000)**, about €231. An inexpensive meal is 13.5 TND (~€4). Monthly costs for a single person, excluding rent, come to 1,633 TND (~€492). Source: Numbeo, 2026-11-10 [S3]. Expat.com (Dec 2025) gives 800–1,300 TND for a 1-bed in the centre and 1,500–3,000 TND for a 4-room flat or villa [S6]. **Neighbourhoods**: by the sea, La Marsa, Carthage, Sidi Bou Saïd and Gammarth. More residential: Mutuelleville and El Menzah. Les Berges du Lac and La Marsa are the most expensive [S6]. Leases run 1 year, with a 2-month deposit [S6]. **Rough monthly budget**: 2,500–3,000 TND (~€750–900) for a single person renting in the centre. This is our own arithmetic from [S3].
- **Work & money**: Tunis concentrates administration, offshore service centres, IT services and the startup scene **[unverified]**. National unemployment was about 15.4% (Q3 2025, cited by Expat.com), and hiring often goes through personal networks [S7]. Remote work for a foreign employer is feasible given broadband prices (~64 TND/month for 60 Mbps+) [S3]. Local salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Tunisia has a dedicated **Startup Act** legal framework (passed in 2018 [S18]) with a "Startup Label". It is run by the government portal startup.gov.tn, and its application sessions were still running in October 2025 (session opened 2025-10-07) [S10]. Labels awarded: 1,146 in total as of 2025-02-22 (Managers.tn) [S17], and 1,352 per MENA Startup Digest (updated 2026-07-19), which also reports funding and red-tape complaints and a "Startup Act 2.0" in preparation [S19]. Lepetitjournal.com published an "Entreprendre en Tunisie" guide in 2026 [S11]. Named hubs and incubators (e.g. The Dot, Flat6Labs Tunis, Smart Capital) **[unverified]**. Crypto: Tunisia's legal stance was not found in the sources opened; widely reported as restrictive **[unverified]**.
- **Daily infrastructure**: broadband 50–100 TND/month [S3]. The FCDO's July 2026 update specifically reviewed "summer power supply", which suggests summer outages are a live issue (FCDO, updated 2026-07-24) [S1]. Transport includes the light metro, the TGM line to La Marsa and the RFR suburban rail **[unverified]**. Ride-hailing apps (Bolt, Yassir, InDrive) **[unverified]**. Water stress and summer cuts **[unverified]**.
- **Health & schools**: private clinics are faster and often English-speaking. A GP visit costs 20–40 TND and a specialist 40–60 TND. Public CNAM care is cheap but slow. The Franco-Tunisian social-security agreement covers private care poorly, so Expat.com advises CFE membership plus top-up or international insurance [S6]. Schools: 30+ AEFE-approved French schools in Tunisia, including **Lycée Gustave-Flaubert** and **Lycée Pierre-Mendès-France** in Tunis (11,000–24,000 TND/yr). British, American, Canadian and IB international schools cost 7,000–26,000 TND/yr [S6]. Tunis is the only city with the full range [S7].
- **Safety**: Tunis is in France Diplomatie's **yellow zone** (heightened vigilance), as is the whole country outside the border areas. Street crime is "relatively under control", but take care in city centres and outer neighbourhoods at night because of bag snatching. **Racist assaults have recently been reported in Grand Tunis**. Protests can be spontaneous, and curfews or traffic bans have been imposed in central Tunis and around Carthage palace (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-15) [S4]. The FCDO advises against all travel only to border military zones (Chaambi, Salloum, Sammamma, Mghila; the Libyan border strip incl. Ben Guerdane) and against all but essential travel to Kasserine governorate and other western and southern border belts; Tunis is not listed (FCDO, updated 2026-07-24, still current 2026-11-14) [S1]. US State Dept page: blocked (403).
- **Social life & vibe**: cafés, the seaside suburbs and La Marsa evenings. Nightlife exists in Gammarth and La Marsa **[unverified]**. Ramadan visibly changes daily rhythms [S7]. Religion is present but daily life is comparatively secular for the region **[unverified]**.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: the Expat.com Tunisie forum; the lepetitjournal.com Tunis edition [S11]; the Institut français and Maisons de France (a Sfax branch exists) [S11]. Sub-Saharan student and worker associations (e.g. AESAT) **[unverified]**. Named Facebook groups: not reached.
- **Climate in practice**: very hot June–September, with July–August highs around 34 °C and a record 49 °C on 2023-07-24. Rain falls mostly December–January (and September), about 486 mm a year. Winters are mild, around 16 °C highs and 8 °C lows [S12]. Afrobarometer: 77% of Tunisians aware of climate change say it is worsening their lives [S13].
- **Watch out**: political climate and speech offences under Decree-Law 54, with journalists and influencers jailed in 2024 [S9]. Anti-Black racism [S4][S9]. Summer power supply [S1]. A non-convertible currency **[unverified]**. Article 230 [S4].

### Sousse

- **Cost of living**: 1-bed in the centre costs **717 TND (500–900)**, about €216. Outside the centre it costs **483 TND (400–600)**, about €146. A meal is 13.5 TND and broadband 58 TND/month. Numbeo flags low contributor counts (32 in 18 months), last updated 2026-11-10 [S14]. Expat.com (Dec 2025) gives a 2-room flat at 850–1,200 TND, and sea views from 2,000 TND. Rent is often paid several months or a whole year in advance [S7]. **Neighbourhoods**: Corniche/Boujaffar (seafront, new builds), Hammam Sousse (quieter, near the beach), Sahloul (new, family favourite), Khezama (mid-range) and the Médina (authentic but needs work) [S7]. **Monthly budget**: Expat.com's own figures for a single person are inconsistent, ranging from €470 to €1,100 [S7].
- **Work & money**: tourism-heavy, with tight competition for jobs and network-based hiring [S7]. The University of Sousse was founded in 2004 [S7]. Remote work is feasible given broadband prices [S14].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: much thinner than Tunis. Named hubs: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: cash is the norm [S7]. The train to Tunis takes about 2h20 [S7]. Power, water and ride-hailing specifics: not found.
- **Health & schools**: **Sousse International School** (English-medium, ages 4–18, founded 2015) and **John Dewey School** (IB PYP, primary) [S7]. French curriculum: **sources conflict**. Expat.com's Sousse guide places the AEFE schools in Tunis [S7], but Expat.com's general Tunisia guide lists a Lycée Philippe-Séguin in Sousse among AEFE schools [S6]. Check the AEFE directory before relying on either. University hospitals (Sahloul, Farhat Hached) **[unverified]**.
- **Safety**: yellow zone. France Diplomatie's terrorism section lists past attacks in Sousse in 2015 and in September 2020. No Sousse-specific crime warning (2026-09-15) [S4].
- **Social life & vibe**: resort town with seasonal bustle, a beach-centred life, and many restaurants closed by day during Ramadan [S7]. Around 10,000–15,000 expats, mainly French, Italian, German, British and Russian (Expat.com estimate) [S7].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com Sousse forum (~1,842 members, 373 threads as of Dec 2025) [S7].
- **Climate in practice**: Mediterranean, hot summers and mild winters **[unverified specifics]**.
- **Watch out**: fewer schools, limited jobs, rent paid upfront, and a strong seasonal swing [S7].

### Sfax (additional city: second economy, not a lifestyle pick)

- Why it matters: Tunisia's second industrial city. It processes phosphate, olives and nuts, and produces about 40% of the country's olive oil. It is the largest fishing port and the University of Sfax is there. Population ~342,000 (2022) [S15]. Hot semi-arid climate with about half Tunis's rain (217 mm) [S15]. A Maison de France with a cultural programme [S11].
- **Watch out**: France Diplomatie reported racist assaults in Sfax (2026-09-15) [S4]. Sfax was the focal point of the 2023 tensions with migrants **[unverified detail]**. **Not recommended for Black diaspora newcomers without a strong local network.**

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are editorial readings of documented conditions. They are not measurements, and no quiz question measures a trait.

**Tunis**
- Openness: cosmopolitan seaside suburbs (La Marsa, Carthage, Sidi Bou Saïd) and a bilingual Arabic/French professional life [S6] suit people curious about a mixed Mediterranean–Arab setting.
- Conscientiousness: residence cards, Interior Ministry authorisations, school places via AEFE and annual leases [S6] reward people who are organised and patient with paperwork.
- Extraversion: hiring through networks [S7] and café and suburb social life favour people who build relationships actively.
- Agreeableness: discretion about politics matters. Speech offences are prosecuted under Decree-Law 54 [S9].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): spontaneous protests and curfews [S4], summer heat up to 49 °C [S12], summer power issues [S1] and, for Black residents, the risk of racist incidents [S4][S9] demand a high tolerance for stress.

**Sousse**
- Openness: less varied than Tunis, with a resort and tourism culture [S7].
- Conscientiousness: rent is often paid a year upfront and payments are cash-based [S7], so it suits planners with savings.
- Extraversion: lively in summer, quieter off-season (editorial inference from its tourism profile [S7]).
- Agreeableness / Emotional stability: a calmer pace than the capital, but a thin job market [S7] means it suits remote workers or retirees more than job-seekers.

Academic cultural frameworks: Hofstede and GLOBE data for Tunisia were not reached in this pass. Afrobarometer Dispatch AD1196 (2026-06-24), "Immigration in Tunisia: Tolerance tempered by economic fears", is directly relevant to how foreigners are received [S13].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: Article 230 of the Penal Code punishes consensual same-sex relations with up to 3 years in prison (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-15) [S4]. Forced anal tests were officially renounced in 2017 but were reported still in use in 2018–2019. Association Shams counted 127 arrests in 2018. Advocacy groups include Shams, Mawjoudin and Damj (Wikipedia, figures to 2020) [S16]. Post-2020 case data: not found.
- **Racism / treatment of Black Africans**: President Saied's February 2023 speech against sub-Saharan migrants triggered "a wave of violent, xenophobic attacks". In May 2024, camps in Tunis were raided: at least 80 people were arrested and more than 400 expelled to the borders. Collective expulsions to desert border areas continued. The anti-racism activist Saadia Mosbah (Mnemty) was arrested on 2024-05-06 (HRW World Report 2025) [S9]. France Diplomatie still reported racist assaults in Grand Tunis, Sfax and Gabès (2026-09-15) [S4]. Afrobarometer (2026-06-24) finds tolerance tempered by economic fears [S13]. **This is the single most important caveat for Là-bas's audience.** Black French or EU passport holders are not the same legal category as irregular migrants, but street-level racism does not check passports. Treat this as a real risk.
- **Conflict zones**: red zone (formally advised against) covers the far south near the Libyan and Algerian borders (a military zone) and the western mountains (Chaambi, Orbata, Selloum, Semema, Mghila). Orange covers the desert south and east of the Bir Rijm Maatoug–Borj Bourguiba–Ben Guerdane line, rural Kasserine, Sbeitla, Sidi Bouzid and Gafsa, and within 5 km of the Algerian border in Jendouba and Le Kef (France Diplomatie, zones updated 2026-03-13) [S4]. The FCDO is broadly aligned, citing cross-border terrorist activity (2026-07-24) [S1]. Tunis and Sousse are not affected.
- **Political freedoms**: Decree-Law 54 (2022) has been used to jail journalists, lawyers and social-media influencers. Saied was re-elected on 2024-10-06 with 90.69% of the vote (HRW World Report 2025) [S9].
- **Women's daily experience**: European women, alone or accompanied, may face persistent unwanted attention up to harassment (France Diplomatie, 2026-09-15) [S4]. Afrobarometer Round 10: 77% say women should have the same chances as men to run for office [S13]. Tunisia's comparatively progressive personal-status code **[unverified, not sourced in this pass]**.
- **Drugs**: even small quantities are severely punished [S4].
