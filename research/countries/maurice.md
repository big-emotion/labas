## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages actually used**: Mauritian Creole is the lingua franca. It is the home language of
  90.0% of people, against 4.4% for French, 5.1% for Bhojpuri and 0.6% for English (2022
  census) [7]. French dominates the media, and English is the language of government, public
  schools and Parliament [7]. A francophone can live day to day in French and Creole. Official
  paperwork comes in English.
- **Currency**: Mauritian rupee (Rs / MUR). Numbeo's figures imply about 52.5 MUR per EUR
  (November 2026) [3]. The Bank of Mauritius raised its key rate to 5% on 11 Nov 2026 to fight
  inflation [9]. A local economist expects inflation to reach 5.6% in 2026 [10].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**:
  - No visa for a stay of under 3 months. Immigration decides the length granted and can shorten
    it. A return ticket is required, and proof of accommodation and of funds (USD 100 a day) may
    be asked for. Work, internships or stays over 3 months need prior steps (France Diplomatie,
    15 Sep 2026) [19]. British passport holders get up to 60 days (FCDO, seen Nov 2026) [20].
  - **Premium Visa**: up to 1 year, renewable and free of charge. Requirements: remote work for
    a foreign employer or foreign clients, no local job, at least USD 1,500/month (+ USD 1,500
    per extra adult, + USD 500 per child), health insurance. Applications go through the EDB
    e-licensing platform (Wise, 17 Dec 2025) [5].
  - Other routes listed by EDB: residence through a real-estate investment, and a 10-year
    residence permit for retirees [6].
  - Occupation Permit (work and residence) rules were tightened in 2025. The Young Professional
    Permit now needs a basic salary of at least MUR 25,000/month. Investor permits are reviewed
    in year 5 against the income the activity generates. Permanent residence now needs 5 years
    on a permit instead of 3 (Fragomen, 12 Sep 2025) [21]. The minimum salary for the standard
    Professional permit is reported as MUR 30,000 or MUR 50,000 depending on the source: not
    confirmed against EDB guidelines (unverified).
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**:
  - Dual nationality has been allowed since 1995 for Mauritians "of origin" (not for naturalised
    citizens) [8].
  - Citizenship by descent covers one generation only. A child born abroad to a Mauritian parent
    who was born in Mauritius gets citizenship, but that child cannot pass it on [8].
  - Naturalisation normally needs 6 years of residence, or 4 years for the spouse of a
    Mauritian [8].
  - No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found. The "African diaspora" audience of
    Là-bas mostly has no Mauritian roots, so the Premium Visa or a work permit is the realistic
    route for most of them.

## Cities

### Port-Louis

- **Cost of living**:
  - 1-bed flat: Rs 24,300/month in the centre (≈ €460), Rs 10,750 outside the centre (≈ €205).
  - Cheap meal: Rs 250 (≈ €4.80).
  - Monthly costs for one person, excluding rent: ≈ €493 (Numbeo, 12 Nov 2026) [3].
  - Most middle-class and expat households live on the Plaines Wilhems plateau (Quatre Bornes,
    Rose Hill, Ebène, Moka) or on the coast, and commute into the capital. Neighbourhood rents
    were not found.
- **Work & money**:
  - Port-Louis is the financial and political capital: Stock Exchange of Mauritius, 10+
    commercial banks [12].
  - Main sectors: offshore/financial services, tourism (> USD 2 bn expected in 2024), ICT/BPO,
    textiles, sugar [11].
  - Average net salary in the country: ≈ Rs 37,300/month (≈ €710) (Numbeo, Nov 2026) [4].
  - Remote work is feasible: broadband 60 Mbps+ costs ≈ Rs 1,340/month [3].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - Turbine incubator offers ideation, incubation and acceleration. Its partner Seed Capital can
    invest up to Rs 5M in startups on the acceleration programme. Through Catalytic Africa,
    Turbine startups can get USD 20–60k grants once they have raised money from ABAN-registered
    business angels. Other partners: EDB, MRIC, MoAngels, Launch Africa, AfriLabs (Turbine site,
    seen 14 Nov 2026) [13].
  - EDB offers free advice on starting a business [6].
  - Crypto: the Virtual Asset and Initial Token Offering Services (VAITOS) Act 2021 came into
    force on 7 Feb 2022. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) licenses crypto service
    providers, and only companies incorporated in Mauritius with a real office there can apply
    (law-firm summaries, 2022) [22]. This is a legal framework, not evidence of a crypto scene;
    the FSC register itself was not read.
  - Company-registration delays: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Metro Express light rail runs Port-Louis Victoria → Rose Hill → Quatre Bornes → Curepipe
    (full line since Oct 2022). Fares were Rs 20–40 (2020–21 figures). Eligible seniors and
    disabled people ride free, and eligible pupils ride free on school days on their school
    route [14].
  - Daytime traffic is heavy with commuters [12]. A new traffic diversion plan for rue
    Monseigneur-Leen was announced in Nov 2026 [10].
  - Water stress: the Mare-aux-Vacoas reservoir stood at 33% on 13 Nov 2026, with a risk of
    falling to 20% [9].
  - Power: the electricity board (CEB) expects about 600 MW peak demand in the hot season
    (Nov 2026) [10]. Power-cut frequency was not found.
  - Ride-hailing apps: not found.
- **Health & schools**: Reference hospitals and clinics not found this session. The French
  embassy is in Moka [2]. French schools: not verified (AEFE listing did not load).
- **Safety**:
  - France Diplomatie (15 Sep 2026): the country is "globally safe" and the whole country is at
    normal vigilance. Assaults are rare but have been rising since 2025, can be violent and
    random, and hit locals and foreigners alike. Burglaries are frequent. Drug offences are
    punished very severely, and about twenty French nationals are in jail for trafficking [2].
  - UK FCDO (undated page, seen Nov 2026): petty crime is common in Port-Louis, Grand Baie and
    Flic en Flac. Avoid walking alone at night in the back streets of Port-Louis's business
    district [1].
  - **October 2026 unrest**: a police shooting on 9 Oct 2026 led to riots and road closures.
    Curfews covered most of the main island from 12 to 18 Oct, lifted at 4:30 am on 19 Oct
    [1][9]. Clashes at St-Hubert in Nov 2026 left
    two police officers injured [10]. The neighbourhoods named in local press are Camp-le-Vieux
    and St-Hubert [9][10].
  - Numbeo, Port-Louis (19 Jun 2026, 29 contributors, perception data):
    - Safety index 48.8.
    - Walking alone at night rated "low" safety (32.6), in daylight "high" (70.5).
    - Drug problems "high" (67.6), corruption "high" (65.7).
    - Worry about attacks for skin colour, ethnicity or religion "low" (21.3) [15].
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Caudan Waterfront has restaurants, shops and the country's largest casino [12].
  - Places of worship are everywhere (Hindu temples most common, plus Jummah Mosque and Saint
    James Cathedral) [12]. Religion is visible in daily life.
  - The city empties after office hours. This is a common expat observation, not verified with
    a dated source this session.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found (Facebook and WhatsApp groups not checked; search
  budget exhausted).
- **Climate in practice**: cyclone season runs Nov–Apr. Authorities can order a 2–3-day
  lockdown, so keep water, candles, batteries, a radio, gas and tins at home [2]. Nov 2026 is a
  drought period [9].
- **Watch out**: rising cost of living (rate hike, rice prices, inflation around 5.6%) [9][10];
  water shortages [9]; drug-related crime [9][15]; fresh social unrest since Oct 2026 [1][9];
  heavy traffic [10][12].

### Grand Baie

- **Cost of living**:
  - Rents not found: Numbeo has no Grand Baie page, and the local property portal blocked
    access.
  - Expect coastal tourist-zone prices above Port-Louis's "outside centre" rate. This is an
    inference, low confidence.
- **Work & money**: a tourism and services economy. A coastal village of about 11,900 people
  (2011 census) in Rivière du Rempart district [16]. Remote work is the realistic option
  (Premium Visa profile) [5].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: no local hub found. Ebène and Moka are the island's business
  hubs [17].
- **Daily infrastructure**: not served by the Metro Express [14], so a car is essentially
  needed (inference). Shopping at La Croisette mall, the island's third mall [16].
- **Health & schools**: not found this session.
- **Safety**: FCDO names Grand Baie among the petty-crime hotspots in tourist areas. It also
  reports sexual assaults on tourists and advises against walking alone on beaches at night
  [1]. France Diplomatie rates the whole country at normal vigilance [2].
- **Social life & vibe**: the north coast's beach and nightlife town, by common reputation. No
  dated source on nightlife was found [16]. Hindu and Catholic places of worship [16].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: same cyclone season (Nov–Apr) [2].
- **Watch out**: tourist-zone prices and petty crime [1]; car dependence; little professional
  network outside tourism.

### Ebène / Quatre Bornes (extra city, the working plateau)

- **Why it matters**: Ebène CyberCity is the planned "hi-tech office area" and IT hub, about
  15 km south of Port-Louis, at about 273 m altitude, which makes it cooler [17]. Quatre Bornes
  Central and Rose Hill are on the Metro line [14]. This is where most office jobs and many
  middle-class households are.
- **Cost of living**: national averages are Rs 22,500 (centre) and Rs 15,160 (outside) for a
  1-bed (Numbeo, Nov 2026) [4]. Neighbourhood figures not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: MRIC (research and innovation council) has a centre at Ebène
  [17]. MRIC is also a Turbine partner [13].
- **Transport**: Wikipedia says the Metro "has been expanded to Ebene" [17]. Le Matinal reported
  the Rose-Hill–Réduit branch (via Ebène) opening on 23 Jan 2023, with a Rs 35 adult fare; the
  article itself did not load, so treat the date as (unverified) [23]. A further extension to
  Côte d'Or was still at the funding/study stage in those sources.
- Safety, schools and community: not found (country-level advice applies [1][2]).

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This is an editorial reading of public facts. It does not measure anyone's personality, and
none of the quiz questions measures a trait. No Hofstede, GLOBE or Afrobarometer personality or
culture data for Mauritius was found this session.

**Port-Louis / Ebène plateau**
- Openness: suits people at ease in a place where several languages and religions sit side by
  side. Creole, French and English are used every day, and temples, mosques and churches stand
  next to each other [7][12].
- Conscientiousness: suits planners. English paperwork, permit thresholds and cyclone lockdowns
  of 2–3 days reward people who prepare [2][5][7].
- Extraversion: a more moderate pull. Social life centres on the Waterfront and on homes, and
  the capital is above all a place of work [12].
- Agreeableness: a small island with dense community and religious life favours tact and
  discretion. Interpretive.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): useful given the heavy traffic, the water shortages
  and the October 2026 unrest [1][9][10].

**Grand Baie**
- Openness and Extraversion: suits people who like a tourist and beach setting with lots of
  foreigners around [1][16].
- Conscientiousness: lower everyday pressure if you work remotely [5]. You still need to plan
  for car dependence and cyclones [2].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): likely a calmer daily pace than the capital
  (interpretive, unsourced), but petty crime in tourist areas calls for basic vigilance [1].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex sexual activity has been legal since 4 Oct 2023: the Supreme Court struck down
    Section 250 of the Criminal Code [18].
  - The Equal Opportunities Act 2008 bans discrimination on sexual orientation. There is no
    same-sex marriage or civil union, and no legal change of gender [18].
  - Collectif Arc-en-Ciel organises Pride. The 2018 march was cancelled after death threats
    [18].
  - FCDO: the country is relatively conservative, and LGBT+ people still face discrimination
    and possible confrontation [1].
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of foreigners**:
  - No reliable dated report was found this session.
  - Numbeo perception data (Jun 2026, 29 contributors, weak sample) rates worry about attacks for
    skin colour or ethnicity as "low" [15].
  - Mauritian politics and society are widely described as organised around communities
    (Hindu, Creole, Muslim, Sino-Mauritian, Franco-Mauritian); no dated source was checked for
    this (unverified). How Black African diaspora newcomers are received should be researched
    further. Not found.
- **Conflict / unrest**: there is no armed conflict. The police shooting of 9 Oct 2026 was
  followed by unrest, island-wide curfews (12/13–18 Oct 2026) [1][9], further violence at
  St-Hubert (Nov 2026) and trials of young people after the disturbances [10].
- **Women's daily experience**:
  - FCDO reports sexual assaults on tourists and advises against walking alone at night [1].
  - Local press (12 Nov 2026) reported a WhatsApp group sharing intimate photos of women [9].
  - No dedicated report found.
- **Drugs**: zero tolerance, with sentences of up to 20 years and no reduction, per France
  Diplomatie (15 Sep 2026) [2]. A boat carrying more than a tonne of drugs was seized
  in Nov 2026 [9].
