## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: English is the official language. Juba Arabic is the most widely spoken
  lingua franca, with about 250,000 first-language and about 1.2 million second-language speakers.
  People in Wau and Malakal use a form closer to Sudanese Arabic rather than Juba Arabic [10].
  Local languages (Dinka, Nuer, Bari, Luo, Fertit groups and others) are used at home [9].
  French is effectively absent, and we found no francophone institutions (not found).
- **Currency**: South Sudanese pound (SSP). Cash only in practice. ATMs are unreliable and dispense
  only SSP. Cards work only in big hotels and a few supermarkets. US dollar notes printed before 2006,
  or marked in any way, are refused [3]. On 20 August 2024 the commercial rate was 2,290.94 SSP per US$1
  and the parallel-market rate was 4,850 [12]. We found no 2025–2026 rate. Inflation was about 90 %
  in 2024, and 2025 figures conflict (65.7 % forecast vs about 102 %) [11].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**: a visa is required, through the eVisa portal (evisa.gov.ss) or an
  embassy. Extensions are handled in person at the Ministry of the Interior in Juba. Work permits
  come from the Ministry of Labour. You need a passport valid 6 months with 2 blank pages and a
  yellow-fever certificate. The FCDO reports travellers "harassed, detained or expelled" at Juba
  airport over visas, yellow-fever cards and work permits [5] (FCDO, page updated 2026-08-25).
  France has no visible residence route for non-NGO and non-diplomatic profiles (not found).
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**: the Nationality Act of 7 July 2011 grants nationality by
  descent to anyone with a parent, grandparent or great-grandparent born in South Sudan, or who
  belongs to an indigenous South Sudanese ethnic group. **Dual nationality has been allowed since
  2011** [8]. Canada confirms that dual citizenship is legally recognised but warns that dual
  nationals may get limited consular help and face different exit rules [3] (2026-09-28).
  Naturalisation needs 10 years of residence, or 5 for the spouse of a national [8]. We found no
  diaspora visa or return programme (not found).
- **Political context**: First Vice-President Riek Machar was placed under house arrest on 2025-03-26.
  He was charged with treason, murder and crimes against humanity on 2025-09-11 and tried from
  2025-09-22 [13]. The UK told Britons to leave in March 2025 [14]. Elections are legally set for
  2026-12-22, but on 2026-11-14 Radio Tamazuj reported that the electoral commission pushed them
  "beyond December deadline set in law" [15][26].

## Cities

### Juba

- **Cost of living**: Numbeo blocked our fetches, so we found no rent figure (not found).
  Expat.com estimates about US$1,200/month for a single person and US$3,500 for a family of four
  (country-wide, undated) [17]. Neighbourhoods named in sources include Hai Malakal, Munuki, Gurei,
  Hai Jalaba, Kator and Nyakurun [9][18]. We found no source for expat-area rents. Prices are mostly
  set in US dollars, and inflation is very high [11][12].
- **Work & money**: the economy is "one of the most oil dependent in the world" (about 80 % of GDP) [11].
  Expat work is overwhelmingly NGO and humanitarian work. Expat.com estimates about 30,000 expats,
  mostly from Uganda, Kenya, Ethiopia, Eritrea and Sudan [17]. Kenyan banks (KCB, Equity) and
  South Sudanese banks are based here [9]. Remote work is hard, because connectivity is unreliable
  and there are no fixed telephone lines [3]. We found no salary data (not found).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: we could not verify any hub or incubator because search was
  unavailable (not found). Starlink got landing rights and a provisional licence in mid-2024. Its
  approved tariffs run from US$38.19 to US$5,005.40/month, sold only through licensed agents [19].
  We found no data on crypto or fintech adoption.
- **Daily infrastructure**: most cities lack grid electricity and running water [11]. A US$267 million
  AfDB-backed project (announced 2026-10-29) will extend Ugandan power from Nimule to Juba, with no
  timeline given [20]. Water comes largely from tankers and private plants. In November 2026 the
  council shut one plant over untreated water [21]. Only about 13–16 % of the population is
  online (2025). Mobile operators are MTN (about 1.7 million subscribers), Zain (about 1.05 million)
  and Digitel [19]. Fuel shortages are more frequent [3]. Taxis are old and scarce, and Canada and
  the UK both call public transport and boda-bodas unsafe [3][6]. We found no ride-hailing app.
- **Health & schools**: Juba Teaching Hospital (with Chinese medical teams), Juba Military Referral
  Hospital and Al Sabah Children's Hospital [9]. Canada calls facilities "inadequate", so serious
  cases need evacuation, usually to Nairobi or Kampala, and the airport closes after dark [3][22].
  Malaria risk is high, and there was a cholera outbreak (date not given) [22]. Universities:
  University of Juba, Catholic University of South Sudan and others [9]. We found no French or
  international school (not found).
- **Safety**: FCDO advises against all travel (2026-08-25) [1]. France Diplomatie puts Juba in the
  orange zone: go only for a compelling reason. Residents should avoid trips on foot and keep a
  week's stock of food and medicine (2026-09-18) [2]. Canada says "avoid all travel" and to leave
  if possible (2026-09-28) [3]. Eye Radio reported on 2026-11-09 that the US advisory is Level 4
  "Do Not Travel" [4]. Canada says violent crime incidences are "especially high in Juba, which has
  seen regular outbreaks of violence and lawlessness" (2026-09-28) [3]. Crime
  peaks around Christmas, New Year and Easter [3]. Armed checkpoints at night demand money, and
  women are especially targeted [6]. There were sporadic gunfire and looting in parts of Juba on
  16–17 January 2025 [6]. We found no neighbourhood-level safety nuance (not found).
- **Social life & vibe**: Hai Malakal tea spots were shut in October 2026 over alleged drug sales
  [18]. Juba Stadium is the city's sports venue [9]. Daily life is shaped by conservative codes:
  Canada advises conservative dress for women [3]. We could not verify nightlife or music scenes
  (not found).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com forum threads such as "Life in Juba" and "Few ideas about
  living in Juba" (undated) [17]. We found no returnee association or public Facebook group (not
  found).
- **Climate in practice**: hot all year. Rains fall from April to October and the drier season runs
  from November to March, with about 1,000 mm a year [9]. During the rains, roads outside Juba are
  cut off and flights can be grounded for hours [6][22].
- **Watch out**: armed crime, political tension around delayed elections, cash-only living with a large
  gap between official and parallel exchange rates, and very little medical care. The UK and
  Canada offer no in-person consular help [1][3].

### Wau

- **Cost of living**: not found.
- **Work & money**: Wau is a trade hub between Darfur, Bahr el Ghazal and Equatoria, with bank branches,
  a cattle market and an airport with a 2,500 m runway. It is the capital of Western Bahr el Ghazal,
  about 650 km north-west of Juba, with about 320,752 inhabitants (2022) [7].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: there are flights to Juba [9]. We found nothing on power or water
  specific to Wau (not found).
- **Health & schools**: the University of Bahr el Ghazal and a campus of the Catholic University of
  South Sudan [7]. We found no hospital data (not found).
- **Safety**: France Diplomatie lists Wau (spelled "Way" on the page) in the orange zone, with the rest of the
  country red (2026-09-18) [2]. In 2016 heavy clashes displaced much of the Fertit population. In April 2017
  there was a massacre of non-Dinka civilians, with up to 50 reported dead [7]. On 2026-11-12,
  residents of Bagari Payam fled an SSPDF deployment linked to a disarmament campaign; the paramount
  chief said homes, a health centre, a women's centre and the court were looted or vandalised [23]. SPLA-IO forces are also present in Wau County [23].
- **Social life & vibe**: a Catholic centre (St Mary's Cathedral, seat of the diocese). In 2016–18
  about 8,500 displaced people sheltered at the cathedral [7]. Mostly Luo and Fertit, with Dinka
  minorities [7].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: tropical savanna, with an annual mean of 27.8 °C. Rains run from May to
  October (about 1,075 mm a year), and March is the hottest month [7].
- **Watch out**: active military operations nearby (November 2026), and a recent history of ethnic
  violence [7][23].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These notes are editorial readings of a context. No city and no quiz question measures a personality
trait. We found no Hofstede, GLOBE or Afrobarometer data for South Sudan (not found).

**Juba**
- Openness: suits people at ease with a very new capital (independence 2011), mixing many ethnic
  groups and a regional expat workforce [9][17].
- Conscientiousness: rewards people who plan ahead, given the advice to keep a week of supplies,
  cash-only budgeting and strict paperwork at the airport [2][3][5].
- Extraversion: social life seems to run through work circles (NGOs, agencies) more than public
  nightlife, because of night-time insecurity [3][6] (interpretive).
- Agreeableness: helps at armed checkpoints and with conservative social codes [3][6].
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needs a very high tolerance for uncertainty. Airport
  closures, evacuation plans and political crises recur [1][3][14].

**Wau**: too little sourced material for a reading (not found).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: illegal. Penal Code 2008, art. 248 ("unnatural offences"): up to 10 years, a fine or both,
  according to the code as summarised by Wikipedia [24]. The FCDO says sex between men can bring up
  to 14 years (page updated 2026-08-25) [6], so the sources conflict. The US State Department (2022
  report, via [24]) says most openly LGBTQI+ citizens have fled and that security forces harass and
  sometimes arrest, detain or beat them; it also notes no known enforcement of the criminal law. Canada reports frequent
  harassment (2026-09-28) [3].
- **Treatment of foreigners / diaspora**: "foreigners have been victims of many crimes, including rape
  and robbery" (Canada, 2026-09-28) [3]. Security services have harassed, detained and expelled
  foreign nationals, including aid workers and journalists, at Juba airport (FCDO, 2026-08-25) [5]. We found no specific report
  on colourism or on how returnees are treated (not found).
- **Conflict zones**: France puts every area outside Juba, Aweil, Wau and Yambio in the red zone
  (2026-09-18) [2]. There was fighting in Nasir (4 March 2025) [6]. In the Abiemnom massacre (Ruweng, 1 March 2026)
  more than 200 people were killed, according to an Amnesty International report published on
  2026-11-11; the government figure is 169 [28][29]. On 2026-11-13 Radio Tamazuj reported two people
  killed in an attack on SPLM delegates in Western Equatoria (Mundri) [27]. Landmines remain in several states [6].
- **Women's daily experience**: armed checkpoints abuse women in particular (FCDO, 2026-08-25) [6],
  and conservative dress is expected (Canada, 2026-09-28) [3]. Radio Tamazuj reports a forced-marriage killing in Bor (2026-11-11) [16].
