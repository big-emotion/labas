## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages actually used**: Seychellois Creole (Kreol Seselwa) is the language of daily life. About 91% of people speak it natively, against 5.1% for English and 0.7% for French. English dominates business and administration: nearly all official websites are in English, and laws are published in English, while parliament debates in Creole. French is official but rarely anyone's first language. Creole is French-based, so a French speaker can follow some of it quickly [S9]. *Editorial note:* francophones can manage socially, but working life needs English.
- **Currency**: Seychellois rupee (SCR) [S9]. Expat.com's 2025 figures imply about 16 SCR to 1 EUR (11,500 SCR is given as about 715 EUR) [S3]. All EUR conversions below use that rate.
- **Economy**: Africa's highest nominal GDP per capita (about USD 21,940 nominal in the 2026 estimate) and highest Human Development Index (0.848, 2023) [S9]. The flip side is a very expensive island economy where most goods are imported.
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**:
  - A free Visitor's Permit is issued on arrival for 3 months. It can be extended in 3-month blocks up to 12 months, for a fee [S2, FCDO, undated page, accessed 2026-11-14].
  - An online Travel Authorisation must be obtained and paid for before travelling, up to 30 days before arrival. You also need a return or onward ticket, confirmed accommodation and enough funds [S2]. The FCDO page does not give the fee. Expat.com mentions a 10 EUR pre-registration fee (Nov 2025) [S3], but it does not clearly say this is the same Travel Authorisation, so treat the amount as unverified.
  - To **work**, you need a Gainful Occupation Permit (GOP), and you cannot apply for it yourself. The employer applies at least 10 weeks before your start date, must show that no qualified Seychellois candidate exists, and must lodge a bank guarantee of at least 20,000 SCR. The permit lasts 1 year and is renewable [S3, Nov 2025].
  - To **live without local employment**, the Residence Permit lasts 5 years and costs about 150,000 SCR (about 9,375 EUR) for the main applicant, plus a bank guarantee of at least 20,000 SCR. It gives no access to free public healthcare or social security [S3].
  - **Permanent residence** comes through the Investor route, from USD 1 million [S3].
  - Foreigners can buy property only in designated zones, usually from about USD 300,000 [S3].
  - The official GOP page on ics.gov.sc returned 404, so the details above could not be checked against the government source. Verify with the Immigration and Civil Status Department before acting.
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**:
  - Seychelles has allowed dual nationality since 1993, provided it is recorded with the government [S10].
  - A child born abroad to a Seychellois parent can be registered as a citizen after at least 2 years of residence [S10].
  - Former nationals who gave up citizenship for socio-economic reasons can be repatriated. They must re-establish residence and pass a citizenship test and a background check [S10].
  - **There is no diaspora visa or Afro-descendant right-of-return scheme.** For anyone without a Seychellois parent, naturalisation normally takes 15 years of residence [S10, Wikipedia summary of the Citizenship Act; check against the primary law].
  - *Editorial:* this is one of the least accessible African countries for diaspora members with no Seychellois family.

## Cities

### Victoria (Mahé)

- **Cost of living**:
  - **Rent**: studio or 1-bed on Mahé about 400–800 EUR a month (about 6,400–12,800 SCR); 2-bed about 800–1,500 EUR; 3-bed in Victoria about 900–1,800 EUR [S3, Nov 2025].
  - **Neighbourhoods**: Victoria centre, Mont Fleuri, Roche Caïman, Beau Vallon (beach life) and Anse Royale (quieter, cheaper). Eden Island, a gated marina with 24/7 security, starts around 2,000 EUR for a 2-room flat [S3, S13].
  - **Monthly budget**: about 1,077 EUR for a single person before health insurance (Numbeo data, June–Sept 2025, quoted by Expat.com). Expat.com advises at least 1,500 EUR net. A family of four needs about 3,100 EUR, or about 4,500 EUR with international school fees [S3].
  - **A meal**: not found. Numbeo returned HTTP 429.
  - Leases are usually annual, paid as first month plus a deposit [S3].
- **Work & money**:
  - Unemployment was 2.3% in Q2 2025, and the average net salary is about 11,500 SCR (about 715 EUR) [S3].
  - **Sectors**: tourism, tuna fishing and canning (Port Victoria), offshore financial services and the blue economy [S3, S9, S8].
  - **Remote work**: feasible on Mahé. Fibre runs at about 20–100 Mbps and costs about USD 65–130 a month [S13].
  - **Taxes**: foreign-source income is not taxed, and a tax treaty with France exists [S3, not checked against the treaty text].
    - The official Seychelles Revenue Commission schedule (rates in force from 1 June 2018, page accessed 2026-11-14) taxes monthly pay in four bands: 0–8,555.50 SCR, then 15% up to 10,000 SCR, then 20% up to 83,333 SCR, then 30% above. **The 0% band is for citizens only: non-citizens pay 15% from the first rupee** [S16].
    - Expat.com (Nov 2025) instead gives a 2025 tax-free threshold of 10,830 SCR and no 20% band [S3]. This could not be confirmed on the SRC site, so treat it as unverified and check with the SRC.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - **Investment agency**: the Seychelles Investment Board (Invest in Seychelles). Its key sectors are the blue economy, tourism, financial services and ICT. There are no foreign-exchange restrictions [S8].
  - **Registering a company**: submit the project to the Seychelles Investment Board, then register with the Seychelles Revenue Commission (SRC) within 28 days of starting activity [S3].
  - **Offshore business**: there is a large offshore company-registration industry, with an International Business Companies Act and a Financial Services Authority [S9]. An IBC Amendment Act was passed in 2025, and France removed Seychelles from its tax blacklist in May 2025 [S3].
  - **Startups and crypto**: no named startup hub or incubator was found. *Unverified context, to be sourced:* several global crypto exchanges are registered in Seychelles, but registration is not local adoption, and no source on local crypto use was found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - **Internet and mobile**: fibre is spreading in Mahé's urban areas. SIM cards come from Airtel and Cable & Wireless [S13].
  - **Transport**: public buses cost under USD 1.30 a trip but run limited schedules. A car is almost essential outside Victoria, from about USD 10,400 second-hand [S13]. Ride-hailing: not found.
  - Mahé roads are narrow and winding, and drink-driving is a problem. Heavy rain from November to March causes floods and landslides [S1].
  - **Power cuts, water, mobile money**: not found.
- **Health & schools**:
  - **Hospital**: Seychelles Hospital in Victoria (404 beds). Specialised care often means evacuation to Mauritius, India or South Africa [S3].
  - **Healthcare access**: free public care is for citizens and permanent residents only. Expats need private insurance, about 150–500 EUR a month [S3].
  - **Schools**: International School Seychelles (British, IGCSE and A-Levels) costs about 5,000–12,000 EUR a year. Independent School is a little cheaper. State schools are free from age 6 to 16 and teach in Creole, English and French. A French curriculum is possible through CNED distance learning [S3]. No French lycée was found.
- **Safety**:
  - The UK FCDO (accessed 2026-11-14) says crime is mainly non-violent: break-ins, burglaries, bag snatching, and car or boat break-ins. Be alert in **the back streets of Victoria**, **Beau Vallon** and isolated areas at night [S1].
  - Beau Vallon and some other beaches can have dangerous rip currents when the sea is rough, and lifeguards are limited. The FCDO also warns that criminals may target beaches and walking trails (accessed 2026-11-14) [S1].
  - Afrobarometer (9 Jul 2026): drug abuse ranks high among citizens' concerns, and confidence in the institutions fighting it is falling [S6].
  - The France Diplomatie page redirected to an unrelated page, and the US State Dept page returned 403, so both are not found.
- **Social life & vibe**:
  - Nightlife is modest: a few bars and clubs in Victoria and Beau Vallon playing Creole music, reggae and sega. The overall tone is relaxed and family-friendly [S13].
  - Sir Selwyn Selwyn-Clarke Market runs Monday to Saturday, and Beau Vallon has a market on Wednesday evenings [S13, S11].
  - Festivals: Victoria International Carnival (April), Festival Kreol (October), SUBIOS underwater festival (November) [S3, S13].
  - Food: Creole cooking built on fish, seafood, curries, breadfruit and coconut [S9].
  - Music and dance: sega, and moutya, which UNESCO listed as intangible heritage in 2021 [S9].
  - Religion: about 75% Christian (mostly Catholic, with Anglican, Baptist and Pentecostal churches), plus Hindu and Muslim minorities [S9, S11].
- **Diaspora & expat community**:
  - About 15,000–17,000 expats (the guide gives both figures) [S13].
  - The Expat.com Seychelles forum has about 6,200 members [S13].
  - Eden Island has the densest international community [S13].
  - Facebook/WhatsApp groups and returnee associations: not found (no searches were possible).
- **Climate in practice**: tropical rainforest climate. December–February is wettest and June–July driest, with about 2,300–2,450 mm of rain a year [S11]. Expect floods and landslides from November to March [S1].
- **Watch out**:
  - Very high cost of living against local salaries.
  - A work permit depends entirely on an employer.
  - No free healthcare for foreigners, and serious cases need medical evacuation.
  - Small, insular market.
  - Hard drugs are a visible social problem [S6].
  - Property purchase is restricted and expensive.

### Praslin

- **Cost of living**: Praslin-specific rents: not found. *Editorial:* expect less long-term stock than on Mahé and prices driven by tourism. Population is about 7,500 (2014). The main villages are Baie Sainte Anne (about 2,500 people), Grand' Anse and Anse Volbert (Côte d'Or) [S12].
- **Work & money**: almost entirely tourism (hotels and resorts) [S12]. Remote work: not found for Praslin. *Editorial:* plausible, but check fibre at each address.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found. *Editorial:* tourism micro-business at most.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Praslin Island Airport is 44 km from Mahé [S12]. The flight takes about 15 minutes and costs about USD 78 one way. The ferry takes about 1 hour and costs about USD 65, and can be cancelled in bad weather [S13].
  - Cat Cocos runs several Mahé–Praslin–La Digue sailings every day [S14].
  - Power, water and ride-hailing: not found.
- **Health & schools**: Praslin's hospital or clinic: not found. Serious cases go to Mahé or abroad (inferred from [S3]). No international school was found on Praslin.
- **Safety**: no Praslin-specific advice found. The FCDO's general Seychelles advice applies [S1].
- **Social life & vibe**: very quiet island life centred on nature and beaches. Nearby are the Vallée de Mai nature reserve (UNESCO-listed coco de mer forest), Anse Lazio and Anse Georgette [S12]. Nightlife: not found, likely limited to hotels (editorial).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: same pattern as Mahé [S11].
- **Watch out**: isolation, dependence on boats and flights for everything, and a narrow job market.

### La Digue (extra, optional)

- Why it matters: the third inhabited island, a car-free life where people get around by bicycle and ox cart. It suits the "slow island life" profile [S13].
- Internet is sometimes unreliable [S13]. Everything else (rent, health, schools): not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These describe who tends to feel at ease, based on the facts above. They are not measurements of any person or place.

- **Victoria/Mahé**
  - Openness: suits people curious about Creole culture and its mix of French, African, Indian and Chinese influences [S9]. The cultural offer stays small: festivals rather than a constant scene [S3].
  - Conscientiousness: rewards people who plan. Permits are tied to an employer, you need to arrange insurance, and buses run limited schedules [S3, S13].
  - Extraversion: modest nightlife [S13]. Outgoing people may find the scene thin, while calm types will like it.
  - Agreeableness: a small society where reputation travels fast, and life is described as relaxed and family-friendly [S13].
  - Emotional stability: the reported crime is mostly non-violent [S1, accessed 2026-11-14], but daily life asks you to cope with high prices, medical evacuation risk and island isolation.
- **Praslin / La Digue**: suits people who are comfortable with solitude, slow time, nature, and deep dependence on transport links [S12, S13].
- No regional Hofstede or GLOBE score was found for Seychelles. Afrobarometer covers public opinion (drugs, media, climate) [S6], not personality.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex activity has been legal since 7 June 2016 (bill passed 18 May 2016) [S5, accessed 2026-11-14].
  - Discrimination at work on grounds of sexual orientation has been banned since 2006 [S5].
  - Same-sex marriage and unions are not recognised [S1, S5].
  - The FCDO says "attitudes vary" (accessed 2026-11-14) [S1].
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of foreigners**: no reliable source found. Expat.com calls it a "multicultural society" (Nov 2025) [S13], but that is an expat-guide claim, not evidence.
- **Conflict**: no conflict zones. The FCDO notes no recent history of terrorism [S1]. The October 2025 presidential election saw a change of power: Patrick Herminie (United Seychelles) won the run-off, held 9–11 October 2025, with 52.73% against 47.27% for the incumbent Wavel Ramkalawan [S7, accessed 2026-11-14]. The source reports protests over the lease of Assumption Island to a hotel developer but no election violence; whether the vote was peaceful is not stated.
- **Women**: the FCDO has no specific advice for women (accessed 2026-11-14) [S1]. Afrobarometer Round 9 (survey 2022): 93% support women standing for office on equal terms [S6]. Data on street harassment: not found.
- **Drugs**: drug abuse is a top public concern, and trust in the institutions fighting it is falling (Afrobarometer, 9 Jul 2026) [S6].
