> Research dossier for Là-bas. Accessed 2026-11-14. Editorial research, not measured data.

> **Research limits for this dossier.** The shared web-search budget ran out after 2 searches, so this
> dossier is built mainly from about 25 pages fetched directly: official advice (UK FCDO, France
> Diplomatie), human-rights reports (HRW, Freedom House, Human Dignity Trust), DataReportal,
> Expat.com and Wikipedia/Wikivoyage. Numbeo returned HTTP 429, and BBC, RFI and the US Embassy
> refused the fetch. No local lifestyle blog, rent listing or public social-media group could be

> **Editorial flag for the product.** As of November 2026 the UK FCDO advises against all but essential
> travel to the rest of Eritrea, Asmara included, and
> against all travel within 25 km of land borders. France Diplomatie puts Asmara and Massawa in a
> yellow "vigilance renforcée" zone, the rest of the country in orange and the borders in red
> [1][2][3]. Ethiopia–Eritrea diplomatic ties were cut on 1 Oct 2026 [17]. Là-bas should decide
> whether Eritrea's cities are flagged `unsafe` or kept with a strong caveat. Living there as a
> foreigner is also very constrained: permits to leave Asmara, no ATMs or cards, no mobile data and
> very little private enterprise.

## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** Tigrinya is the lingua franca in Asmara and the highlands. Arabic
  and English are the other working languages, with Tigre, Saho, Afar, Bilen and Kunama spoken
  regionally [5][13]. Italian survives mostly among older people and some urban business owners [13].
  French is practically absent: the French community numbered **10 people** in 2026 [5].
- **Religion:** France Diplomatie estimates Sunni Muslims at 50%, Orthodox at 30%, Catholics at 13%
  and others at 7% [5]. Unrecognised groups such as Jehovah's Witnesses and Pentecostals face arrests:
  HRW counted 63 Witnesses imprisoned in 2024 [8].
- **Currency:** The currency is the nakfa (ERN). The official peg is about US$1 = 15 ERN, and France
  Diplomatie gives €1 = 17 ERN (Oct 2026) [5][13]. Wikivoyage reports a 2025 black-market rate of
  50–58 ERN per US$ and warns that using it brings serious legal risk [13]. The currency is
  non-convertible, and you may not take more than 1,000 ERN out of the country [4][6].
  **There are no ATMs, and cards are not accepted** [4]. Foreign money must be changed at Himbol,
  the state exchange bureau, and you may have to account for what you spent when leaving [6].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders:** A visa is required in advance from the Eritrean embassy in Paris,
  and none is issued on arrival except by prior arrangement. Processing takes 2–3 weeks [6]. A
  business visa needs an invitation from a resident and is rarely issued, so firms usually get a
  one-month tourist visa renewable once [7]. A *travel permit* is needed to go more than 25 km from
  Asmara or your place of residence [6]. All electronic devices must be declared on arrival, and the
  rules are "très strictement appliquées" [6]. No residence-permit or long-stay route was found in
  open sources: **not found**.
- **(b) Diaspora with Eritrean roots:**
  - Nationality passes by descent from at least one Eritrean parent. Dual nationality has broadly
    been tolerated since the 1992 Nationality Proclamation, but naturalised citizens must give up
    their other nationality [10].
  - **Key caveat:** Eritrean authorities treat dual nationals and people of Eritrean origin as
    Eritrean. They "may be subject to obligations including taxes and national service", and the
    British Embassy cannot help dual nationals in Eritrea [1]. Dual nationals entering on an Eritrean
    ID card need an exit permit from the Immigration Office in Asmara [6b].
  - National service is 18 months in law but has been "extended indefinitely" in practice since 1998
    (HRW, Jan 2025) [8].
  - The BTI 2026 country report (covering events to early 2025) says Eritreans abroad "must pay a 2%
    diaspora tax", often "collected through coercion" [26]. Several European governments have
    contested its collection. Check the current practice with the embassy before any return.
  - No right-of-return or diaspora visa scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found:
    **not found**.
- **Connectivity:** In January 2025 the country had 714,000 internet users (20%), 793,000 mobile
  connections (22%) and only 14,300 social-media identities (0.4%) [11]. The state operator EriTel is
  the only telecom infrastructure provider [12]. There is no 3G or 4G, SMS to and from abroad is
  impossible, and SIM cards are hard to get for non-residents [7].
- **Flights:** There have been no direct flights to Addis Ababa or Djibouti since Ethiopian Airlines
  was suspended in September 2024, and the land borders are closed [6].

## Cities

### Asmara

Asmara is the capital, at 2,325 m, with about 1.07 million people (2023 estimate). Its modernist
centre has been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2017 [14].

- **Cost of living:**
  - **Rent:** 1-bed rent figures were **not found** (Numbeo blocked, no listings found).
  - **Monthly budget:** Expat.com gives roughly US$800 for one person and US$2,200 for a family of
    four, undated [9].
  - **Short stays:** Budget travellers manage on under US$50 a day. The Intercontinental costs about
    US$150 or more a night [13].
  - **Neighbourhoods:** Central Harnet Avenue, Tiravolo, Mai-Temenai, Abashawel, Paradiso, Gejeret and
    Arbaete Asmara [14][15]. No source says which ones expats live in.
  - **The real cost:** Prices depend on whether you use the official or the black-market rate. This is
    a legal minefield [13].
- **Work & money:**
  - Expat.com: "expats looking for a job here won't find many opportunities", and NGO or volunteer
    roles are the realistic option [9].
  - The main foreign employers are embassies, UN agencies and mining. Detailed sector data was **not
    found** (CIA Factbook discontinued in Feb 2026 [16]).
  - **Remote work:** effectively not feasible. Internet is limited to hotels and cafés at slow speeds
    [1b][7].
  - **Salaries:** not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - Freedom House scores property rights and private business freedom at 0/4 (2025) [18].
  - No startup hubs, incubators, fintech or crypto activity were found.
  - No investor presence was found, and AFD is not active in the country [7].
  - Company registration process: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - **Internet and phones:** Internet cafés cost about 20 ERN an hour and are slow. The Asmara Palace
    Hotel charges about 100 ERN an hour [15]. YouTube and diaspora sites critical of the government
    are blocked [12].
  - **Money:** no mobile money was found. Everything is paid in cash [4].
  - **Transport:** Red city buses cost about 2 ERN and stop around 19:00. Shared yellow taxis cost
    about 5 ERN, and contract taxis start at about 70 ERN [15]. There is no ride-hailing app.
  - **Power cuts and water:** no dated source found. Drink bottled water [15].
- **Health & schools:**
  - **Medicines:** imported medicines are limited to the WHO essential list, and shortages occur [19].
  - **Evacuation:** insurance covering medical evacuation is "indispensable" [19].
  - **Hospitals:** none named in the sources.
  - **Schools:** the Asmara International Community School exists [14], but its site did not resolve.
    No French school was found [5].
- **Safety:**
  - FCDO (updated 9 Nov 2026): Asmara is "relatively safe", with "a risk of street crime, especially
    at night". Women may face harassment [1b].
  - France Diplomatie (zones updated 10 Jul 2026): Asmara is in the yellow zone, and theft is "très
    limitée" [3].
  - Wikivoyage calls the city "extremely safe" for petty crime (undated, community source) [15].
  - **The main risk is political rather than criminal.** Criticising the government can lead to
    indefinite detention [13]. Photographing official buildings is illegal [1b][4].
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - **Cafés and food:** an Italian-Eritrean café culture of espresso, gelato and pasta with berbere
    [14], with places like Bar Zilli, Cherhi café (Abashawel), Albergo Italia and Casa degli Italiani
    [15].
  - **Nightlife:** small. The Green Pub at the Asmara Palace holds discos Wednesday, Friday and
    Saturday, and Zara Bar is popular with expats. Some listings may be dated [15].
  - **Sport:** cycling is the national sport, and Asmara often hosts the start of the Tour of Eritrea
    [14].
  - **Pace:** the pace of life is slow. Gatherings of more than seven people outside official events
    are prohibited [8], so public social life is low-key.
- **Diaspora & expat community:**
  - The Expat.com Eritrea forum has 419 members and 12 topics [9].
  - About 10,000 Eritreans live in France (2025) [5].
  - The diaspora is politically polarised. HRW (Jan 2025) reports violent clashes abroad, including in
    the Netherlands, and "transnational repression" reported by the UN rapporteur [8].
  - No public returnee network was found.
- **Climate in practice:** Mild all year at altitude, with frost possible at night (record low
  −4.5 °C). About 60% of the ~518 mm of rain falls in July–August, and December–February is driest
  [14]. Altitude means cool evenings, so bring layers.
- **Watch out:**
  - Cash only, and no ATMs [4].
  - Permits are needed to leave the city [6].
  - No mobile data [7].
  - Diaspora members can be treated as Eritrean citizens, with tax and national-service obligations
    [1].
  - LGBT people face criminal penalties [20].
  - Diplomatic crisis with Ethiopia (Oct 2026) [17].

### Massaoua (Massawa)

Massawa is the Red Sea port, about 115 km from Asmara by road. It had about 53,000 people in 2012,
and the city was heavily damaged in 1990 [21].

- **Cost of living:**
  - **Rent:** not found.
  - **Hotels:** in Tualud, hotels include the Corallo, Central, Dahlak and Red Sea. At Gurgusum
    Beach there are the GB Hotel and the Hamassien [22]. Hotel prices are dated (≤US$65/night,
    2007) [13].
- **Work & money:**
  - The port exports copper and zinc from the Bisha mine and imports fuel and consumer goods.
    Traffic is irregular, from 0 to 10 ships a month [21].
  - There is fishing. No other employers were found.
  - Remote work: not feasible.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** A free-trade area on Batsi island is mentioned [22]. Otherwise nothing
  was found.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - **Getting there:** buses from Asmara leave before dusk [22]. A travel permit is required [3].
  - **Air and rail:** no scheduled flights at Massawa airport as of mid-2023 [22]. The railway is
    museum-only [22].
  - Internet and power: not found.
- **Health & schools:**
  - **Disease risk:** malaria and dengue are endemic on the coast and islands, including Massawa
    [19]. The peak mosquito season is November–February [22].
  - Hospitals and schools: not found.
- **Safety:**
  - France Diplomatie (10 Jul 2026): Massawa is in the yellow zone. The mountain road from Asmara is
    "dangerous, especially in the evening", and a permit is required [3].
  - Navigation in the Red Sea is discouraged (Nov 2026), and pleasure boaters should postpone [2][3].
  - FCDO: vessels have been impounded for entering Eritrean waters without permission [1b].
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - The old town on Batsi comes alive after dark, once the heat drops [22].
  - The food is grilled Red Sea fish in clay ovens, and *khobzen* (flatbread with butter and honey)
    [13][22].
  - The heritage is Ottoman, Hadhrami, Yemeni and Indian, with the 15th-century Sheikh Hanafi mosque
    [21].
  - The coast is mostly Muslim, so dress modestly [4].
- **Diaspora & expat community:** none found.
- **Climate in practice:** Hot desert climate. Highs average about 40 °C in June–August (July
  40.8 °C), and the annual mean is about 30 °C [21]. Summer heat makes daytime life very hard, and
  locals traditionally move to the highlands.
- **Watch out:** Extreme summer heat, malaria and dengue, a city still partly in ruins, very little
  economic activity, permit dependency, and Red Sea tensions [2][21].

### Kérén (Keren), extra city

Keren is the second-largest city, with about 120,000 people (2016). It sits at 1,590 m, 91 km
northwest of Asmara [23]. It matters as the main market town between the highlands and the
lowlands, with Tigrinya, Bilen and Tigre communities, a Saturday-style livestock and craft trade
culture (market details undated), and a hot semi-arid climate (above 33 °C March to mid-June)
[23]. It is outside the 25 km radius, so a permit is needed [6]. No data was found on rent, jobs or
services. Keren is mentioned for completeness and is **not** recommended for addition.

*Assab, the other port, was left out on purpose.* Wikivoyage reports that governments advise
against travel there [13], and it sits at the centre of Ethiopia's Red Sea access claims [17].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No Hofstede or GLOBE scores were found for Eritrea, and Eritrea is not in recent Afrobarometer
rounds as far as could be checked: **not found**. What follows is editorial interpretation of the
cited facts only. It does not measure anyone's personality.

**Asmara**

- **Openness:** People drawn to architecture, history and Italian-Eritrean café culture may enjoy the
  UNESCO modernist centre [14]. But access to outside information is very restricted, with blocked
  sites and almost no social media [11][12], which may frustrate people who need novelty and
  information.
- **Conscientiousness:** Life rewards people who plan carefully: permits, cash-only budgeting, device
  declarations and strict currency rules all have severe penalties [4][6].
- **Extraversion:** Nightlife is small, and gatherings of more than seven people are restricted [8][15].
  Quieter people who like long café sittings may adapt better than people looking for an energetic
  scene.
- **Agreeableness:** A low-key, courteous public life and political discretion are necessary.
  Criticism of the government is dangerous [13].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** Much is needed: isolation, surveillance concerns
  (calls possibly monitored [1b]), and regional crisis risk [17].

**Massawa**

- **Openness and Extraversion:** The evening street life of the old town and the Red Sea heritage
  may suit people who like outdoor evenings and old towns [22].
- **Emotional stability:** Tolerating extreme heat (about 40 °C) and a nearly idle local economy
  matters a lot [21].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:** Same-sex sexual activity is criminalised for men and women under Article 310 of the 2015
  Penal Code, with **5–7 years' imprisonment**. The US State Department's 2023 report says the law is
  "actively enforced", while Human Dignity Trust also notes limited accessible evidence of recent
  prosecutions (Human Dignity Trust, updated 11 Jan 2026) [20]. FCDO (Nov 2026) confirms a prison
  sentence is possible [1b]. There are no legal
  organisations and strong societal taboos [24]. France Diplomatie (9 Nov 2026) says homosexuality
  is illegal and "pas acceptée socialement" [4].
- **Political freedom:** Freedom House 2025 scores Eritrea 3/100, "Not Free". There have been no
  elections since 1993 and no independent media since 2001 [18][8]. France Diplomatie (Oct 2026)
  describes a one-party state with no parliament [5].
- **Treatment of diaspora:** People of Eritrean origin are treated as nationals, with possible tax
  and national-service obligations and no consular help for dual nationals (FCDO, Nov 2026) [1]. The
  UN rapporteur reported transnational repression of the diaspora in 2024 (HRW 2025) [8].
- **Treatment of foreigners / racism:** No reliable report on racism or colourism towards foreigners
  or Black diaspora visitors was found: **not found**.
- **Conflict:**
  - FCDO (Nov 2026): avoid all travel within 25 km of land borders, and "tensions between Eritrea
    and Ethiopia are high" [1].
  - France Diplomatie (4 Nov 2026): tensions are rising in the Red Sea and northern Ethiopia, and air
    travel may be disrupted [2].
  - Wikipedia (accessed 14 Nov 2026, secondary): Ethiopia closed its embassy on 1 Oct 2026 and
    Eritrea cut diplomatic ties. The US lifted sanctions on 18 Sep 2026 [17]. The same article notes a
    partial reopening at Zalambessa in June 2025, but France Diplomatie (9 Nov 2026) lists the land
    borders as closed [6][17]; treat them as closed. Mines are present near the borders [3].
- **Women's daily experience:**
  - Women may face verbal and physical harassment (FCDO, Nov 2026) [1b].
  - HRW 2025: 41% of girls are married by 18, female genital mutilation is highly prevalent, there is
    sexual violence against female conscripts, and maternal mortality is 322 per 100,000 [8].
  - Wikivoyage notes women's visible role in public life, and that women who look local face stricter
    dress and smoking expectations (undated community source) [13].
- **Religion:** Members of unrecognised churches are arrested. More than 200 Christians were
  arrested in the 12 months to July 2024 (Freedom House 2025) [18].
