## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day**: Sango is the national language and, together with French, an official language. Sango is the street, market and family language. French is used for administration, school and business [3][9].
- **Currency**: Central African CFA franc (XAF), pegged to the euro at 655.957 XAF = 1 EUR (CEMAC zone) [3].
- **Religion**: mostly Christian. The 2003 census gives 80.3% Christian and 10% Muslim, and a 2010 Pew estimate gives 89.8% and 8.9% [3]. Both figures are old.
- **Entry for French/EU passport holders**: a passport and a visa are compulsory, and a yellow fever vaccination certificate is checked on arrival. France Diplomatie asks for a passport valid at least 6 months after your planned *departure* and points to the CAR embassy in Paris (30, rue des Perchamps, 75016) [15] (updated 2026-09-15). The UK FCDO, writing for British citizens, also routes visa applications through the Paris embassy but counts the 6 months from *arrival* [2] (page current 2026-11-14); follow the stricter French rule. France Diplomatie advises non-resident French nationals against travel unless they are on an imperative professional mission in the capital [1] (updated 2026-09-10). No sourced information was found on a long-stay or residence permit procedure.
- **Diaspora with family roots**: under the 1961 Nationality Code, a child born anywhere to at least one native-born Central African parent is a national. **Dual nationality is generally not allowed**: the only exception is a foreign wife who marries a Central African and keeps her original nationality [10]. That source draws on Manby 2010, so it is old, and the rule should be re-checked against any newer code. There is no diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's: none was found. The 2023 constitution, adopted by referendum on 30 July 2023, bars dual nationals from standing for president, which shows the restriction is still alive in politics [18]; whether the 1961 code itself was amended was not checked.
- **Big picture**: the World Bank reports that about 67% of the population lives in extreme poverty (51% in cities) and that nearly nine in ten households lack electricity [8] (page undated, references 2026 data). Internet penetration was 15.5% in January 2025 [7]. MINUSCA's UN mandate was renewed to 15 November 2027 [6] (2026-11-13). Russian Wagner forces have backed President Touadéra and have been accused of abuses against civilians [3].

## Cities

### Bangui

Bangui is the capital, on the Oubangui river facing Zongo (DRC). The city proper had 812,407 inhabitants in 2021, and Grand Bangui, including Bimbo and Bégoua, had about 1.43 million [4][9]. It has 8 arrondissements and 205 quartiers [4].

- **Cost of living**: not found. Numbeo was rate-limited and no expat blog with prices could be reached. The working assumption, not sourced, is that imported goods and secure housing are expensive relative to local incomes, as in most CEMAC capitals. **Verify before any score is used.** On 2026-11-13 the opposition platform BRDC marched in Bangui "contre la vie chère", which shows how strongly the cost of living is felt locally [6].
- **Work & money**: Bangui is the administrative and commercial centre. Local industries are textiles, food, beer, shoes and soap [4]. National exports are diamonds (40–55% of export revenue), timber, cotton and coffee [3][9]. In practice, formal employers for a diaspora profile are the state, the UN (MINUSCA), NGOs and humanitarian agencies, mining firms and telecoms. This is inferred from the FCDO's mention of attacks on "UN peacekeepers, humanitarian workers and foreign mining companies" [2b], and no salary data was found. Remote work is hard because internet reaches 15.5% of the population [7].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: no named incubator or hub could be confirmed this session. CAR made Bitcoin legal tender in April 2022, but **parliament repealed that law unanimously on 23 March 2023** after pressure from its CEMAC partners, so crypto is no longer legal tender [16] (2023-03-27). The state-backed "Sango coin" (July 2022) sold poorly and its listing was postponed (unverified beyond secondary reports). President Touadéra announced a "$CAR" memecoin on X in February 2025 [17] (2025-02-11). None of this is an ecosystem a newcomer can plug into, and daily crypto use is very unlikely with 15.5% internet penetration [7].
- **Daily infrastructure**: 34.6% of urban residents had electricity access, according to the figure cited on Wikipedia [3], and the World Bank says nearly 90% of households nationally lack power [8]. There were about 2.07 million mobile connections (38.1% of the population) in January 2025 [7]. The operators on record are Telecel, Orange and Moov, plus Socatel, but that list is old [11]. Only toll roads are paved [4]. No ride-hailing app was found. Floods in late October 2026 killed 3 people in Bangui [6].
- **Health & schools**: public care is described as poor and minimal, and wealthier residents use private clinics [4]. A general hospital is on the city's eastern side [4]. The country had roughly 1 doctor per 20,000 people in 2009, and malaria is endemic [3]. France Diplomatie flagged a measles outbreak on 2026-03-13 (updated 2026-04-07) [1], and the FCDO added Ebola-outbreak information on 2026-05-28 after a declaration in neighbouring DRC [2]. The city has a French lycée, the Lycée français Charles-de-Gaulle, in the east of the city, opened in 1992 [9]. Its current AEFE status and fees are not verified, because the AEFE directory could not be read. The University of Bangui opened in 1969/1970 [4][9].
- **Safety**: France Diplomatie (2026-09-10) says that in Bangui you should keep to routine movements, stop for official convoys, and avoid walking on the slopes of the Bangui hill [1]. The FCDO (updated 2026-05-28, current 2026-11-14) advises against all but essential travel to Bangui, including the airport, Bimbo, Bégoua and Colline, and against all travel to the rest of the country. It describes theft and robbery as common and armed gangs as active in outlying districts such as **PK5**, and warns that the situation "could deteriorate rapidly" and that night travel should be avoided [2][2b]. It also notes that protests can start with little notice and have caused deaths [2b].
- **Social life & vibe**: most of the city's nightlife, and its largest market, sit in the residential heart about 5 km north of the centre [4]. Basketball is the most popular sport, and pirogue races are a big local event [9]. Church life (Catholic, Protestant, Baptist) is central, and mosques are also present [9]. Nightlife is restricted in practice by the advice to avoid travel at night [2b].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found. Expat.com's forum listing showed no active CAR threads [12]. The expat population is dominated by UN and NGO staff (inference).
- **Climate in practice**: tropical savanna. The rains run from March to November, with August the wettest month at about 218 mm [4]. Flooding happens in low-lying quartiers [6].
- **Watch out**: the security advisories, the very weak power grid, the very limited health care, the no-dual-nationality rule, the high cost of living relative to incomes, and the political tension around the 2025–2026 electoral cycle (residual elections were postponed to 25 October 2026 [6]).

### Berbérati

Berbérati is the third-largest city (105,155 inhabitants in the 2012/2013 census), in Mambéré-Kadéï prefecture, 516 km west of Bangui on RN6. Locals call it the "economic capital of the west" [5][13].

- **Cost of living**: not found.
- **Work & money**: it is the centre of the country's main diamond region. Diamond buyers' premises were looted in February 2014, and export networks are run mostly by Muslim traders [13]. Coffee and timber are probable but not sourced this session.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found. It is realistically marginal.
- **Daily infrastructure**: GSM coverage reached the town in June 2006 [11]. The town has an airport (BBT), and RN10 runs from it south to Nola and the Cameroon border [13]. Rural electricity access nationwide is 1.5% [3]. No local figure was found.
- **Health & schools**: the Hôpital Régional Universitaire de Berbérati is the reference hospital for health region 2 [13]. The English Wikipedia describes it as an unfenced complex of bungalows built in the 1950s [5]. Schools include Lycée Barthélemy Boganda and Saint Marcellin Champagnat [13]. No French or international school was found.
- **Safety**: it lies outside Bangui and Bimbo, so France Diplomatie advises formally against all travel there (2026-09-10) [1], and the FCDO advises against all travel there [2]. Outside the capital, the FCDO cites armed conflict, kidnapping and attacks on roads [2b].
- **Social life & vibe**: a small, church-centred town with mosques too [13]. Local media are Radio Zoukpana (105.9 FM) and Radio Ndeke Luka (100.9 FM) [13]. Its nickname is "Berbérati la flamboyante" [13].
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: tropical savanna, about 1,617 mm of rain a year and a mean daily maximum of about 31 °C [5].
- **Watch out**: it is in a no-go zone by both France and UK advice. Health care is limited, and the road to Bangui is long and exposed.

### Bimbo (additional, part of Grand Bangui)

Bimbo is the southern twin of Bangui within Grand Bangui [9], and France Diplomatie names it as the only area besides Bangui where travel is not formally advised against [1]. For someone with family there, it is the realistic alternative to central Bangui. No cost, infrastructure or social data was found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

This is about Bangui only, since it is the only city where settling can be discussed at all. It describes the kind of person who copes well with conditions there, not a measurement of anyone.

- Openness: suits people who are comfortable in a Sango-speaking environment where French is the language of offices, and who can live in a city of 205 quartiers with very few international-standard services [4][9].
- Conscientiousness: rewards planners. With fewer than 1 in 10 households on the grid nationally [8], you need generators, backup water and a health plan, and residence formalities need to be sorted in advance (visa via Paris [2]).
- Extraversion: social life is real (basketball, church, the northern quartier nightlife [4][9]), but advice against moving at night [2b] keeps it daytime and home-based, which suits people happy with smaller circles.
- Agreeableness: church and community networks are central [9], and political protests can flare up [2b]. Tact and staying out of politics help.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): this matters most. The situation "could deteriorate rapidly" [2b], health care is minimal [3][4], and there are floods and power gaps [6][8]. Only people with a high tolerance for uncertainty, usually on a job contract with employer support, settle comfortably.
- No academic regional personality data (Hofstede, GLOBE) for CAR was found. Afrobarometer has not been confirmed to cover CAR; this was not verified.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex sexual activity is legal, but the penal code punishes a "public expression of love" between people of the same sex with 6 months to 2 years in prison or a fine of 150,000–600,000 XAF [14], citing the US State Dept 2012 report, which is old. The 2016 constitution defined marriage as the union of one man and one woman [14]. The 2016 text was replaced by the 2023 constitution [18]; whether the new text keeps the same wording was not verified, so assume no legal recognition of same-sex couples. The FCDO (page current 2026-11-14) says LGBT+ travellers may be arrested for public displays of affection and that same-sex relationships are not widely accepted [2b].
- **Racism / treatment of diaspora and foreigners**: no reliable source was found. The FCDO notes that attacks have targeted UN peacekeepers, humanitarian workers and foreign mining companies [2b].
- **Conflict zones**: France advises against all travel outside Bangui and Bimbo (2026-09-10) [1]. The UK advises against all travel outside Bangui and all but essential travel to Bangui (2026-05-28) [2]. Improvised explosive devices have been reported in the north-west [2b]. There were armed attacks in Amdafock on 2026-06-30 and hostage-taking in Haut-Mbomou in August 2026 [6]. Inter-communal tensions affect the diamond trade, for example the 2014 looting of Muslim traders in Berbérati [13].
- **Women's daily experience**: no dedicated, dated source was found. The FCDO safety page has no women-specific section; its general advice is to travel in groups and not walk alone, especially at night [2b] (current 2026-11-14).
