## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** French and Arabic are the official languages. In the street, N'Djamena runs largely on *Chadian Arabic* (the Wikipedia N'Djamena page lists King Faisal University, a private Arabic-language university, beside the French-language University of N'Djamena [9]). In the south (Moundou, Sarh), Sara languages and French dominate (Sarh is named after the Sara people [13]). The detailed language split is editorial and not sourced here.
- **Currency:** Central African CFA franc (XAF), pegged at 1 EUR = 655.957 XAF (fixed peg, CEMAC zone).
- **Entry and residence for French/EU passport holders:** a visa is required. France and the EU are not on Chad's visa-exempt list. An e-Visa system launched on 11 Dec 2024 (evisa.td), and all nationalities must register with the police within 72 hours of arrival [16, page edited Oct 2026]. Travel outside N'Djamena needs a permit from the Ministry of Interior and Public Security, which can take several days (Canada travel advice, seen in search results [5]). Residence permits: details not found; Expat.com's guide mentions them only generically [20, 2018].
- **Diaspora with Chadian roots:** dual nationality has been allowed since the Nationality Code of 14 Aug 1962, except for presidential candidates [14]. Nationality by descent needs *both* parents Chadian. With one Chadian parent, the child gets it only if otherwise stateless, which is a real limit for mixed-heritage diaspora children. People of African origin who have lived 15 years in Chad and "assimilated" can acquire it [14]. **No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found** (nothing comparable to Ghana's or Benin's laws). About 5,000 Chadians lived in France as of 2019 [21].
- **France–Chad context:** France's official page (dated 4 Nov 2026) still lists 1,377 registered French residents (2019 figure), an AEFE-contracted school, and French-language centres in Abéché, Adré, Moundou, N'Djamena and Sarh [21]. Chad announced on 28 Nov 2024 that it was ending its defence and security cooperation agreement with France, and the last French air base (Sergent Adji Kossei, N'Djamena) was handed over from 31 Jan 2025 [25].

## Cities

### N'Djamena

Capital on the Chari river, across from Kousséri (Cameroon). Population: 721,081 (2022 estimate); Wikipedia gives about 1.7 million for the urban area, undated [9].

- **Cost of living:** rent for a 1-bedroom flat: **not found** (Numbeo blocked). The best-known residential areas for expatriates and the middle class, per Wikipedia, are Chagoua, Moursal and Paris Congo; the "Nassara Strip" is described as a commercial centre with some housing, and the colonial core along the Chari holds the government district [9]. An Expat.com forum thread asks about "Cité LAMY" [20]. Expat.com estimates a monthly budget of about **€1,100 for one person and €2,600 for a family of four** (≈ 720,000 / 1,705,000 XAF), but its guide was **last updated 27 Apr 2018**, so treat it as a floor [20]. Meal price: not found.
- **Work & money:** most residents work in agriculture-linked trades; meat, fish and cotton processing; regional trade in livestock, salt, dates and grain. Demand for skilled people exists in **oil and gas, NGOs, medical services and English teaching** [9]. The World Bank and the AfDB are major funders [9]. Remote work is technically hard: internet penetration was 13.2% in early 2025 [18]. Starlink, whose unlicensed use the regulator had denounced in Sept 2023 [17], received an operating licence in March 2025 and was declared operational nationwide on 4 Jul 2025, with plans at 12,000–32,000 XAF/month through ARCEP-approved resellers [26]; a ministerial order of Aug 2025 requires user identification (unverified, seen in search results only). Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** **WenakLabs**, which calls itself "le premier hub technologique du Tchad", was founded in 2014 at the Marché à Mil. It runs incubation, a FabLab, a MediaLab and the WS Academy, and its blog was active in Sept 2026 [19]. Fintech and crypto: no verified source found. Mobile money exists (Airtel Money, Moov Money), but no dated adoption figure was verified. Investors and company registration: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure:** 14.5 million mobile connections (69.8% of the population), 2.74 million internet users (13.2%), and 1.68 million social-media identities, all early 2025 [18]. There are no railways [9]. Direct flights go to Paris, Istanbul, Sharjah, Cairo, Addis Ababa, Douala, Abuja and Niamey [9]. Air quality: in a 2024 report the city ranked among the 20 most polluted cities in the world [9]. Power cuts and water: no dated source verified. There was a **cholera outbreak in N'Djamena in Sept 2026** (France Diplomatie [1]). Ride-hailing apps: not found. FCDO advises pre-arranged secure transport and no walking after dark [3]. Canada says walking is not recommended even in daytime [5].
- **Health & schools:** reference hospitals were not verified (the US advisory cites "inadequate health infrastructure" [6]). Schools: **Lycée Montaigne** (French international school, AEFE network), American International School of N'Djamena, George Washington International Academy, N'Djamena English International School and Wellsprings Academy. Their pupils are often children of executives, diplomats and NGO staff [9][21].
- **Safety:**
  - FCDO (updated 4 Nov 2026) advises against **all but essential travel**, N'Djamena included [2].
  - FCDO safety page: robbery is common in some residential areas, and foreigners are targeted because they are seen as wealthier. FCDO calls terrorist attacks "very likely" and lists past incidents: an armed clash in the capital in Feb 2024, a fatal shooting near the Presidential Palace in Jan 2025, and celebratory gunfire that has caused civilian casualties [3].
  - The US State Dept raised Chad to **Level 4 – Do Not Travel** on 28 Apr 2026. US government staff in N'Djamena must use armoured vehicles and face night-driving limits, and OSAC rates the city "critical" for crime (OSAC/State, seen in search results [6]).
  - Switzerland (21 Oct 2025) says demonstrations can break out at any time, especially in N'Djamena [4].
  - Neighbourhood-level nuance: not found in a reliable source.
- **Social life & vibe:** it was named Capital of Islamic Culture for 2009. Cultural venues include the Chad National Museum (with the Toumaï skull), the National Library, the Al-Mouna cultural centre and Notre-Dame de la Paix cathedral [9]. Religion weighs visibly on daily life. Nightlife and music scene: no dated source verified.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** the Expat.com Chad section has about 1,652 members, with new-member threads still appearing in 2026 [20]. No public Facebook or WhatsApp groups and no returnee network were verified.
- **Climate in practice:** among the hottest big cities on earth. **March–May is the hottest period**, and only August has average highs under 32 °C. The rains run June–September and peak in August (≈510 mm/year) [9]. In the rainy season roads become impassable and floods hit, especially in the south and east [3].
- **Watch out:** a Level-4 / "all but essential" travel rating means expatriate insurance may be void (FCDO [2]). Other downsides: a permit is needed to leave the capital [5], extreme heat, air pollution, cholera and measles alerts in 2026 [1], very low internet penetration (Starlink now licensed but costly [26]), and a political climate after the 2025 opposition conviction (Succès Masra was sentenced to 20 years and a 1 billion XAF fine on 9 Aug 2025 [15]).

### Moundou

Second city and "economic capital", 470 km south of N'Djamena on the Logone river; seat of Logone-Occidental [11]. Population: 137,929 (2012) [10].

- **Cost of living:** rent, meal and monthly budget: **not found**. The city has 4 arrondissements and 20 quartiers [11], but none were named in the sources.
- **Work & money:** the city's employers are the **CotonTchad** cotton ginning, oil and soap plants, the **Brasseries du Logone** (Gala beer) and the **Manufacture des Cigarettes du Tchad** [10][11]. Cotton was Chad's main export before oil [11]. A sesame producer–buyer project was reported on 13 Nov 2026 (Tchadinfos [22]). Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** no hub found. Remote work is not realistic.
- **Daily infrastructure:** Moundou airport (MQQ) has a paved runway, and the city sits on the southern road axis Léré–Pala–Kélo–Doba–Koumra–Sarh [10]. **Built-in flood risk:** the early city plan misjudged the slope, so Logone floodwater drains towards the city, and the mayor has said floods can "inonder entièrement" Moundou [11]. Power, water and ride-hailing: not found.
- **Health & schools:** Université de Moundou (2008) and Université catholique d'Afrique centrale (2022) [11]. There is a French-language centre (CALF) and an honorary French consulate [21]. Hospital: not found.
- **Safety:** FCDO puts the whole country, outside the red border zones, at "all but essential", and does not mention Moundou specifically [2]. **Logone-Occidental clashes on 14 May 2025 left 42 dead** and preceded the arrest of opposition leader Succès Masra [15]. Tchadinfos reported an attack in neighbouring **Logone Oriental on 13 Nov 2026** (headline only [22]). Roads in the south are often impassable July–October [3].
- **Social life & vibe:** the south is more Christian than the north (Catholic diocese, Evangelical Church of Chad), although Wikipedia lists mosques as the most numerous places of worship [10]. The local beer culture centres on Gala [10]. Pace: provincial. Nightlife sources: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** twinned with **Poitiers** since 1990 and with Shanghai since 2007 [11], which gives a possible link for French-based diaspora associations. No groups were verified.
- **Climate in practice:** tropical savanna. Rains run May–September, with August the wettest (≈285 mm), and November–March is almost dry [10][11]. It is greener and wetter than N'Djamena.
- **Watch out:** recurrent flooding, the 2025 political violence in the region, isolation in the rainy season, and very thin sourcing on daily services.

### Sarh (additional — third city, southern hub)

Population 103,269 (2012). Capital of Moyen-Chari, ≈563 km from N'Djamena [13]. The economy rests on cotton, fishing on the Chari and the central market, and the city has an airport (SRH) [13]. Education: University of Sarh and Saint Charles Lwanga University. It is twinned with Cherbourg-Octeville [13]. Rainfall is ≈969 mm/year, with March–May the hottest period [13]. It matters for Sara-heritage diaspora families. Rent, safety detail and services: not found.

### Abéché (additional — eastern hub)

Population 76,492 (2012). It is the main camel export market, and its regional market trades textiles, crafts and food [12]. It lies 164 km from Adré on the Sudan border [12]. The Sudan-war refugee and humanitarian presence is widely reported but **was not verified in an opened source**. The city was taken by rebels in Nov 2006 [12]. It is relevant mainly for NGO and humanitarian careers, not for family relocation.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These describe who tends to cope well. Neither a city nor a quiz question measures a trait. Chad is not covered by Hofstede's published country scores or by Afrobarometer survey rounds, as far as was found this session (**not verified**). No academic work on regional personality in Chad was found.

**N'Djamena**
- Openness: suits people curious about a Sahelian, Arabic-and-French, mostly Muslim capital (Capital of Islamic Culture 2009 [9]) and willing to live without a big nightlife or tech scene.
- Conscientiousness: it helps to be organised. Leaving the city needs permits [5], visas go through the e-Visa and police registration within 72 hours [16], and security rules (secure transport, no walking after dark [3]) structure every day.
- Extraversion: social life is largely private and runs through work networks (NGOs, embassies, oil). People who recharge at home cope better than people who need a lively street scene. This is editorial and inferred from the security restrictions [3][6].
- Agreeableness: discretion and respect for religious and social codes matter. The political climate is tense (2025 conviction of the main opposition leader [15]).
- Emotional stability: high stress tolerance is needed. Expect heat over 40 °C in spring [9], Level-4 / "all but essential" advisories [2][6], cholera and measles alerts [1], and occasional gunfire incidents [3].

**Moundou**
- Openness: suits people drawn to a provincial, agro-industrial, more Christian southern town (cotton, brewery [10]).
- Conscientiousness: you need to plan around the rainy season, when roads are impassable July–October [3] and floods recur [11].
- Extraversion: smaller and calmer than the capital. Few documented venues.
- Agreeableness: community and church life matter (Catholic and Evangelical presence [10]).
- Emotional stability: political violence in the region in 2025 (42 dead [15]) and flooding call for resilience.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:** same-sex relations have been criminalised since the Penal Code took effect on **1 Aug 2017**. Art. 354 sets 3 months to 2 years in prison and a fine of 50,000–500,000 XAF [8]. FCDO confirms this as of Nov 2026 [3]. There are no anti-discrimination protections and no recognition of couples [8].
- **Conflict zones:** FCDO (4 Nov 2026) advises against **all travel** to Borkou, Ennedi Ouest and Est, Tibesti, western Kanem (incl. Nokou), the Lake Chad region, and within 30 km of borders [2]. Boko Haram and ISWAP are active around Lake Chad, with an attack in May 2026 [3]. There is a high kidnapping risk near the Cameroon, Nigeria and Niger borders [3]. The US warns of minefields along the Libya and Sudan borders (Mar 2025 advisory, seen in results [6]).
- **Political:** Succès Masra was arrested on 16 May 2025 and sentenced on 9 Aug 2025 to 20 years in prison and a 1 billion XAF fine [15]. FCDO cites armed clashes in N'Djamena in Feb 2024 and Jan 2025 [3].
- **Treatment of foreigners and diaspora:** FCDO says robbers target foreigners because they are seen as wealthier [3]. No reliable report on racism or colourism was found.
- **Women's daily experience:** **not found** in a reliable dated source this session. FCDO has no Chad-specific section on women [2].
