## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages actually used**: Darija (Moroccan Arabic) on the street; French dominant in business,
  banking, administration paperwork and private schools; Tamazight (Amazigh) regionally; Spanish
  common in the north (Tanger); English growing among young urban professionals. Public schools teach
  mainly in Modern Standard Arabic and French [3]. French "helps" for residence paperwork [4].
- **Currency**: Moroccan dirham (MAD). Rough rate used here: 1 EUR ≈ 10.8 MAD (editorial
  approximation, not sourced. Check before publishing).
- **(a) French / EU passport holders**: visa-free stay up to 90 days. Morocco has no 90/180 rolling
  rule, and whether you get back in after a "border run" is up to the border officer [4] (Feb 2026; the source is
  written for US citizens, so confirm the French/EU rule on France Diplomatie's entry-formalities page, which was not re-opened here). To live there you need a
  *carte de séjour* from the police prefecture. It is renewable yearly, needs proof of accommodation, means/income, an apostilled
  criminal record, a medical certificate and a fee of about $30–50, and takes 1–3 months [4] (the source is US-oriented; check with the prefecture).
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**: Morocco has allowed dual nationality since 1958. Children
  born abroad to a Moroccan father are Moroccan at birth. Since Law 62-06 (in force 3 March 2007), a
  Moroccan mother can also pass on nationality, but **not retroactively** (children born before 2007
  are not covered by this route). A Moroccan woman still cannot pass nationality to a foreign husband.
  Naturalisation needs 5 years' residence plus Arabic or Tamazight [9]. No diaspora visa or
  "right of return" scheme for non-Moroccan Afro-descendants was found. For sub-Saharan diaspora
  without Moroccan roots, the standard foreigner route applies.

## Cities

### Casablanca

- **Cost of living**: furnished one-bed by district (MAD/month): Hay Hassani 2,500–5,000
  (~230–460 €), Bourgogne 3,000–6,000 (~280–555 €), Gauthier 4,000–9,000 (~370–830 €), Maârif
  5,000–12,000 (~460–1,110 €), Racine/Anfa 10,000–20,000 (~925–1,850 €), Aïn Diab 12,000–25,000 [5]
  (Sep 2026). Expat.com/Numbeo gives 4,000–8,000 MAD for a central one-bed [3] (Nov 2025). Cheap
  meal ~47 MAD (~4.5 €). Dinner for two (3 courses) ~300 MAD [5]. A mid-range meal in Gauthier/Maarif costs 100–180 MAD per person
  (search-result snippet, aggregator, low confidence). Monthly budget for a single person in a
  mid-market district: 9,000–14,000 MAD (~830–1,300 €). A family of four in Anfa/Racine with a car
  spends 30,000+ MAD plus school fees [5].
- **Work & money**: Morocco's economic capital and main industrial zone, home to most of the banking sector and
  the Casablanca Stock Exchange. Casablanca Finance City ranked 38th in the GFCI (Sep 2026) [10].
  About 30% of Morocco's banking network is in Casablanca, with the head offices of Attijariwafa, BCP, BMCI, Crédit du Maroc and Bank of Africa [10]. Remote work is feasible thanks to fibre in
  Maarif [4]. Salaries: not found (city-specific).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Technopark (founded about 25 years ago, with sites in Casablanca, Rabat, Tanger, Agadir and Essaouira).
  In 2025 its network counted 3,600+ entrepreneurs and 203 AI startups (up from 119), and ran
  programmes such as Morocco Accelerator (with Plug and Play), Startup Venture Building (Maroc Digital 2030), Glovo Startup Lab and TechnoVentures [11].
  Crypto: officially banned since the Office des Changes statement of 20 Nov 2017 [12]. A regulatory
  draft law has been widely reported but was **not verified** in this pass. Company registration ease: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: tram lines T1–T4 and busways [3]. Ticket 8 MAD (6 MAD with a card), monthly
  pass 230 MAD. Petit taxis start at 8 MAD (insist on the meter) [5]. 60+ Mbps broadband ~418 MAD/month,
  10 GB mobile ~98 MAD/month [5]. FTTH fibre 50–100 Mbps isn't available everywhere, so check before signing a lease [4].
  Power cuts: not reported as a daily issue in the sources read (not found). Ride-hailing: Careem and
  inDrive are listed for Rabat [8], and their Casablanca availability was not verified.
- **Health & schools**: international schools Casablanca American School, American Academy
  Casablanca, British International School of Casablanca and International School of Morocco, at about
  $8,600–16,000+/year [3]. Lycée Lyautey is run directly by the AEFE (French curriculum), with about 3,500 pupils, 1,582 of them French nationals [19].
  Reference private clinics: not found in the sources read.
- **Safety**: France Diplomatie (updated 15 Sep 2026) asks for *heightened vigilance in Casablanca
  and Marrakech because of thefts by thieves on motorbikes*, plus thefts from Airbnb-type rentals [2].
  FCDO: pickpocketing and bag-snatching are common, occasional knife crime, and the terrorist threat is
  considered likely [1]. Gen Z 212 protests (27 Sep–18 Oct 2025) included a motorway blockade in Casablanca on 29 Sep.
  Later sit-ins were peaceful [13].
- **Social life & vibe**: nightlife in the coastal districts, Gauthier and Racine. Aïn Diab Corniche is the
  café/promenade strip [3]. It is a big, fast, noisy business city. Football culture (Raja/Wydad) is well known
  but was not sourced in this pass.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found in this pass (Facebook/InterNations groups not reached).
- **Climate in practice**: mild Atlantic climate. Rain mostly Nov–Feb, warmest month August, with highs
  around 27 °C [10]. It is the most comfortable of the big cities in summer.
- **Watch out**: rents in the "expat" districts (Anfa, Racine, Aïn Diab) are near-European, and
  service charges add 200–1,500 MAD [5]. Motorbike phone snatching [2]. Traffic and noise.

### Marrakech

- **Cost of living**: one-bed in the centre 3,000–6,000 MAD (~280–555 €). Single person ~$900/month,
  family of four ~$2,500 [6] (Dec 2025). Guéliz one-bed $300–500, a renovated riad in the medina $400–700 [4].
  Districts: Guéliz (modern, Carré Eden mall), Hivernage (quiet, upscale), Targa (families) [6].
  Average net salary quoted: 4,891 MAD [6].
- **Work & money**: tourism-driven economy with 2M+ visitors a year [14]. Remote workers and retirees
  are a visible group. Guéliz has the most reliable fibre [4]. Outside tourism, hospitality and
  real estate, local career options are thinner (editorial).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: no Technopark site listed for Marrakech [11]. Ecosystem: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: petit taxis are usually metered, though some drivers push a fixed price. ALSA buses are
  crowded and confusing for newcomers, and grand taxis serve nearby towns [6].
- **Health & schools**: private clinics Clinique Al Madina, Polyclinique du Sud, Clinique
  Internationale Marrakech, Hôpital Privé de Marrakech [6]. Schools: British Academy School
  Marrakech, American School of Marrakech, Lycée Victor Hugo (French curriculum), 40,000–120,000
  MAD/year, with waiting lists [6].
- **Safety**: heightened vigilance for motorbike thefts (France Diplomatie, 15 Sep 2026) [2]. Fake guides and
  scams in the medina (FCDO) [1]. The Argana café bombing (28 Apr 2011) killed 17 [20]. Women travelling
  alone report it is less relaxed than Rabat [4].
- **Social life & vibe**: tourist and festival city, restaurants, rooftop bars and the Jemaa el-Fna
  square. A lively but touristic social scene (editorial).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: large retiree/remote-worker expat population [6]. Named
  groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: hot semi-arid. Average highs of 34–38 °C from June to August, rain mostly
  winter/March [14]. Summer is hard without air conditioning.
- **Watch out**: summer heat, a tourist-price economy, harassment in the medina, and the city was affected by the
  September 2023 earthquake [14][1].

### Tanger

- **Cost of living**: one-bed in the centre 3,000–6,000 MAD (~280–555 €). Single person
  7,500–11,000 MAD/month, family of four 26,000–48,000 MAD including school fees [7] (Dec 2025; the
  guide has internal inconsistencies). Districts: Playa/Marina Bay (upscale waterfront), Boulevard
  Pasteur (central), California/Iberia (families, near schools), Marshan (views of the Strait),
  Boukhalef (from ~2,500 MAD, student area) [7]. City-centre one-bed $250–450 [4].
- **Work & money**: Morocco's second industrial centre after Casablanca. Tanger Med port (opened 2007,
  ~85% transhipment), free zones, textiles, machinery and shipbuilding [15]. The automotive industry is widely
  reported but not sourced in this pass. Remote work: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: there is a Technopark Tanger site [11]. Rest: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: metered petit taxis 10–30 MAD a ride, ALSA bus 4–5 MAD. The Al Boraq high-speed
  train reaches Casablanca in just over 2 hours, and the airport bus costs 40 MAD [7].
- **Health & schools**: American School of Tangier ($8,000–15,500), Anglo-Moroccan School, Lycée
  Regnault (AEFE, French bac), Colegio Español Ramón y Cajal [7]. Hospitals: not found.
- **Safety**: the France Diplomatie security section does not single out Tanger [2]. The Rif hinterland
  (hashish region) calls for caution on secondary roads at night [2]. During the Gen Z 212 protests, force was used to
  disperse demonstrators on 1 Oct 2025 [13]. A trans woman was assaulted in Nov 2022 [16].
- **Social life & vibe**: Mediterranean and Atlantic beaches, a Spanish-influenced north, and close
  to Europe (ferries to Spain). Specific nightlife: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: the wettest of the three. Rain Nov–Jan (~130 mm/month), dry July–August,
  highs 16 °C (Jan) to 29 °C (Aug) [15]. Damp winters.
- **Watch out**: wet, windy winters. The city is a transit point for migrants trying to reach Europe, and
  the border tension is real (HRW on the Ceuta events in Sep 2024) [17].

### Rabat (additional: capital, calmer, family-friendly)

- **Cost of living**: one-bed in the centre ~4,250 MAD, ~2,400 MAD outside. Districts: Agdal, Souissi
  (embassies), Hay Riad, Hassan, Youssoufia/Mabella (cheaper) [8] (Dec 2025). Agdal/Hassan one-bed
  $350–550 [4].
- **Work**: administration, embassies, international organisations (editorial). A Technopark Rabat site exists [11].
- **Transport**: 2 tram lines (27 km) linking Rabat and Salé, 6 MAD a ride. Careem and inDrive operate. Al Boraq
  reaches Tanger in 1h20 and Casablanca by train in about 1h [8].
- **Schools**: Rabat American School, Khalil Gibran School, Lycée Descartes (AEFE) [8].
- **Why it matters**: described as more relaxed for women alone than Marrakech [4]. A good choice for
  families who want a calmer life than Casablanca with the same paperwork speed [4].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These describe city *environments* and who may feel at ease in them. They are not measurements of anyone's personality.

**Casablanca**
- Openness: rewards curiosity about a fast-changing, business-driven city with a cosmopolitan
  coastal scene (Gauthier, Racine, Aïn Diab) [3].
- Conscientiousness: suits planners, since housing prices vary 5x between districts [5] and a
  residence permit takes 1–3 months of paperwork [4].
- Extraversion: plenty of nightlife and cafés for sociable people [3].
- Agreeableness: daily bargaining with taxis (insisting on the meter) [5] asks for some firmness.
- Emotional stability: big-city noise, traffic and motorbike theft [2] reward a high stress tolerance.

**Marrakech**
- Openness: tourism, festivals and a mix of expats suit people who enjoy constant novelty [6][14].
- Extraversion: a sociable, visitor-oriented city. Introverts may prefer Guéliz/Targa over the medina [6].
- Emotional stability: scams, fake guides [1] and summer heat of 34–38 °C [14] test patience.

**Tanger / Rabat**
- Tanger suits people drawn to a Mediterranean, Spain-facing city in transition (port, industry) [15].
- Rabat suits people who value calm and routine, with an administrative pace and a reputation for being easier for women [4][8].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex relations are illegal under Penal Code Art. 489, with 6 months to 3 years in prison [16][17]
  (HRW World Report 2025; FCDO 2026). The April 2020 online "outing" campaign led to people losing their housing [16].
  Arab Barometer 2018–19: 21% of Moroccans found homosexuality acceptable [16]. Dating apps carry legal and safety risks [1].
- **Sex outside marriage**: illegal for Moroccan nationals [1]. This matters for diaspora members who hold
  Moroccan nationality.
- **Women**: street harassment is frequent, especially for women alone (France Diplomatie Sep 2026,
  FCDO 2026) [2][1]. The family code gives women half the inheritance share of male relatives, marital rape is not
  explicitly criminalised, and abortion is criminalised except when the mother's health is at risk [17] (HRW 2025).
- **Racism / treatment of Black Africans**: HRW 2025 documents tough migration control. A 2003 law
  still criminalises irregular entry, ~18,000 refugees and asylum seekers are registered with UNHCR, and in Sep 2024
  crowds trying to reach Ceuta were stopped, followed by 152 arrests [17]. Dated reports on everyday colourism or anti-Black
  racism in Moroccan cities were **not found** in this pass and are still needed before publishing any remark.
- **Conflict / zones**: France Diplomatie (zones updated 11 Mar 2026) formally advises against the Mauritanian border
  (red zone). Heightened vigilance applies on the Algerian border, in Western Sahara off the coastal road and in mined areas of
  south-east Guelmim [2]. Terrorist threat: "likely" per FCDO [1].
- **Social unrest**: Gen Z 212 protests, 27 Sep–18 Oct 2025, over health, education and World Cup
  spending. 3 people were killed in Lqliâa and 2,400+ arrested [13].
- **Freedom of expression**: prosecutions over Facebook posts in 2024 [17].
