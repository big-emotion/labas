## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day**: English is the official language [6]. Liberian English (Kolokwa) and indigenous languages (Kpelle, Bassa and others) are widely spoken. No source was fetched to confirm these, so treat them as general knowledge. French is not used in daily life, so francophones need working English.
- **Currency**: a dual-currency economy. The US dollar circulates alongside the Liberian dollar (L$) and is widely used for rent and larger purchases [2][7]. ATMs are rare and often empty, card payments work only in some Monrovia hotels and supermarkets, and the advice is to carry USD cash [2][3]. In September 2022 coins returned and a new banknote series added an L$1,000 note [7]. No dated exchange rate was found. Expatistan's conversion implies roughly L$100,636 ≈ AU$793 [10].
- **Entry and residence for French/EU passport holders**: a visa obtained in advance is required [4]. (GOV.UK states this for British citizens; France Diplomatie's "Entrée / séjour" tab was not read, so the French rule is assumed to match. Verify.) A visa on arrival exists under conditions, for example when there is no embassy in the applicant's country or for business travel. It needs online pre-approval and is issued only at Roberts International Airport [2]. Single-entry visas last 1–3 months; multiple-entry visas last up to 1–3 years [2]. A visa can be extended by up to 60 days at the Immigration Office in Monrovia for USD 25; longer stays need a residence permit [4]. A yellow fever certificate is required [2][4]. Work permits are separate, usually arranged by the employer, cost up to about USD 1,000 a year and take 2–3 weeks [2]. Some professions are reserved for Liberians, law among them [2]. *Sources: Jarnias guide, Jan 2026; GOV.UK, undated page that cites a May 2026 Ebola-screening advisory.*
- **Diaspora with family roots**:
  - **Dual citizenship**: a July 2022 amendment signed by President Weah lets Liberians of "Negro descent" who lost their citizenship by taking another nationality reclaim it. Dual nationals are barred from some public offices [5].
  - **Constitutional "Negro" clause**: citizenship by birth and by naturalisation is limited to people "of Negro descent" [5]. Naturalisation means a court declaration of intent, at least 2 years of residence (the president can waive this), and **renouncing your other nationalities** [5]. For an Afro-descendant without Liberian parentage this is a real route, but a heavy one. A dedicated "right of return" visa scheme like Ghana's or Sierra Leone's was not found.
  - **Land**: only Liberian citizens can own land [2]. This matters a lot to diaspora members thinking of building.

## Cities

### Monrovia

Capital. Population 1,761,032 in the 2022 census; the metro area (Montserrado and Margibi) held 2,225,911 people, about a third of the national population [6].

- **Cost of living**
  - Monrovia ranked **1st among African cities for expatriate living costs**, with an index of 94.8 (New York = 100), as of 1 Oct 2026 [8]. This reflects an imported-goods, secure-housing expat lifestyle, not local life.
  - **1-bed rent**: the Jarnias guide (Jan 2026) gives a studio or 1-bed downtown at about USD 220–230 (low estimate) or USD 600–830 (other data), and a 1-bed outside the centre at USD 95–150; it gives no per-neighbourhood figures [2]. Per-neighbourhood ranges seen only in search snippets, **(unverified)**: Sinkor or Paynesville USD 250–400, Mamba Point USD 500–800 [1, Jobicy May 2026]; Sinkor USD 600–1,200, Congo Town USD 500–800 [1, Digital Nomad Exchange, undated]. Africarrieres gives USD 150–400 for an unfurnished 1-bed in central Monrovia and USD 800–2,500 for a furnished expat flat; it cites "as of 2024" figures on a 2026 page [9]. A furnished 85 m² flat in a "normal" area costs about AU$793 [10].
  - Landlords often ask for **6–12 months' rent up front** [2].
  - **Neighbourhoods**: Mamba Point (diplomatic quarter, considered safer, high rents); Sinkor (midtown, near the beach, bars, nightlife around Airfield); Congo Town (quiet, residential, schools); Paynesville (cheaper, big markets); Old Road, Caldwell, Gardnersville and Logan Town (cheaper, mixed security); West Point (high risk) [2][6].
  - **Meal**: lunch about USD 3.20; dinner for two USD 20–25 [2].
  - **Monthly budget**: about USD 2,500–4,500 for a single expat with generator, security and a car [2]. A cost table in the same guide gives about USD 1,580 for an expat and about USD 535 for a local resident (undated table) [2]. One line in the expat budget: **USD 400–800 a month for electricity, generator and water** [2].
- **Work and money**
  - The job market rests mostly on government and the port [6][11]. Monrovia is the flag-of-convenience registry for 10–15% of world merchant shipping [6]. Other employers are NGOs and UN agencies, mining and rubber.
  - Few jobs exist for people who arrive without a posting; most expats are recruited from abroad [2].
  - Local median salary is about USD 118–700 a month, depending on the source [2].
  - Remote work is possible, but power and connectivity make it costly (see infrastructure).
- **Entrepreneurship and tech**
  - Coworking hot desks cost about USD 50 a month [2]. iCampus Liberia is a known hub, but its site did not resolve when fetched, so its status is unverified.
  - The **Orange Digital Center** in Monrovia (inaugurated Oct 2022) offers free tech training and startup support; Orange Liberia and UNDP signed an MoU on 5 Nov 2025 to extend its training and entrepreneurship work, and a second ODC club opened at Tubman University in Harper in March 2025 [17]. These are operator and UNDP announcements, not independent ecosystem data.
  - Mobile money exists through Orange Money and MTN MoMo; the operators are Orange Liberia and Lonestar Cell MTN [12]. Adoption figures: not found.
  - Startup ecosystem data, investors, incubators, crypto adoption and company-registration steps: **not found**, because no tech-press access was possible.
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - **Power**: the public grid is unreliable, and diesel generators are the norm in expat housing [2].
  - **Internet**: Liberia connects to the ACE submarine cable [12]. Internet users were 32.4% of the population in 2025 [12]. Fixed broadband had only 14,759 subscriptions in 2022 [12]. Starlink received a one-year provisional licence in January 2025 [12]. Home fibre costs USD 50–150 a month [9].
  - **Water**: tap water must be treated [2].
  - **Transport**: shared taxis, minibuses, motorbike taxis and kekes (tricycles) are cheap but often unsafe [2]. Ride or taxi trips cost USD 3–10 [9]. A ride-hailing app could not be verified. Most expats use a car with a driver, at USD 300–600 a month [2]. Roberts International Airport is about 60 km from the city [2].
- **Health and schools**
  - Healthcare is very limited. St Joseph's Catholic Hospital and private clinics handle routine care [2]. JFK Medical Center is the national referral hospital [6]. Serious cases need **evacuation to Accra, Johannesburg or Europe** [2][11].
  - Health risks include malaria, typhoid, cholera, dengue and Lassa fever [2]. GOV.UK cites a 25 May 2026 Liberian advisory on heightened Ebola screening for arrivals, prompted by outbreaks in Uganda and the DRC; travellers may have to submit health information [4].
  - **Schools**: the American International School of Monrovia (US-accredited) [2]. Other private schools include French-speaking and British/Lebanese ones, of uneven quality [2]. The name of a French school was not found.
- **Safety**
  - GOV.UK says crime is high and thieves are often armed with knives or machetes, occasionally firearms. Foreigners have been mugged in **Mamba Point and Sinkor**, robberies have happened in taxis, and it advises against walking after dark. Criminals operate on beaches and in nightclubs [3, GOV.UK, page undated, read Nov 2026].
  - Rape rates are high, but attacks on foreign women are described as rare [3].
  - France Diplomatie's Liberia page was last updated 10 Sep 2026; its security zone map was not readable [13].
  - The US State Department page was inaccessible (403).
- **Social life and vibe**
  - Nightlife is concentrated in Sinkor and Airfield [6]. Sports venues include the SKD Sports Complex and Antoinette Tubman Stadium [6].
  - **Not found** (no local lifestyle source was reachable): the music scene (hipco), food culture and how much religion weighs on daily life.
- **Diaspora and expat community**
  - InterNations Monrovia runs events and trips to Robertsport and Harper [2]. Liberian diaspora mentoring networks exist [2].
  - Named Facebook groups and returnee associations: **not found**.
- **Climate in practice**
  - Described as the **wettest capital in the world**, with about 4,600 mm of rain a year. September is the wettest month and January the driest (1991–2020 normals at Roberts International Airport) [6].
  - The rainy season runs May to Oct/Nov, when roads outside the city degrade sharply [2][3]. The Harmattan brings dust in the dry season [2].
- **Watch out**
  - The most expensive African city for an expat-standard life [8].
  - Generator dependence, a cash economy and empty ATMs [2][3].
  - Rent paid 6–12 months in advance [2].
  - Medical evacuation needed for anything serious [2].
  - Corruption and land-tenure insecurity [2].
  - Very little French spoken.

### Buchanan

Capital of Grand Bassa County and the country's third city. Population 34,270 (2008 census, old) [14]. It lies on the Atlantic coast, about 110 km south-east of Monrovia, on a paved road [2][14].

- **Cost of living**: **not found**. Likely cheaper than Monrovia, with a thinner rental market for expat standards.
- **Work and money**: an iron-ore port at the end of the 250 km railway from Yekepa (Nimba), revived by ArcelorMittal (as of 2012) [14]. Fishing is also important [14]. Jobs are mostly tied to mining and logistics.
- **Entrepreneurship and tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: not found; expect worse power and health services than in Monrovia.
- **Health and schools**: not found. Use Monrovia for referrals.
- **Safety**: no city-specific advisory was found. GOV.UK advises security planning for road travel outside Monrovia and warns of illegal checkpoints at night [3].
- **Social life and vibe**: a small coastal town with "isolated beaches and lagoons" [14]. Nightlife is very limited (low confidence).
- **Diaspora and expat community**: not found.
- **Climate**: same coastal monsoon pattern as Monrovia [6].
- **Watch out**: a small labour market dependent on mining, limited healthcare, and 2008 population data.

### Robertsport (suggested addition, for lifestyle and nature profiles)

Capital of Grand Cape Mount, about 80 km north-west of Monrovia, between Lake Piso and the ocean [15].

- It is known for its surf: Fisherman's Point, Cotton Trees and Cassava Point. It hosts the national surf titles, and a film about them was shown at the Ocean Film Festival in 2025 [15].
- Population was 3,933 in 2008. It is mostly a fishing and rice-farming village [15].
- It suits remote workers with Starlink or nature seekers, not careers.
- Cost, health and schools: not found. All referral care is in Monrovia.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These describe who tends to cope well, based on the facts above. No city or quiz question measures any trait. No academic regional-personality study (Hofstede, GLOBE) covering Liberia was found in this pass; Afrobarometer surveys Liberia, but no dated figure could be fetched.

**Monrovia**
- **Openness**: suits people curious about a country rebuilding after war, with its American-founded history (1822) [11] and few ready-made expat comforts.
- **Conscientiousness**: helps a lot. Life involves generator logistics, cash planning with often-empty ATMs [3], up-front rent [2] and visa and work-permit paperwork [2].
- **Extraversion**: networking-driven. Most opportunities come through NGO, diplomatic and InterNations circles [2]. Sinkor's nightlife exists, but night movement is constrained by safety advice [3].
- **Agreeableness**: the workplace is described as hierarchical [2], so patience with authority and relationship-building matter.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance)**: needed. Crime advisories [3], weak emergency services and medical evacuation [2], heavy rain and flooding roads [3][6], and the highest expat costs in Africa [8].

**Buchanan and Robertsport**
- Small-town pace, so more **tolerance for isolation** and self-reliance is needed. **Openness** to a nature and surf lifestyle (Robertsport) [15] matters more than appetite for a career.

## Legal and sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex sexual activity is illegal under penal code sections 14.74 and 14.79 ("voluntary sodomy", a misdemeanour of up to 1 year, with possibly heavier penalties for repeat offences). GOV.UK confirms it is illegal [3]. In 2012, bills to harshen the penalties failed [16]. On 19 July 2024, Rep. Johnson Williams's "Anti-Homosexuality Law of Liberia 2024" was sent to a joint House committee [18]. On 18 November 2025 the Speaker had deferred it in committee, and a group of lawmakers chanted "No gay bill, no FGM bill!" to tie it to President Boakai's harmful-practices bill [19]. Its status after Nov 2025 was not found. A 2020 UN report found "serious, widespread" rights violations. In November 2016, residents attacked people leaving a Transgender Network of Liberia event in Monrovia [16]. The 2015 Decent Work Act bars employment discrimination based on orientation [16].
- **Racism, colourism and treatment of diaspora/foreigners**: the constitution restricts citizenship to people "of Negro descent" [5], which privileges Afro-descendants and excludes others (for example the long-established Lebanese community). How returnees from the diaspora are received: no reliable source found.
- **Conflict**: there is no active armed conflict; the civil wars ended in 2003 [6]. Near borders, the risks are land disputes, illegal mining and armed groups, according to an expat guide (Jan 2026) [2]. This is not from an official source; verify it.
- **Women's daily experience**: GOV.UK reports high rates of rape and sexual assault, with attacks on foreign women rare [3]. The Jarnias guide notes a persistent gap on gender-based violence [2]. FGM remains prevalent in 11 of 15 counties; on 18 Nov 2025 the House sent President Boakai's bill banning FGM and child marriage out for district consultations instead of voting on it [19].
