## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Political situation (the first thing to know):** soldiers seized power on 26 Nov 2025, a day before results of the 23 Nov general election were due; General Horta Inta-A Na Man has led a military transition since 27 Nov 2025. ECOWAS and the African Union suspended the country (27–28 Nov 2025). An overnight curfew and border closure were imposed on 26 Nov 2025 [5]. A transitional charter set a 12-month transition; a constitutional referendum (August 2026) expanded presidential powers and cut parliament from 102 to 65 seats, boycotted by the PAIGC; presidential and legislative elections are set for **6 Dec 2026** [9][5]. Expect uncertainty around that date.
- **Languages day to day:** Kriol (Guinea-Bissau Creole, Portuguese-based) is the real common language — ~54% first language, ~40% second language (2012 study cited by Wikipedia) [12]. Portuguese is official but a first language for only ~2% (second language ~33%) [12]. French is "widely spoken" per Expat.com (undated guide) [10]; the country is a member of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie [12], which says nothing about everyday use — plausible given Senegal and Guinea as neighbours and a large Senegalese/Guinean community, but not verified by a second source. **A French speaker can get by in town, but Kriol/Portuguese is needed for real integration.**
- **Currency:** West African CFA franc (XOF), member of the West African monetary union since 1997 [12][17]; fixed peg of 655.957 XOF = 1 EUR (so prices are stable in euros). Inflation 0.9% in 2025, forecast ~2.0% for 2026 [9].
- **Religion:** Islam 49.6%, Christianity 37.8%, traditional religions 6.4%, none 5.4% (Afrobarometer 2025 via Wikipedia) [12].
- **Entry & residence (a) French/EU passport:** a visa is required (FCDO, entry page updated 10 Dec 2025; applies to UK nationals, handled by the Guinea-Bissau embassy in Paris, 94 rue Saint-Lazare — embgbfr@gmail.com, +33 1 48 74 36 39 or +33 1 48 78 36 69 — or possibly an embassy in a neighbouring country such as Senegal) [2]. The FCDO entry page does not mention any e-visa [2]. Yellow-fever vaccination certificate required [2]. E-visa availability, cost, residence-permit route for French nationals: **not found** in this pass. French nationals staying >6 months are advised to register with the consular register; civil-status matters go through the Consulat général de France in **Dakar** [8].
- **Entry & residence (b) diaspora with family roots:** dual-nationality rules, citizenship by descent and any diaspora/right-of-return scheme: **not found** (the nationality-law page could not be fetched). No equivalent of Ghana's or Sierra Leone's diaspora drives was found. ECOWAS citizens normally enjoy free movement, but note the country's ECOWAS suspension since Nov 2025 [5] — practical effect on border formalities not found.
- **Push factors context:** 77% of Guineans have thought about emigrating (49% "a lot"); 75% of would-be migrants want Europe (Afrobarometer AD1164, 9 Apr 2026) [15]. Moving *to* Bissau runs against a strong outflow, which shapes jobs, prices and family expectations of returnees.

## Cities

### Bissau

- **Cost of living:** Numbeo (last updated 16 May 2025, **0 contributors in the past 12 months — very weak data**): 1-bed city centre 300,000 XOF/month (~€457); outside centre 100,000 XOF (~€152); cheap restaurant meal 3,000 XOF (~€4.60); mid-range meal for two 15,000 XOF (~€23); broadband 60 Mbps+ ~26,900 XOF (~€41) [11]. Expat.com (undated) estimates ~$800/month for a single person and ~$2,200 for a family of four [10]. Neighbourhood names found: Bandim (main market area), Ajuda, Alto Crim, Mindara, plus peri-urban Safim, Prabis and Nhacra [18]. Which ones expats favour (e.g. the commonly cited central districts): **not found**.
- **Work & money:** Bissau is the administrative, port, fishing and agro-processing centre (cashew, drinks, dairy) [3][18]; commerce is highly informal [18]. Cashew is the main export crop and the 2025 harvest drove 5.8% GDP growth [9][17]. Most expats work in embassies or NGOs [10]. Salaries: **not found**. Remote work depends on unstable electricity (see below).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** startup hubs, incubators, investors, fintech/crypto adoption, company-registration process: **not found**. Financial sector is shallow (about 4% of GDP in financial intermediation in 2013, four banks in 2015 — old data) [17].
- **Daily infrastructure:** only 28% of the population had electricity access in 2019 (56% urban) and generation was ~99% fossil (2016) [13]; the Portuguese Wikipedia describes Bissau's electricity as unstable with frequent blackouts (undated) [18]. A World Bank–financed ECOWAS access project has added ~20,650 household connections, closing Dec 2026 [9]. Mobile operators, mobile money, fibre, ride-hailing apps: **not found** (only 2012 telecom statistics available — 2.9% internet use, flagged outdated) [—, see note]. Only international airport: Osvaldo Vieira [3].
- **Health & schools:** Hospital Nacional Simão Mendes (national reference), Hospital Militar, Hospital Raoul Follereau, Hospital 3 de Agosto, plus many clinics [18]. FCDO health page advises insurance covering medical evacuation (via search results) [1]. Universities: Amílcar Cabral (public), Católica, Jean Piaget, Lusófona [3][18]. French or international schools: **not found** in this pass (French embassy home page does not mention one) [8].
- **Safety:** FCDO (still current 14 Nov 2026, last updated 10 Dec 2025): political instability; avoid military installations, government buildings and demonstrations; pickpocketing and bag/phone snatching "relatively common" in the capital; carry ID (passport or residence permit) especially when driving or in taxis, police check regularly; Bissau declared landmine-free by CAAMI [1]. Gunfire was heard outside the presidential palace, the interior ministry and the electoral commission on 26 Nov 2025 (coup day) [5]. Night-time curfew imposed 26 Nov 2025 [5]; no lifting date found, and the FCDO safety page (updated 10 Dec 2025, read 14 Nov 2026) no longer mentions a curfew [1] — current status **not confirmed**. Neighbourhood-level nuance: **not found**.
- **Social life & vibe:** **not found** in fetched sources beyond landmarks (Fortaleza da Amura with Amílcar Cabral's mausoleum, Pidjiguiti memorial, cathedral, National Arts Institute) [3]. Carnival, music (gumbé), nightlife: not sourced in this pass.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** Expat.com forum: 603 members, 6 topics (low activity); top expat nationalities Senegalese, Guinean, Portuguese, Chinese, Nigerian; Expat.com claims 15,000–20,000 expats but also calls the community "small" — treat with caution [10]. French community in Bissau is served by an embassy that held consular elections on 30–31 May 2026 [8]. Facebook/WhatsApp groups, returnee networks: **not found**.
- **Climate in practice:** dry November–May with almost no rain; rainy season June–October, heaviest July–September; daily highs ~31–36 °C [3]. FCDO: rainy season makes roads difficult, avoid night travel [1].
- **Watch out:** a military transition with elections due 6 Dec 2026 [9]; weak power supply [13][18]; very thin data (Numbeo has no recent contributors) [11]; Guinea-Bissau is described as the leading West African cocaine transit country (Wikipedia, undated claim) [17]; visa required, consular services for French nationals partly in Dakar [2][8].

### Bubaque

- **Overview:** island and town in the Bijagós archipelago — island population 6,427 and town 4,299 (2009 census, old) [4]; 48 km², including ~18 km² of marsh flooded at high tide [19]. The archipelago became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2025 ("Coastal and Marine Ecosystems of the Bijagós Archipelago – Omatí Minhô"), and has been a Biosphere Reserve since 1996 and Ramsar site since 2014; Bubaque hosts the biosphere-reserve headquarters and a museum [4][14].
- **Cost of living:** **not found** (no Numbeo or rental data).
- **Work & money:** most families live from subsistence farming and fishing [14]; tourism is limited, mostly boat charters from Senegal [14]. Realistic for someone with remote income or a tourism/conservation project — and only if connectivity works, which is **not verified**.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** **not found**.
- **Daily infrastructure:** a weekly ferry to Bissau (frequency per Wikipedia, undated; crossing time not found) plus a small airport; all transport on the island is by motorbike or on foot [4]. Electricity, water, internet: **not found** — national rural electricity access was 7% in 2019 [13].
- **Health & schools:** **not found**. Serious care means Bissau, a boat ride away — assume no specialist care on the island.
- **Safety:** island-specific advice: **not found** in FCDO pages fetched. Wikipedia notes several Bijagós islands have served as narcotrafficking transit depots since the early 2000s (undated, editorial claim) [14].
- **Social life & vibe:** Bijagó culture is often described as matriarchal (women choosing husbands, running households), though other authors call it patriarchal with important female roles — contested [14]. Bijagó, Kriol and Portuguese spoken; Portuguese rarely used [14]. Very slow pace, nature-dominated.
- **Diaspora & expat community:** **not found**.
- **Climate in practice:** same regime as Bissau (rains June–October) [3]; rainy season likely makes boat links harder — not sourced.
- **Watch out:** isolation, medical distance, weekly transport links, no verified data on power or internet; protected-area rules apply.

### Gabú (suggested addition — the country's second city)

- Second most populous city (37,525 in 2010), capital of the east, home of the country's second-largest market with traders from Guinea, Senegal, Mali and Sierra Leone; mainly Fula and Muslim; about a quarter less rain than Bissau [6]. Why it matters: the inland trade hub and the most likely place where French is useful because of cross-border trade with francophone neighbours (inference, not sourced). Cost, safety, services: **not found**.

### Cacheu (possible addition — heritage & mangroves, low priority)

- Small town (9,882 in 2009) with a 16th-century fort, the Memorial of Slavery and Black Trafficking, a market, and Cacheu River Mangroves Natural Park nearby [7]. Interesting for heritage-minded diaspora; too small for most movers. Services: **not found**.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No academic regional-personality study (Hofstede, GLOBE) covering Guinea-Bissau was found in this pass; Afrobarometer's first survey of the country (Round 10, 2025) exists [15][16]. The notes below describe which kind of person tends to cope well with documented conditions; they are not measurements of anyone.

**Bissau**
- Openness: suits people curious to learn Kriol and live in a Lusophone, multi-ethnic, religiously mixed city (≈50% Muslim, ≈38% Christian) [12].
- Conscientiousness: an informal economy [18], a shallow banking sector [17], and consular procedures split between Bissau and Dakar [8] reward people who plan ahead and keep paperwork in order.
- Extraversion: a small expat scene (Expat.com forum with 6 topics) [10] means building a network through locals and NGOs rather than ready-made expat clubs.
- Agreeableness: daily life runs through personal relationships in markets like Bandim [18]; patience and good relations matter more than formal channels (editorial inference).
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed — military transition, elections on 6 Dec 2026 [9], frequent blackouts [18], street-level petty theft [1].

**Bubaque**
- Openness: for people drawn to a distinctive Bijagó culture and a UNESCO natural setting [14].
- Conscientiousness: weekly ferry links and remote location [4] demand self-reliance and stock planning.
- Extraversion: very small community (≈4,300 town residents in 2009) [4] — suits people comfortable with solitude.
- Agreeableness: living within a community with its own customs and protected-area rules [14] requires deference to local norms.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): medical care far away and uncertain connectivity call for high tolerance of uncertainty.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:** consensual same-sex relations are not criminalised (no such provision since the 1993 Penal Code) [1][20]; no anti-discrimination protection in employment or services; a 2023 US report notes hiring discrimination reported by some LGBTQ+ people [20]. FCDO (page updated 10 Dec 2025, still current 14 Nov 2026): the country is conservative and public displays of affection by same-sex couples may attract negative attention [1].
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of diaspora:** no source on racism, colourism or returnees specifically. Closest proxy: 82% of Guineans say they would have no problem living next to immigrants, 54% see immigrants' economic impact as positive vs 28% negative, and 65% favour letting foreign workers in (Afrobarometer AD1164, 9 Apr 2026) [15]. These are attitudes towards foreigners in general, not evidence about how diaspora returnees are treated.
- **Conflict & political violence:** military coup 26 Nov 2025 with gunfire in central Bissau, curfew and border closure (Wikipedia, events to 30 Jan 2026) [5]; opposition leader Domingos Simões Pereira released but placed under house arrest on 30 Jan 2026 [5]; elections 6 Dec 2026 [9]. Landmines remain outside Bissau; stay on paved roads [1].
- **Women's daily experience:** 70% of Guineans say women should decide for themselves whether and when to marry, but only 46% support women's autonomy on motherhood decisions (Afrobarometer AD1199, 7 Jul 2026); 27% of women aged 20–24 had a child before 18 (2020 survey cited there); early/forced marriage and gender-based violence named as persistent obstacles [16]. Bijagó society is often cited for women's strong roles [14]. Street harassment and safety for women alone: **not found**.
