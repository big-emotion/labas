## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day.** English is the official language and the language of business and administration [13]. Bemba is the largest first language (34%), followed by Tonga (12.8%), Nyanja (11.4%), Chewa (6.7%) and Lozi (5.4%) [13]. In practice, Nyanja and its "town slang" is the lingua franca of Lusaka; Wikipedia's Lusaka references include a 2007 study of Nyanja slang in Lusaka [4]. Bemba dominates the Copperbelt, and Tonga and Lozi are spoken around Livingstone. These regional patterns are editorial knowledge; the fetched pages confirm only the national shares. **French is barely used**, so a French-only speaker will struggle.
- **Currency.** Zambian kwacha (ZMW) [13]. Rents in expat areas are often quoted or paid in USD [7]. Inflation was 9.4% (CPI estimate, January 2026) and GDP growth 6.2% in 2025 (IMF, via [12]).
- **Religion.** 98% of people are Christian (2022 census), and the state religion is Christianity [13]. Church life matters in daily routines.
- **Entry for French/EU passport holders.** The Department of Immigration lists France, all EU states, the UK, the USA and Canada as "nationals who do not require visas" [8]. A return ticket is needed for air arrivals, or enough funds for land arrivals [8]. The UK FCDO entry-requirements page (no update date shown; checked 2026-11-14) gives the limits: up to 90 days in any 12 months as a tourist, and 30 days in any 12 months for business. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months, with 2 blank pages [2]. Wikipedia gives the same 90/30-day split for EU states [10]. *Note:* a separate 25 Dec 2024 immigration notice on a 53-country waiver, effective 1 Jan 2025 under SI 78 of 2024, does not name France, UK or US [9]. The general exemption list does [8].
- **Living there long term.** You need an immigration permit [11]:
  - **Employment Permit.** The employer, a lawyer or a consultant applies. It can be extended up to 10 years, and holders cannot buy land.
  - **Investor's Permit.** Requires at least US$250,000 for a new business, or US$150,000 to join an existing company.
  - **Retiree route (under Residence).** Requires proof of a pension or annuity.
  - **Spouse Permit.** For spouses of Zambians or residents.
  - **Residence Permit (permanent).** Open after more than 10 years on an employment permit, more than 3 years on an investor permit, or 5 or more years on a spouse permit.
  - **Visiting Permit.** For stays beyond 90 days, up to 9 months a year.
  - Fees are not shown on that page [11].
- **Diaspora with Zambian roots.**
  - Since the 2016 constitutional amendments, **dual nationality is allowed** through a "simple registration process" [14]. The UK FCDO confirms that Zambia recognises dual nationality and advises leaving on the same passport you entered with (2026) [2].
  - A person born abroad with at least one Zambian parent is a citizen by descent. Former citizens who lost nationality can apply to regain it ("bestowal"/repatriation). People denaturalised for holding another nationality may also repatriate under the 2016 provisions [14].
  - Children born abroad to a Zambian parent can register after 5 years of residence [14].
  - **No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme** for Afro-descendants without Zambian ancestry was found, unlike Ghana or Benin. Such people fall under the ordinary permit system: "not found".
- **Political context, 2026.** General elections were held on 13 Aug 2026, and President Hakainde Hichilema (UPND) was re-elected with about 60.5% (the article's infobox; its results table says 61.3%) [15]. The EU observer mission's preliminary statement called it "competitive" but held on an "uneven playing field", citing restrictions on freedoms and misuse of state resources. It was followed by the arrest of 11 people including opposition candidate Brian Mundubile on "insurrection" suspicions, the fatal shooting of former minister Mutotwe Kafwaya during a raid in Kabulonga (14 Aug 2026), and courts closing on the petition deadline day, 24 Aug 2026 [15].
  - Human Rights Watch (page dated 1 Nov 2026) reports a "post-election crackdown", with enforced disappearances and arbitrary detention of opposition supporters, lawyers and journalists [17].
  - France's embassy issued a vigilance alert on 6 Aug 2026 [18].
  - Editorial reading (unsourced): the documented targets are opposition members, lawyers, journalists and critics [17]; foreign residents who stay out of politics appear less exposed, but protests can still turn violent [2].

## Cities

### Lusaka

Capital and economic hub, with 2,204,059 inhabitants at the 2022 census [4].

- **Cost of living**
  - **Neighbourhoods expats use** [6][7][4]:
    - Kabulonga, plus Roma and Roma Park: gated, near the embassies.
    - Woodlands: quieter, large plots, the traditional elite and expat area.
    - Rhodes Park and Ibex Hill: modern secure complexes.
    - Leopards Hill Road: new developments.
    - Also Makeni and Ridgeway.
  - **Rent, two very different readings.**
    - Expat.com's Lusaka guide (12 Dec 2025): a 1–2-bed flat in those areas costs **US$800–2,000/month (≈ €740–1,840)**, and furnished US$1,500–3,500 [7].
    - Expat.com's Zambia guide (Numbeo-based figures): a 1-bed downtown costs about **US$211/month**, a 3-bed about US$959 [6]. This reflects the local market, not secured expat compounds. (The US$121 suburban figure in an earlier draft could not be found on the page and was removed.)
    - A middle-class diaspora household would probably sit in between. This is editorial and low confidence.
  - **Deposits** are usually 1–3 months, and leases at least 12 months [6][7].
  - **Monthly budget.**
    - Single person: about **US$1,100/month** (≈ €1,010), per Expat.com [6] (corrected 2026-11-14; an earlier draft said US$600–900).
    - Family of four: about **US$2,800/month** excluding schooling [6].
    - International school is the largest expense: about US$12,000–36,000 a year for 2 children (≈ US$1,000–3,000/month) [6].
  - **A meal:** price not found (Numbeo blocked).
  - **Other costs:** utilities about US$70/month for 85 m², gym about US$47, mobile plan with 10 GB about US$12 [6].
- **Work & money**
  - Services and wholesale/retail dominate the economy. The city hosts bank headquarters and the Lusaka Stock Exchange [4].
  - Nationally, copper is about 85% of exports [12], but mining jobs sit in the Copperbelt rather than Lusaka.
  - Other employers: embassies, NGOs and UN agencies (implied by the embassy-area housing [7]; employer list not sourced).
  - Youth unemployment is a major issue: Afrobarometer, 10 Apr 2025, finds most young Zambians are unemployed despite more schooling [16].
  - **Remote work** is possible on fibre (about US$46/month for 60 Mbps+ [6]), but power cuts make backup power essential (see infrastructure).
  - Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**
  - **BongoHive** (ZEP-RE Business Park, Mass Media, Alick Nkhata Rd) has run since about 2011 and calls itself "Zambia's leading innovation hub". It reports 200+ startups supported. In 2026 it runs an AI Lab and an "AI for Business Accelerator" (12 weeks, up to US$50k follow-on) [19].
  - The **Zambia Development Agency** offers incentives: 100% profit repatriation, no foreign-exchange controls, and 0% tax on profits and dividends in industrial parks. Its 7 Special Economic Zones include multi-facility economic zones [20]. It also runs a 7-step "start a business" guide [20].
  - Remember the **US$250k investor-permit floor** for foreigners without Zambian nationality [11]. That is a real barrier for small diaspora founders without citizenship.
  - **Crypto rules (2026):** there is no comprehensive crypto law. The Bank of Zambia ordered every provider of virtual-asset or stablecoin services in Zambia, resident or not, to register by 27 Mar 2026; from 30 Mar 2026 regulated banks may not deal with unregistered providers. Registration is not a licence, and a full framework is still to come (Zambia Monitor, 16 Mar 2026) [33]. Anyone planning a crypto business should talk to BoZ and the SEC first.
  - Fintech and mobile-money adoption figures: not found. MTN MoMo and Airtel Money are widely known to operate, but this is unsourced here.
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - **Electricity is the main daily pain point.**
    - Zambia relies on hydropower for about 90% of its generation (2022 data) [21].
    - In the 2024 drought, ZESCO initially promised only "seven hours of daily power supply", cut to **five hours by mid-2025**. Nationwide blackouts were reported in Nov 2024 [22].
    - Higher Kariba water allocations in March 2025 eased shortages: from 185 to 485 MW for each country, per Lusaka Voice, 29 Mar 2025 [23].
    - A 100 MW solar plant at Chisamba was commissioned in June 2025, with 332 MWp more contracted for 2026 [22].
    - Afrobarometer (12 May 2025): Zambians rate the government poorly on reliable electricity [16].
    - France Diplomatie flagged an **El Niño vigilance** on 3 Nov 2026 [3], which could mean another poor rainy season and more cuts. Budget for solar plus batteries or an inverter.
  - **Water** can be interrupted [6].
  - **Transport.** Ride-hailing apps **Yango and Bolt** work, at about ZMW 20–50 per short trip. Minibuses cost ZMW 6–15 [7]. Rush hours are 07:00–09:00 and 16:30–18:30 [7]. There are no commuter trains [4].
- **Health & schools**
  - **Private hospitals** used by expats: **Fairview Hospital** and **Medlands Hospital** [6].
  - **Public hospitals:** University Teaching Hospital (1,655 beds, highest level of care) [4] and Levy Mwanawasa Hospital [6].
  - Public primary care has been free since 2021 but suffers shortages. Medical-evacuation cover is strongly advised [6].
  - **Schools** [6][4]:
    - American International School of Lusaka (IB; 2025 fees about US$6,000–21,430).
    - International School of Lusaka (IB, about 700 pupils).
    - Lusaka International Community School.
    - Lusaka Oaktree School (Cambridge).
    - Banani International (girls).
    - **Lycée Français de Lusaka** (Plot 22725, Alick Nkhata Avenue, Longacres, behind the Alliance Française). Its site says it belongs to the AEFE network [34]; which grades are homologated was not confirmed on the page (a search snippet suggests primary only, with CNED above; unverified).
  - Schools run mid-August to mid-June [7].
- **Safety**
  - **UK FCDO (2026):**
    - "Travel in larger cities ... is generally safe during daylight hours"; pre-arrange transport rather than walking at night [2].
    - Watch for carjackers when approaching locked gates at night, or if an object blocks the road [2].
    - Protests in Lusaka can turn violent.
    - Road crashes are frequent [2].
  - **France Diplomatie** (updated 3 Nov 2026) shows a security map. The only details captured were health alerts: Ebola screening at the northern borders, with no Zambian case declared; and measles [3].
  - **Neighbourhood level:** expats settle in guarded compounds and gated estates. Expat.com advises 24/7 guards, especially with children [6].
  - Wikipedia notes that most residents live in unplanned settlements on the fringes, such as Matero, Chilenje and Libala [4].
  - Matero was the site of a fatal election-day assault in Aug 2026 [15].
  - No neighbourhood crime data was found (OSAC blocked).
- **Social life & vibe**
  - Dining and nightlife cluster in Kabulonga and Rhodes Park [7].
  - Malls are a social backbone: Manda Hill, Levy Junction, EastPark, Arcade and others [4].
  - Markets: Sunday Pakati and the Northmead market [7].
  - **Football** is king. Six of the 18 Super League clubs are based in Lusaka, including Zanaco and Green Buffaloes [24]. Cricket is played at Lotus Sports Club [24].
  - Music heritage: **Zamrock** of the 1970s (WITCH, Paul Ngozi) [25].
  - With 98% Christians [13], church and Sunday rhythms are strong.
  - Nature escapes: Lusaka National Park (about 30 km), Lilayi Elephant Nursery, Chaminuka (about 45 km) [7].
  - Pace and noise level: editorial impression only. It is a calmer, more spread-out capital than Lagos or Kinshasa (not sourced).
- **Diaspora & expat community**
  - Expat.com Zambia forum: about 4,680 members. The Lusaka forum has about 781 members [6][7].
  - Large South African, Chinese, Indian, British and Zimbabwean communities [6].
  - French embassy in Lusaka; registration on the consular register is recommended for stays over 6 months [18].
  - Alliance Française Lusaka: exists (the French school sits behind it [34]); its own website did not resolve.
  - Returnee or diaspora associations, Facebook and WhatsApp groups: **not found** (no search available).
- **Climate in practice** [4]:
  - Warm and wet from Nov to Mar.
  - Cool and dry from Apr to Aug. July averages 14.9 °C, so nights are genuinely cold: bring jumpers.
  - Hot in Sep–Oct, with highs around 32 °C. These are also the months when power cuts hurt most before the rains.
  - Rains wash out roads [2].
- **Watch out**
  - Blackouts that depend on hydropower.
  - Expat-grade rent priced in USD.
  - International school fees.
  - Political tension after the 2026 election [15][17].
  - Criminalised homosexuality (see below).
  - Investor-permit thresholds [11].
  - Almost no French spoken.

### Livingstone

177,393 inhabitants (2022). A tourist town on the Zambezi next to Victoria Falls and Mosi-oa-Tunya National Park; it was the capital of Northern Rhodesia from 1911 to 1935 [5].

- **Cost of living:** rent, meal and budget figures **not found** (Expat.com has no Livingstone rents [26]). Editorially, expect cheaper local housing than Lusaka but tourist-inflated restaurant prices (low confidence).
- **Work & money:** jobs are in hospitality, guiding, adventure activities and conservation [26]. Zambia had about 2.2 million visitor arrivals in 2024 [12]. Outside tourism there is little formal employment. Remote work depends on fibre availability: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** tourism businesses such as lodges, tour operators and food. No hubs found.
- **Daily infrastructure:** same national grid and outage problem as Lusaka [22]. The airport (Harry Mwaanga Nkumbula International) has flights to Lusaka, Johannesburg, Cape Town and Nairobi [5]. The KAZA UNIVISA (US$50, 30 days) eases trips into Zimbabwe; EU citizens are visa-exempt in Zambia anyway [2][10]. Ride-hailing coverage: not found.
- **Health & schools:** reference hospitals and international or French schools are **not found**. Private care is concentrated in Lusaka and Ndola [26], so serious cases mean travelling to Lusaka or South Africa. A French honorary consul is based in Livingstone [18].
- **Safety:** the FCDO points to accidents in Victoria Falls adventure sports rather than crime [2]. No city-specific crime data was found.
- **Social life & vibe:** small-town and outdoorsy:
  - Rafting the Batoka Gorge (Aug–Dec) and Devil's Pool (late Aug–Dec) [26][27].
  - An action festival in July [27].
  - Nightlife centres on backpacker bars and lodges (editorial, not sourced).
- **Diaspora & expat community:** small. A tourism-professional crowd [26]. Groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice:** hot semi-arid, with about 646 mm of rain a year. Very hot before the rains (record 43.6 °C) and mild in the dry season [5]. The Falls are at peak flow in Mar–Apr and lowest from Sep–Nov [26].
- **Watch out:**
  - Limited healthcare and schooling.
  - An economy dependent on tourist seasons.
  - Extreme heat in Oct–Nov.
  - Far from Lusaka services (about 470 km by road; editorial estimate).

### Kitwe (extra: Copperbelt mining capital)

Why it matters: Kitwe is Zambia's second city, with 661,901 people (2022) [28]. It is where mining-sector careers are found: Mopani Copper Mines, Konkola Copper Mines and Sandvik Mining, plus the Nkana Cobalt Plant [28].

- **Universities:** Copperbelt University, with 11,000+ students [28].
- **Hospitals:** Kitwe Central and private clinics such as Hillview and Progress [28].
- Mostly Bemba-speaking (editorial).
- Rent and safety specifics: not found.
- **Watch out:** mining pollution is a documented concern in Zambia. HRW reported an acid spill in Sept 2025 and covers lead poisoning in Kabwe (2025–2026) [17]. The Kabwe case is not in Kitwe, but it is relevant to mining towns.

### Ndola (extra: Copperbelt commercial hub)

Why it matters: Ndola is the third city (about 625,000, 2022), the "industrial and commercial center of the Copperbelt" [29].

- Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe International Airport has flights to Johannesburg, Nairobi and Addis Ababa [29].
- Cement and lime industries, and the Indeni refinery, which was placed on care and maintenance in 2022 [29][30].
- Private healthcare exists [26]. **Simba International School** offers boarding [6] (Expat.com itself flags this entry with a question mark; unverified).
- Cost of living: not found.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

*This is an interpretation of documented living conditions. Neither the cities nor any quiz question measure personality.* No Hofstede, GLOBE or other regional-personality data for Zambia was retrievable during this session; the Hofstede Insights page loaded without scores. Afrobarometer Round 10 offers civic-attitude data, such as 86% support for regular, open elections [16], but nothing on personality.

**Lusaka**
- **Openness:** suits people curious about a less-known, non-francophone capital with a multinational mix (South African, Chinese, Indian, British, Zimbabwean) [6]. Its rewards are quieter, such as music history and nature at its edge [7][25], rather than a famous cultural scene.
- **Conscientiousness:** the permit system is bureaucratic, and an employer must file for work permits [11]. Power cuts demand planning (backup power, schedules) [22]. Organised planners will cope better.
- **Extraversion:** social life runs through compounds, malls, church and football [4][13][24] rather than street nightlife. A moderate extravert finds enough; a party seeker may find it tame (editorial).
- **Agreeableness:** a strongly church-going, socially conservative society [13]. Very high opposition to LGBT neighbours (Afrobarometer 2021–23, 91.9%) [31]. Comfort with conservative social codes helps day to day.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):**
  - Long blackouts (5–7 hours of supply a day at the worst) [22].
  - Night-time crime and carjacking risk [2].
  - Post-election political tension in 2026 [15][17].
  - People who are easily stressed by unreliable services will find it hard.

**Livingstone**
- **Openness and extraversion:** suits outdoor and adventure profiles who enjoy rafting, the Falls and a tourist crowd [26][27].
- **Conscientiousness:** suits self-starters who can build a tourism business.
- **Emotional stability:** lower stress from traffic, but more isolation from healthcare [26].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**
  - Same-sex sexual activity is **illegal** for men and women. Penal Code s.155 provides 15 years to life; s.156 (attempt) 7 years; s.158 (gross indecency) 7–14 years. This is per the Wikipedia article (accessed 2026-11-14, latest events cited from 2018) [32].
  - Equaldex (accessed 2026-11-14) lists "up to life in prison" and a constitutional ban on same-sex marriage (2010 draft and 2007 law). It also lists censorship provisions (s.178(g)) [31].
  - The UK FCDO (2026) warns that convictions can bring a "long prison sentence" [2].
  - **Public opinion:**
    - Afrobarometer 2021–23: 91.9% would not want homosexual neighbours.
    - Gallup 2024: 11% say their area is good for gay and lesbian people [31].
  - Documented incidents include a 2014 trial with forced anal examinations (Amnesty) and a 2015 mob attack [32].
  - **High risk for LGBT diaspora.**
- **Racism, colourism and treatment of foreigners:** no reliable dated source found in this session. Large Chinese communities exist [6]. Reports of tension were not verified here.
- **Conflict and political violence:** no armed conflict. The FCDO says terrorism "cannot be ruled out" (2026) [2]. The **2026 post-election crackdown** is documented by HRW (1 Nov 2026) [17] and Wikipedia [15]: disappearances, detentions, one person killed during the arrest operation, and courts closed on the petition deadline.
- **Health alerts:** Ebola screening at borders with the DRC; no Zambian case had been declared as of France Diplomatie's update (3 Nov 2026) [3]. Measles alert (13 Mar 2026) [3]. Malaria, typhoid and HIV are cited by Expat.com (Oct 2025) [6].
- **Women's daily experience:** no dated quantitative source retrieved; data on gender-based violence was not found. The FCDO safety page has no women-specific section (checked 2026-11-14) [2]. **Gap.**
- **Press freedom:** constitutionally guaranteed but restricted in practice, with self-censorship (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14) [25]. Afrobarometer (25 Aug 2026): citizens want a free, watchdog media [16].
