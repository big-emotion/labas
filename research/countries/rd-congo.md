## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: French is the language of government, education, media and
  high-end commerce; **Lingala** is the street lingua franca in Kinshasa [6]. In Lubumbashi the
  main lingua franca is **Swahili** (Kiswahili), with Kiluba, Chokwe and Bemba also spoken [7].
  A Lingala-speaking diaspora member will feel at home in Kinshasa but needs Swahili in Lubumbashi.
- **Currency**: Congolese franc (CDF), but the economy is heavily **dollarised**: USD is used for
  rent, hotels, restaurants and supermarkets, CDF for small purchases. US notes must be recent
  and undamaged; old or worn $100 notes are refused or discounted [1]; $1 notes are refused almost
  everywhere [3]. Many prices are indexed on the USD, so the CDF/USD rate drives volatility [11].
  Card payments: France Diplomatie says only big hotels and a few Kinshasa restaurants (updated
  15 Sept 2026) [1]; Belgium says cards are "increasingly accepted" in Kinshasa and Lubumbashi
  hotels, restaurants and supermarkets (updated 27 Oct 2026) [3]. ATMs are rare in the capital [1].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders**: visa required; tourist visa for stays under 6 months,
  passport valid 6 months, **yellow-fever certificate mandatory** [1][4]. For stays over six months
  you apply for a residence permit in the DRC, with the Direction générale de Migration (DGM) or
  the Ministry of Foreign Affairs [1]. To work: a **Visa d'établissement de travail (VET)** from
  the Labour ministry, while the employer requests a **Visa d'établissement systématique (VETS)**
  from the DGM; a "visa de courtoisie" does not allow work [1] (France Diplomatie, updated
  15 Sept 2026).
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots**: Congolese nationality is **exclusive** (Constitution, art. 10):
  acquiring another nationality means losing the Congolese one [12][13]. In February 2026 the
  government announced a **gradual** reform, starting with a "statut spécial" for Congolese abroad
  as an interim measure, with no published content yet [12]. The Ifri study (Oct 2026) sums it
  up: dual nationality is "interdite de jure et tolérée de facto", and change is "peu probable
  dans un avenir proche" [14]. The minister for the diaspora floated dual nationality excluding
  nationals of neighbouring countries in November 2025 [15]. **No diaspora visa or right-of-return
  scheme found.** In practice a Franco-Congolais enters with a French passport and a visa.
  The diaspora got the right to vote in 2023 [14].

## Cities

### Kinshasa

- **Cost of living**
  - 1-bed apartment, centre: **USD 1,200–1,500/month** (≈ EUR 1,030–1,290); outskirts USD 400–800
    (Expat.com, updated 24 Nov 2025) [8]. Numbeo: centre USD 1,675, outside centre USD 550
    (15 contributors, updated 6 Nov 2026) [9].
  - Neighbourhoods: **Gombe** (business and government centre, "most reliable services"; flats
    USD 1,500–5,000), **Ngaliema** (residential, river views, from USD 2,000), **Ma Campagne**
    (families), **Kintambo** (from USD 1,000) [8]. Cheaper popular communes: Masina, Ndjili, Lemba,
    with a 2-bed at USD 200–600 [11]. Limete, Kasavubu and Kinshasa-Clémenceau also cited as
    middle-to-upper areas [10][11].
  - Leases often require **three months' rent upfront**, plus a deposit of one to three months;
    utilities USD 150–300/month on top [8].
    Africarrières adds generator USD 200–600 and security guard USD 100–200 [11].
  - Meal: local restaurant USD 3–5, expat restaurant USD 15–30 (OpenShores, 2 May 2026) [10];
    Numbeo "inexpensive restaurant" USD 18 [9].
  - Monthly budget: USD 1,500–2,500 for a comfortable expatriate life, USD 800–1,200 on a tight
    budget [10]. Kinshasa is the most expensive city in the country [10]; in a 2016 ranking it was
    Africa's most expensive city for expatriate staff [6] (old, flag).
- **Work & money**: business, government, telecoms, banking, manufacturing and entertainment [6].
  An estimated 30,000–50,000 expatriates, mostly Lebanese, Chinese, French, Belgian, Indian [8].
  Salaries: not found. Remote work: possible only with a generator and private internet (see below).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: **Kadea** (its social accounts still use the "Kinshasa Digital"
  handle; the former-name link is not stated on the site) runs Kadea Academy since 2019
  (tech talent training and placement) with partners Vodacom, Rawbank, GIZ, Enabel, Digital Africa;
  offices in Kinshasa, Lubumbashi, Kolwezi, Brussels, Paris [16]. Other hubs, investors, fintech and
  crypto adoption, company-registration ease: **not found in this pass**. The Ifri study warns that
  returning diaspora entrepreneurs often hit established business circles that see them as
  competitors, and many go back to France in debt [14].
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - Power cuts frequent; a generator is "essential" [8]. Expat housing comes with generators and
    water storage [8].
  - Internet USD 30–80/month (OpenShores) to USD 80–150 (Africarrières) [10][11]; Numbeo broadband
    60 Mbps+ USD 70 [9]. Mobile operators: Orange, Vodacom, Airtel [3]. Internet use was 22.9 % of the
    population in 2023 [17]; the country has a history of internet shutdowns around political
    events (2011, 2015, 2016) [17].
  - Traffic is severe: a 25 km trip can take 2–3 hours at peak [8]; traffic worsened with
    unfinished road works [1]. Public transport, two-wheelers and walking alone are strongly
    discouraged for foreigners [1]. **Yango** is the only ride-hailing app authorised since the
    July 2025 ban on seven competitors [6]. MetroKin urban rail: planned, not before 2026 [6]. The
    Matadi–Kinshasa railway reopened to passengers in 2025 [6].
- **Health & schools**
  - Good GPs, specialists and labs exist, mostly in Kinshasa and Lubumbashi; everything is paid
    upfront; public hospitals rarely give quality care; evacuation insurance strongly advised [1][4].
    Named reference hospitals: not found.
  - Schools: **Lycée français René Descartes** (French curriculum), **TASOK** (American, IB,
    USD 15,000–25,000/yr), **Jewels International School** (IB), **Lycée Prince de Liège**
    (Belgian curriculum); other schools USD 8,000–20,000/yr; enrolment March–June [8].
  - Disease context: Ebola outbreak declared 15 May 2026 in Ituri, a WHO public health emergency;
    eight provinces affected as of 16 Oct 2026 (Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Haut-Uélé, Tshopo, Sud-Kivu,
    Bas-Uélé, Sud-Ubangi, Nord-Ubangi), **Kinshasa not among them** [1][4]. A prolonged cholera
    epidemic running since early 2025 now affects **21 provinces** (France Diplomatie, updated
    16 Oct 2026); bottled or treated water only, vaccination may be advised [1]. A few mpox cases in
    Kinshasa [4]; chikungunya present, notably in Kinshasa and Kongo Central [1].
- **Safety** (dated)
  - France Diplomatie (updated 15 Sept 2026): whole country orange "sauf raison impérative";
    insecurity in the capital "tend à se dégrader avec la situation économique"; armed robbery,
    burglary, car-jacking, **fake police checkpoints**, blackmail on false accusations, especially
    against foreigners alone at night; permanent home guarding advised [1].
  - Belgium (updated 27 Oct 2026): "very high" urban crime in Kinshasa and Lubumbashi; avoid
    walking, public transport and taxis at night; a violent protest targeted embassies on
    28 Jan 2025 [2]. French and Belgian embassies advised staying home during an opposition march
    on 22 July 2026 [5] (unverified: source seen in search results only).
  - Batéké plateau (RN1, RN17) and Kwamouth territory near the capital: Mobondo militia;
    Kwamouth, Kwilu and Kwango are red zones for France [1][2].
  - Neighbourhood nuance: Gombe, Ngaliema and gated compounds are where expatriates live [8];
    youth gangs operate from poorer areas [6]. Finer street-level data: not found.
- **Social life & vibe**: UNESCO City of Music since 2015; cradle of Congolese rumba (Franco,
  TPOK Jazz) [6]. Music and *la sape* are identity markers of the Congolese diaspora [14].
  Specific nightlife districts, food scene, sport, weight of religion: not found in sources opened.
  Population ≈ 18.5 million (2026 estimate) [6]: dense, loud and fast.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com Kinshasa forum ≈ 2,400 members [8]. Returnee networks:
  **Congo na Paris** forum (since 2015, founder Charlotte Kalala) and its fair **Tonga Mboka**
  (7th edition, France, Sept 2025) [14]. Congolese say "diaspo" for a diaspora member; Ifri notes
  that "être d'origine congolaise n'est pas suffisant pour être le bienvenu" in business [14].
  YouTube "return advisers" push real estate in the Maluku special economic zone; Ifri treats this
  with scepticism [14]. Public Facebook/WhatsApp groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: rainy season October–May, wettest November (≈ 247 mm); dry season
  June–September, July–August almost rainless; daily highs 28–33 °C, humidity ≈ 80 % [6]. Rains
  worsen traffic and flooding (flooding not sourced in this pass).
- **Watch out**: very high rents for secure housing; generator + guard + driver costs; extreme
  traffic; crime and fake police; protests around political dates; dollar-note fussiness;
  returnee projects often fail on local business networks [14].

### Lubumbashi

- **Cost of living**
  - 1-bed, centre: **EUR 550–1,350/month**; periphery EUR 270–720; 3-bed house in **Golf**
    EUR 2,700–4,500; simple house in a local neighbourhood EUR 180–450 (Expat.com, updated
    19 Dec 2025) [18]. Numbeo: centre USD 850, outside USD 375 (only 4 contributors, low
    confidence, updated 17 June 2026) [19].
  - Neighbourhoods: **Golf** (preferred by foreigners: golf course, enhanced security, next to the
    business district), **Centre-ville**, new subdivisions near the university [18]. Historic popular
    quarters: Kenya, Katuba, Ruashi, Kamalondo [7].
  - Deposit 3 months + 1 month agency commission [18].
  - Rents 20–50 % below Kinshasa according to OpenShores and Africarrières [10][11]; mining has
    pushed prices up [10]. Meal: Numbeo USD 10 inexpensive restaurant [19].
  - Monthly budget: not found specifically (estimate editorially ≈ 20–40 % below Kinshasa).
- **Work & money**: centre of the Copperbelt mining industry, with many of the country's largest
  mining companies, plus copper smelting, textiles, food and drink, and a major bank headquartered
  there [7]. Expatriates ≈ 15,000–20,000, mostly Chinese, Belgian, Indian, Lebanese, South
  African [18]. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: Kadea has an office in Lubumbashi [16]. Other hubs, investors,
  crypto: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: intermittent power, outages sometimes lasting hours or days;
  expat housing has generators and water tanks [18]. Mobile coverage adequate (Vodacom, Airtel,
  Orange); internet "slow and expensive" [18]; Numbeo broadband USD 60 [19]. No organised public
  transport: minibus taxis, moto-taxis; most expats use a car with a driver [18]. Ride-hailing
  apps: not found. Luano airport links to Kinshasa and some international routes [18].
- **Health & schools**: good private doctors concentrated in Kinshasa and Lubumbashi [1][4].
  Schools: **AFEEL** (British curriculum), **British International School Lubumbashi**; Expat.com
  says there is **no accredited French lycée**, French families use CNED distance learning or
  boarding in southern Africa [18]. France Diplomatie does list an **École française Blaise Pascal
  de Lubumbashi** (15 Sept 2026) [1]; the grades it covers were not confirmed, so assume
  primary/lower secondary until checked. International fees EUR 4,500–22,500/yr [18]. Hospitals: not found. Cholera endemic in the Katanga region [1].
- **Safety** (dated): Belgium (27 Oct 2026): volatile situation, heightened vigilance evening and
  night, violent banditry regularly reported in Lubumbashi, militia activity rising in
  Haut-Katanga, Lualaba (Kolwezi's province), Haut-Lomami and Tanganyika [2]. France (15 Sept 2026): crime rising in Lubumbashi [1]. Haut-Katanga is not a red
  zone for France or Belgium. Neighbourhood nuance: Golf is the secured area [18].
- **Social life & vibe**: music mixing Swahili, Lingala, Kiluba and French (Jean-Bosco Mwenda);
  an art **biennale**, national museum, botanical garden, zoo; football club **TP Mazembe**, the
  most successful Congolese club in Africa [7]. Smaller (≈ 2.6 million urban, 2021) and calmer in
  scale than Kinshasa [7]. Nightlife, religion: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com forum 667 members [18]; Belgian consulate-general [3].
  Diaspora groups: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: altitude ≈ 1,208 m keeps it cooler than Kinshasa; rains in the southern
  summer (roughly Nov–Apr, ≈ 1,238 mm/year); **June–August almost fully dry** and cool [7].
  Noticeably more comfortable heat-wise than Kinshasa.
- **Watch out**: Swahili not Lingala; no accredited French lycée (a French school exists, levels
  unconfirmed); long power cuts; prices pulled up by
  mining salaries; rising banditry and militia activity in the wider province [2].

### Kolwezi (extra city, low confidence)

Why it matters: capital of Lualaba province (since 2015), copper and cobalt mining hub whose
population is rising with the cobalt boom; rail and road link to Lubumbashi [20]. Kadea lists it
as an office location [16]. Population ≈ 573,000 (2015) [20]. A city for mining/engineering
careers, not for lifestyle. Cost, safety and services: not found in this pass.

*Not proposed: Goma and Bukavu.* Both are occupied by M23/AFC (Belgium, 27 Oct 2026) and North
and South Kivu are red zones [1][2]; Goma airport is closed [2]. Under Là-bas rules they are
`unsafe` and never proposed.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No academic regional personality study on the DRC was found in this pass (Hofstede, GLOBE and
Afrobarometer DRC data not checked). Below are editorial readings of cited facts, not measurements.

**Kinshasa**
- Openness: rewards people who love music, street culture and improvisation: UNESCO City of Music,
  birthplace of rumba [6], sape as a cultural marker [14].
- Conscientiousness: helps in a city where leases need 3 months upfront, utilities need a generator
  plan, and work requires the right visa (VET/VETS) [1][8].
- Extraversion: suits people energised by a crowded megacity of ≈ 18.5 million [6] and by networks:
  Ifri shows that without local networks, returnees fail [14].
- Agreeableness: helpful but not enough; returnees with "pro-business" approaches clash with
  entrenched interests [14]. Diplomatic patience matters more than frankness.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed: 2–3 h commutes [8], fake checkpoints and crime
  [1], protests around political dates [2][5].

**Lubumbashi**
- Openness: multilingual (Swahili, French, Lingala, Kiluba) art-biennale city [7].
- Conscientiousness: a mining-economy city; structured professionals find their place [7][18].
- Extraversion: football (TP Mazembe) is a strong social glue [7]; smaller scale than Kinshasa.
- Agreeableness: expatriate life is compound- and community-based (Golf) [18].
- Emotional stability: cooler climate [7] and smaller city, but long power cuts and rising
  banditry [2][18].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**: same-sex activity has never been explicitly criminalised (equal age of consent); same-sex marriage banned by
  the 2006 Constitution (art. 40); no anti-discrimination protection; public-indecency provisions
  are sometimes used against same-sex couples, and LGBTQI+ identity "remained a cultural taboo"
  with harassment and violence (US State Dept 2021 report, as quoted by Wikipedia, accessed
  2026-11-14) [21]. Any 2023–2026 criminalisation bill: not found.
- **Treatment of diaspora/foreigners**: foreigners alone on foot or alone at night are prime
  targets for crime and blackmail (France Diplomatie, 15 Sept 2026) [1]. Ifri (Oct 2026): returning
  Franco-Congolais are often disappointed; "être d'origine congolaise n'est pas suffisant pour être
  le bienvenu", and some return to France in debt [14]. A Kinshasa political discourse that "tout
  Congolais n'est pas Congolais" weighs on the dual-nationality debate [14]. Racism/colourism
  reports: not found.
- **Conflict**: M23/AFC, backed by Rwandan forces, holds Goma and Bukavu; ADF attacks in Beni,
  Lubero, Mambasa, Irumu; Ituri and North Kivu under state of siege since May 2021 (Belgium,
  27 Oct 2026) [2]. Red zones for France also include Kwilu, Kwango and Kwamouth (Mobondo militia)
  near Kinshasa (15 Sept 2026) [1]. UK FCDO advises against all travel to Haut-Uélé, Ituri and
  border zones; Kasaï zones were downgraded on 16 May 2026 [22] (unverified: FCDO changes seen in
  search results, live page not opened). France still advises against non-essential travel to
  the three Kasaï provinces (15 Sept 2026) [1].
- **Health emergency**: Ebola outbreak since 15 May 2026, WHO PHEIC, eight provinces as of
  16 Oct 2026, not Kinshasa or Haut-Katanga [1][4].
- **Women's daily experience**: sexual assault is listed among violent crimes (France Diplomatie,
  15 Sept 2026) [1]. No reliable dedicated source found in this pass.
