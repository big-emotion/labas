## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: Swahili is the national language and most people speak it. English is used in business, tourism and secondary education [S7, 2025-11-07]. French is marginal: no source found on French-speaking networks. For a francophone, life in Swahili and English is the norm.
- **Currency**: Tanzanian shilling (TZS). Mobile money (M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa, Airtel Money) is widely used. ATMs give TZS 400,000–800,000 per withdrawal, with fees of about TZS 5,000–10,000 on foreign cards [S7, 2025-11-07].
- **Entry (French/EU passport)**: a visa is required. An e-visa is advised, and single-entry visas are available on arrival at main ports [S3, undated FCDO page, read 2026-11-14]. **New since 1 Oct 2026**: all foreign visitors who are not residents must buy government inbound travel insurance, US$44 per person, from ZIC (Zanzibar) or NIC (mainland). It covers stays of up to 92 days, and you may be refused entry without it [S3]. France Diplomatie strongly advises keeping private medical and repatriation cover, because many health facilities do not accept the state insurance [S4, France Diplomatie, 2026-11-12].
- **Residence/work**: permits go through the online OWAIS system. Class A covers investors, Class B employees and Class C retirees (usually 60+). Class B costs about US$2,000–3,000 at first and lasts up to 2 years, renewable, with an 8-year maximum. A Special Temporary Permit costs US$550 for 6 months [S7, 2025-11-07]. Work or volunteering needs a work permit on top of the visa, and overstaying can lead to arrest and deportation [S3]. The official immigration site (immigration.go.tz) did not return usable content when fetched, so fees are unverified from the official source.
- **Zanzibar residency through property**: buyers registered with ZIPA (Zanzibar Investment Promotion Agency) who buy property worth US$100,000 or more can get residency permits for themselves, a spouse and up to 4 children. This applies to the Fumba special zone [S17, Wikipedia, undated].
- **Diaspora with Tanzanian roots**: Tanzania **does not allow dual nationality** for adults. Multiple nationals lose Tanzanian citizenship at majority unless they renounce the other nationality (Tanzania Citizenship Act 1995) [S11, Wikipedia, last edited 2025-08-25]. A diaspora "special status" with a "Diaspora Tanzanite Card" (residence and land rights for ex-Tanzanians holding another citizenship) was tabled in 2024. On 31 Jan 2025 the Speaker pulled the Immigration Act and Land Act amendments from Bill No. 4 of 2024 again, saying Parliament was "still working on" them [S20, Mwananchi, 2025-01-31]. No source checked in this pass shows that it was enacted after that, so treat it as **not in force (unverified after Jan 2025)**. There is no Afro-descendant right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's (none found).
- **Political context**: the general election of 29 Oct 2025 gave President Samia Suluhu Hassan 97.66%. Protests followed, with a near-nationwide internet shutdown and a curfew in Dar es Salaam until 4 Nov 2025. The UN human rights office confirmed at least 10 deaths. The opposition first claimed ~700, and Chadema raised its count to 2,000 on 5 Nov 2025. SADC and AU observers said the vote fell short of their standards [S13]. France Diplomatie's security item (updated 2026-06-08) still describes political instability and protests "d'une ampleur inédite" in Oct–Nov 2025. It warns that protests over fuel prices could bring internet cuts, shortages and night curfews in large cities [S4, 2026-11-12].

## Cities

### Dar es Salaam

The economic capital: about 5.4 M people in the 2022 census, more than 7 M by 2023 estimates. Dodoma has been the official capital since 1996, but most government offices remain in Dar [S16].

- **Cost of living**:
  - Numbeo (8 Nov 2026): a 1-bed in the city centre costs ~TZS 1.40 M/month (~€480), with a range of TZS 600k–2.2 M. Outside the centre it is ~TZS 517k (~€177) [S8].
  - Expat.com (Dec 2025): a 1-bed in the city centre costs US$500–800 and a 2-bed in an expat area US$800–1,600. A 3-bed house in Masaki or Oyster Bay costs US$2,500–4,000 [S6].
  - Rio Times (Nov 2026, unconfirmed asking prices): a 2-bed in Masaki costs US$1,200–1,500 and a furnished 1-bed ~US$1,700. Some landlords ask for **6 months upfront** [S5]. Expat.com says leases need 3–12 months upfront, plus a deposit of 1–3 months [S6].
  - Neighbourhoods:
    - Masaki, Oyster Bay and Msasani: premium, close to embassies, schools, The Slipway and Sea Cliff Village.
    - Mikocheni: cheaper, with good road links.
    - Mbezi Beach: families, larger homes [S6].
  - A cheap meal costs TZS 5,000 (~€1.70) and a mid-range dinner for two TZS 50,000 (~€17) [S8].
  - Monthly budget: no sourced all-in figure was opened. Search snippets suggested US$1,000–3,000 for an expat lifestyle, but these come from aggregators with unclear methods (low confidence).
- **Work & money**:
  - Main hub for business and finance: Bank of Tanzania, Dar es Salaam Stock Exchange, and a port that handles ~90% of national cargo and serves Rwanda, Burundi and Zambia [S16].
  - Remote work is feasible in principle: 60 Mbps unlimited internet costs about TZS 90k/month (~€31) [S8]. But the internet was shut down nationally around the 2025 election [S13], so do not depend on it for client deadlines during political events.
  - Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - Hubs: in March 2026 COSTECH (the national science and technology commission) launched the Tanzania Ventures Lab, a four-year programme that aims to turn 1,000 startup ideas into businesses by 2029. It runs with the Dar Teknohama Business Incubator (DTBi), Buni Innovation Hub and Sahara Ventures, all Dar-based [S18, TanzaniaInvest, 2026-03-16].
  - Mobile money is widespread [S7].
  - Crypto: it is not legal tender and the Bank of Tanzania does not regulate it. In July 2026 the central bank sent a draft regulatory concept for crypto, stablecoins and other digital assets to the Ministry of Finance, with no date set for rules [S19, The Citizen, 2026-07-30]. There is no sign of a crypto hub comparable to Nairobi's.
  - Investor presence and salaries: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - Transport: the DART bus rapid transit ("mwendo kasi") runs Phase I from Kimara to Kivukoni, opened 2016 [S16], and Phase II from Gerezani to Mbagala, opened late 2025, at ~TZS 750 a trip [S6].
  - Traffic is heavy at 7–9 AM and 4–7 PM. Uber operates [S6].
  - Utilities for an air-conditioned home add US$200–400/month [S6].
  - Power cuts and water: not found (Rio Times could not confirm electricity prices [S5]).
- **Health & schools**:
  - Private hospitals: Aga Khan Hospital and Premier Care Medical Center [S7].
  - International schools:
    - International School of Tanganyika (IB, founded 1963): US$16,700–34,600/year plus a US$8,500 building fee [S6].
    - Braeburn (British curriculum).
    - Dar es Salaam International Academy (IB).
    - HOPAC (Cambridge, ~US$5,000–8,000) [S6].
  - French school: École française Arthur Rimbaud, a bilingual French–English school for ages 2–18 that is reported to be in the AEFE network (unverified: seen only in search snippets from school directories and the French embassy, without opening an official page). Check the AEFE directory before relying on it.
- **Safety**:
  - FCDO reports crime against tourists and residents in the city centre, in **Masaki and Oyster Bay (especially Coco Beach)** and along Toure Drive, and reports **express kidnappings** [S1, read 2026-11-14].
  - The curfew and live-fire episodes after the Oct 2025 election were centred on Dar [S13]. France Diplomatie advises avoiding gatherings and travel at night [S4, 2026-11-12].
- **Social life & vibe**: Bongo Flava (Tanzanian hip hop and R&B) is the dominant local genre, alongside the older muziki wa dansi and taarab [S16]. Expat social life centres on Masaki, Oyster Bay and The Slipway [S6]. Nightlife venues: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community**:
  - Expat.com's Dar community lists ~2,321 members [S6]. Expat.com estimates ~70,000 expats in Tanzania, the largest groups being Indian, Chinese, British, Kenyan and South African [S6].
  - Facebook and WhatsApp groups and returnee networks: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: hot and humid all year. The long rains fall in April–May and the short rains in Nov–Dec, and the driest months are July–August [S16]. France Diplomatie warns of above-normal El Niño rains and floods in the coming rainy season [S4, 2026-11-12].
- **Watch out**:
  - Large advance rent payments.
  - Traffic.
  - Express kidnapping and robbery even in expat districts [S1].
  - Protests, curfews and internet shutdowns around political flashpoints [S4, S13].
  - Very high school fees: two primary-age children at IST cost ~US$54,350/year [S5].

### Zanzibar

Zanzibar City and Stone Town (UNESCO site) on Unguja, a semi-autonomous region of Tanzania. The archipelago has 1.89 M people (2022), 219k of them in Zanzibar City [S15].

- **Cost of living**:
  - Numbeo (2 Oct 2026, only 8 contributors, so low confidence): a 1-bed in the centre costs ~TZS 397k (~€136) and outside the centre ~TZS 264k (~€91). A cheap meal costs TZS 12,500 (~€4.30), more than in Dar, which reflects tourist pricing. A mid-range dinner for two costs TZS 57,500 (~€20) [S9].
  - Internet is expensive: ~TZS 130k/month (~€45) [S9].
  - Neighbourhoods: Stone Town, and the new Fumba Town on the Fumba peninsula, a special economic zone [S17].
- **Work & money**: tourism dominates (917,000 arrivals in 2025), followed by spices (cloves, nutmeg), fishing and seaweed or algae farming [S15]. Outside tourism and hospitality, the local job market is narrow (inference).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - The **"Silicon Zanzibar"** initiative started in August 2022 and promised a streamlined work visa for tech firms. In the Fumba zone, investors pay zero tax on worldwide net income for 10 years [S17].
  - Results have been mixed: Wasoko moved 40+ tech staff there, then closed its Zanzibar office around its 2024 merger with MaxAB [S17].
  - IIT Madras opened a Zanzibar campus in Fumba in 2023 [S17].
- **Daily infrastructure**: not found (power, water, ride-hailing).
- **Health & schools**: Zanzibar Medical and Diagnostic Centre [S7]. Schools: not found.
- **Safety**: FCDO reports incidents in Stone Town, at hotels and on popular beaches, and a risk of armed robbery and bag snatching across the island [S1]. It advises modest dress outside resorts [S1].
- **Social life & vibe**: ~98% Muslim [S15], so religion shapes dress, the rhythm of daily life and Ramadan. Taarab music has strong roots here (general knowledge, not separately sourced). The pace is slow and island-like.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: tropical monsoon climate. The main rains fall in March–May, with a second peak in Nov–Dec, and July is the driest month [S15].
- **Watch out**:
  - Conservative social norms.
  - Zanzibar has its own penal code, which also criminalises same-sex unions [S12].
  - A tourist-priced economy.
  - Expensive internet [S9].
  - A thin job market outside tourism.

### Arusha

617,631 residents (2022) [S14]. The gateway to the northern safari circuit and Kilimanjaro, and a diplomatic and legal hub.

- **Cost of living**:
  - Numbeo (1 Nov 2026, only 6 contributors, so low confidence): a 1-bed in the centre costs ~TZS 750k (~€257) and outside the centre ~TZS 625k (~€214). A cheap meal costs ~TZS 8,850 (~€3) and a mid-range dinner for two ~TZS 70,800 (~€24) [S10].
  - Rio Times found no published 2025–2026 rent survey for Njiro, Sakina or the centre [S5].
  - Gated neighbourhoods: Dolly Estate (near Usa River, popular with expats), PPF (Njiro), Kwa Mawala, Westlands (Sakina) and Kili Golf [S14].
- **Work & money**:
  - Headquarters of the **East African Community**, the **African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights** and a branch of the **IRMCT** (UN criminal tribunals). These institutions have pushed up local living costs [S14].
  - Other sectors: flower and horticulture exports to Europe [S14], and safari tourism.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: not found.
- **Daily infrastructure**: internet ~TZS 125k/month (~€43) and basic utilities ~TZS 75k [S10]. Everything else: not found.
- **Health & schools**:
  - Hospital: Arusha Lutheran Medical Centre [S7].
  - Schools: Arusha International School (US$10,000–15,000/year) and Braeburn Arusha (US$11,000–16,000). UWC East Africa in Moshi is a boarding school at ~US$48,000 [S7].
- **Safety**: city-specific FCDO advice: not found [S1].
- **Social life & vibe**: a smaller and greener city, with an outdoor and safari life and a community of international civil servants and NGO staff (inference from [S14]). Nightlife: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: not found.
- **Climate in practice**: cooler because of the altitude. Highs are ~28°C in Dec–Feb and ~20°C in July. The long rains fall in March–May (April ~360 mm) and the short rains in Nov–Dec, and June–Oct is dry [S14].
- **Watch out**: thin data. International salaries push up costs. The city depends on tourism seasons.

### Dodoma (other city: official capital)

Dodoma has been the official capital since 1996 (relocation completed that year), but most government offices remained in Dar [S16]. It matters for anyone working with government. **No other data collected (not found).**

### Moshi (other city: family/education angle)

Home of UWC East Africa [S7] and close to Kilimanjaro. It could suit families who prefer a quiet life near Arusha. **No other data collected (not found).**

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These describe who tends to thrive in each city, judged from the cited facts. They are not measurements. No academic study of regional personality in Tanzania was opened in this pass. Hofstede and GLOBE country scores were not verified here.

**Dar es Salaam**
- Openness: suits people who enjoy a large, mixed port city with Indian, Chinese, British and Kenyan expats [S6] and a strong local music scene (Bongo Flava) [S16].
- Conscientiousness: helps with landlords who want 3–12 months upfront [S6] and with OWAIS permit paperwork [S7].
- Extraversion: rewarded by a social life that clusters in Masaki, Oyster Bay and The Slipway [S6].
- Agreeableness: Swahili-first daily life [S7] rewards people who make the effort to adapt.
- Emotional stability (stress tolerance): needed for heavy traffic [S6], express-kidnapping risk [S1], and curfews or internet cuts around politics [S4, S13].

**Zanzibar**
- Openness: suits people interested in Swahili-Arab heritage (Stone Town, UNESCO) [S15].
- Conscientiousness: helps for anyone investing through ZIPA or Fumba [S17].
- Extraversion: life is calmer outside tourist venues (inference).
- Agreeableness: comfort with conservative codes matters. The island is ~98% Muslim and modest dress is advised [S15, S1].
- Emotional stability: tested by a narrow job market and the gap between the tourist economy and local life (inference).

**Arusha**
- Openness: suits people drawn to nature and the outdoors (Kilimanjaro, the parks) [S14].
- Conscientiousness: fits structured careers at international institutions (EAC, African Court, IRMCT) [S14].
- Extraversion: a smaller social scene (inference).
- Agreeableness: an internationalist community (inference).
- Emotional stability: a calmer, cooler city than Dar (inference from climate [S14]).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex sexual activity is illegal on the mainland and in Zanzibar [S1, FCDO, read 2026-11-14]. Mainland Penal Code s.154 carries a minimum of 30 years and up to life imprisonment [S12, Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-14].
  - Zanzibar punishes male acts with up to 14 years, female acts with up to 5 years, and same-sex unions with up to 7 years [S12].
  - In 2018, the Dar es Salaam Regional Commissioner announced a committee to identify gay people, and Human Rights Watch documented forced anal exams [S12, accessed 2026-11-14; events of Nov 2018]. Public displays of affection can lead to arrest [S1].
- **Political repression / unrest**:
  - The Oct 2025 election was followed by deadly protests, an internet shutdown and a curfew [S13].
  - FCDO says security services may search phones for content related to the election, and sharing protest footage can lead to detention [S1].
  - Political instability is still ongoing per France Diplomatie [S4, 2026-11-12].
- **Conflict zones**: FCDO advises against all but essential travel within 20 km of the border with Cabo Delgado, Mozambique, because of IS-linked attacks [S2, updated 2026-10-30]. Terrorism "cannot be ruled out" anywhere [S1].
- **Women's daily experience**: there are reports of verbal harassment of women travelling alone and of sexual assaults, including at beach resorts. Modest dress is advised in Zanzibar and Pemba [S1].
- **Racism/colourism or treatment of the diaspora**: no reliable source found in this pass.
- **Health alerts**: the WHO declared the Ebola outbreak in the DRC a public health emergency of international concern, and Kenya confirmed its first cases in October 2026. No case had been declared in Tanzania. Since 17 Oct 2026, arrivals fill in an online health declaration (Afyamsafiri) and have their temperature checked. France Diplomatie also lists a regional alert about rising measles cases, dated 2026-03-13 [S4, 2026-11-12].
