## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages day to day**: Portuguese is the official language (administration, schools, press); Cape Verdean Creole (Kriolu / Kabuverdianu) is what people actually speak at home, in the street and in music [S5]. 56% of Cabo Verdeans want Creole made co-official with Portuguese (Afrobarometer, Round 10, page accessed 2026-11-14) [S9]. French is taught in schools and is understood by part of the educated population, but the country is **not francophone in daily life**. No dated source measures how much French is spoken: "not found".
- **Currency**: Cape Verdean escudo (CVE), pegged to the euro since 1999 [S6]. The fixed rate is 110.265 CVE = 1 EUR, backed by a Portuguese government credit facility (Wikipedia, Cape Verdean escudo, accessed 2026-11-14) [S21]; it is used for the conversions below.
- **Population and diaspora**: 491,233 residents at the 2021 census [S5]. The diaspora is larger than the resident population, with an estimated 700,000 to 1 million abroad, mainly in the USA, Portugal, the Netherlands, France and Luxembourg (Africarrières, 2026-08-06) [S11]. Remittances add more than 20% of GDP [S6]. 40% of Cabo Verdeans say they have thought a lot about emigrating (Afrobarometer R10) [S9].
- **Economy**: services make up 69.4% of GDP (2024 est.). Unemployment was 11.9% and youth unemployment 28.4% in 2025. GDP per capita was USD 5,420 nominal (2025) [S6]. Tourism is concentrated on Sal and Boa Vista, and Sal held 48.2% of the country's hotel beds in 2017 [S16].
- **Politics**: a stable democracy. Cape Verde was ranked joint 2nd in Africa on V-Dem electoral democracy in 2026 [S5].
- **(a) Entry and residence for French and EU passport holders**:
  - Before arrival, complete the online **EASE** pre-registration and pay the airport security fee (TSA). Expat.com forum guidance (circa 2022–2024, unofficial) says to do this 2–3 weeks before travel and no later than 5 days before [S4]. The TSA is due to rise from 3,400 to 4,400 CVE, and the Tourism Chamber has asked for a delay until May 2027 (Expresso das Ilhas, 2026-11-14) [S10].
  - EU citizens (including French) have not needed a visa since 1 January 2019 under the EU–Cabo Verde agreement; the visa-free stay is 30 days (EEAS archived notice, January 2019 [S22]; Visa policy of Cape Verde, accessed 2026-11-14 [S23]). The claim that it can be extended to 90 days comes only from an Expat.com consultant (circa 2022–2024) and is unverified [S4].
  - **Non-EU passports (most African diaspora passports)**: visa on arrival was abolished for citizens of 96 countries from 1 January 2026, so they must get a visa before travelling; EASE registration remains mandatory for everyone [S23]. Check the exact list on ease.gov.cv before booking.
  - **Remote Working Program (digital nomad)**: 6 months, renewable once, so 12 months at most. The fee is €20 per person plus a €34 airport fee. Applicants need health insurance, a criminal record check and proof of remote income. Local employment is not allowed. Older coverage cites an average bank balance of about €1,500 per month for one person and €2,700 for a family, but no official figure is published. A one-year income-tax (IRPS) exemption is claimed but not confirmed in tax law, and a stay of more than 183 days triggers tax residence. Sources: Stamped Nomad (dated 2026-10-27, checked 2026-10-09) and Nomad Watch (verified 2026-10-09). The two sources contradict each other on income and fees [S7][S8].
  - **Longer residence**: apply after arrival at immigration in Praia, which takes several weeks. The documents listed are a passport, bank statement and apostilled criminal record [S4]. Stamped Nomad says the separate remote-work residence visa (fee CVE 5,000) was marked "coming soon" on the immigration portal and not accepting applications when it checked (October 2026) [S7]. Naturalisation requires 5 years of residence, which can be waived for major investment [S12].
- **(b) Diaspora with Cape Verdean roots**:
  - Dual nationality has been allowed since 1992. Only the President must hold Cape Verdean nationality alone [S12].
  - Children of Cape Verdean nationals born abroad acquire nationality by descent, and people with at least one Cape Verdean grandparent are eligible. People of Cape Verdean ancestry can naturalise **without a residence period** (Law 80/III/90, as summarised on Wikipedia) [S12]. In practice this is a strong right-of-return route for the large Lusophone and Franco-Cape Verdean diaspora.
  - The government encourages return migration (Africarrières, 2026-08-06) [S11]. No named official returnee programme was found: "not found".
  - There is no Afro-descendant scheme for people without Cape Verdean roots, like Ghana's or Benin's: "not found".

## Cities

### Praia (Santiago island) — capital

- **Cost of living**:
  - A 1-bedroom flat costs on average 56,667 CVE (~€514) in the centre, range 30,000–80,000 (~€272–725). Outside the centre it averages 28,333 CVE (~€257), range 20,000–35,000 (~€181–317). Source: Numbeo, 68 entries from 7 contributors, updated 2026-10-02, so a small sample [S3].
  - A cheap restaurant meal costs about 496 CVE (~€4.50), and home internet (60 Mbps+) about 5,067 CVE (~€46) per month [S3].
  - Neighbourhoods often cited for expats and the middle class are Palmarejo, Achada Santo António, Prainha/Quebra Canela and the Plateau (the historic centre). This is editorial knowledge; no rent source by neighbourhood was found.
  - No monthly budget estimate was found. An editorial guess is €900–1,300 for a single person renting in the centre (?).
- **Work & money**: the city's economy runs on government, commerce, health, education and services [S13]. The main formal employers are the state, banks, telecoms and NGOs or international organisations. The ECOWAS and UN presence is editorial knowledge, not sourced. No salary data was found. Remote work is feasible through the Remote Working Program, and immigration headquarters are in Praia [S4][S7].
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**:
  - TechPark CV, a state technology park with a data centre and incubation, exists according to editorial knowledge, but its site failed to load (certificate error), so it is unverified.
  - In October 2026 the bank BAICV opened the country's first mobile bank branch (Expresso das Ilhas) [S10].
  - On 2026-11-13, 18 of 23 Cape Verdean expressions of interest were selected to submit full proposals to the Luxembourg-backed Blue Natural Capital Financing Facility (blue-economy projects, not a startup fund; selection is not yet funding) [S10].
  - Not found: a named incubator or coworking space, crypto adoption data, company registration time.
- **Daily infrastructure**:
  - About 70% of the population had internet access and 99% owned a mobile phone (early 2023) [S5].
  - About 20% of energy came from renewables in 2023, with a 50% target by 2030 [S5].
  - Water comes from desalination on most islands (editorial knowledge, not sourced here).
  - Praia has 12 city bus lines and a ring road [S13]. No ride-hailing app was found ("not found"), and taxis are the norm.
  - Not found: power-cut frequency, mobile money.
- **Health & schools**: the country has 6 hospitals (2 central, 4 regional) and 28 health centres [S5]. Praia hosts one of the two central hospitals [S5]; its name, Hospital Dr. Agostinho Neto, is editorial knowledge not confirmed by an opened page (unverified). France Diplomatie notes there is no medical helicopter, no sea rescue and no decompression chamber, and that serious cases may be evacuated to Dakar or Europe (2026-10-13) [S1]. A French school is said to exist in Praia (editorial knowledge), but the AEFE page did not load, so it is unverified. The city has several universities [S13].
- **Safety**:
  - France Diplomatie puts the whole country in a "vigilance renforcée" zone (page updated 2026-10-13). It reports a **recent increase in violent armed robberies in Praia, day and night**, more sexual assaults (lone women are the main victims) and snatch thefts targeting gold and phones. It advises not walking alone outside busy central areas and taking taxis at night [S1].
  - GOV.UK (page date not displayed when re-checked on 2026-11-14; the original pass recorded 2026-07-28) says crime is generally low but singles out Praia, Sal and Boa Vista for pickpocketing, bag-snatching, burglary and muggings [S2].
  - Murders are concentrated in Praia and Mindelo (Wikipedia, undated, accessed 2026-11-14) [S5].
  - Neighbourhood-level detail is "not found" in official sources.
- **Social life & vibe**: Praia is the political and economic centre of the country [S13]. Football clubs include Sporting Praia, Boavista and Travadores, playing at Estádio da Várzea [S13]. The Kriol Jazz Festival (April) and the Atlantic Music Expo are editorial knowledge, unverified because the page returned 404. Religion is mostly Catholic (72.5%, 2021), with 15.6% non-religious [S5], so faith shapes daily life only lightly. The city is more of an administrative and business place than a beach town.
- **Diaspora & expat community**: Expat.com forum threads such as "Moving to Praia in Santiago island" are about 2 years old [S14]. No named Facebook or WhatsApp group could be verified without search: "not found".
- **Climate in practice**: desert climate with average highs near 27 °C and roughly 180–210 mm of rain a year (the article's text says ~210 mm, its 1991–2020 table 176.9 mm), concentrated in a short wet season [S13]. France Diplomatie flags flash floods and landslides from August to October [S1]. Dust-laden harmattan haze is common in winter (editorial knowledge).
- **Watch out**: rising violent street crime [S1]; a weak medical evacuation chain [S1]; an island economy where most goods are imported (editorial knowledge); rents high compared with local incomes; immigration paperwork can drag [S4].

### Mindelo (São Vicente island) — cultural capital

- **Cost of living**: Numbeo's Mindelo page returned errors (404 and 429), so no figures are available. Editorially, prices are likely close to Praia's or slightly lower (?). Commonly named neighbourhoods are the centre (Rua de Lisboa, Pracinha), Laginha and Alto Miramar (editorial knowledge).
- **Work & money**: business, fishing, shipping, the port and boating, with growing tourism [S15]. Porto Grande is the main deep-water port, with ferries to Santo Antão [S15]. Remote work is feasible as in Praia. No salary data was found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**: the University of Cape Verde has an engineering and maritime-sciences faculty here, alongside Jean Piaget University and the University of Mindelo [S15]. Startup hubs: "not found".
- **Daily infrastructure**: Cesária Évora Airport is 9 km from the city [S15]. Nationally, a wind project built in 2011 across four islands, including São Vicente, supplies about 30% of electricity [S5]. Not found: power-cut frequency, ride-hailing.
- **Health & schools**: São Vicente hosts the second central hospital [S5]; its name, Hospital Baptista de Sousa, is editorial knowledge (unverified). Universities are listed above [S15]. French or international school: "not found".
- **Safety**:
  - France Diplomatie advises against walking alone outside busy central areas of São Vicente's urban zones and reports night-time burglaries of sailing boats (2026-10-13) [S1].
  - Murders are concentrated in Praia and Mindelo (Wikipedia, undated, accessed 2026-11-14) [S5].
  - Whether Mindelo is calmer than Praia: no dated source found ("not found").
- **Social life & vibe**: considered the country's cultural capital, with the country's best-known Carnival (February) [S15]. Morna, inscribed by UNESCO in 2019, was enriched by São Vicente's "cosmopolitanism and openness" [S17]. The Baía das Gatas music festival is held on an August full-moon weekend and has run since 1984 [S18]. It is the home city of Cesária Évora [S15]. Cape Verde's first Pride march was held in Mindelo in June 2013 [S19]. Daily life here may suit people who want music and street events close to home (editorial).
- **Diaspora & expat community**: a sailing and yachting community gathers around the marina (editorial knowledge). Named groups: "not found".
- **Climate in practice**: desert climate, average 24.6 °C, about 84–100 mm of rain a year mostly August to October, and about 2,981 sunshine hours a year [S15]. Constant trade winds make it feel cooler than Praia (editorial knowledge).
- **Watch out**: a small city (70,468 residents in 2010 [S15]) with a thin job market outside the port and tourism; it is very dry; inter-island travel depends on flights and ferries.

### Santa Maria (Sal island) — extra: the resort town where many foreigners actually settle

- **Why it matters**: Sal held 48.2% of the country's hotel beds in 2017 and tourism is its main industry [S16]. Santa Maria grew from 1,343 residents in 1990 to 6,258 in 2010 [S16], and it is the most international town. It is 16 km from Amílcar Cabral International Airport, which has the most direct flights to Europe [S16].
- **Climate**: arid, with about 3 rainy days a year and temperatures of 22–27 °C [S16].
- **Safety**: GOV.UK singles out Sal for theft [S2]. An Expat.com consultant calls immigration on Sal "hostile to foreigners" (circa 2022–2024, anecdotal) [S4].
- **Watch out**: a mono-economy that depends on tourism, with a resort bubble and prices inflated by tourism (editorial). It suits remote workers and beach or kitesurf lovers, not career seekers. Cost data: "not found".

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No Hofstede or GLOBE scores for Cape Verde were found: the Hofstede overview [S20] does not list the country. Afrobarometer gives the only measured cultural indicators. 71% prefer democracy, 56% back co-official Creole and 40% have thought a lot about emigrating [S9]. 80% would welcome or not mind a gay neighbour (2020, as cited by Wikipedia) [S19]. The lines below describe who may feel comfortable in each city. They are not measurements, and no quiz question measures a trait.

**Praia**
- Openness: suits people curious about an administrative capital with a mixed Creole and Lusophone culture. It is less arty than Mindelo [S13][S15].
- Conscientiousness: an advantage. Immigration paperwork takes weeks and is done in person [S4], and security advice asks for constant vigilance [S1].
- Extraversion: moderate. There is a social life (football, festivals) but it is less centred on the street than in Mindelo [S13].
- Agreeableness: helpful, since a small society rewards relationships, and Creole matters more than diplomas socially (editorial).
- Emotional stability: needed, given the rising armed-robbery reports and the weak medical evacuation chain [S1].

**Mindelo**
- Openness: a strong fit. It is the music and Carnival capital, with UNESCO-listed morna and a cosmopolitan port history [S15][S17][S18].
- Conscientiousness: less demanding day to day (editorial), but island logistics still require planning.
- Extraversion: rewarded, through Carnival, Baía das Gatas, bar music culture and the first Pride in the country [S15][S18][S19].
- Agreeableness: a small, close-knit city. Fitting in socially matters (editorial).
- Emotional stability: useful for coping with isolation and a thin job market (editorial).

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**:
  - Same-sex relations have been legal since 2004 [S19].
  - Employment discrimination based on sexual orientation has been banned since 2008 (Labour Code), and hate-crime law has covered sexual orientation and gender identity since 2015 (Legislative Decree 4/2015) [S19].
  - There is no recognition of same-sex couples. The Prime Minister said in July 2017 that marriage was not on the agenda [S19].
  - The first Pride was held in Mindelo in June 2013 and in Praia three years later, in 2016 [S19].
  - 80% would welcome or not mind a gay neighbour (Afrobarometer 2020, Dispatch 362, cited by Wikipedia) [S19].
  - Among the most tolerant contexts in Africa, though societal discrimination is still reported (older US State Dept reports, 2010–2012) [S19].
  - GOV.UK's local-laws page (2026-07-28) gives no Cape Verde-specific LGBT warning [S2].
- **Racism, colourism and treatment of the diaspora**: no reliable dated source was found ("not found"). Anecdotally, immigration on Sal is described as hostile to foreigners (forum, circa 2022–2024) [S4]. West African mainland migrants' experience is a known topic but no source was opened: "not found".
- **Conflict**: no armed conflict. Natural risks are volcanic (Fogo), earthquakes and flash floods from August to October [S1].
- **Women's daily experience**: France Diplomatie reports an increase in sexual assaults, with lone women the main victims (2026-10-13) [S1]. GOV.UK says sexual assaults are "rare but have been reported" [S2]. Afrobarometer Dispatch AD1245 (2026-10-26) reports support for gender equality alongside persistent barriers in employment and insecurity in public spaces; only its summary was read [S9].
