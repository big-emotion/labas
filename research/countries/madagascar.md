## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Political context (most important point, Nov 2026).** The "Gen Z Madagascar" protests started on 25 Sept 2025, set off by water and power cuts of up to 12 hours in Antananarivo. Then the CAPSAT army unit mutinied (11–13 Oct 2025), President Rajoelina left the country, and Colonel Michael Randrianirina was sworn in as interim president on 17 Oct 2025. The UN counted 22 dead [5]. The African Union suspended Madagascar (Al Jazeera headline of 15 Oct 2025, as cited in [18]). On 17 Nov 2025 he announced a roadmap: a national consultation, then a referendum, then a presidential election within two years. The prime minister was replaced in March 2026 [18]. According to Midi Madagasikara (19 Nov 2025), the government plans a constitutional referendum first and then a presidential election in the first half of 2027 [19]. Cyclone Gezani hit Toamasina in February 2026 [18]. France Diplomatie (updated 13 Nov 2026) says order "returned to calm for several months" in Tana and the provinces, and advises extra vigilance around 25 September, the anniversary of the protests [1].
- **Languages day to day.** Malagasy is used everywhere. French is used in administration, business, schooling (AEFE network) and with the urban middle class. English use is limited (editorial; no dated source found).
- **Currency.** Malagasy ariary (MGA) [13]. The rate implied by Expat.com's rent figures is about 4,500 MGA = 1 € [10]. Card acceptance is patchy, so bring euros or dollars at first. Foreign currency above 7,500 € must be declared on arrival [10].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders.**
  - Up to 15 days without a visa, with a 10 € fee and an onward ticket. Visas on arrival cover 30 or 60 days, payable in USD, EUR or MGA, and can be extended to 90 days in total. Passports need 6 months' validity [4][10].
  - The e-visa was reintroduced in April 2023 [10].
  - To live there: take a **long-stay visa** (visa long séjour) from a Malagasy consulate before leaving. It lasts 1 month and is converted into a **carte de résident** at the Ministry of the Interior. You need a criminal-record extract, proof of housing and proof of resources [10].
  - The **work permit** goes through the EDBM and takes about 60 days [10]. The EDBM also issues the "visa transformable" [14].
- **(b) Diaspora with Malagasy roots.**
  - **Malagasy citizens by origin cannot hold dual nationality.** Under article 42 of the 1960 Nationality Code (ordinance 60-064), an adult Malagasy who voluntarily takes another nationality loses Malagasy nationality. On 24 Oct 2025 a decree applied this rule to former president Rajoelina because of his French nationality, though his supporters dispute the procedure [6][20]. Wikipedia says naturalised citizens may keep their first nationality [6]. A commercial guide (OpenShores, Apr 2026) says the opposite (unverified, not opened).
  - A reform of the nationality code "notamment en matière de double nationalité" is on the transition's agenda (Midi Madagasikara, 19 Nov 2025). No new law had been found as of Nov 2026 [19].
  - Since January 2017 (Law 2016-038), mothers can pass on nationality to their children [6].
  - No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme was found.
  - The rules for diaspora born abroad who want to recover Malagasy nationality were not found [6].
  - For Afro-descendants without Malagasy roots, naturalisation needs 5 years' residence plus knowledge of the language and customs [6].
- **Health alerts (Nov 2026).**
  - An Ebola-related entry ban covers foreigners coming from or via the DRC within 21 days. Travellers from Kenya or Nigeria must sign a commitment letter [1][2].
  - A measles alert is in place (March 2026) [1].
  - An El Niño weather alert is in place (Nov 2026) [1].

## Cities

### Antananarivo (Tana)

- **Cost of living.**
  - A 1-bed in the centre costs about 1,700,000 MGA (≈378 €). On the outskirts it is about 425,000 MGA (≈95 €). A 3-bed in the centre costs about 3,900,000 MGA (≈870 €) [10].
  - Utilities for 85 m² run about 46 € a month, and the deposit is usually 2 months [10].
  - A single person spends 380–490 €/month excluding rent (Numbeo data of Sept 2025, via Expat.com). A family of four spends 2,900–4,700 € excluding rent, mostly because of international school fees [10].
  - Expat.com's own "key figures" box (650 € single) contradicts its tables, so treat these numbers with care [10].
  - Neighbourhoods named in sources: Isoraka (residential, nightlife) and Analakely (commercial centre, market) [9]. The French lycée's school D is due to move to Ivandry [11].
  - Ivandry, Ankorondrano and Ambatobe are commonly described as the expat and upper-middle-class districts, but this is editorial and **not verified** in this pass.
  - Meal prices: not found (Numbeo returned HTTP 429).
- **Work & money.**
  - Industry is concentrated in the capital: food processing, brewing, textiles, soap [9].
  - Garment export-processing zones (EPZs) around Tana and Antsirabe sell under AGOA (US trade scheme) and EBA (EU trade scheme) [13]. The US "reciprocal" tariff of 47% (April 2025) put the US market at risk [13].
  - Large informal sector [9]. GDP per capita: $616 nominal, $2,040 PPP (2025) [13].
  - Offshoring/BPO for French clients (call centres, data entry) is widely reported but was **not sourced** in this pass. Salaries: not found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech.**
  - The EDBM is the single window for company creation. Its ORINASA online platform claims "company created in 48 h" for Analamanga (the Tana region), Anôsy, Sava and Fitovinany. A fee simulator exists, but no amounts are published [14].
  - Named startup hubs and training:
    - **42 Antananarivo** is a coding school in the 42 network, in the north of the city. The 42 Network blog called it one of its newest campuses (22 Jan 2024) [21].
    - In March 2023, UNFPA opened a digital-project incubation space for young people, especially young women, in its Tana offices, with an annex in Toliara [22].
    - Habaka (an innovation hub founded around 2012), Zafy Tody (an incubator founded in 2019), Axian's NextA / Tanashore Innovation Center, Orange Digital Center and French Tech Antananarivo all appear in search results. They were **not opened or checked** in this pass (unverified).
    - Reports describe the ecosystem as small and short of capital (unverified secondary summary).
  - Fintech: mobile operators are Yas (formerly Telma), Orange and Airtel [10]. Their mobile-money services (MVola, Orange Money, Airtel Money) are well known, but adoption figures were not found.
  - Crypto: not found.
  - Note: in Oct 2023 Mediapart reported that the former presidency used the Predator spyware [12].
- **Daily infrastructure.**
  - Power and water cuts were severe enough to trigger the 2025 uprising (up to 12 h) [5]. Afrobarometer (May 2026) says citizens still disapprove of government performance on water and electricity [17].
  - Fibre exists in the main cities, alongside 3G/4G. Home broadband at 60 Mbps+ costs about 74 €/month (Sept 2025). Starlink is said to be rolling out (undated) [10].
  - Transport is mostly on foot or by minibus ("taxi-be"): about 4,400 minibuses on 90 routes carry about 700,000 passengers a day. Older Renault/Citroën taxis are common, and traffic congestion is chronic [9].
  - Ride-hailing apps: not found.
  - FCDO advice: keep car doors locked and windows closed in Tana [3].
- **Health & schools.**
  - Hospitals: HJRA (public), CHU Befelatanana, and the private Polyclinique de Tsaralalana and Polyclinique d'Ilafy [10]. CHUA Anosiala is also cited, but the source does not name it (unverified). Serious cases are evacuated to La Réunion, Mauritius or South Africa, so take insurance that covers evacuation [10].
  - Schools:
    - Lycée Français de Tananarive (AEFE-run, about 3,000 pupils, campuses in Ampefiloha, Ampandrianomby, Ambohibao and Analamahitsy) [11]
    - Lycée Peter Pan, Collèges de France, Lycée La Clairefontaine and the Alliance française school in Antsahabe [9][11]
    - American School of Antananarivo (IB, Alarobia; 2019–20 fees of 12,500–21,320 USD, outdated) [10]
    - British School of Madagascar and International School of Madagascar [10]
  - Expect waiting lists [10].
- **Safety.**
  - FCDO (current at 14 Nov 2026): don't walk alone after dark in city centres or poorly lit areas [3]. Kidnappings for ransom happen occasionally, mostly targeting Malagasy people seen as wealthy, so hold business meetings in secure places [3].
  - Political rallies are possible anywhere [3]. France Diplomatie (Aug–Nov 2026): calm restored [1].
  - Afrobarometer (July 2025): most citizens feel insecurity is a major challenge [17].
  - Neighbourhood-level nuance: not found in dated sources.
- **Social life & vibe.**
  - Tana has the country's biggest arts scene: music venues, cinemas, cultural centres, and dance schools such as K'art Antanimena [9].
  - Isoraka is known for nightlife [9].
  - Rugby is the popular sport (Mahamasina stadium; Sevens honours in 2007) [9].
  - Famadihana (ancestor reburial ceremony) is still practised by Merina and Betsileo families [9].
  - The churches carry weight in public life: the FFKM church council mediated during the 2025 crisis and leads the national consultation [5][18].
- **Diaspora & expat community.**
  - Not found in this pass: no Facebook groups, associations or returnee networks were verified.
  - The CCIFM (French chamber of commerce in Madagascar) works with the EDBM [14]. Expat.com runs a Madagascar forum [10].
- **Climate in practice.**
  - Highland climate at about 1,280 m. The rainy season runs Nov–April. July is cool (mean about 15.6 °C), so you need warm clothes and some heating [9].
  - Floods in Jan 2022 damaged about 7,000 homes [9].
- **Watch out.**
  - Utility cuts.
  - Political fragility under a military-led transition, with elections promised around 2027 [18].
  - Traffic.
  - Evacuation needed for serious health care.
  - International schools are expensive.
  - In Aug 2023 a crowd crush at Mahamasina stadium killed at least 12 people [9].

### Nosy Be

- **Cost of living.** Not found. Tourist-island prices are likely higher than Tana for imported goods (editorial).
- **Work & money.** The economy rests on ylang-ylang (essential oils) and tourism. It is "Madagascar's largest and busiest tourist destination" and the only place with big all-inclusive resorts. Sugar and rum used to dominate [8]. In practice, work means hospitality, diving, property or remote work.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech.** Not found.
- **Daily infrastructure.**
  - Fascene airport has flights from Air Austral, Airlink, Ethiopian and Ewa, plus direct European flights on Neos [8].
  - The island has roundabouts and no traffic lights. You reach the mainland by boat from Ankify [8].
  - Internet quality and power: not found.
- **Health & schools.**
  - École primaire française Lamartine (primary school only) [8].
  - Hospitals: not found. Assume evacuation to Tana or La Réunion for anything serious (editorial).
- **Safety.**
  - FCDO (current at 14 Nov 2026): violent robberies and assaults, some in daylight, on popular coasts including Nosy Be, Ankify and Ambanja. Don't go to isolated beaches alone [3].
  - In 2013 a mob lynched two French tourists and a local man over a rumour [8].
- **Social life & vibe.** Beach and resort life. The island is twinned with Naples, and Italians have a visible presence (editorial, not sourced). Its pace is slow. Specific nightlife sources: not found.
- **Diaspora & expat community.** Not found.
- **Climate in practice.** Tropical, about 30 °C all year. The wet season runs Oct–early May and is most humid Dec–Feb. Rainfall is about 1,728 mm a year [8].
- **Watch out.**
  - Small, tourism-dependent job market.
  - Coastal crime [3].
  - Coral reefs damaged by unregulated tourism and urban growth [8].
  - Health care far away.
  - Sex tourism is a known issue on the island (editorial; no dated source found).

### Toamasina (Tamatave) — extra city, for work

- Chief seaport and railhead, population 325,857 in 2018 [15]. Relevant for logistics, trade and mining-related jobs. The Ambatovy nickel project is linked to the region, but this was not confirmed in the sources.
- Lycée Français de Tamatave [15].
- In February 2026 Cyclone Gezani damaged the city, and the interim president appealed for international aid [18]. Check the state of housing and infrastructure before you move.
- Climate: rainforest type, wettest Feb–April, average highs of 24–30 °C. Malaria and chikungunya outbreaks still occur [15].

### Antsiranana (Diego-Suarez) — extra city, for a quieter coastal life

- Population 129,320 in 2018. One of the largest deep-water harbours in the Indian Ocean, but remote [16]. Lycée Français Sadi-Carnot and a university [16].
- Dry climate for a coastal city: about 942 mm of rain a year and about 3,039 hours of sunshine, with the wet season Dec–March [16].
- Six deaths were reported there during the Sept 2025 protests [5].

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

These are interpretations of cited living conditions. No city and no quiz question measures a personality trait. No academic work on regional personality in Madagascar was found. Hofstede and GLOBE do not cover Madagascar, as far as this pass could check (not verified).

**Antananarivo**
- **Openness:** suits people curious about a dense arts and music scene and highland traditions such as famadihana [9].
- **Conscientiousness:** helps with long-stay visa → resident card → EDBM work permit paperwork [10][14], and with planning around power and water cuts [5].
- **Extraversion:** Isoraka nightlife, rugby crowds and church life reward sociable people [9][5].
- **Agreeableness:** church-mediated public life and the consensus-oriented national consultation suggest that conciliatory manners help [18].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** high tolerance is needed for a military transition, rallies, traffic and cuts [3][5][18].

**Nosy Be**
- **Openness:** suits nature- and sea-oriented people [8].
- **Conscientiousness:** less paperwork-driven daily life, but self-reliance matters because health care is far away (editorial).
- **Extraversion:** a resort and tourist social scene. Small-island life also suits introverts (editorial).
- **Agreeableness:** a small community where reputation matters. The 2013 lynching shows how fast rumours can escalate [8].
- **Emotional stability:** isolation and coastal crime [3] call for calm and caution.

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT.**
  - Same-sex sexual activity has been legal since 1972, but only from age 21. The Penal Code sets 2–5 years' prison and a fine of 2–10 million MGA for same-sex acts with someone under 21 [7]. FCDO (Nov 2026) confirms the 21+ threshold [3]. The source gives no heterosexual age of consent, so "unequal" is inferred, not quoted.
  - Same-sex marriage is banned by Law 2007-022. Adoption is reserved for heterosexual couples. There is no anti-discrimination protection, including in the 2024 Labour Code.
  - In July 2021 the authorities cancelled an LGBT party in Tana for "incitement to debauchery" [7].
  - FCDO (current Nov 2026): public attitudes are "less tolerant than in the UK", so avoid public displays of affection [3].
- **Racism / colourism / treatment of diaspora.** No reliable dated source was found in this pass. The highland–coastal (Merina vs côtier) ethnic and caste-descent tensions are well documented in the literature but were not sourced here. Flag before publishing.
- **Conflict zones.**
  - No armed conflict. Armed criminals have boarded boats on the Tsiribihina River in the west [3].
  - Cattle-raider (dahalo) banditry in the south is known but not covered in the sources fetched.
  - The 2025 protests left 22 dead (UN figure) [5].
- **Women.**
  - Afrobarometer Round 10: 67% think men should have priority for jobs when jobs are scarce [17].
  - The FCDO gives no Madagascar-specific guidance for women [3].
  - Day-to-day experience: not found.
- **Political.** Military-led transition since Oct 2025, with an AU suspension reported (Oct 2025). Afrobarometer (Dec 2025): citizens reject military rule but are open to army intervention when leaders abuse power [17][18].
