## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages used day to day:** Setswana is the national language and English is the official language [7]. English works for administration, business and schooling. Setswana carries social life and builds trust, and naturalisation requires familiarity with Setswana or another tribal language [6]. French is marginal. The Alliance Française de Gaborone, in Extension 10, teaches French, Setswana and English and holds about 50 events a year [12].
- **Currency:** Botswana pula (BWP), which is fully convertible. Its value is pegged to a basket weighted heavily toward the South African rand [8]. EUR figures below use an *indicative* rate of about 15 BWP = 1 EUR, which is not taken from a fetched source. Check the live rate.
- **Economy context:** Lab-grown diamonds have hit the diamond-dependent economy, which shrank in 2024 (per-capita growth −4.6%). Unemployment was 23.4% in 2023 [8]. In November 2026 the government committed to a bigger stake in De Beers [10].
- **(a) French/EU passport holders:** No visa is needed. Entry is free at the border for up to 3 months, capped at 90 days per year. Stays beyond 3 months require a residence permit, which in turn requires an employment contract for you or your spouse. France Diplomatie, updated 2026-09-15 [2].
  - Work permits run up to 5 years and cost about USD 110–220, with processing in 7–60 days. Employers must first advertise the job to citizens for at least 14 days. Residence permits are usually for 2 years. Expat.com, 2025-11-03 [5].
  - The Botswana One Stop Service Centre (BOSSC), run by the investment agency BITC, gathers government agencies so investors can get authorisations quickly [9].
- **(b) Diaspora with family roots:** Citizenship passes by descent to anyone born anywhere to at least one Motswana parent. Birth on Botswana soil has not conferred citizenship since 1984 [6].
  - **Dual nationality: the law is changing, check before you plan.** The older rule (still described by Wikipedia [6] and Expat.com, 2025-11-03 [5]) was that adults could not keep a second nationality and minors had to choose at majority. The Citizenship Amendment Act No. 23 of 2024, published 2024-10-29, provides for dual citizenship to be permitted for adults [22]. On 2025-12-17 Parliament approved at second reading a further Citizenship Amendment Bill allowing *multiple* citizenship and an investor "economic citizenship" route; it still had to pass committee stage and receive assent (Daily News, 2025-12-18) [23]. Commencement and implementing rules could not be confirmed on 2026-11-14 (unverified), so confirm with the Department of Immigration and Citizenship.
  - Naturalisation takes 10 years of residence, or 5 years for the spouse of a citizen [6].
  - No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found. The 2024–2025 amendments above [22][23] are the closest thing to an opening.
  - Botswana's diaspora is overwhelmingly southern African and English-speaking. Few readers of Là-bas will have Tswana roots.

## Cities

### Gaborone

Capital and economic centre. Population 246,325, metro area 534,842 (2022 census) [3].

- **Cost of living**
  - 1-bed rent: about £258/month in the centre and £153 outside it. That is roughly BWP 4,600 / EUR 300 and BWP 2,700 / EUR 180 (Wise, 2026) [1].
  - 2-bed apartment: BWP 5,000–8,000 in Village and Extension (Expat.com, 2025-11-03) [4]. Another section of the same guide gives BWP 4,500–7,000 [5].
  - 3-bed family house: BWP 8,000–15,000 in Gaborone West and Phakalane [4]. A room in a flatshare costs BWP 2,500–4,000 including bills [4].
  - Inexpensive restaurant meal: about £8, roughly BWP 140 / EUR 9.5 [1].
  - Monthly budget for one person: about £640 on Wise's headline figure, £669 with a central 1-bed, £411 without rent [1]. Expat.com gives about EUR 1,100–1,160 depending on the table [5].
  - Neighbourhoods [4]:
    - Phakalane: affluent suburb about 13 km north of the centre [3].
    - Gaborone West and Phakalane: popular with families.
    - Broadhurst: a balance between access and quiet.
    - Village: central and pricey.
    - Extension: more affordable.
- **Work & money**
  - Gaborone hosts the headquarters of the Bank of Botswana, the Botswana Stock Exchange, Debswana, SADC, Botswana Telecommunications Corporation and Air Botswana. About 19.7% of residents work in finance, a figure Wikipedia itself flags as outdated [3].
  - Sectors hiring foreigners: mining, tourism, finance, health, international education and IT [5].
  - Average salary is about BWP 7,500/month. Skilled roles pay BWP 12,000–45,000 (Expat.com, citing Lamna, 2025) [5].
  - The jobs market is tight. Employers must give citizens priority, and unemployment is 25–27% [5] (23.4% in 2023 per [8]).
  - Remote work for a foreign employer: no specific digital-nomad visa found.
- **Entrepreneurship & tech**
  - The Botswana Digital & Innovation Hub (BDIH) is a science and technology park at Plot 69184, Block 8. It offers business support, IP advice, technology entrepreneurship development and a carrier-neutral data centre. Its 2025/2026 Acceleration Programme call and a 2026 call for mentors were listed on the site in 2026 [11].
  - BITC runs the BOSSC for investors [9].
  - Named private incubators, VC presence and crypto adoption: **not found**, because searches were exhausted. Do not assume a scene comparable to Kigali or Nairobi.
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - Most electricity is imported from South Africa's Eskom, with domestic coal power at Morupule [8]. Current load-shedding status: **not found**. Electricity is often prepaid [5].
  - Internet: 60 Mbps+ costs about £32/month [1]. Orange Botswana sells Orange Money mobile money and 5G home internet (Flybox) [13].
  - Water and ride-hailing apps: **not found**.
  - The FCDO advises licensed taxis with blue number plates or taxis booked through a recognised company [14]. Driving is on the left [4].
  - The airport links to Johannesburg and Addis Ababa [4].
- **Health & schools**
  - Hospitals: the public sector is saturated, so private care is recommended, including Gaborone Private Hospital and Bokamoso. A private consultation costs about BWP 500–800 [5].
  - Medical evacuation cover is advised: an evacuation, often to South Africa, can quickly cost several thousand euros [5]. (A monthly premium figure previously given here was not in the source and was removed.)
  - International schools:
    - Westwood International School, Maru-a-Pula, Hillcrest International, St Joseph's College and others [3].
    - Fees: Gaborone International School (British curriculum) BWP 50,000–90,000/yr; Westwood (American curriculum) BWP 55,000–95,000/yr; Enko Botho (IB) BWP 60,000–100,000/yr [5].
    - **There is no AEFE-accredited French school.** Francophone families often use the CNED distance-learning programme [5].
  - Enrol 6–12 months ahead [5].
- **Safety**
  - France Diplomatie (2026-09-15) puts the whole country under heightened vigilance. Incidents are fairly uncommon, but there has been recent violence including bag snatching, so stay alert in Gaborone, Francistown and Maun [15].
  - The UK FCDO (undated page, fetched 2026-11-14) says violent crime is rising in Gaborone, Francistown and Maun [14]. It warns of cars targeted at traffic lights, restaurant hold-ups at peak hours and armed house burglaries [14].
  - Local press (Mmegi, November 2026) reports several murder and rape cases in recent headlines [10].
  - Neighbourhood-level crime data: **not found**.
- **Social life & vibe**
  - Gaborone has the richest social life in the country: restaurants, bars, golf and tennis clubs, and the Maitisong Festival (held since 1987) [3][4].
  - The National Museum and Art Gallery is in the city [3].
  - Religion: about 86.5% Christian (2022) [7]. Its weight in daily life was not documented beyond that figure.
  - Nightlife venue names: **not found**.
  - Pace of life: a quiet, spread-out, car-dependent capital. This is an editorial inference from the suburb layout and taxi advice [3][14].
- **Diaspora & expat community**
  - About 110,600 international migrants, 4.3% of the population (2019) [5].
  - The Expat.com Botswana forum has 4,014 members and 15 threads [4].
  - Facebook expat groups are widely used for housing [5]. No specific group could be verified in this run.
  - The Alliance Française at Extension 10 is the francophone anchor [12].
- **Climate in practice**
  - Hot semi-arid. Rain falls October–April, with January the wettest month [3].
  - June–July nights drop to about 4°C, so plan for heating, which is unusual in Africa [3].
  - Hot spells peak in October before the rains and in January–February in drought years [3].
- **Watch out**
  - The job market strongly favours citizens [5].
  - Dual-nationality rules are in transition; do not assume you can keep your passport until the 2024–2025 amendments are confirmed in force [22][23].
  - There is no French school [5].
  - Housing and private schools are expensive by regional standards [4][5].
  - The economy is fragile while diamond demand stays weak [8].
  - The FCDO reports violent crime is rising [14].

### Maun

Tourism capital and gateway to the Okavango Delta. Population 85,293 (2022) [16].

- **Cost of living:** A family house rents for BWP 4,000–8,000/month (Expat.com, 2025-11-03) [4]. 1-bed rent and meal prices: **not found**. Expect higher prices for imported goods because of the distance (editorial).
- **Work & money**
  - Tourism dominates the economy. Many safari and air-charter companies have their headquarters here [16].
  - Other activity: cattle trading and supplying mineral-exploration camps [16].
  - Jobs suit guides, pilots, lodge managers, conservation and research staff. Remote work depends on connectivity (see below).
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** **Not found.** Opportunity lies in tourism services rather than tech.
- **Daily infrastructure**
  - Maun International Airport serves the town. The A3 highway runs to Francistown [16].
  - Mobile coverage reaches only about 20–25 km beyond town, depending on weather [16]. That source is old, so verify current coverage.
  - Power, water and ride-hailing: **not found**.
- **Health & schools**
  - Letsholathebe II Memorial Hospital is the main referral hospital. Maun Private Hospital opened in July 2021 [16].
  - Malaria is present in the north and the Okavango, especially November–April [5].
  - International schools: **not found**. Expat.com judges Maun isolated and poorly suited to families with young children [4].
- **Safety**
  - Both the FCDO and France Diplomatie name Maun among the towns needing vigilance [14][15].
  - Wildlife is a daily hazard: wildlife accidents are frequent and bathing in rivers is discouraged [14][15].
  - Night driving is risky because of livestock and wildlife [14].
- **Social life & vibe:** A small, outdoor-oriented town that lives around safari logistics [16]. Nightlife and culture are limited (editorial, consistent with [4]).
- **Diaspora & expat community:** A tourism-industry expat community exists (editorial). No named group was verified.
- **Climate in practice**
  - Botswana's hottest town: record 44°C in January 2016, mean maximum about 35°C in October [16].
  - Rain falls November–March, about 452 mm a year [16].
  - June–July nights drop to about 6°C [16].
  - The FCDO flags flooding risk from November to March [14].
- **Watch out**
  - Isolation [4].
  - Malaria [5].
  - Extreme heat [16].
  - Healthcare is limited outside the cities [17].
  - The job market depends entirely on tourism.

### Francistown (additional: second city, cheaper, northern hub)

Population about 103,417, metro area 147,122 (2022) [18]. Francistown is the main road and rail hub toward Zimbabwe and Zambia [18].

- **Cost of living:** A 3-bed house costs BWP 3,500–7,000, 30–40% less than Gaborone. Popular areas are Aerodrome and Tati [4].
- **Work:** Mining (nickel, copper, cobalt at Tati Nickel), the Botswana Meat Commission and the Dumela Industrial Complex [18].
- **Health:** Nyangabwe Referral Hospital [18]. Private option: Baobab [5].
- **Safety:** Named alongside Gaborone and Maun in both government advisories [14][15].
- **Vibe:** Calmer and more provincial than Gaborone, with a smaller expat community [4].
- **Climate:** Short wet season December–March. Cold June–August nights [18].

### Kasane (additional, niche: conservation and tourism living)

A small town (9,244 in 2011, old data) [19]. It sits near the Four Corners of Botswana, Namibia, Zambia and Zimbabwe, at the edge of Chobe National Park, about 70 km from Victoria Falls [19]. It has an airport and the Kazungula bridge into Zambia [19]. It is relevant only for conservation or tourism careers. Data is too thin for scoring.

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

*This section is interpretive. No city or question measures personality.* Hofstede scores for Botswana could not be retrieved, because the page returned empty. No academic regional-personality study was found in this run.

**Gaborone**
- **Openness:** suits people curious about a quieter, institution-centred capital, with culture from the Maitisong Festival, the museum and the Alliance Française events [3][12]. People seeking a big-city creative scene may find it thin.
- **Conscientiousness:** suits planners. Permits require 14-day local advertising, medical and police certificates, and renewal 6 months before expiry [5]. School places need booking 6–12 months ahead [5].
- **Extraversion:** social life runs through clubs (golf, tennis), private circles and networking [4] rather than street life.
- **Agreeableness:** settling in rewards politeness and learning Setswana. Naturalisation itself requires language familiarity [6].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** daily stress is lower than in megacities such as Lagos or Nairobi (editorial). Rising violent crime warnings [14] and a fragile job market [5][8] require resilience.

**Maun**
- **Openness:** suits nature lovers and adventurers. Life is organised around the Okavango [16].
- **Conscientiousness:** remote logistics demand it, such as carrying water and fuel and planning trips [15].
- **Extraversion:** a small community where everyone works in tourism. It is rewarding for sociable people, but there are few venues [4].
- **Agreeableness:** not documented.
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** isolation, 44°C heat and wildlife hazards demand it [4][15][16].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT**
  - Same-sex sexual activity was decriminalised by the High Court on 2019-06-11, and the Court of Appeal confirmed it on 2021-11-29. The invalidated provisions were formally deleted in March 2026 [20].
  - Workplace discrimination has been banned since 2010 [20].
  - Trans people can change their legal gender without surgery, following 2017 rulings [20].
  - There is no same-sex marriage or adoption [20].
  - Attitudes: 50% would welcome or not mind a gay neighbour (Afrobarometer 2021, reported May 2023), up from 43% in 2016, with younger people more tolerant [20]. The FCDO says public attitudes are less tolerant than in the UK and public displays of affection may attract negative attention [14].
- **Political stability:** The October 2024 election brought the first change of ruling party since 1966. President Masisi conceded and Duma Boko (UDC) was sworn in on 2024-11-01 [21]. There are no conflict zones. Political stability is noted, but protests are possible [15].
- **Women's daily experience**
  - The FCDO notes a high number of sexual assaults. Foreign visitors are unlikely to be targeted, but women should avoid walking alone at night [14].
  - Local press (Mmegi, 2026-11-10 to 12) reports killings of women and rape cases, including of a minor, in its headlines [10].
- **Racism, colourism and treatment of foreigners:** **No reliable dated source found** in this run. Citizen-first hiring rules [5] structurally disadvantage foreigners in the job market.
- **Corruption:** Local press in November 2026 identifies corruption as the main money-laundering risk [10].
