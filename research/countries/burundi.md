## Country at a glance (for someone moving, not visiting)

- **Languages in daily use:** Kirundi (spoken by almost everyone), French (administration, business, schooling in towns), Swahili (trade, especially in Bujumbura's markets) and English (spreading since Burundi joined the East African Community). Expat.com lists all four and notes that job seekers "will need to master the language" [9]. Naturalisation requires understanding Kirundi [5].
- **Currency:** Burundian franc (BIF / FBu) [8]. **The parallel market rate is roughly twice the official rate** (IMF data cited by Iwacu, 11 Nov 2026) [10]. Any EUR conversion depends on which rate you use, so this dossier gives prices in FBu only. Inflation averaged 34% in 2025 (peak 45.5% in April 2025) and fell to 8.4% in August 2026 (World Bank) [7]. The basket of consumer goods rose about 46% over two years (Iwacu, Nov 2026) [10]. In 2020 the mayor of Bujumbura banned leases priced in foreign currency (Iwacu, 6 Nov 2020) [16].
- **Entry, French/EU passports:** a visa is required. The UK FCDO page (checked as current on 14 Nov 2026) describes a one-month visa issued on arrival for **USD 90**. It is single or multiple entry, payable online or in cash. Travellers must first complete the online "Airport entry visa" form to get a validation certificate. Passports must be valid for 6 months, and a **yellow fever certificate is mandatory** [3]. The France Diplomatie entry page could not be read. The rules above are written for UK nationals, so French nationals should check them against the official portal migration.gov.bi [11]. **Residence permit (carte de séjour) rules and costs: not found.** The Commissariat Général des Migrations is on Avenue de l'OUA, Kigobe, Bujumbura [11].
- **Entry, diaspora with Burundian roots:**
  - Burundi has **allowed dual nationality since the 2000 Nationality Code**. People who lost Burundian nationality under the 1971 code (often refugees) could recover it [5].
  - Nationality passes by descent, wherever the child is born. A Burundian father is enough. Under the 2000 code a Burundian mother passes it on only if the father did not recognise the child or is stateless or unknown. Article 12 of the 2018 Constitution gives children of Burundian men and women equal nationality rights, but in practice mainly the father's nationality is still recognised [5]. This is a real barrier for diaspora members whose Burundian parent is the mother.
  - Naturalisation needs 10 years of residence, or 5 years for the husband of a Burundian woman, plus knowledge of Kirundi [5].
  - **No diaspora visa or right-of-return scheme comparable to Ghana's or Benin's was found.**
- **Administrative reform:** in 2025 the 18 provinces were merged into 5: Bujumbura, Gitega, Butanyerera (capital Ngozi), Burunga (capital Makamba) and Buhumuza (capital Cankuzo) [12]. Older guides use the former province names.
- **Macro context:**
  - Low-income, landlocked country of about 12 million people (AFD) [13]. Real GDP growth was 4.2% in 2025 (World Bank) [7].
  - Only about 10% of the population has electricity (Wikipedia, undated figure) [8].
  - Internet penetration was 12.5% in January 2025 (DataReportal) [14].
  - A **fuel shortage now in its fifth year** shapes daily life (Iwacu, 27 Jul 2026; FCDO 2026) [2][17].

## Cities

### Bujumbura

Economic capital and largest city on the north-east shore of Lake Tanganyika. The city proper had about 375,000 people (2023) and the urban area about 1.1 million [6]. It holds the main hospitals, the university, and most banks and businesses (fr.wikipedia) [19].

- **Cost of living:**
  - **1-bed rent in a middle-class neighbourhood: not found from a dated source.** Rents rose 18.4% year on year to September 2026 (INSBU via Iwacu) [10].
  - Neighbourhoods named in sources include Rohero (Mukaza commune), Kinindo (Muha), Kiriri-Vugizo and Kabondo [6], plus working-class areas such as Bwiza, Kinama, Musaga and Tenga/Rubirizi [18][20]. Which ones expatriates prefer could not be confirmed from a dated source.
  - Food prices at the Cotebu market (Nov 2026) [10]:
    - 25 kg maize flour: 105,000 FBu
    - beef: 35,000–45,000 FBu/kg
    - local rice: 7,500 FBu/kg
    - potatoes: 3,500 FBu/kg
  - Restaurant and hotel prices rose 14.4% year on year [10]. **Meal price: not found.**
  - Expat.com gives USD 900/month for one person and USD 2,500 for a family of four. These figures are **undated and probably old** [9].
- **Work & money:**
  - About 70% of urban jobs are in services (fr.wikipedia) [19].
  - The lake port carries most of the country's external trade, including coffee, cotton and tin ore exports [6].
  - Main employers for newcomers: international organisations, NGOs, banks, development projects (AFD has worked in the country since 1978 and Expertise France since 2023) [13].
  - Remote work is technically possible but fragile, because of power cuts and unreliable internet (see infrastructure). **Salaries: not found.**
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:**
  - The investment agency, **Agence de Développement du Burundi (ADB)**, approved 39 companies for Investment Code benefits in 2025–26. About 90% were Burundian-owned and about 10% were foreign investment. It is planning a digital one-stop shop for investors (Burundi Eco, 28 Aug 2026) [15].
  - In 2025–26, 15,985 individuals and 10,393 companies started activity [15].
  - **Named hubs (added in verification):**
    - **BujaHub** (Business Jam Hub), an NGO created in 2019 with a co-working space downtown, an incubator, an acceleration programme, "Digital Ladies" for women in digital and an annual Innovation Week (WeAreTech Africa, 21 Nov 2023) [38].
    - **Burundi Business Incubator (BBIN)**, a non-profit with a co-working space supporting Burundian SMEs (AfriLabs member page, undated) [39]. It co-ran digital-entrepreneurship trainings in Bujumbura in February 2026 under the EU-funded BURUDIGI project (MSM, 2026, unverified: seen only as a search excerpt) [40].
    - Current activity levels of both hubs were not confirmed.
  - A government–UNDP digital strategy event with an AI focus took place in April 2026 [21].
  - **Crypto: no adoption evidence or legal position found.**
  - Mobile money exists, but this pass found no dated source on providers.
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - **Fuel.** Rationing is run by the state firm Sopebu, created in February 2024. Priority goes to MPs, some ministries, the central bank (BRB), the revenue office (OBR), banks and international organisations [17].
    - Private motorists can queue for several weeks. A taxi driver quoted by Iwacu said only two stations serve the country's more than 6,000 taxis (one person's account, not confirmed by the paper).
    - The official price is about 4,000 FBu/L, against up to 15,000 FBu/L on the black market (Iwacu, 27 Jul 2026) [17].
    - FCDO: "do not count on buying fuel" [2].
  - **Power.** In September 2026, workshops in Tenga/Rubirizi only had power from about 7 pm to 7 am for two weeks. Regideso blamed low dam levels in the dry season (Iwacu, 25 Sep 2026) [18].
  - **Internet.**
    - Repeated complaints about slow, unreliable connections (Iwacu, Dec 2024, Feb 2025, Mar 2025) [22].
    - Starlink has been available since 24 Sep 2024: USD 372 for the kit and USD 44/month residential [23].
    - A USD 92m World Bank broadband project (PAFEN) aims to cover over 80% of the territory (Iwacu, 29 May 2026) [22].
  - **Waste.** Overflowing bins, and collection can be delayed for months (Iwacu, 4 Nov 2026) [20].
  - **Transport.** Taxis, tricycles and motorbike taxis [19]. Police control access in and out of the city at night [2]. **Ride-hailing apps: not found.**
- **Health & schools:**
  - Main hospitals include Prince Regent Charles Hospital and Kira Hospital (private), plus many clinics [6].
  - The city hosts the University of Burundi and "many international schools" [6]. **The name of an AEFE French school could not be confirmed** (the embassy site was reached but does not name one) [24].
  - The Institut français du Burundi is active (tender notice, 6 Nov 2026) [24].
  - Health alerts in 2026: cholera in the north-west, regional Ebola vigilance (WHO international emergency, 17 May 2026), measles [1].
- **Safety:**
  - FCDO (updated 21 May 2026, still current 14 Nov 2026) [2][4]:
    - robbery at gun and knife point, bag snatching, burglary and armed carjacking, "even in Bujumbura city centre";
    - do not walk or take public transport after dark;
    - two grenade attacks in crowded central Bujumbura on 10 May 2024;
    - avoid gatherings.
  - FCDO advises against travel to parts of Ntahangwa commune (the RN5 north of the airport towards Cibitoke, and west of the Rusizi river) and several communes of the new Bujumbura province (Mugina, Cibitoke, Bukinanyana, Bubanza, Mpanda) [2].
  - France Diplomatie (updated 2 Nov 2026, border alert 17 Sep 2026) formally advises against the DRC border zone, citing the Ruzizi plain in South Kivu (DRC side) as especially volatile [1].
  - The land border with Rwanda has been closed since 11 Jan 2024, though flights still run [4].
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - Lakeside beaches on the city's northern side [6].
  - Stade Intwari (22,000 seats) and the Monument de l'Unité [19].
  - Bars (cabarets) are a social institution; beer at cabarets rose 25.4% in a year [10].
  - Regina Mundi Cathedral is a landmark [6]. A strongly Christian society; mosques are present, notably in Buyenzi [19].
  - **Music and nightlife scene: no dated source found.**
- **Diaspora & expat community:**
  - About 30,000–40,000 expatriates live in the country. The largest groups are Congolese, Rwandan, Tanzanian, Belgian and French [9].
  - The Expat.com Burundi forum has 1,673 members [9].
  - French nationals can register with the embassy's consular register [24].
  - **Named diaspora associations, Facebook/WhatsApp groups or returnee networks: not found in this pass.**
- **Climate in practice:** about 774 m altitude, hot and steady (highs about 29 °C, lows about 19 °C). The rainy season runs roughly October–April [6]. The dry season (June–September) brings power cuts as dam levels fall [18]. Rising lake levels threaten low-lying districts [19]. In November 2026 the government launched a 630 bn FBu El Niño response [25].
- **Watch out:** chronic fuel rationing; power cuts; slow internet; inflation in essentials; armed crime at night; grenade-attack history; political repression (see legal section); risk zones along the north-western border.

### Gitega

Political capital since Parliament's vote of 16 Jan 2019, with a three-year transfer of institutions planned [26]. About 130,000–135,000 people (2020), at 1,504 m on the central plateau [26][27].

- **Cost of living:** **rent and meal prices: not found.** Rents were already rising when it became the capital (Iwacu, 28 Feb 2019, old) [16]. In October 2026 a commune–butchers deal on meat prices broke down within days [28].
- **Work & money:** administration (ministries, parliament) and the Polytechnic University of Gitega (2014), plus five higher-education institutions [26][27]. Otherwise an agricultural market town. **Salaries: not found.**
- **Entrepreneurship & tech:** **no hub, incubator or startup activity found.**
- **Daily infrastructure:**
  - Fuel rationing was cut to 20 L for private vehicles and 50 L for public transport (Iwacu, 8 Oct 2026) [29].
  - When the weekly station list is published, races to the pumps cause fatal crashes; three people died on 6 Oct 2026 [29].
  - Water access was lost on a Nyamugari avenue after road works (Jun 2026) [28].
  - Upgrades to streets and pavements are uneven ("paysage urbain à double vitesse", Jun 2026) [28].
- **Health & schools:**
  - **Reference hospital: not named in sources found.**
  - **The École internationale de Gitega** (43 years old, about 736 pupils) was ordered closed in April 2026 in a dispute over state buildings. Its accounts were frozen and parents petitioned the Prime Minister (Iwacu, 10 Jul 2026) [30]. **Current status: not found.** This matters for families.
- **Safety:** no Gitega-specific warning in the FCDO or France Diplomatie pages read [1][2]. Country-wide advice still applies: no driving outside Bujumbura after dark, and unofficial roadblocks [4]. Street children are a growing concern (Iwacu, Jul 2026) [28].
- **Social life & vibe:**
  - Calmer and more provincial than Bujumbura. Local press covers noise disputes, such as a loudspeaker billboard looping ads from 7 am (Iwacu, 13 Nov 2026) [31].
  - Cultural heart of the old kingdom: National Museum, royal court (ibwami), karyenda drum sanctuaries [26].
  - Catholic, Anglican, Baptist and Pentecostal churches, and mosques [27].
  - **Nightlife: not found.**
- **Diaspora & expat community:** **not found.** It is likely tied to diplomatic and institutional staff as the transfer continues, but this is unverified.
- **Climate in practice:** cooler at altitude (highs about 26–28 °C). About 1,259 mm of rain a year, mostly October–April [26]. Ruvubu National Park is about 26 km away [26].
- **Watch out:** schooling uncertainty, fuel rationing, few services, and the institutional transfer still incomplete as of late 2025 [6].

### Ngozi (additional: university town in the north)

- Chief town of the new Butanyerera province [12]. It had 39,884 people in 2008, now outdated [32].
- Home to the private **University of Ngozi** (campus opened 1999) [32].
- Tea processing, a Rwanda–Burundi power interconnector, and a cool climate (highs about 21–24 °C) [32].
- It matters for students and academics and as a quieter northern base.
- FCDO advises against the RN6/RN10 roads west from Kayanza through Kibira forest, but not against Ngozi town itself [2].
- **Cost, schools and safety detail: not found.**

### Rumonge (additional: lakeside port in the south)

- About 36,000 people in 2008 [33]. Described as Burundi's fourth city and its main lake landing site, with a regional public hospital [33].
- It matters for anyone drawn to Lake Tanganyika life away from the capital.
- Wikipedia says 2025 attacks linked to the M23 reduced trade through the port. This is **single-source and unverified**, so treat it as low confidence [33].
- **Cost and services: not found.**

## Social atmosphere through a Big Five lens (interpretive, editorial)

No Hofstede or GLOBE scores exist for Burundi (the Hofstede tool returned nothing). Afrobarometer's latest Burundi round is **Round 6 (2014)** [34]: 62% favoured a two-term presidential limit, and 70% wanted media free from government interference. This is too old to describe today's mood. The lines below interpret documented daily conditions. They are not trait measurements.

**Bujumbura**
- **Openness:** suits people curious about a multilingual, regional crossroads (Kirundi, French, Swahili, English; Congolese, Rwandan and Tanzanian neighbours) [9]. The cultural scene is thinly documented.
- **Conscientiousness:** rewards planners. Fuel, electricity and water have to be organised: back-up power, Starlink, vehicle choices [17][18][23].
- **Extraversion:** cabaret culture and lakeside beaches give sociable people outlets [6][10]. The night-time safety advice limits spontaneous outings [2].
- **Agreeableness:** a socially conservative country with a strong religious presence [4][6]. Discretion on politics is prudent because of press restrictions and arrests (see below) [35][36].
- **Emotional stability (stress tolerance):** needed in high doses. Queues lasting weeks for fuel, inflation, cuts, and a grenade-attack history [2][10][17].

**Gitega**
- **Openness:** suits people interested in history and royal heritage (drum sanctuaries, museum) more than urban novelty [26].
- **Conscientiousness:** suits people at ease with administration, since the city's role is government.
- **Extraversion:** quieter; few documented leisure options.
- **Agreeableness:** a small-town setting where community ties matter (inferred, low confidence).
- **Emotional stability:** less street crime is documented than in Bujumbura. Schooling and fuel uncertainty remain stressful [29][30].

## Legal & sensitive context (each with a dated source)

- **LGBT:**
  - Same-sex sexual acts have been criminal since the Penal Code that took effect on 22 Apr 2009 (Article 567): 3 months to 2 years in prison plus a fine of 50,000–100,000 FBu [37]. HRW (World Report 2026) cites Article 590 of the current code: up to 2 years [35].
  - Same-sex marriage is constitutionally banned (Article 29) [37].
  - In December 2023, President Ndayishimiye said gay people "should be taken to stadiums and be stoned" (BBC, 29 Dec 2023, via Wikipedia) [37].
  - FCDO (current 14 Nov 2026): "Same-sex sexual acts are illegal", and showing affection in public "may receive unwanted and negative attention" [4].
- **Political climate:**
  - The ruling CNDD-FDD won 96.5% in the 2025 elections, held "without any real opposition" [35].
  - UN experts recorded at least 892 arbitrary detentions, 605 extrajudicial killings and 58 enforced disappearances between Jan 2024 and May 2025, mostly linked to state agents or the Imbonerakure youth league (HRW, World Report 2026) [35].
  - FCDO notes arrests and disappearances of people seen as opposing the government [4].
  - Press freedom: RSF ranked Burundi 119/180 in 2026. Journalist Sandra Muhoza was released in April 2026 after time served [36].
- **Conflict zones:**
  - The DRC border, the Rusizi/Ruzizi plain, Cibitoke, Bubanza and Kibira forest are flagged (FCDO 21 May 2026; France Diplomatie 17 Sep 2026) [1][2].
  - More than 70,000 Congolese refugees had arrived by September 2025, and hundreds were detained in September 2025 (HRW) [35].
- **Women:** no dedicated source found. HRW notes sexual violence, including against children, survivors' limited access to services, and harassment of women journalists [35]. **A reliable source on women's daily experience in cities: not found.**
- **Racism, colourism and treatment of diaspora or foreigners:** **no reliable source found.** The diaspora's relationship with the state is politically loaded: much of civil society works from exile [35]. Returnees should not assume neutrality.
